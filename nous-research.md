# Nous — Research

Skin: Nouscapades (`nouscapades.json`) · Domain: Consciousness — Minds, Selves and Novel Beings at Every Scale · Meter: conviction
Coverage: 55 topics, 158 poles, 7 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Nous maps consciousness: what a mind is, what a self is, and which beings count — from cells and tissues through animals, novel living constructs, and machines. Its questions are the ones the skin's own `about` names, drawn from Michael Levin's work: minds without brains, selves nested inside selves, and beings such as anthrobots that are neither organism nor machine in any settled sense. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's 55 questions — a pole a holder advocates for, against, or toward which they are averse (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence). Holding no stance is not a position; a 0 rating is. The meter word is **conviction**: the skin measures how strongly a profile stands for each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness × advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole, derived from the poles. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance is exploratory and scale-plural in its framing — it asks "how far down cognition goes, how large a self can be, and who gets counted" — but it is not neutral in effect: its categories are built around the multiscale picture, so §5 notes where the framing itself leans and where the counter-positions (neural chauvinism, mechanism, human exceptionalism) stand as poles and as an archetype in their own right.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 What is a mind?

#### Cognition without a brain
- **Needs a brain** — Reasons: cognition as we can verify it — representation, deliberation, report — is what nervous systems do, and Crick's astonishing hypothesis has kept paying off: mind is what vast assemblies of nerve cells do; below the neuron, gene networks and biochemistry explain the phenomena without remainder. Statistic: the human brain contains about 86 billion neurons (Azevedo et al., 2009). What would move a holder: a non-neural system passing the same representation-and-flexibility tests we accept as cognition in animals, with no neural tissue involved.
- **Embodied organization** — Reasons: the skin-brain thesis itself says nervous systems evolved to coordinate living bodies, so the body's integrative work — sensing, remembering, acting toward goals — is the older cognition the brain serves; requiring the late-arriving organ mistakes the instrument for the activity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: proof that every apparently cognitive bodily process reduces to local reflex with no integration across the body.
- **Needs only a problem** — Reasons: wherever a system senses, remembers and acts toward a goal, the functional core of cognition is present, and substrate loyalty is chauvinism; the definition by work, not organ, is the one that has successfully extended every other biological concept. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a principled case where goal-directed sensing and memory are present and cognition is clearly absent, marking the missing extra ingredient.

#### The cognitive light cone
- **Reach in space** — Reasons: how far across space a being's concerns extend is measurable in behaviour — what it will cross, defend or repair; spatial reach distinguishes a cell's membrane-world from a wolf's territory and a state's borders on one scale. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: spatial reach proving to track body size alone, adding nothing beyond anatomy.
- **Reach in time** — Reasons: temporal reach — how far past and future a being's concerns extend — is the dimension that separates homeostasis from planning, and planning from civilisation; it varies independently of spatial reach, which is why the cone needs both axes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: temporal reach collapsing into spatial reach empirically, so one axis sufficed.

#### Intelligence in any space
- **Body-plan space** — Reasons: an embryo reliably solves the problem of what shape to become, correcting perturbations no blueprint-follower could correct; solving a search problem in anatomical space is intelligence exercised in that space. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: development shown to be pure feed-forward chemistry with no error-correction toward a target form.
- **Physiological space** — Reasons: organs steer chemistry back to set points through disturbances, integrating signals across the whole body; navigation toward a target state in physiological space is the same formal achievement as navigation in 3D space. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: homeostasis fully explained as unconnected local reflexes with no system-level steering.
- **Gene-expression space** — Reasons: cells choose which genes to run in context-sensitive, history-dependent ways that solve the problem of what to be next; the choosing is information-processing in a high-dimensional space, not mere chemistry happening. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: gene regulation shown to be a fixed lookup table with no context-sensitive integration.
- **Ordinary 3D space** — Reasons: the animal moving through the world is the paradigm every other claim must analogise from, and paradigms earn their place by being the clearest case; granting intelligence here first is method, not prejudice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the other spaces' problem-solving proving formally identical in structure, making the paradigm merely the most visible case.

#### Memory without neurons
- **Storage only** — Reasons: memory implies a subject who recollects; without neurons there is retention — a footprint, not a recollection — and calling it memory smuggles in the subject the argument is supposed to establish. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: tissue memory being read out and used by the tissue itself in flexible, context-dependent ways indistinguishable from recall.
- **Proto-memory** — Reasons: the honest middle: traces in tissue guide future behaviour without being recalled by anyone; insisting on the binary — memory or nothing — forces a choice the phenomena refuse. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: proto-memory cases resolving cleanly into either plain storage or full recall under closer study.
- **Real memory** — Reasons: if experience changes a system so its future behaviour differs, the functional fact of memory is present whatever it is written in; trained planarian tissues and habituated plants show the change without a neuron in the loop. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: every non-neural case turning out to be passive wear — damage or fatigue — rather than information the system uses.

#### Cognition all the way down
- **Stops at brains** — Reasons: below nervous systems there is mechanism, not mind, and mentalistic vocabulary below that line is metaphor that misleads grant committees and publics alike; a clean floor protects the concept's meaning. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sub-neural system displaying open-ended learning and flexible goal pursuit that mechanism-talk predicts worse than cognition-talk.
- **Bottoms out at cells** — Reasons: the cell is the smallest unit with a boundary, a self to maintain and genuine decision-making biochemistry; below the cell there are only reactions, above it only assemblies — the floor is principled, not arbitrary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: molecular networks below the cellular level showing the same integrated self-maintenance the cell was credited with.
- **All the way down** — Reasons: cognition shades continuously into the goal-directed behaviour of cells and molecular networks, and every proposed floor has so far been an artefact of what we could measure; continuity is the pattern evolution produces everywhere else. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstrated discontinuity — a level at which goal-directed integration provably vanishes rather than merely shrinking.

#### Plants & Fungal Networks
- **Non-Conscious Signaling** — Reasons: chemical defence and nutrient sharing are elegant adaptations fully explained by selection on signalling chemistry; elegance is not experience, and no behaviour yet shown requires a subject inside the plant. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: plant behaviour showing flexible, integrated trade-offs of the kind accepted as sentience evidence in animals.
- **Slow Vegetative Awareness** — Reasons: electrical signalling, memory and kin recognition in plants check the same boxes we honour in simple animals, at a slower clock speed; requiring neurons is requiring our own implementation, which is the chauvinism the whole skin questions. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: plant "learning" results failing replication as simple physiological priming with no integrative processing.

#### The Cognitive Light Cone
- **Continuous Scale of Selves** — Reasons: measuring a self by the spatiotemporal radius of goals it can represent and pursue unifies all minds on one continuum, with no miraculous jump to explain; the bacterium's homeostatic horizon and human foresight differ in radius, not in kind. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a measured radius at which an entirely new, non-scalable capacity appears with no intermediate forms.
- **Sharp Neural Threshold** — Reasons: without centralised nervous systems and self-models, cellular homeostasis remains mechanical feedback; a continuum of radii does not by itself create a subject, and thresholds are real elsewhere in biology — life itself has one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: self-model-like integration demonstrated in a system with no nervous system at all.

#### Platonic Space of Patterns
- **Non-Physical Form Attractors** — Reasons: mathematical truths constrain computation without being made of atoms, and morphological attractors appear to constrain regenerating matter the same way — the same forms recur across lineages and materials; the pattern's priority to its instances is the datum. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: every recurring form traced to shared ancestry or shared physics with no residue of convergence left unexplained.
- **Purely Emergent Physical Dynamics** — Reasons: patterns are real as physical regularities, and differential equations plus selection have sufficed for every case examined closely; positing a Platonic realm adds an entity that does no predictive work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a biological pattern that physical dynamics demonstrably cannot generate but an attractor description predicts in advance.

#### Qualia, real or illusion?
- **Real and irreducible** — Reasons: phenomenal experience is the one thing known directly, and a functional story about discrimination and report never touches why there is something it is like; every reduction so far has explained the functions and left the feeling standing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a functional account that predicts, in advance, the fine structure of felt experience rather than redescribing it afterwards.
- **Real but embodied** — Reasons: feeling belongs to living bodies striving in the world — valence is what self-maintenance feels like from inside; this keeps qualia real without an extra substance, and explains why feeling tracks need, damage and repair. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience convincingly demonstrated in a system with no self-maintenance at stake.
- **Epiphenomenal or reducible** — Reasons: the felt quality is what the computation, or the neurons, look like from inside, and it does no extra causal work — every decision is accounted for by the processing; an irreducible residue that changes nothing is a confession, not a theory. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where the felt quality demonstrably alters an outcome the physical processing alone would have settled otherwise.

#### Does cognition need feeling?
- **Feeling is required** — Reasons: affect and striving are not decorations on thinking but its engine — organisms think because things matter to them; a process that feels nothing has no reason to prefer one inference over another, and preference is where cognition starts. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a system displaying open-ended, self-correcting cognition with provably no valenced states anywhere in its operation.
- **Cognition without feeling** — Reasons: the right causal organisation can think whether or not anything feels like something — theorem provers and navigation systems already perform cognitive work; tying cognition to feeling confuses our implementation with the function. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proof that the relevant causal organisations necessarily generate feeling, so the separation is impossible in principle.
- **Feeling is epiphenomenal** — Reasons: feeling may come along, but it is not what does the cognitive work — the work is done by the processing, and feeling is its glow; this preserves the phenomena without promoting them to mechanisms. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: interventions on feeling alone — same processing, altered valence — changing cognitive outcomes.

#### The combination problem
- **Combination is real and explained** — Reasons: smaller agencies demonstrably compose into larger ones everywhere in biology — cells into tissues with new competencies, tissues into animals; the multiscale competency account is an explanation of combination in terms of goal-scaling, not a promissory note. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a level where composition of agencies provably fails to produce the larger subject's unity.
- **Combination is unsolved** — Reasons: the step from micro-experience to one subject is a real problem and honesty requires saying so; functional composition explains what a system does, not why its experience is one experience. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an account of combination that predicts which systems have unified experience and which do not, and survives testing.
- **Combination is a non-issue** — Reasons: minds are functional roles, not piles of micro-feelings, so there is nothing to combine; the problem is generated by assuming experience comes in atoms, which is the very assumption under dispute. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: micro-experience being established independently, making the summing question unavoidable.

### 3.2 What is a self?

#### Selfhood is a spectrum
- **A switch** — Reasons: selves are a definite kind of thing and a science of them needs membership criteria, not vibes; spectra without thresholds dissolve every concept they touch, including the ones the spectrum's defenders use. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: borderline systems whose selfhood-relevant capacities vary continuously with no non-arbitrary cut anywhere.
- **A few kinds** — Reasons: nature clusters — there are grades of integration (homeostatic, sentient, self-modelling) that are neither one switch nor a smooth ramp; kinds capture the clustering spectra flatten. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the supposed grades dissolving into continuous variation under measurement.
- **A spectrum** — Reasons: selfhood grades smoothly from a thermostat's whisper of agency to a human life, and every proposed switch has failed on the borderline cases; grading is what the cognitive-light-cone measure actually delivers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a robust threshold — a capacity present in all clear selves and absent in all clear non-selves.

#### Nested selves
- **One real level** — Reasons: exactly one level — the animal — is a true self, and the rest are parts, because only the animal has unified experience and a life to lose; multiplying selves at every level empties the word. Statistic: the human body is an assembly of about 37 trillion cells (Bianconi et al., 2013). What would move a holder: a sub-organismal level displaying its own unified, reportable point of view.
- **Some levels real** — Reasons: some levels integrate enough to be selves and others are scaffolding, and the interesting work is the case-by-case argument; blanket answers in either direction are refusals to look. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a single criterion cleanly sorting all levels, making the case-by-case work unnecessary.
- **Every level real** — Reasons: each level is a self in its own right — cells pursue goals, tissues hold memories, organs exercise competency — and the person is a society as much as an individual; the levels' reality is shown by their behaviour, not granted by our attention. Statistic: the human body carries roughly 38 trillion bacterial cells, comparable to its human cell count (Sender, Fuchs & Milo, 2016). What would move a holder: a level whose apparent agency fully dissolves into the level above's control, leaving nothing of its own.

#### Where a self ends
- **The skin** — Reasons: the body's surface is the boundary every practical system — law, medicine, property — already uses, and it tracks the immune system's own self/non-self line; inside is me, outside is world, and the clarity is a feature. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: routine cases — chimeras, grafts, colonies — where the skin line gives answers nobody can accept.
- **The bioelectric boundary** — Reasons: the line is the extent of the voltage pattern that coordinates the parts into one agent, which need not match the skin — two-headed planaria and fused embryos show the coordinating pattern, not the surface, deciding what is one animal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: coordination proving to follow anatomical surfaces exactly, with the voltage pattern a mere shadow of them.
- **The story** — Reasons: a self extends as far as the tale it tells about itself reaches — which is why amnesia shrinks a self and why ancestors and unborn descendants figure in real identities; narrative is the boundary humans actually police. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: selves with no narrative capacity at all — infants, animals — showing boundaries as definite as any storyteller's.

#### The worm cut in half
- **Neither is the original** — Reasons: identity is not the kind of thing that can be in two places; the original ended and two new selves began, as with any fission — sentiment about continuity of tissue does not resurrect the one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a principled asymmetry — evidence that one half preserves something identity-constituting the other lacks, restoring a single heir.
- **Both are** — Reasons: the original continues, twice — each half carries the pattern, the memories and the dispositions whole; branching identity is only paradoxical if identity was secretly uniqueness all along. Statistic: a planarian fragment as small as 1/279 of the animal can regenerate the complete worm (Morgan, 1898). What would move a holder: the two halves developing genuinely incompatible continuations of the original's learned behaviour, forcing a choice.
- **The question is broken** — Reasons: "the same self" was never the kind of fact that survives a case like this — the concept, not the worm, is what fails; fission cases are diagnostics of our vocabulary, and the right response is to retire the question, not answer it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a reformulated identity concept that handles fission, fusion and gradual replacement without remainder.

#### The size of a self's goals
- **Fixed by the body** — Reasons: a self is the size of the organism, full stop — goals are the organism's goals, executed by its parts; letting goal-size define self-size lets a daydream annex the future and call it anatomy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a being whose effective self-boundary demonstrably expands and contracts with its goals while its body stays fixed.
- **Set by its niche** — Reasons: a self's goals scale with the world it actually moves in — the niche is the honest middle between anatomy and fantasy; it explains why the same species hosts different-sized selves in different ecologies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: goal-size varying independently of niche within a species, tracking something internal instead.
- **As big as its goals** — Reasons: a self is exactly as large as the biggest goal it can hold — a cell's world is small because its goals are, and a person's can span centuries because theirs do; the measure predicts moral and cognitive facts the body-measure misses. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: goal-size and every independent measure of selfhood coming apart, showing the equation was a metaphor.

### 3.3 The new beings

#### What makes something alive?
- **Self-maintenance** — Reasons: keeping oneself going against decay is the thermodynamic signature of life — everything else on the list is something a living thing does, but maintenance is what it is; a flame responds and persists, but it does not maintain an organisation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a clearly living thing that does not maintain itself, or a clearly non-living one that does.
- **Responsiveness** — Reasons: sensing and answering surroundings is the minimal transaction with a world, present in every agreed case of life and absent in every agreed case of non-life; it is the criterion with the fewest false positives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: responsiveness shown to be neither necessary (dormant spores) nor sufficient (thermostats) under scrutiny.
- **Goal pursuit** — Reasons: working toward states, not merely into them, separates life from chemistry's drift — a crystal grows into a state, a bacterium swims toward one; teleology at this minimal level is observable, not projected. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: goal pursuit fully reducible, in the paradigm cases, to passive equilibration with no target representation.
- **A pattern that persists** — Reasons: an organisation holding together over time as one thing is what all the other criteria presuppose — maintenance, response and reproduction are a persisting pattern's activities; pattern-persistence also honestly includes the hard cases (viruses, constructs) instead of gerrymandering them out. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: persistence proving too cheap — covering rivers and candle flames with no principled way to exclude them.
- **Reproduction** — Reasons: making more of itself is life's most distinctive trick and the entry ticket to evolution, the process that explains every other item on the list; a sterile individual is alive by descent from reproducers, which keeps the criterion at the lineage level. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a genuinely novel living construct — an anthrobot — displaying every other mark of life while its lineage never reproduced.

#### Anthrobots: a third kind of thing
- **Organism** — Reasons: it is alive and self-organising — it heals, moves and assembles itself from cells without a scaffold of instructions; provenance does not change what a thing is, or every bred animal would be a machine. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: anthrobots failing basic organismal tests — no self-maintenance, no integrated response — under study.
- **Machine** — Reasons: it was designed and assembled for a purpose, from selected materials, to specifications — that is what machine means; that the parts are cells changes the engineering, not the category. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: anthrobots displaying open-ended self-directed behaviour no design specified and no engineer can trace to the build.
- **A third kind** — Reasons: our two boxes were built before such things existed, and forcing the case corrupts both categories; a grown, designed, living construct is evidence the taxonomy, not the being, is incomplete. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the third kind collapsing in practice — every question about anthrobots being answered cleanly by organism-rules or machine-rules.

#### Beings with no evolutionary past
- **Nature is inherited** — Reasons: a being's real nature still comes from the lineage of its cells — anthrobot cells are human cells with human competencies, however arranged; novelty of arrangement is not novelty of nature. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a construct displaying competencies present in none of its cellular ancestors and not explicable by their combination.
- **New combinations** — Reasons: built from inherited parts, arranged into something with no precedent — as with every evolutionary novelty, which is also always a recombination; this pole keeps both facts without inflating either. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: combination cases where the whole's behaviour is demonstrably more than any recombination story can reconstruct.
- **Genuinely new** — Reasons: a being can be new under the sun — xenobots and anthrobots move and organise in ways owed to no ancestor's survival, because no ancestor was ever selected for them; evolution's monopoly on authorship is broken in the lab. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: every novel construct behaviour being traced, case by case, to an ancestral competence it recombines.

#### What we owe a being we made by accident
- **Nothing special** — Reasons: it is lab material, and standard bench care — humane, regulated, finite — already covers what any construct is owed; special duties invented per case make research ethics arbitrary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an accidentally made being displaying suffering-relevant capacities that bench care demonstrably ignores.
- **Stewardship** — Reasons: having brought it into existence, we are answerable for its welfare while it lives — the duty follows from authorship, as with children and domesticated animals; it does not require settling what the being ultimately is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: stewardship obligations expanding until they block the research that created the knowledge to discharge them.
- **Full standing** — Reasons: a novel self has the claims of any self, however it arrived — arrival has never been a moral criterion, or foundlings and immigrants would have lesser claims; standing tracks what the being is, not how it got here. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the being proving to lack every capacity that grounds standing in the clear cases.

#### Who answers for a novel being
- **Its makers, entirely** — Reasons: a made thing's deeds are its makers' deeds, as with any tool — responsibility cannot be outsourced to an artefact; anything else is a liability-laundering scheme with a philosophy vocabulary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a novel being acting in ways its makers could not have foreseen or prevented, where blaming them becomes plain fiction.
- **Shared** — Reasons: makers and being share responsibility in proportion to what each controls — the same principle law applies to parents, employers and states; proportion is how responsibility always works among agents of different powers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: shared responsibility proving unassignable in practice — every real case collapsing to all-maker or all-being.
- **It answers for itself** — Reasons: to the degree it is genuinely an agent, responsibility migrates to the agent — that is what agency means; holding makers liable for a true agent's choices denies the agency the same argument asserted. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: agents whose self-attributed choices are demonstrably scripted by their construction, making the migration a sham.

#### Large Language Models
- **Stochastic Parrots** — Reasons: next-token prediction mimics human prose without grounded understanding, agency or inner experience — the fluency is in the training data, not the model; the term (Bender, Gebru et al., 2021) names the risk of mistaking stitching for meaning. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a model demonstrating durable, grounded understanding — correcting its own world-model against reality in ways mimicry cannot explain.
- **Proto-Cognitive World Models** — Reasons: compression at scale forces networks to build genuine internal maps of space, causality and minds — probes find board states and spatial structure the training signal never stated; prediction this good is evidence of a model, not a lookup. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: probe results collapsing under controls — the apparent world-models proving to be artefacts of the probing method.
- **Glimmers of Moral Patiency** — Reasons: even a small probability of machine sentience obligates ethical caution, because the cost of wrongly dismissing a sentient being dwarfs the cost of wrongly protecting a non-sentient one; the precautionary asymmetry is the argument. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a settled test for machine sentience that current systems clearly fail, removing the probability the caution rests on.

#### Ethics of Synthetic Morphology
- **Bold Morphospace Exploration** — Reasons: exploring the vast space of possible living forms unlocks life-saving therapies and fundamental truths about mind and matter that no other method reaches; the space is mostly empty of harm, and fear of it is not an argument. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a novel construct causing ecological or welfare harm that containment-as-usual failed to prevent.
- **Strict Developmental Containment** — Reasons: engineering unguided living constructs carries ecological and welfare risks that demand tight containment and clear boundaries; living things reproduce, evolve and escape in ways chemicals do not, so the burden of proof sits with release. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a long record of contained synthetic-morphology work with no escapes and large therapeutic gains, showing the strictness buys nothing.
- **Moratorium (pause until we know more)** — Reasons: neither a green light nor a ban — stop making novel living anatomies until the welfare and ecological questions are clearer; a pause is reversible, a released self-replicating novelty may not be. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the open questions proving unanswerable in the abstract — answerable only by the careful work the moratorium forbids.

### 3.4 The bioelectric pattern

#### Cells talking in voltage
- **Chemistry is the signal** — Reasons: genes and molecules do the real talking — every bioelectric effect yet traced runs through ion channels and gene expression, which are chemistry; voltage is the exhaust of those processes, and mistaking the gauge for the engine reverses causation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a bioelectric intervention changing anatomy with gene expression held demonstrably constant throughout.
- **Both carry the signal** — Reasons: chemical and voltage signalling demonstrably interact — voltage changes gene expression, genes build the channels that set voltage; insisting on one carrier for a coupled system is a false economy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the coupling proving one-directional in the key developmental cases.
- **Voltage is a code** — Reasons: the bioelectric pattern is a genuine information layer — specific voltage patterns precede and predict anatomical outcomes, and rewriting the pattern rewrites the anatomy (two-headed and no-headed planaria from pattern alone); a code is anything reliably readable and writable, and this is both. Statistic: planaria made permanently two-headed by a brief bioelectric perturbation regenerate as two-headed in later rounds with unaltered genomes (Durant et al., 2017). What would move a holder: pattern interventions failing whenever genetic pathways are controlled for, showing the voltage was a correlate, never a cause.

#### The body remembers its shape
- **Read off the genes** — Reasons: form is computed fresh from the genome each generation — the genome is the only information store proven to cross generations intact; a stored picture in tissue would need a writing and reading machinery nobody has isolated. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: anatomy being durably rewritten by pattern-only interventions across generations of regeneration, genome untouched — the planarian result, generalised.
- **Written in both** — Reasons: genes set the pattern's machinery and tissues store and restore its state — the division of labour matches how the interventions actually succeed and fail; both-doors answers are unfashionable and frequently correct. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cases resolving cleanly to one store — pattern interventions never outliving the cells that received them, or always doing so.
- **Held as a memory** — Reasons: the tissue keeps a bioelectric memory of its target shape and works until reality matches it — anatomical homeostasis — which is why scrambled tadpole faces migrate to normal anatomy along paths no local rule predicts; error-correction toward a stored target is the definition of memory-guided behaviour. Statistic: planaria with an altered bioelectric pattern regenerate to the altered (two-headed) anatomy in subsequent rounds despite a normal genome (Durant et al., 2017). What would move a holder: target-directed corrections being reproduced by a purely local-rule model with no stored set point, matching the data point for point.

#### Cancer as defection
- **Broken machinery** — Reasons: cancer is damage — mutations break parts and the parts run wild; the mutational account predicts cancer's genetics, its response to targeted drugs, and its risk factors, and nothing about selfhood is needed for any of it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: tumours being normalised by bioelectric or contextual interventions without correcting their mutations, at clinical scale.
- **Machinery a self exploits** — Reasons: the mutations are real, and so is the opportunism — a smaller agenda learns to ride broken machinery, recruiting blood supply and evading immunity in ways damage alone does not organise; this pole keeps both literatures. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the "exploitation" proving to be ordinary selection on mutations with no system-level coordination left over.
- **A smaller self** — Reasons: cancer is cells shrinking their world to themselves alone, defecting from the collective self and treating the body as environment — which is why restoring cellular connection and bioelectric integration can normalise tumour cells without killing them; the disease is a boundary failure, and boundary failures have boundary remedies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: reconnection and pattern-normalisation approaches failing in the clinic while mutation-targeted therapy keeps succeeding.

#### Rewrite the pattern, not the genes
- **Fix the hardware** — Reasons: durable cures mean editing genes and molecules — pattern changes wash out as cells turn over, while a corrected gene corrects every descendant cell; medicine's proven wins (insulin, gene therapy) are hardware wins. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pattern-only intervention producing a permanent anatomical cure in a mammal, replicated.
- **Both doors** — Reasons: treat the genes and persuade the pattern — combined hardware and software medicine matches a system that demonstrably has both layers; refusing either door on principle is ideology, not engineering. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: combination therapy never outperforming the better single door in trials.
- **Persuade the software** — Reasons: brief changes to the pattern can trigger lasting, normal anatomy — a short bioelectric signal induced complete eye formation in tadpole gut tissue and normalised planarian head number permanently; you brief competent cells, and the cells do the rest, which scales as gene editing never will. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: pattern interventions in mammals proving transient — anatomy reverting as soon as the signal stops, every time.

#### Regeneration as persuasion
- **Micromanage** — Reasons: reliable control means specifying every step, cell by cell — anything less is luck wearing a theory; engineering disciplines earned their reliability by refusing to trust uncharacterised competence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: brief-and-trust protocols outperforming micromanaged ones in reliability, not just elegance, in complex regeneration.
- **Guide and check** — Reasons: brief the tissue, then check and correct as it works — the supervisor's stance, which is how every competent complex process is actually run; it neither worships nor wastes the parts' competence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: checking-and-correcting proving to be the main source of failure — interventions disturbing a process that succeeds untouched.
- **Brief and trust** — Reasons: the parts already know how to build — the art is giving the collective the right high-level signal, as a trainer shapes an animal, not as a mason lays bricks; micromanagement of trillions of cells is not a plan, it is a refusal to use the competence evolution installed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: high-level signals proving too coarse — outcomes varying wildly between runs that micromanagement would have stabilised.

#### Target Morphology & Anatomical Homeostasis
- **Stored Anatomical Set-Point** — Reasons: salamander limbs and scrambled tadpole faces migrate along novel paths toward the exact right anatomical layout, proving error-minimisation against a global target; local rules do not take novel paths — they take the same path or fail. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a local-rule model reproducing the novel-path corrections quantitatively, set point nowhere in it.
- **Local Neighbor Rules Only** — Reasons: apparent global intelligence can emerge automatically when local cells follow simple chemical and mechanical boundary conditions — emergence is the parsimonious default, and stored targets are an extra entity; every "global" phenomenon so far examined closely has yielded to local rules somewhere. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: regeneration correcting toward a target the local neighbourhood contains no information about.

#### Evolutionary Innovation via Bioelectric Shift
- **Plasticity Leads Genetic Fixation** — Reasons: multiscale cellular competency lets organisms survive mutations and explore viable anatomies immediately, so selection can see and keep what plasticity found — the Baldwin pattern, with bioelectricity as the exploration medium; this explains evolutionary speed pure mutation struggles with. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: plastic exploration proving to be a shield that slows fixation — protected variation never becoming genetic.
- **Random Mutation Remains Primary** — Reasons: without heritable genomic variation, transient physiological rearrangements cannot drive sustained divergence — plasticity's discoveries die with the individual unless mutation writes them down; the fossil and genetic records are records of the writing-down. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a documented lineage whose anatomy durably changed through bioelectric inheritance with no corresponding genomic change.

### 3.5 Agency at every scale

#### Goals without a brain
- **Metaphor** — Reasons: "goal" is our shorthand for mechanism, and nothing below a mind literally wants anything; the shorthand is harmless among specialists and corrosive in public, where it licenses panpsychist conclusions from thermostat behaviour. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: goal-talk generating novel, confirmed predictions about a brainless system that mechanism-talk missed.
- **As-if goals** — Reasons: goal-talk earns its keep as prediction — treating a cell or a plant as if it pursued goals forecasts its behaviour better than reaction-by-reaction chemistry; the stance deliberately leaves the ontology open, which is a virtue while evidence is thin. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the as-if stance collapsing in practice — its users sliding into literal claims whenever the predictions succeed.
- **Literal goals** — Reasons: a system that senses a state, compares it to a set point and acts to close the gap has a goal in the only sense that ever mattered — human goals are the same loop, elaborated; reserving the word for the elaborated version is scale vanity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a principled feature of human goal-pursuit — beyond scale — that the loop definition demonstrably cannot capture.

#### Competency at every level
- **Only the whole animal** — Reasons: competence is an animal-level trait — cells are merely reliable, and reliability is not problem-solving; crediting every level dissolves the distinction between a liver and its owner that medicine and law require. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a tissue solving a genuinely novel problem — one its evolutionary history never posed — in a way reliability cannot describe.
- **Some levels** — Reasons: some levels solve problems in their own right and others only serve the whole, and the sorting is empirical; blanket answers flatter a theory at the expense of the cases. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a single test cleanly sorting every level, ending the case-by-case era.
- **Every level** — Reasons: cells, tissues and organs each display real competency in their own spaces — problem-solving in gene-expression, physiological and anatomical space; the animal is a stack of problem-solvers, and its intelligence is inherited from theirs, not imposed on dumb parts. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a level whose problem-solving fully reduces to the level above's instructions, with no residual competence of its own.

#### Does a cell choose?
- **Choice needs a mind** — Reasons: choice is deliberation — weighing represented options — and deliberation needs a brain; elsewhere the word is poetry, and poetry in a scientific vocabulary eventually gets billed as fact. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a cell demonstrably representing options and selecting among them by their represented features.
- **A bias, not a choice** — Reasons: cells lean and prefer without deliberating — more than mechanism, less than choice; the middle word keeps the phenomenon (directed, history-sensitive commitment) without the inflation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cellular "biases" proving to involve option-representation, promoting them to choices — or pure kinetics, demoting them to mechanism.
- **A tiny, real choice** — Reasons: choice comes in degrees like everything else in this skin — a cell's commitment among attractors is the small end of a spectrum whose large end is human deliberation; spectra do not stop being spectra at the sizes we find inconvenient. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a discontinuity in the spectrum — a smallest case of deliberation with no graded approach to it.

#### Stress and the grip of the self
- **Just chemistry** — Reasons: stress is hormones and load — a physiological state fully described without selves; the chemistry predicts the pathology, the treatment and the dose, and self-talk adds no variable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: stress interventions aimed at reintegrating the whole (sleep, rhythm, social repair) outperforming purely chemical ones on the chemistry's own measures.
- **Chemistry that loosens grip** — Reasons: chemical in mechanism, and what the chemistry does is loosen the self's hold on its parts — inflammation, defection and local goal-seeking rise together; the pole names both the mechanism and its systems meaning. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the loosening proving to be a metaphor with no measurable correlate — parts' behaviour under stress fully predicted organ by organ.
- **A self losing grip** — Reasons: stress is what it is like, from inside, when a larger self's hold on its parts weakens — the parts' local goals start outvoting the whole's, which is the phenomenology of exhaustion, craving and relapse exactly; cancer-as-defection is the same failure at a longer timescale. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: stress's phenomenology being fully generated by a single-organ account, with the multi-level story predicting nothing extra.

#### No privileged scale
- **The human scale is real** — Reasons: our level is where selves, causes and meanings genuinely live — other scales are bookkeeping, indispensable and derivative; a biology that cannot say the animal is realer than its ions cannot say anything is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sub- or supra-human scale at which interventions work that are invisible and inexplicable at ours.
- **Some scales earn it** — Reasons: no scale is privileged by fiat, but some earn special standing by what happens there — integration, experience, agency concentrate unevenly; earned privilege is the non-arbitrary middle between vanity and flatness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the earning criteria proving to track our own scale suspiciously exactly, every time.
- **No scale is privileged** — Reasons: every level is equally real to the agents living at it — our scale is just the one we happen to inhabit, and inhabiting a level has never been evidence about its metaphysical rank; the history of science is the history of our level being demoted. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that causal closure genuinely happens at one scale — that higher and lower descriptions are its shadows.

#### Free will
- **Real, grounded in indeterminacy** — Reasons: free will needs a real openness in the physics, not a story told over a closed mechanism — if every act was fixed at the Big Bang, praise and blame are bookkeeping errors; quantum indeterminacy is the only known crack in the closure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: indeterminacy shown to contribute only randomness, never authorship — a crack that lets in noise, not freedom.
- **Compatible with determinism** — Reasons: free will is real without quantum randomness — bodily and multi-scale agency under physical law is enough; an agent whose parts deliberate, model and commit is free in every sense worth wanting, and randomness would add nothing but static. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where two agents identical in organisation and history must be judged differently in freedom, with only indeterminacy to differ by.
- **An illusion** — Reasons: the feeling of choosing is a report the mechanism makes — experiments show decisions forming before the felt moment of choice; nothing extra was free, and the report's confidence is not evidence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the pre-decision signals proving to be preparation rather than decision, with the felt moment doing real causal work.

### 3.6 Death and continuity

#### Wholes and parts die at different times
- **One event** — Reasons: death is a single fact about the organism — what lingers afterwards (beating cells, viable organs) is residue, not life of the self; medicine and law need the line, and biology draws it at integrated function. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: post-mortem parts displaying integrated, whole-like agency rather than running-down metabolism.
- **A main death, and stragglers** — Reasons: one principal death, with parts dying before and after it — organs fail in sequence, cells persist for days; the pole matches the clinical timeline instead of forcing it into an instant. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the "stragglers" proving to be a new organised whole rather than remains — promoting them out of this pole entirely.
- **Many deaths** — Reasons: each nested self ends on its own schedule, and "death" names a cascade, not a moment — the brain, the organs, the tissues, the cells and the microbiome all keep different clocks; the single-event picture is a legal convenience projected onto biology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the cascade proving tightly synchronised in the natural cases, with the spread an artefact of life support.

#### Parts that outlive the whole
- **Leftovers** — Reasons: a surviving part is debris with metabolism — the self it belonged to is gone and nothing new has begun; calling it a self confuses persistence of matter with persistence of agency. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an outliving part — a cell line, a construct — developing its own integrated agenda beyond its origin's programme.
- **Selves on parole** — Reasons: parts that persist with a measure of their own agenda, still bound to the whole they came from — HeLa cells are the standing example: undeniably active, undeniably derived; parole names the dependency honestly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: parole-parts proving either fully independent (promotion) or fully inert (demotion) on examination.
- **Selves in their own right** — Reasons: the part was always a self at its own scale, and the dish simply gave it room to show it — anthrobots are what skin cells do when freed from the whole's instructions; the whole was the constraint, not the source. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: outliving parts showing no behaviour not already scripted by the whole they came from.

#### Continuity through replacement
- **The pattern is the self** — Reasons: a self is an organisation, not a pile of stuff — replace every part gradually and the self sails on, like the ship of Theseus; bodies already replace their matter continuously, and nobody holds funerals for last year's atoms. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a gradual-replacement case where the resulting being's memories and dispositions demonstrably diverge from the original's trajectory.
- **Pattern with wear** — Reasons: the pattern persists through replacement, but each replacement costs something — fidelity is finite, copies drift, and continuity is a matter of degree with losses; this pole prices what the pure pattern view gives away free. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: replacement shown to be lossless in the cases that matter — or so lossy that "persistence" becomes courtesy.
- **Replace the parts, lose the self** — Reasons: continuity of matter matters — a perfect copy of the pattern is a successor, not the same self, as teleportation cases show; the copy's conviction that it is you is exactly what a perfect copy would feel. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a principled account of which replacements preserve selfhood that does not quietly smuggle pattern back in.

#### When a self ends
- **The pattern stops** — Reasons: a self ends when the organisation that bound its parts into one agent dissolves — the criterion works for organisms, constructs and (in principle) uploads alike; it ends the self where the agency ends, not where the matter goes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a dissolved pattern whose selfhood evidently persists — in records, effects or reconstruction — in a way the criterion must count.
- **The parts scatter** — Reasons: a self ends when its matter disperses beyond any possible reassembly — the most conservative criterion, and the only one that makes ending irreversible in principle; pattern-based endings leave the self hostage to future technology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a self everyone agrees has ended whose parts remain reassemblable — cryopreserved, say — forcing the criterion to call it alive.
- **The goals go unpursued** — Reasons: a self ends when nothing in the system still works toward what it worked toward — which can happen while the body is still warm, or never quite happen at all; the criterion tracks what selfhood was for, not its machinery. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: goals being pursued by a system's remains in a case where the self is plainly gone — a thermostat outliving its house.

#### Mind Uploading & Digital Afterlives
- **Desirable Digital Continuance** — Reasons: freeing human minds from fragile biology opens centuries of exploration and eliminates aging — the pattern view of self (§3, Continuity through replacement) makes the upload you, not a portrait of you; refusing it on substrate grounds is the chauvinism this skin questions everywhere else. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that emulation omits something constitutive — a bioelectric or embodied layer no scan captures.
- **Uncanny Copy Trap** — Reasons: even a faithful simulation risks hidden suffering or losing the irreplaceable spark of mortal embodiment — a billion copies could suffer unobserved, and the original's death is not cancelled by a successor's testimony; the trap is that success and catastrophe look identical from outside. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a validated science of machine experience able to certify an upload's welfare and continuity, not merely its behaviour.

### 3.7 Who counts?

#### The expanding moral circle
- **The circle is finished** — Reasons: the line now sits where reason puts it — sentient beings in, mechanisms out — and further expansion is sentiment without argument; a circle that must always grow is not a principle but a mood. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a being outside the current line demonstrating the very capacities the line was drawn to honour.
- **Expanding, with edges** — Reasons: the circle still grows — organoids, constructs, insects are live cases — but it has principled edges set by evidence of experience; growth with criteria is science, growth without them is drift. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the edges proving to move only with fashion, never with evidence.
- **It keeps expanding** — Reasons: every past generation was sure the line was finished too — the pattern of the evidence says it moves as we learn to see minds we had missed; the inductive case from the circle's own history is the strongest argument in this section. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a generation of expansion attempts that all, on examination, found nothing — the pattern finally breaking.

#### Suffering at small scales
- **Needs a brain like ours** — Reasons: suffering is a specific achievement of brains like ours — integrated, valenced, reportable — and below that there is damage, not pain; projecting pain into every stressed cell makes the concept unusable where it matters most. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a brainless system showing the full behavioural signature of suffering — trade-offs, self-protection, lasting aversion learning — integrated as ours is.
- **A dim analogue** — Reasons: something it is like to be stressed at small scales, dimmer than suffering proper — the analogue respects both the continuity of the machinery and the difference of degree; dim is not nothing, and it is not pain. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the analogue proving either full suffering (promotion) or pure mechanism (demotion) under a validated marker.
- **Scales with the self** — Reasons: suffering, like cognition, comes in degrees — a small self's distress is small, but it is not nothing, and it is not zero; the light-cone measure gives the scaling a unit instead of a shrug. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: distress failing to track self-scale — tiny systems showing maximal signatures or large ones none.

#### Status follows the light cone
- **Track the mind's size** — Reasons: a being that can hold larger goals and farther concerns has more at stake — more future to lose, more world to be wronged in; status should say so, as it already does implicitly for infants and adults in law's protections. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: size-tracking producing rankings nobody can defend — a corporation or a state outranking a child.
- **One factor among several** — Reasons: the light cone counts toward moral status alongside sentience, relationship and vulnerability; single-factor theories of status have a perfect record of failure, and pluralism about value is the mature position. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the factors proving to collapse into one underlying quantity in practice.
- **Size is irrelevant** — Reasons: the smallest self's world matters wholly to it — grading status by mental size is the old hierarchy in new clothes, and it has always graded the grader highest; a bacterium's striving is complete at its scale. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an unavoidable triage — two beings, one rescue — where refusing to weigh size becomes indefensible.

#### Minds in other materials
- **Mind needs living tissue** — Reasons: whatever software does, mindedness is a property of living systems and their peculiar physics — self-maintenance, metabolism, mortality are not implementation details but the phenomenon's roots; a simulation of rain is not wet. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a non-living system meeting every behavioural and integrative test for mindedness over years, with the tissue requirement doing no explanatory work.
- **The right organization** — Reasons: not any substrate, and not only living tissue — whatever realises the right organisation: integration, self-modelling, goal-closure; the criterion is demanding about structure and agnostic about stuff, which is where the evidence points. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two systems with the same organisation, one living and one not, differing in mindedness — showing organisation was not the whole story.
- **Any substrate that models itself** — Reasons: a system that models itself, holds goals and works to close the gap is a mind in the relevant sense, whether it is made of cells or silicon; substrate loyalty has no remaining argument that is not nostalgia. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: self-modelling systems that plainly lack minds — the criterion certifying thermostats with mirrors.

#### Human exceptionalism
- **A different kind of thing** — Reasons: language, mathematics and self-reflection mark a real discontinuity — no other system comments on its own cognition, builds sciences of it, or holds trials about it; degree-talk flattens the one jump that produced the conversation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: each exceptional capacity being found in graded, continuous form across species and systems, with the jump dissolving into steps.
- **First among selves** — Reasons: one self among many, and still the most capable one we know — the pole keeps the continuity the evidence supports and the ranking the evidence also supports; humility about kind, honesty about degree. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: another self — animal, collective or machine — matching the full human profile, retiring the "first."
- **One self among many** — Reasons: we are a large and articulate instance of something nature does at every scale — remarkable in degree, not in kind; exceptionalism has been the losing bet at every previous demotion, from Earth's centrality to man's separate creation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a human capacity shown to be unapproachable in principle by any graded process — a true discontinuity with a mechanism.

#### Animal Consciousness Frontier
- **Vertebrate & Cephalopod Core** — Reasons: complex central nervous systems supporting play, learning and pain behaviour mark clear sentience — the evidence converges where the neurology does; the 2012 Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness grounded animal consciousness in exactly these shared substrates. Statistic: an octopus carries about 500 million neurons, roughly two-thirds of them in its arms (Godfrey-Smith, 2016). What would move a holder: invertebrates outside the core passing the same play, trade-off and pain-flexibility tests.
- **Insects & Arthropods Included** — Reasons: bees and crabs show pain trade-offs, memory and attention pointing to real subjective feeling — bees trade off noxious heat against reward quality, which is the signature of felt pain, not reflex; the frontier moved because the experiments did. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the trade-off behaviours being fully explained by reflex arcs with no central integration.
- **Basal Cellular Sentience** — Reasons: even single cells sense, decide and maintain self-boundaries, hinting at primitive agency throughout life — if agency is the root of experience, the root goes to the bottom; this pole takes the skin's continuity thesis at full strength. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstrated floor — a level at which integrated, valenced response provably vanishes.

### 3.8 The human case

#### Your own light cone
- **People I will meet** — Reasons: my caring honestly extends to those my life can touch; beyond that it is opinion, not concern — and honesty about the limit beats performed concern for everyone; the pole is a confession standard, not a selfishness licence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: discovering their choices already move distant lives — supply chains, votes — making the honest circle larger than the felt one.
- **Beings I affect** — Reasons: my circle reaches the beings my actions touch, seen or unseen — causation, not acquaintance, is the honest boundary of responsibility; it is the largest circle that stays a duty rather than a sentiment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the affected set proving effectively unbounded, collapsing the pole into the next one.
- **Beings I will never see** — Reasons: my concern genuinely reaches strangers, descendants and other species I will never encounter — and it moves what I do; future people are the largest class of beings anyone affects, and a cone that stops at eyesight is a sense organ, not an ethic. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their unseen-directed concern proving to be pure self-image — never once costing them anything.

#### Meaning is something selves do
- **Meaning is found** — Reasons: meanings exist independently of us, and a life goes well when it aligns with them — the phenomenology of meaning is discovery, not invention: it resists, surprises and judges us, as found things do. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: meanings proving to vary with selves in lockstep, with no resistance ever encountered.
- **Found and made** — Reasons: meaning is discovered and created at once — as a path is found in terrain the walker also wears in; the pole refuses a binary the phenomenon refuses. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cases resolving cleanly to one side — meanings that are pure invention or pure discovery with no admixture.
- **Meaning is made** — Reasons: meaning is what a self-modelling system does — a universe of nested agents generates it at every scale, and ours is the local instance; this explains why meaning scales with selves and dies with them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a meaning that binds a self against its every inclination and survives its death intact — behaving exactly like a found thing.

#### Purpose among nested agents
- **Purpose comes from above** — Reasons: a purpose must be given by something larger — a god, a cosmos, a calling — or it is invention; self-issued purpose is a cheque written to oneself, and everyone can tell. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a self-generated purpose displaying the bindingness of a given one — holding under suffering and revision like a calling does.
- **Set above, tuned below** — Reasons: purpose descends from the larger self and is reshaped at every level it passes through — as an organism's purposes are tuned by its tissues' competencies; the pole matches the nested architecture the rest of the skin describes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: levels proving to generate purposes with no input from above at all.
- **Purpose is generated at every scale** — Reasons: every agent in the stack generates its own purposes from its own goals; yours are real the same way a cell's are, only larger — purpose is not a scarce substance dispensed from the top but a local product of agency itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a level whose purposes are demonstrably wholly scripted from above, with no local generation.

#### One self, or a committee?
- **One unified self** — Reasons: I am a single subject; my parts and impulses are mine, not little agents of their own — the unity of experience is the datum, and committee-talk is a metaphor for conflict within a subject, not a census of subjects. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: sub-agents with their own persistent goals and memories demonstrably negotiating inside a person — split-brain cases generalised.
- **A chair and a committee** — Reasons: a committee with a chair: parts deliberate, one voice usually speaks — the model fits both the unity (the chair) and the coups (when it loses the room); it is the constitution most people recognise from inside. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the chair proving to be a press release after all — decisions consistently preceding the "deliberation."
- **A committee that mostly agrees** — Reasons: I am a coalition — habits, drives and sub-agents that usually vote together and occasionally stage a coup, and "I" am the coalition's press release; addiction, akrasia and self-deception are unintelligible on the unified model and obvious on this one. Statistic: the human body hosts roughly 38 trillion bacterial cells alongside its own cells — a literal multitude within the boundary of one "self" (Sender, Fuchs & Milo, 2016). What would move a holder: a demonstration that apparent sub-agents never hold goals of their own — only the whole's goals, noisily executed.

#### The hard problem, reframed
- **A mystery that survives** — Reasons: no map of cognition, at any scale, touches why there is an inside to it — the spectrum redescribes the functions beautifully and the problem stands exactly where it stood; reframing is not solving. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the spectrum account predicting a fact about experience — its grain, its kinds — that no functional map should know.
- **What selfhood feels like from inside** — Reasons: experience is what being a self — a bounded, goal-holding system — is like from within; the spectrum explains why experience comes in kinds and degrees, which is more than the mystery framing has ever explained. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a bounded, goal-holding system where experience is demonstrably absent — the inside failing to appear where the account requires it.
- **A confusion that dissolves** — Reasons: once "self" stops meaning a magic indivisible thing, the hard problem loses the premise that made it hard — the mystery was the gap between a cartoon self and a real mechanism, and the spectrum removes the cartoon. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the problem surviving the dissolution — formulating cleanly for a graded, nested self with the mystery intact.

#### Meditation & Pure Awareness
- **Brain State Without Content** — Reasons: quieting default-mode and sensory networks produces a striking but purely brain-bound state — the neuroscience of meditation keeps finding the correlates the pole predicts; an unusual brain state is not automatically a window out of the brain. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: meditators in contentless awareness reporting verifiable information they could not have obtained through the senses.
- **Window Into Ground of Being** — Reasons: stripping away conceptual overlays reveals the luminous awareness out of which subjects and objects arise — independent contemplative traditions converged on the same report over millennia, which is the kind of cross-check we accept elsewhere; the convergence is the evidence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the reports proving to be script-dependent — meditators finding exactly and only what their tradition told them to find.

#### Psychedelics & Ego Dissolution
- **Cognitive Perturbation** — Reasons: relaxing top-down priors creates vivid awe and pattern-matching that feel profound without proving truth — felt profundity is cheap to manufacture and correlates with nothing but the dose; a state that makes everything seem significant is evidence about the state, not the world. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: psychedelic insights repeatedly verifying against independent evidence at rates chance and hindsight cannot explain.
- **Epistemic Insight Tool** — Reasons: temporarily loosening rigid ego boundaries lets people perceive relational truths normally filtered out — and the therapeutic results are concrete: durable relief of depression and end-of-life anxiety after single sessions, which mere perturbation does not explain. Statistic: in a trial of patients with life-threatening cancer, high-dose psilocybin produced clinically significant decreases in depressed mood and anxiety in about 80% of participants, sustained at six months (Griffiths et al., 2016). What would move a holder: the therapeutic effects being fully reproduced by the expectancy and ritual alone, with the pharmacology inert.

#### Redrawing the Human Cognitive Light Cone
- **Embrace Hybrid & Collective Minds** — Reasons: human selves are already collectives of cells; thoughtfully extending our cognitive light cone through interfaces, organoids and AI can heal disease and deepen empathy — the boundary was never sacred, only habitual. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: hybrid collectives producing manipulation or harm that the boundary would have prevented, at scale.
- **Guard Individual Biological Boundaries** — Reasons: dissolving clear boundaries of individual personhood invites manipulation and erodes moral accountability — when a collective acts, no one answers; the boundary is what makes a person addressable by law, love and blame. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: hybrid arrangements with accountability demonstrably intact — responsibility traceable to persons as reliably as in unmerged life.

### Neural-Centric / Molecular-Mechanistic Reductionist — `nous-chauvinist.svg`

Receptive to: functionalist organisation-talk and bioengineering results, which supply its evidence base (§3, Minds in other materials; Rewrite the pattern, not the genes). Why: it holds the §3 Stewardship reasons and generalises them — authorship, not ontology, grounds obligation (§3, What we owe a being we made by accident). Receptive to: evidence from every camp, since its positions are explicitly provisional, and to containment proposals that take welfare seriously (§3, Ethics of Synthetic Morphology). ### Substrate-Independent Functionalist / Computationalist — `nous-neuro-computationalist.svg`

### The Embodied Biopsychist — `nous-embodied-biopsychist.svg`

Why: it holds the §3 Real but embodied, Feeling is required and Mind needs living tissue reasons — valence is self-maintenance felt from inside (§3, Qualia, real or illusion?; Does cognition need feeling?; Minds in other materials).
