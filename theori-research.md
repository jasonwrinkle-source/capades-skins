# Theori — Research

Skin: Theoricapades (`theoricapades.json`) · Domain: Pure & Theoretical Mathematics — what math is, what makes it true, and how you live as a mathematician · Meter: position
Coverage: 74 topics, 212 poles/positions, 18 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Theori maps pure and theoretical mathematics: what mathematics is, what makes a statement true, what counts as a proof, which territories matter, what makes mathematics beautiful, and how a mathematician should live and work. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's questions — a pole a holder advocates for or against (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence), or, on the skin's single-pole topics, a strength of affinity for the one named position, where a negative rating is aversion to it. Holding no stance is not a position; a 0 rating is. What does not count as a position is mere competence or taste in notation: the skin rates commitments about truth, proof, and value, not whether someone can do the mathematics. The meter word is **position**: the skin measures how strongly a profile stands for each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Two widget rulings apply throughout: the one xy-switchbox topic (Examples vs. Axioms & Computation vs. Functoriality) crosses two independent yes/no axes that are not opposites — the status quo is both axes negative — and multi-pole poles are independent positions that need not be opposites of one another. Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness \u00d7 advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole, derived from the advocacy values. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance is pluralist and internal to mathematics: rival schools — Platonist, formalist, intuitionist, structuralist, and the working styles built on them — are placed on one map in one vocabulary, with no school treated as the default.

## 2. Topic rationale


### Foundations & Ontology

**Foundations of Mathematics** — The root question of the whole skin: every other topic presupposes some answer to what mathematics is. The Platonism pole: Mathematical objects exist independently; we discover them. The Formalism pole: Math is symbols and rules; meaning is irrelevant. The Intuitionism pole: Math is a construction of the mind; only what can be built is real. The Logicism pole: Math reduces to logic; all truths are logical truths. The Structuralism pole: Math is about structures and patterns, not objects. The Quine–Putnam Indispensability pole: Mathematics is true because it is indispensable to our best science — a naturalistic argument for mathematical realism, not a claim about structures.
**Foundations** — Where structure ultimately lives decides what a proof is a proof *in*, so the choice of foundation colours every branch topic below. The ZFC Set Theory pole: Classical cumulative hierarchy of sets; the universal standard. The Category Theory / Topoi pole: Arrows, functors, universal properties; relations over elements. The Homotopy Type Theory (HoTT) pole: Constructive type theory; identity is homotopy equivalence.
**Excluded middle** — The excluded middle is the sharpest single test separating classical from constructive mathematics. The Classical Logic pole: LEM holds; proof by contradiction is fully valid. The Classical, constructively careful pole: Classical logic, with constructive proofs preferred where they exist. The Constructive Logic pole: LEM rejected; existence requires an explicit witness.
**Mathematics** — Discovery versus invention is the folk version of the ontology question, and it predicts a holder's answers almost everywhere else. The Discovered pole: Math is out there; we find it. The Invented, then discovered pole: We invent the systems; their consequences are discovered. The Invented pole: Math is made up by humans.
**The Mathematical Universe** — Whether reality itself is mathematical is the strongest form realism can take, and the skin needs its full gradient. The Radical Mathematical Realism pole: Our universe literally is a mathematical structure. The Structural realism pole: Reality has mathematical structure; whether it is one stays open. The Epistemic Anti-Realism pole: Mathematics is a human cognitive language and mapping tool.
**Infinity** — Infinity is where ontology becomes concrete: completed infinities are the objects classical and constructive mathematics most plainly part over. The Actual infinity pole: A completed thing (Cantor). The Working actual infinity pole: Completed infinities used as tools, without committing to their existence. The Potential infinity pole: A process, never finished (Aristotle). The Finitism & Ultrafinitism pole: Only concrete, feasibly constructible finite objects truly exist; even astronomically huge exponents like 10^(10^10) merit scrutiny (Nelson 1986; Zeilberger 2001).
**Can mathematics be revised?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Can we change mathematics, or do we only discover it?” Revisability tests whether mathematical truth is the kind of thing a community could vote to change. The Revisable (conventions) pole: Axioms are choices. We can adopt different ones, and the mathematics changes with them. The Not revisable (discovered) pole: Mathematical truths are found, not voted in. We cannot revise what is already true. The Revisable in form, not structure pole: The structures are real. The way we write them down is conventional and can change.
**How do we know mathematical truths?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “How do we know a mathematical truth — by seeing it, by proving it, by recognizing a pattern, or by building it?” Epistemology needs its own topic because holders who agree on ontology still part over how truth is known. The Intuition pole: We grasp abstract objects directly. Proof writes down what insight already saw. The Proof pole: We know a statement when a derivation from the rules reaches it. There is no seeing behind the proof. The Pattern recognition pole: We know a structure by grasping the pattern of relations, not a private object and not a mere string of symbols. The Construction pole: We know a statement only by constructing it. An existence claim without a construction is not yet knowledge.
**One true mathematics or many?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is there one mathematics, or many — and if many, are they all equally real?” One mathematics or many is the pluralism question that set theory, foundations, and practice all feed into. The One true mathematics pole: There is one universe of mathematical fact. Other systems are notations for it, or mistakes. The Many mathematics pole: Different axiom systems are different mathematics. None is the one true game. The One structure, many presentations pole: The structure is one. The languages and axiom sets are many ways of presenting it.
**Set-Theoretic Universe vs. Multiverse** — The Continuum Hypothesis forces the universe-versus-multiverse question into a single concrete case no holder can dodge. The Unique True Universe (V) pole: Independence merely shows ZFC is incomplete; deeper canonical axioms (such as Ultimate-L or large-cardinal principles) will settle CH in the one true V. The Pragmatic Working Pluralism pole: Use whichever set-theoretic environment best serves a given domain without dogmatizing about metaphysical uniqueness. The Full Set-Theoretic Multiverse pole: Forcing reveals a rich multiverse of equally legitimate mathematical universes—some where CH holds and others where it fails.
**Unreasonable Effectiveness & Indispensability** — Wigner's puzzle — why pure mathematics fits physics so well — is the strongest empirical argument any ontology must answer. The Quine–Putnam Physical Indispensability pole: Mathematics earns ontological commitment precisely because it is indispensable to our best empirical scientific theories. The Cognitive & Structural Filtering pole: We select and refine mathematical abstractions drawn from symmetry, continuity, and pattern—so their match with physics reflects selection bias and shared structural origins. The Autonomous Platonic Harmony pole: The physical cosmos is mathematically structured at its core, which is why pure aesthetic exploration anticipates physical laws decades ahead.
**Adopting New Axioms Beyond ZFC** — Gödel showed ZFC cannot settle everything, so the skin must ask by what right stronger axioms are adopted. The Freeze Core Foundations at ZFC pole: ZFC already suffices for virtually all mainstream mathematics; adding stronger axioms risks inconsistency for marginal gain. The Intrinsic Conceptual Justification pole: New axioms must follow directly from our clear, intuitive concept of set—not merely from convenient theorems they imply. The Extrinsic Fruitfulness Justifies Large Cardinals pole: Just as physical laws are verified by explanatory power, large-cardinal and determinacy axioms earn adoption through sweeping structural regularity.
**What Do Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems Mean?** — Incompleteness is the theorem every philosophy of mathematics claims as evidence, so its rival readings belong on the map. The Truth Outruns Proof (Realist Vindication) pole: Incompleteness shows mathematical truth is objective and inexhaustible by any single mechanical axiom list—vindicating Platonism and the search for higher axioms. The Technical Limit — Work Goes On (Stronger Systems & Machine Checking) pole: Incompleteness is a crisp theorem about fixed formal systems, not a crisis for working mathematics; adopt stronger axioms when needed and machine-verify inside rich formal frameworks. The No Surprise: Truth Is Provability With a Witness pole: From a constructive standpoint there is no mystical gap between truth and proof: mathematical truth consists in explicit construction and witnessed provability.
**Reverse Mathematics & Axiom Economy** — Reverse mathematics turns foundational commitment into a measurable quantity: exactly how much axiom each theorem needs. The Value of Finding Exact Minimal Axioms pole: Knowing the exact logical strength of a theorem exposes hidden compactness, computability, and structural equivalences that working lazily inside ZFC conceals.

### Method & Style

**Method** — Method is the working mathematician's daily fork: build machinery that dissolves problems, or attack them directly. The The Rising Sea pole: Build vast conceptual machinery until the theorem becomes obvious. The The Hammer & Chisel pole: Direct assault: hard combinatorics, brutal inequalities, ad-hoc ingenuity.
**Abstraction style** — Abstraction style separates those who love the general pattern from those who love the exceptional object that breaks it. The Extreme Structural Abstraction pole: Infinity-categories, motives, universal properties; the meta-pattern. The Exceptional Concrete Jewels pole: The Monster group, the Leech lattice, E8; unique idiosyncratic structures.
**Abstraction level** — Abstraction level is the dosage question underneath the style question — how much generality a problem actually wants. The High abstraction pole: Category theory, the general. The Problem-pitched pole: Abstraction pitched to the problem, no higher. The Low abstraction pole: Concrete computation, the particular.
**Erdős's "The Book": Proof Aesthetics** — Erdős's Book names proof aesthetics directly: what makes one proof of a true theorem better than another. The Brevity & Economy pole: Zero wasted motion—every line carries essential weight and nothing can be removed. The Shocking Twist of Insight pole: A sudden, breathtaking change of perspective that turns an impossible problem inside out (Hardy's "unexpectedness"). The Effortless Inevitability pole: Once the right viewpoint is chosen, the conclusion falls out so naturally you wonder how anyone missed it. The Elementary Accessibility pole: Deep truth delivered with minimal prerequisites so even an undergraduate can relish its clarity. The Deep Structural Unity pole: Illuminating a hidden link connecting seemingly distant branches of mathematics.
**Thinking** — Thinking in pictures or in symbols is a real cognitive divide that shapes collaboration, teaching, and taste. The Visual pole: Pictures, diagrams, geometry. The Symbolic pole: Formulas, algebra, symbols.
**Intuition** — Trust in intuition decides how much of a holder's unpublished certainty they will act on before proof arrives. The Trust pole: Intuition guides you right. The Trust, but verify pole: Intuition leads; proof checks its work. The Distrust pole: Intuition misleads; only proof counts.
**Examples vs. Axioms & Computation vs. Functoriality** — This xy-switchbox crosses two independent learning axes — examples-first versus axioms-first, and computation versus functoriality; the axes are not opposites, and starting from neither (both negative) is the unexamined status quo. The Concrete examples or general axioms pole: Negative: start from two or three rich, non-trivial concrete examples (Halmos, Arnold). Positive: start from clean general axioms stripping away accidental distractions. The Explicit computation or functorial abstraction pole: Negative: compute small cases, matrices, tables, and expansions directly. Positive: characterize objects by universal properties and natural transformations (Mac Lane).
**Purity of Methods** — Purity of method asks whether a proof should stay inside its theorem's native domain or may import foreign machinery. The Elementary Self-Contained Purity pole: Proving a number-theoretic or geometric theorem using only concepts intrinsic to its statement reveals its deepest direct cause. The Value Both Elementary & Transcendental pole: Elementary proofs clarify logical dependence, while external-machinery proofs uncover unexpected bridges across disciplines. The Celebrate Cross-Field Heavy Machinery pole: The glory of Wiles's proof of Fermat's Last Theorem is precisely that it links elementary arithmetic to modular forms and Galois representations.
**Concrete Classification vs. Universal Properties** — How objects are defined — by construction or by universal property — decides what a student, and a proof assistant, can actually do with them. The Explicit Canonical Models pole: Concrete constructions show what an object actually looks like and let you compute with its elements directly. The Bridge Models & Universal Maps pole: Define by universal property for conceptual clarity, then immediately exhibit one concrete construction to prove existence and enable calculation. The Pure Universal-Property Definitions pole: Implementation details are noise; knowing how an object maps to and from every other object tells you everything that matters.
**Experimental Mathematics & Symbolic Pattern-Hunting** — Experimental mathematics asks whether computation is a scout, a servant, or a discovery engine in its own right. The Pure Deductive Thought First pole: Relying on computer tables encourages shallow pattern-matching and distracts from deep conceptual thinking—a few well-chosen hand examples suffice. The Computation Inspires Conjectures Only pole: Computing a million cases can never prove a theorem (beware Skewes's number!), but it kills false guesses instantly and points proofs in the right direction. The Full Experimental-Math Discovery Engine pole: High-precision numerical exploration, OEIS, and LMFDB have transformed pure math into a laboratory where astonishing identities (BBP formula for π, Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer) are discovered empirically first.
**What Makes a Problem Worth Working On?** — A finite life forces a choice of problems; this topic records what a holder thinks makes a problem worth their years. The Crisp & Elementary pole: A problem anyone can state in two lines—combinatorial, number-theoretic, or extremal—whose solution demands a dazzlingly clever idea (Erdős, Gowers). The Fertile & Theory-Forcing pole: A landmark question whose real prize is the sweeping new conceptual machinery and bridges it forces mathematics to build (Grothendieck, Langlands). The Beautiful & Serious pole: Depth, inevitability, economy, and generality of pure mathematical ideas judged on their own timeless aesthetic standards (Hardy's Apology). The Physically & Geometrically Grounded pole: Problems born from physical reality, dynamics, and low-dimensional geometry where spatial intuition anchors mathematical meaning (Arnold).

### Branches & Territory

**The Quadrivium of Pure Mathematics** — The quadrivium locates a holder's home territory, against which every branch topic below is a border crossing. The Algebra & Symmetry pole: Groups, rings, fields, Galois theory; symmetry and transformation. The Geometry & Topology pole: Manifolds, knot theory, shape and invariants under deformation. The Analysis & Continua pole: Limits, measures, continuous variation; real and complex analysis. The Number Theory & Arithmetic pole: Primes, modular forms, elliptic curves; the Queen of Mathematics.
**Combinatorics** — Combinatorics is a single-pole affinity topic: it measures passion for the finite and discrete, with negative values recording aversion. The Affinity for Discrete Mathematics & Combinatorics pole is one rated position on this topic.
**Continuity** — Continuity poses the discrete-versus-continuous temperament as one axis running under many branch choices. The Discrete pole: Combinatorics, number theory, graphs. The Continuous pole: Analysis, topology, geometry.
**Purpose** — Purpose is the skin-defining divide: mathematics for its own sake, for use, or for the fertile middle Stokes named Pasteur's quadrant. The Pure pole: Math for its own sake. The Use-Inspired Basic Research (Pasteur's Quadrant) pole: Basic research chosen for the uses it serves — Stokes' quadrant. The Applied pole: Math for what it does.
**Analytic vs. Algebraic Number Theory** — Analytic versus algebraic number theory is the oldest methodological split inside the queen of mathematics. The Analytic Sieves, L-Functions & Estimates pole: Riemann's zeta zeros, circle methods, and bilinear sieve bounds attack additive and prime-distribution mysteries directly. The Arithmetic-Geometric Synthesis pole: Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer and Langlands prove analytic L-functions and algebraic cohomology are mirror images of one unified arithmetic reality. The Algebraic Galois, Cohomology & Motives pole: Hidden structural symmetries of rational solutions reveal themselves only through number fields, schemes, and motivic Galois groups.
**Archimedean Real/Complex vs. p-Adic & Non-Archimedean Worlds** — The p-adic worlds test whether a holder's geometric intuition survives leaving the Archimedean continuum. The Classical Archimedean Continuum pole: Real and complex manifolds anchor analysis, differential equations, and physical geometry intuitively. The Adelic Local–Global Balance pole: Hasse–Minkowski and Tate's thesis show ℝ is just one prime at infinity—true arithmetic lives adelically across all places simultaneously. The p-Adic & Perfectoid Ultrametric Beauty pole: Non-Archimedean geometry, Berkovich spaces, and Scholze's perfectoid tilting turn characteristic 0 into characteristic p with breathtaking clarity.
**Classical Algebraic Varieties vs. Derived & ∞-Categorical Geometry** — Derived and ∞-categorical geometry asks how much homotopy coherence modern geometry must carry in its foundations. The Concrete Classical Varieties & Schemes pole: Classical equations, divisors, and Grothendieck schemes already solve enormous geometry without dragging homotopy coherence into every definition. The Pragmatic Derived Categories When Needed pole: Invoke derived functors and Deligne–Mumford stacks cleanly when non-transverse intersections or moduli automorphisms actually demand them. The Full Higher Stacks & ∞-Category Paradigm pole: Truncating higher homotopies breaks gluing and duality; Lurie's ∞-topoi and derived algebraic geometry are the true native setting of modern geometry.
**Effective Diophantine Algorithms vs. Ineffective Finiteness Theorems** — Ineffective finiteness theorems pose the satisfaction question in its purest form: existence without any way to find the objects. The Explicit Computable Bounds Only pole: An ineffective finiteness theorem leaves us unable to check if we found every solution; Baker's linear forms in logarithms and Chabauty–Kim algorithms deliver real answers. The Finiteness First, Effectivize Later pole: Structural finiteness establishes the conceptual landscape and motivates decades of productive work turning qualitative proofs into explicit bounds. The Celebrate Pure Structural Finiteness pole: Proving Mordell's conjecture via Arakelov geometry and abelian varieties is a triumph of pure structural insight regardless of algorithmic height bounds.
**Group Theory Ethos: Finite Classification vs. Geometric Group Theory** — Group theory's ethos splits between the finished finite classification, continuous symmetry, and groups seen as geometry. The Finite Simple Groups & Representation Tables pole: The classification of finite simple groups and Monstrous Moonshine represent one of humanity's strangest and deepest structural discoveries. The Lie & Algebraic Group Symmetry pole: Continuous Lie groups, root systems, and Dynkin diagrams organize geometry, representation theory, and fundamental physics simultaneously. The Infinite Geometric & Hyperbolic Groups pole: Viewing discrete groups up to quasi-isometry via Cayley graphs and negative curvature turns algebra directly into large-scale geometry (Gromov).
**Homological Algebra & Cohomology as Universal Lens** — Cohomology as universal lens is a single-pole affinity topic: it measures belief that local-to-global obstruction theory is modern mathematics' master key. The Centrality of Cohomological & Homological Machinery pole: From de Rham and singular cohomology to sheaf, étale, and motivic cohomology, turning nonlinear geometric mysteries into linearized homological invariants is the master key of modern math.
**Soft Functional Analysis vs. Hard Harmonic & PDE Analysis** — Soft versus hard analysis is a temperament divide inside analysis itself: structure first, or estimates first. The Soft Banach/Hilbert & Operator Algebras pole: Infinite-dimensional spectral theory, Hahn–Banach, and von Neumann algebras capture sweeping structural truths cleanly without messy constant-chasing. The Dual Soft-Hard Synthesis pole: Use soft compactness and concentration-compactness to extract critical blow-up profiles, then destroy them with hard quantitative estimates (Kenig–Merle, Tao). The Hard Oscillatory Integrals & Quantitative PDEs pole: Real nonlinear waves, Navier–Stokes, and Kakeya problems yield only to explicit frequency localization, decoupling, and sharp quantitative inequalities.
**Low-Dimensional Geometric Topology vs. High-Dimensional Surgery & Homotopy** — Low dimensions are where topology is wildest and least algebraic; this topic asks where its deepest fascination lives. The Wild 3- & 4-Manifold Geometry (Thurston/Donaldson) pole: Dimensions 3 and 4 are uniquely rich—where knots, Ricci flow, hyperbolic manifolds, and Seiberg–Witten invariants defeat purely algebraic classification. The Cross-Dimensional Gauge & Floer Bridges pole: Floer homology, Khovanov categorification, and cobordism hypotheses weave low-dimensional knots straight into higher algebraic topology. The High-Dim Surgery & Stable Homotopy pole: Above dimension 4, Whitney's trick unlocks systematic h-cobordism, surgery theory, and chromatic stable homotopy towers of astonishing regularity.
**Metric/Riemannian Geometry vs. Symplectic & Complex Geometry** — Three great geometries — Riemannian, symplectic, complex — compete for the soul of differential geometry. The Riemannian Curvature & Geometric Flows pole: Curvature bounds, minimal surfaces, and Ricci/mean-curvature flows bend spaces dynamically into canonical shapes (Hamilton, Perelman, Gromov). The Symplectic Flexibility & Pseudoholomorphic Curves pole: Despite having no local curvature invariants (Darboux), symplectic manifolds exhibit startling global rigidity—non-squeezing, Floer homology, and mirror symmetry (Gromov, Floer). The Complex Kähler & Calabi–Yau Rigidity pole: Holomorphicity fuses Riemannian, symplectic, and algebraic geometry into an astonishingly rigid world governed by Hodge theory and Yau's Calabi theorem.
**Classical Manifolds vs. Noncommutative & Synthetic Metric Spaces** — Generalizing geometry beyond smooth manifolds tests how far the word 'space' can stretch before it breaks. The Smooth Classical Manifolds First pole: Smooth calculus, tensors, and differential forms remain the sharpest, clearest laboratory for geometric insight. The Singular Limits & Alexandrov/RCD Spaces pole: Degenerating families of smooth manifolds naturally converge to singular metric-measure spaces; synthetic curvature bounds (Lott–Sturm–Villani) handle them seamlessly. The Noncommutative Spectral Triples (Connes) pole: Quotients of foliations, Penrose tilings, and quantum phase spaces have no ordinary points—only noncommutative C*-algebras and Dirac operators capture their true geometry.
**Probabilistic Method & Random Structures vs. Explicit Deterministic Constructions** — The probabilistic method proves existence without exhibition; whether that satisfies is a foundational taste, not a technique. The Explicit Deterministic Constructions Only pole: Knowing a haystack is 99.9% needles without exhibiting one leaves the real structural mystery unsolved—explicit Ramanujan graphs and algebraic constructions show why they work. The Probabilistic Existence Then Derandomize pole: Use random ensembles first to pin down sharp thresholds, then build deterministic algebraic or pseudorandom constructions matching them. The Random Objects Reveal Typical Truth pole: Random matrices, Erdős–Rényi graphs, and Schramm–Loewner Evolution (SLE) expose universal statistical laws that atypical deterministic constructions hide.
**Dynamical Systems & Ergodic Theory vs. Static Structure** — Dynamics versus statics asks whether a holder's intuition runs on iteration and flow or on timeless structure. The Time-Evolution & Ergodic Dynamics pole: Furstenberg's correspondence, Ratner's theorems, and Teichmüller dynamics show that watching orbits move across spaces solves even static number-theoretic and combinatorial puzzles. The Static Algebraic & Topological Invariants pole: Computing homotopy groups, cohomology rings, and representation decompositions pins down timeless structural skeletons cleanly.
**Computability vs. Computational Complexity as Mathematical Foundation** — Computability and complexity offer two different lessons from computation: what can be known at all, and what can be afforded. The Classical Turing Computability & Degrees pole: Computability theory cleanly separates what is algorithmically knowable from uncomputable infinities without arbitrary polynomial-time cutoffs. The Bridging Logic & Complexity pole: Descriptive complexity (Fagin, Immerman) and bounded arithmetic reveal exact mirrors between logical expressiveness and computational resources. The Feasible Complexity (P vs. NP) Is Primary pole: The difference between O(n²) and 2ⁿ is the deepest structural boundary in mathematics, physics, and cryptography—far more consequential than halting oracles.
**Model Theory & O-Minimality vs. Classical Algebraic Geometry** — Model theory's claim to organize mainstream geometry is recent enough, and strong enough, to need its own topic. The Classical Core Algebra & Geometry pole: Model-theoretic proofs often repackage algebraic-geometric facts in logical jargon; direct geometric arguments remain sharper and more explicit. The Model-Theoretic Tame Bridge pole: O-minimality and tame topology (van den Dries, Pila–Zannier) cleanly conquered the André–Oort conjecture and Hodge-theoretic definability where classical tools stalled. The Model Theory & Stability Organize All Math pole: Classification theory, NIP, and definable groups reveal the exact logical anatomy of "tame" versus "wild" mathematics across algebra, dynamics, and combinatorics.
**Descriptive Set Theory & Determinacy vs. General Topology** — Descriptive set theory asks whether classification itself — how hard a classification problem is — belongs at the centre. The Classical Point-Set & Algebraic Topology pole: Mainstream mathematics lives in locally compact manifolds, CW complexes, and Zariski topologies without needing projective-hierarchy machinery. The Borel Equivalence & Polish Pragmatism pole: Measuring whether a classification problem is "smooth" or hyperfinite in the Borel hierarchy explains why some invariants exist and others are provably impossible. The Descriptive Set Theory & Determinacy Paradigm pole: Infinite-game determinacy links large cardinals directly to regularity of every definable real set, unifying logic, ergodic theory, and operator algebras (Kechris).
**Extremal/Ramsey Combinatorics vs. Structural Graph Theory** — Extremal thresholds versus structural decomposition is combinatorics' internal version of the numbers-versus-architecture divide. The Sharp Extremal & Ramsey Thresholds pole: Quantitative density bounds, hypergraph containers, and additive combinatorics strike straight to the heart of disorder vs. inevitable pattern. The Regularity & Pseudorandomness Bridge pole: Szemerédi's Regularity Lemma and graphons (Lovász) split every giant discrete object into clean structured + pseudorandom + tiny-error components. The Structural Minors & Decomposition Theory pole: Robertson–Seymour's Graph Minor Theorem and Strong Perfect Graph Theorem prove discrete worlds obey deep, clean architectural decompositions.
**Category-Theoretic Categorification & Higher Algebra** — Categorification is a single-pole affinity topic: it measures commitment to lifting equations to isomorphisms and numbers to categories. The Commitment to Categorification (Numbers → Vector Spaces → Categories) pole: Lifting classical polynomials and identities to homological categories reveals richer invariants, functoriality, and surprising connections invisible in decategorified shadows.

### Proof & Verification

**Computer Proof** — The Four Color Theorem made computer proof a live question; this topic records whether machine computation can be proof. The Accept pole: A computer-assisted proof is a real proof. The Accept, with audit pole: Computer proofs count, when the computation can be audited. The Reject pole: If a human can't check it, it isn't a proof.
**Proof verification** — Proof verification asks who should pay the cost of formalization, and for which theorems. The Distraction from Creative Discovery pole: Translating human insight into finicky type-checker syntax consumes time better spent discovering new mathematics. The Targeted Tool for Complex Landmarks pole: Formalize ultra-technical, error-prone cornerstones (like the Kepler conjecture or Liquid Tensor Experiment) while keeping routine exploration informal. The Future Standard for All Research Proofs pole: Modern proofs have grown too long and intricate for human refereeing alone; machine-checked libraries will become the bedrock of mathematical reliability and AI-assisted research.
**Axiom of Choice (AC) Stance** — The Axiom of Choice is the one axiom working mathematicians actually argue about at the blackboard. The Unconditional Acceptance (ZFC) pole: Indispensable; without AC, functional analysis collapses. The Skeptical Restriction (ZF + Dependent Choice) pole: Accept weak choice; reject non-measurable sets. The Defiant Rejection (Constructive / Determinacy) pole is one rated position on this topic.
**Conjecture orientation** — Conjecture orientation separates hunters of famous problems from builders of worlds in which problems dissolve. The Conjecture Solver pole: Hunts white whales: Riemann, BSD, Goldbach. The Solve by building pole: Attack famous problems by building the theory that dissolves them. The Theory Builder pole: Develops new conceptual languages where old problems dissolve.
**Proof** — Proof itself — rigor against intuition — is the trust question every other verification topic presupposes. The Rigor pole: Formal proof, no gaps. The Intuition-guided rigor pole: Rigor as the standard, intuition as the guide. The Intuition pole: Insight, the feel of it.
**Primary Purpose of a Mathematical Proof** — The primary purpose of proof decides what a proof that merely verifies, without explaining, is worth. The Ironclad Logical Certitude pole: Above all, proof guarantees beyond doubt that a theorem follows deductively from stated axioms with zero gaps. The Human Conceptual Understanding pole: A proof succeeds only when it illuminates why a theorem is true and upgrades the reader's mental model (Thurston). The Generating New Methods & Concepts pole: The real gold of mathematics lies in the machinery and conceptual bridges forged inside proofs—often far richer than the theorem itself (Rav).
**Informal Conceptual Rigor vs. Bourbaki Precision** — How much detail a paper owes its reader is where rigor becomes an ethic of communication, not just of logic. The High-Level Intuitive Fluency pole: Burying key geometric ideas under tedious ε–δ and diagram-chasing boilerplate kills comprehension and slows communication. The Standard Peer-Reviewed Rigor pole: State hypotheses accurately and prove every non-trivial step cleanly while leaving truly routine checks concise. The Airtight Axiomatic Completeness pole: "Left to the reader" and "it is clear" are where fatal bugs hide for decades; spell out every edge case and hypothesis ruthlessly.
**Speculative "Theoretical Mathematics" & Heuristics** — The Jaffe–Quinn question asks how much speculation the literature can carry before credit and reliability rot. The Publish Only Complete Proofs pole: Floods of unproven "theorems" erode credit for those who do the hard work of rigorous proof and pollute the literature with false foundations. The Clearly Flagged Conjectural Program pole: Welcome bold heuristics and physical analogies so long as authors label conjectures strictly and never blur heuristic arguments with proofs. The Bold Speculative Synthesis Leads the Way pole: Great revolutions (mirror symmetry, Langlands, Ramanujan's formulas) start from visionary intuition long before rigorous proofs catch up.
**Refereeing Duty & Verification of Ultra-Long Proofs** — Refereeing duty is a single-pole topic: it measures how uncompromising verification must be before an ultra-long proof is accepted as proved. The Strict Line-by-Line Public Verifiability Before Acceptance pole: No theorem is proved by authority or insider consensus; if competent independent experts cannot follow and verify the core step, the theorem remains unproved.
**Do You Trust the Published Literature?** — Trust in the published literature is the working prior every mathematician actually uses, made explicit. The Broadly Reliable — Trust the Refereed Canon pole: Peer review, seminar scrutiny, and downstream use catch serious flaws; the refereed canon of mathematics is the most reliable body of knowledge humans have built. The Trust, But Audit the Landmarks Case by Case pole: Most routine papers are basically sound, but ultra-long or heavily used landmark proofs require careful community audits and independent re-derivations. The Systematically Unreliable Until Formalized pole: Human refereeing routinely misses subtle edge cases and hidden gaps in complex proofs; only machine-checked formalization warrants full confidence.

### Beauty & Reality

**Values** — Values pits the beautiful proof against the powerful theorem as the unit of mathematical worth. The Elegance pole: A beautiful proof. The Power pole: A powerful result.
**Results** — Simplicity against depth is the results-level version of the elegance-versus-power question. The Simplicity pole: Clean, spare results. The Depth pole: Rich, complex structures.
**Beauty** — Beauty as a guide to truth is the aesthetic claim Platonists lean on and formalists suspect. The Essential pole: Beauty is a sign of truth. The Beauty as a clue pole: Beauty is evidence to weigh, not a verdict. The Incidental pole: Beauty is a nice bonus.
**Hardy's stance** — Hardy's stance frames the purity question as pride: uselessness as honour, or applicability as miracle. The Proud Inutility (Hardy's Apology) pole is one rated position on this topic. The Pleased when it applies pole: Purity prized; applicability received as a pleasant surprise. The Unreasonable Effectiveness (Wigner) pole is one rated position on this topic.
**Math-physics boundary** — The math-physics boundary asks who may steer pure mathematics: its own logic, or physical insight. The Autonomous Self-Reference pole: Math answers only to its own internal logic. The Autonomous, physically nourished pole: Mathematics answers to its own logic, fed by physical insight. The Physical Symbiosis pole: Breakthroughs are fertilized by quantum field theory and relativity.
**Grand Unity vs. Healthy Archipelago** — Grand unity versus archipelago is the geography question for the whole discipline, Langlands against local cultures. The Grand Unification (Langlands Ideal) pole: Number theory, geometry, representation theory, and quantum field theory are facets of one breathtaking unified architecture. The Web of Local Analogies pole: Unexpected cross-field bridges are thrilling when genuine, but shouldn't be forced where domains have distinct character. The Irreducible Archipelago of Subfields pole: Combinatorics, PDEs, logic, and algebraic geometry have legitimately different instincts and thrive without a single grand umbrella.
**Assessing Bourbaki's Historical Legacy** — Bourbaki's legacy is the historical verdict every structuralist and every intuitionist must render differently. The Sterile Over-Abstraction pole: Bourbaki's arid style and "New Math" excesses stripped intuition, examples, and physical meaning out of generations of textbooks. The Essential Cleanup That Overshot pole: Bourbaki gave mathematics indispensable terminology and structural clarity, though later pedagogy needed to re-inject geometry and examples. The Monumental Structural Triumph pole: Bourbaki's unified architecture of algebra, topology, and Lie theory made modern algebraic geometry and representation theory possible.
**Counterexamples & Mathematical "Monsters"** — Counterexamples and monsters test whether pathology is noise, fence, or teacher. The Distracting Pathologies pole: Obsessing over artificial pathological counterexamples distracts from the smooth, well-behaved spaces that carry real mathematical and physical substance (Poincaré's early critique). The Necessary Boundary Fences pole: Counterexamples serve as safety railings confirming we didn't omit an essential hypothesis like compactness or separability. The Vital Teachers of Structure pole: Yesterday's "monsters" (Weierstrass functions, Cantor sets, exotic 7-spheres) became tomorrow's foundational objects (fractals, Brownian motion, differential topology).

### The Mathematical Life

**Social mode** — Social mode records how a mathematician actually works: itinerant collaboration, solitude, or open mass collaboration. The Hyper-Collaborative / Itinerant (Erdős Mode) pole is one rated position on this topic. The Collaborative with retreats pole: Itinerant collaboration, with monastic stretches to finish. The Ascetic Monastic Solitude (Perelman / Grothendieck Mode) pole is one rated position on this topic. The Massively Open Polymath pole: Opening scratch work online lets dozens of mathematicians pool specialized tricks and shatter conjectures in weeks (Gowers & Nielsen 2009).
**Authorship & Credit in Collaboration** — Authorship and credit become urgent when proofs have five, or five hundred, authors. The Alphabetical & Egalitarian (Erdős) pole: Traditional mathematical alphabetical order treats every coauthor as an equal partner in an indivisible creative conversation. The Contribution-Tracked (Mathlib / Polymath) pole: Large-scale formalization and massively collaborative proofs thrive when git commits, PR logs, and explicit contribution graphs credit every lemma and tactic transparently. The Single-Author Ownership (Monograph) pole: Deep, unified architectural visions are best authored and owned by a single mind taking full responsibility from first definition to final theorem.
**Instrument** — The instrument question — chalkboard against computation — is the material culture of mathematical thought. The Traditional Chalkboard pole: Dust on slate, live seminars, pure mental calculation. The Digital & Computational Experimentation pole is one rated position on this topic.
**Reward** — Reward names what the mathematical life is ultimately for: private understanding or public immortality. The Private Aesthetic Epiphany pole: The silent ecstasy of understanding an eternal truth. The Institutional Immortality pole: Fields Medal, Abel Prize, named theorems, legacy in the literature.
**Output** — Output tempo — publish fast or polish forever — is a real strategic and ethical choice in a priority-driven field. The Publish quickly pole: Get it out. The Publish, then perfect pole: Get it out; polish in the open. The Polish forever pole: Perfect it first.
**Academic life** — Teaching against research is the academic allocation question every employed mathematician answers with their calendar. The Teaching pole: Passing it on. The Research-led teaching pole: Research first, taught as it is made. The Research pole: Pushing it forward.
**AI & Automated Theorem Proving in Pure Math** — AI theorem proving forces the understanding question again, now with a machine producing the proofs. The Threat to Human Understanding pole: Outsourcing proof search to opaque neural engines risks generating mountains of true but incomprehensible theorems that hollow out mathematical insight. The Conjecture & Formalization Copilot pole: AI excels at auto-formalizing routine lemmas, searching literature, and spotting pattern conjectures—freeing humans for deep conceptual framing. The Co-Creative Discovery Partner pole: Human-AI-verifier loops will open scales of collaborative mathematical exploration completely unreachable by human brains alone.
**Legacy Prestige Journals vs. Open Overlay Publishing** — Publishing's economics — legacy journals against open overlays — decides who owns mathematical prestige. The Flagship Journals Anchor Quality pole: Elite legacy journals (*Annals*, *Inventiones*, *JAMS*) maintain indispensable, rigorous editorial filters that hiring committees can trust. The Hybrid arXiv + Traditional Review pole: Post everything freely on arXiv while gradually reforming traditional society journals toward fair pricing. The Diamond-Open Overlay Journals pole: Mathematicians write, edit, and referee for free—community-run arXiv overlay journals (like *Discrete Analysis*) make commercial publishers obsolete.
**Mathematical Pedagogy: Intuition First vs. Axioms First** — Pedagogy asks in what order intuition and rigor should meet a student meeting analysis for the first time. The Concrete & Geometric Intuition First pole: Motivate every definition with compelling problems, pictures, and applications before introducing formal ε–δ or abstract spaces. The Spiral from Intuition to Rigor pole: Introduce intuitive meaning first, expose where naive intuition breaks, and let rigor enter as the hero that resolves paradox. The Clean Axiomatic Rigor from Day One pole: Teaching sloppy heuristics first creates bad habits students must painfully unlearn; crisp definitions build lasting mastery fastest.
**Stewardship of Mathematical Terminology & Notation** — Terminology stewardship asks whether historical names are continuity or accumulated friction. The Preserve Classical Historical Names pole: Changing notation breaks continuity with a century of classic papers and confuses readers more than quirks ever did. The Pragmatic Clarifying Tweaks pole: Keep established names unless a genuine collision or ambiguity actively traps working researchers. The Systematic Structural Refactoring pole: Crisp, self-consistent terminology (like Bourbaki's *injective/surjective/bijective* or modern categorical naming) permanently lowers cognitive friction.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position


### 3.1 Foundations & Ontology


#### Foundations of Mathematics
- **Platonism** — Reasons: mathematical objects exist whether or not anyone thinks of them, which is why separated cultures converge on the same theorems; discovery explains the compulsion proofs exert — a correct proof feels found, not stipulated Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that a central mathematical truth was genuinely optional — that an equally coherent mathematics with the opposite answer served every purpose as well.
- **Formalism** — Reasons: the game of symbols is checkable by anyone, which is what actually secures agreement; talk of abstract objects adds metaphysics without adding a single theorem Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mathematical fact that could be established only by appeal to the objects themselves, with no formal derivation behind it.
- **Intuitionism** — Reasons: a construction can be exhibited, checked, and run, so constructive truth never outruns our grasp; mental construction explains why mathematics feels both made and binding Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a non-constructive existence proof whose object was later shown to be unbuildable in principle, yet indispensable everywhere.
- **Logicism** — Reasons: reducing mathematics to logic would give it the certainty of logic itself, with no special intuition of objects required; Frege's and Russell's programme shows how much of arithmetic really does unfold from definitions Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mathematical truth shown to need genuinely non-logical content — a set-theoretic axiom no logic alone supplies.
- **Structuralism** — Reasons: what survives every change of representation is structure, and structure is what theorems are actually about; the same group, space, or category appearing in distant fields is the datum ontology must explain Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mathematical object whose identity demonstrably matters beyond its relational place in any structure.
- **Quine–Putnam Indispensability** — Reasons: mathematics earns its objects the way physics earns electrons — by being indispensable to our best science; treating proof as fallible and revisable matches how mathematics actually grows, through conjecture and refutation Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a successful fundamental physics formulated without the mathematics it was claimed to need.

#### Foundations
- **ZFC Set Theory** — Reasons: one cumulative hierarchy hosts essentially all mainstream mathematics in a single, well-understood language; a shared standard lets distant fields compare results without translation loss Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mainstream field that provably cannot be faithfully developed inside ZFC-style set theory.
- **Category Theory / Topoi** — Reasons: relations, not membership, are what mathematics uses — a theorem rarely cares what an object is made of; universal properties capture exactly the content proofs depend on Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a central construction that resists categorical formulation while its set-theoretic version stays simple and fertile.
- **Homotopy Type Theory (HoTT)** — Reasons: identity as equivalence matches how mathematicians actually identify structures in practice; univalence makes transport along equivalence automatic, which is how informal mathematics already reasons Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that univalent foundations misdescribe ordinary practice or block a major body of classical results.

#### Excluded middle
- **Classical Logic** — Reasons: proof by contradiction has delivered central theorems for two millennia without a single inconsistency; refusing it abandons most of analysis for a scruple about infinite domains Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a genuine contradiction traced to a classical proof by contradiction in ordinary mathematics.
- **Classical, constructively careful** — Reasons: keeping classical logic while preferring constructions where they exist gets both security and information; a constructive proof tells you more, so it is worth seeking even when not required Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where constructive preference systematically cost more than the extra information was worth.
- **Constructive Logic** — Reasons: an existence claim without a witness asserts a truth nobody can exhibit, which is assertion without knowledge; constructive proofs compute — they return the object, the bound, or the algorithm Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an indispensable theorem that provably admits no constructive proof, in a domain where its classical use never misleads.

#### Mathematics
- **Discovered** — Reasons: independent invention converges — calculus, zero, and prime patterns arose separately and agree; the resistance mathematics offers to our wishes is the signature of something found, not made Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where two mature mathematical cultures developed genuinely incompatible answers to the same question, both fully workable.
- **Invented, then discovered** — Reasons: we plainly choose axioms, notations, and definitions, so the systems are ours; but once chosen, their consequences surprise us — nobody invented the distribution of the primes inside a system we did invent Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: proof that the consequences were also, in some strong sense, chosen — that a different community could coherently find different primes.
- **Invented** — Reasons: notation, proof styles, and even which problems matter are historical and cultural through and through; calling mathematics discovered disguises human decisions as fate and discourages revising them Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mathematical truth established before any human system existed to state it, in a form no culture could have shaped.

#### The Mathematical Universe
- **Radical Mathematical Realism** — Reasons: the unreasonable fit between pure structures and physical law is explained at a stroke if the universe is a mathematical structure; Tegmark's hypothesis takes mathematics' own completeness seriously — every consistent structure exists physically Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a physical phenomenon shown to have no mathematical structure at all, not merely one not yet found.
- **Structural realism** — Reasons: physics keeps finding mathematical structure in reality, which realism about structure explains without claiming reality is nothing but structure; keeping the identity question open is honesty about what the evidence shows Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration either that structure exhausts reality or that the structural fit is a selection effect of our tools.
- **Epistemic Anti-Realism** — Reasons: we build mathematics from embodied cognition — counting bodies, moving through space — and then select the parts that map the world; the fit is no more mysterious than a key fitting a lock it was cut for Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pure structure, developed with no physical motivation, that later fit physics exactly in ways selection cannot explain.

#### Infinity
- **Actual infinity** — Reasons: Cantor's paradise works: completed infinities give clean, powerful theorems across analysis and topology; the diagonal argument exhibits infinities of different sizes with elementary clarity Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an inconsistency derived from completed infinity in ordinary mathematics, not merely a paradox of naive talk.
- **Working actual infinity** — Reasons: working analysts need completed infinities as instruments whatever their ultimate status; a tool can be reliable without being real, as infinitesimals were for two centuries Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a central result that collapses unless completed infinities are treated as fully real objects.
- **Potential infinity** — Reasons: only a process that can always be continued is ever actually given to us; Aristotle's distinction avoids pretending a never-finished totality is sitting there completed Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a physical or mathematical task that demonstrably requires a completed infinity, not an arbitrarily long finite process.
- **Finitism & Ultrafinitism** — Reasons: huge finite numbers already exceed any physical instantiation, so existence claims about them are as unchecked as infinity claims; Nelson's and Zeilberger's scruples keep mathematics answerable to what can in principle be exhibited Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a feasible construction or physical count that genuinely reaches the magnitudes ultrafinitism questions.

#### Can mathematics be revised?
- **Revisable (conventions)** — Reasons: axioms are adopted, and adoption is a human act — non-Euclidean geometry began when a convention was changed; what a community counts as proof has demonstrably shifted across history Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mathematical truth that survived an attempted communal revision intact, showing it was never up to us.
- **Not revisable (discovered)** — Reasons: theorems do not bend to votes: a community that 'revised' the primes would simply be wrong; the history of mathematics is correction toward the truth, not renegotiation of it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where a community's change of standards changed the mathematics itself, not just its presentation.
- **Revisable in form, not structure** — Reasons: changing coordinates does not change the terrain, and changing axioms-as-presentation does not change structure; this explains both the freedom we observe and the convergence we observe Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two presentations of 'the same' structure that turned out to support genuinely different theorems.

#### How do we know mathematical truths?
- **Intuition** — Reasons: working mathematicians routinely see that a theorem is true before any proof exists, and the seeing guides the proving; Gödel held that perception of abstract objects is analogous to sense perception Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: systematic evidence that pre-proof intuition is wrong as often as right in the cases that matter.
- **Proof** — Reasons: a derivation anyone can check is knowledge that does not depend on private insight; intuition is unshareable and unrevisable, proof is public property Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mathematical truth the community rightly accepted for decades with no derivation behind it.
- **Pattern recognition** — Reasons: recognizing a pattern of relations is learnable, teachable, and checkable against new cases; structural grasp explains both the flash of insight and its reliability better than object-perception does Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a structural pattern confidently grasped by experts that turned out to be an artefact of notation.
- **Construction** — Reasons: knowledge you cannot exhibit is a promissory note; construction redeems it — the built object, sequence, or algorithm is the knowledge Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an existence result the community needed and used correctly for a century with no construction ever found.

#### One true mathematics or many?
- **One true mathematics** — Reasons: independence results show our axioms are incomplete, not that truth is plural — CH is true or false in the one universe of sets; rival systems are partial descriptions of a single reality, or errors Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mature, fully successful mathematics built on negating a truth of the one mathematics, with no loss anywhere.
- **Many mathematics** — Reasons: forcing and alternative axioms produce different mathematics, each internally complete as a practice; calling one of them the true one adds a metaphysical crown no theorem needs Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a canonical extension of our axioms, justified intrinsically, that settles the contested questions the pluralist treats as open.
- **One structure, many presentations** — Reasons: plurality of language with unity of structure explains why translations between systems preserve theoremhood; the many presentations are like many charts of one manifold Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two systems presenting 'the same' structure that provably cannot be translated into one another.

#### Set-Theoretic Universe vs. Multiverse
- **Unique True Universe (V)** — Reasons: independence is a fact about ZFC's weakness, not about sets — the cumulative hierarchy is a definite structure CH is true or false in; large cardinals and Ultimate-L already settle many questions ZFC left open, which is evidence the programme works Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a principled demonstration that the concept of set is inherently plural — that no intrinsic axiom could privilege one universe.
- **Pragmatic Working Pluralism** — Reasons: working set theorists already move between forcing extensions and inner models as tools; insisting on one metaphysical universe adds commitment without adding a single usable theorem Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a canonical axiom, intrinsically justified, that settles CH and reorganizes the field around one universe.
- **Full Set-Theoretic Multiverse** — Reasons: forcing does not merely model other universes, it reveals them — the multiverse is the natural reading of fifty years of independence results; each universe is a legitimate mathematical world, and CH's varying truth is a discovery, not a defect Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an intrinsic principle that collapses the multiverse to one universe while preserving all its mathematics.

#### Unreasonable Effectiveness & Indispensability
- **Quine–Putnam Physical Indispensability** — Reasons: science cannot be stated without mathematics, so a realist about science is already committed to the mathematics inside it; confirmational holism gives no principled line where commitment to atoms ends and commitment to sets begins Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fundamental physical theory reformulated nominalistically, with no loss of empirical content.
- **Cognitive & Structural Filtering** — Reasons: we keep the mathematics that fits and discard the rest, so the surviving fit is partly our own selection; shared origins in symmetry and continuity explain the resonance without Platonic harmony Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pure structure with no selection history — developed for beauty alone — that fit new physics exactly on first contact.
- **Autonomous Platonic Harmony** — Reasons: structures explored for purely aesthetic reasons — Riemannian geometry, group representations — later became physics' language, which selection among human-made tools does not explain; the cosmos being mathematical at core explains it directly Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the celebrated fits are survivorship: a full census showing most pure structures fit nothing physical.

#### Adopting New Axioms Beyond ZFC
- **Freeze Core Foundations at ZFC** — Reasons: ZFC has hosted a century of mathematics without a contradiction and suffices for virtually everything mainstream; each stronger axiom is an uninsurable risk taken for questions most mathematics never touches Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mainstream theorem that genuinely requires axioms beyond ZFC and cannot be recast without them.
- **Intrinsic Conceptual Justification** — Reasons: an axiom should be believed because the concept of set itself demands it, as the power-set and replacement axioms are demanded; consequences alone cannot justify, since a useful falsehood is still false Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a large-cardinal axiom with overwhelming fruitful consequences that intrinsic reflection refuses, where refusing plainly costs mathematics.
- **Extrinsic Fruitfulness Justifies Large Cardinals** — Reasons: physics accepts laws for their explanatory fruit, and mathematics has done the same — determinacy axioms organize the theory of definable reals beautifully; Gödel himself endorsed extrinsic justification for axioms with verifiable consequences Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fruitful axiom system later shown inconsistent, where the fruit had been taken as its warrant.

#### What Do Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems Mean?
- **Truth Outruns Proof (Realist Vindication)** — Reasons: a truth no axiom system captures is a truth about something — incompleteness is the strongest formal evidence that mathematical reality outruns our formalism; it licenses the search for new axioms as discovery, not invention Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proof that the unprovable truths of arithmetic are all of a self-referential kind no realist needs to prize.
- **Technical Limit — Work Goes On (Stronger Systems & Machine Checking)** — Reasons: the theorems are about fixed formal systems and say nothing mystical — working mathematics absorbed them and continued; stronger systems and machine checking handle in practice what incompleteness marks in principle Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a working field actually halted, not merely annotated, by an incompleteness phenomenon.
- **No Surprise: Truth Is Provability With a Witness** — Reasons: constructively there was never a gap to explain: truth just is provability with a witness, so incompleteness surprises only those who separated them; the theorems confirm that unprovable 'truths' were never knowledge Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an unprovable arithmetic truth the community can know by other means, with the knowledge checkable by skeptics.

#### Reverse Mathematics & Axiom Economy
- **Value of Finding Exact Minimal Axioms** — Reasons: knowing a theorem is equivalent to WKL₀ or ACA₀ tells you what the theorem really uses — compactness, computability, comprehension — which ZFC's excess power conceals; the Big Five phenomenon, that most theorems land on a few subsystems, is a discovery about mathematics itself Statistic: Most ordinary theorems calibrate to one of the 'Big Five' subsystems of second-order arithmetic (Simpson, Subsystems of Second Order Arithmetic, 2009). What would move a holder: a demonstration that calibration results mislead — that the equivalences are artefacts of coding choices with no mathematical content.

### 3.2 Method & Style


#### Method
- **The Rising Sea** — Reasons: Grothendieck's rising sea: build the right concepts and the theorem submerges — what was a heroic calculation becomes a definition unfolding; machinery compounds, while ad-hoc tricks die with their problem Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a landmark problem that yielded only to direct assault after decades of machinery failed to dissolve it.
- **The Hammer & Chisel** — Reasons: Erdős-style directness finds the truth of this problem, not of a general theory nobody asked for; elementary ingenuity is checkable, teachable, and often shorter than the machinery built to avoid it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a problem class where direct methods provably stall and only new machinery moved the field.

#### Abstraction style
- **Extreme Structural Abstraction** — Reasons: the meta-pattern is where the new mathematics lives — ∞-categories and motives reorganize whole fields at once; generality is not decoration, it is the discovery of what the examples were examples of Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a universal framework that, once built, explained less than the concrete cases it subsumed.
- **Exceptional Concrete Jewels** — Reasons: the Monster, the Leech lattice, and E₈ are where structure shows its individuality — no general theory predicted them; exceptional objects stress-test every abstraction and several became fields of their own Statistic: The Monster group's order is 808017424794512875886459904961710757005754368000000000, about 8×10^53 (ATLAS of Finite Groups, 1985). What would move a holder: a general theory that predicted an exceptional object before any example of it was found.

#### Abstraction level
- **High abstraction** — Reasons: generality is economy: one categorical proof replaces fifty case arguments; pitched too low, mathematics drowns in computations that a definition would have dissolved Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a high-abstraction programme that produced notation without theorems over a decade of work.
- **Problem-pitched** — Reasons: abstraction is a tool with a correct dosage per problem — too little and you compute blindly, too much and you prove the wrong theorem beautifully; masters are recognizable by dosage, not by allegiance Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that one fixed level of abstraction serves all problems in a field equally well.
- **Low abstraction** — Reasons: concrete computation keeps mathematics honest — you can check a number, and numbers have refuted many elegant generalities; the particular case is where understanding is tested, not merely illustrated Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field transformed by abstraction whose concrete methods had been stuck for a generation.

#### Erdős's "The Book": Proof Aesthetics
- **Brevity & Economy** — Reasons: a proof with nothing removable has the necessity of the theorem itself; economy is respect for the reader's finite life Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a famously economical proof that taught its readers less than a longer, more wasteful one.
- **Shocking Twist of Insight** — Reasons: Hardy's unexpectedness: the twist that turns a problem inside out is the moment mathematics advances; a Book proof should make a reader gasp, not merely nod Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a twist-driven proof later found to hide the real reason the theorem is true.
- **Effortless Inevitability** — Reasons: inevitability is the signature of the right viewpoint — the proof that makes the theorem obvious has found its natural home; such proofs reorganize a field's understanding, not just its results Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an 'inevitable' proof that turned out to depend on a viewpoint the next generation discarded.
- **Elementary Accessibility** — Reasons: a proof an undergraduate can savour democratizes mathematics and proves the idea needs no priesthood; accessibility is a test of whether the prover understands or merely operates Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a deep result whose only known proofs are irreducibly technical, where accessibility demands would simply exclude it.
- **Deep Structural Unity** — Reasons: a proof that links distant branches doubles the value of both — unity is mathematics' scarcest resource; Book proofs should be the ones that redraw the map Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a celebrated unifying proof that turned out to be a translation, adding no theorem either side lacked.

#### Thinking
- **Visual** — Reasons: diagrams carry information symbols flatten — a picture of a flow or a surface can be *seen* to force a conclusion; many mathematicians report thinking in images and translating to symbols only for the record Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that visual reasoning systematically misleads in the holder's own field, beyond the usual cautionary examples.
- **Symbolic** — Reasons: symbols can be checked, pictures cannot — a formula means the same in every reader's hands; symbolic manipulation scales to complexities no picture survives Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major result that existed first as a picture and resisted symbolic capture for years without loss.

#### Intuition
- **Trust** — Reasons: intuition is compressed experience — in masters it detects truth years before proof, as Ramanujan's and Thurston's records show; refusing its guidance wastes mathematics' best instrument Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a careful audit showing expert intuition in the holder's field is right no more often than chance on open conjectures.
- **Trust, but verify** — Reasons: intuition proposes, proof disposes — this division of labour uses intuition's speed without inheriting its errors; the history of analysis is intuition's failures being repaired by exactly this discipline Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a domain where verification is impossible in principle, so intuition must be trusted or the domain abandoned.
- **Distrust** — Reasons: intuition's famous failures — naive set theory, 'obvious' continuity, plausible prime patterns that reverse at Skewes-scale — are the curriculum; only proof has never had to be retracted Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where a community's refusal to trust strong intuition delayed a true theorem by a generation.

#### Examples vs. Axioms & Computation vs. Functoriality
- **Concrete examples or general axioms** — Reasons: starting from rich examples keeps definitions answerable to something real — Halmos and Arnold both taught from cases; and computing small cases by hand is how the pattern is actually found, before any functorial characterization exists Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where example-first development demonstrably produced a tangle that only an axiomatic restart could clean.
- **Explicit computation or functorial abstraction** — Reasons: clean axioms strip accidental features so the theorem proved is the theorem meant; and characterizing objects by universal properties tells you what they are *for*, which a construction alone never says (Mac Lane) Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an axiomatic framework so clean it excluded the motivating examples, or a universal property that hid the computation users needed.

#### Purity of Methods
- **Elementary Self-Contained Purity** — Reasons: a proof inside the native domain shows the theorem's real cause — Selberg–Erdős revealed what the Prime Number Theorem actually rests on; imported machinery can prove while leaving the dependence invisible Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an elementary proof that, once found, taught less than the transcendental proof it replaced.
- **Value Both Elementary & Transcendental** — Reasons: each kind of proof buys something the other cannot — logical dependence from the elementary, bridges from the imported; a mature field keeps both and reads them against each other Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that one of the two kinds is uniformly redundant — that nothing is ever learned from it.
- **Celebrate Cross-Field Heavy Machinery** — Reasons: Wiles's proof is the glory of modern mathematics precisely because Fermat's elementary question needed modular forms and Galois representations; refusing imports would have kept the theorem unproved for further centuries Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a celebrated imported-machinery proof later replaced by an elementary one that explained strictly more.

#### Concrete Classification vs. Universal Properties
- **Explicit Canonical Models** — Reasons: a construction shows what the object is — you can compute with a tensor product built as a quotient, not with a universal property alone; explicit models are also what a proof assistant, and a student, can hold Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a construction whose explicit details actively misled users about the object's real behaviour.
- **Bridge Models & Universal Maps** — Reasons: the universal property gives the concept, the model gives the existence — together they are complete, and neither alone is; this is the working settlement most textbooks actually teach Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major object where the bridge settlement failed — where model and property pulled apart in practice.
- **Pure Universal-Property Definitions** — Reasons: implementation is noise: two models of the same universal property are uniquely isomorphic, so everything provable lives at the level of maps; construction-first definitions mistake a representative for the thing itself Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a theorem that turned on which model of an object was chosen, showing the details were not noise.

#### Experimental Mathematics & Symbolic Pattern-Hunting
- **Pure Deductive Thought First** — Reasons: pattern-matching over tables is how conjectures go wrong — Skewes's number is the standing warning that a million cases can lie; depth comes from deductive thought, with a few hand examples as sanity checks Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major identity or theorem found by large-scale computation that hand examples had missed for a century.
- **Computation Inspires Conjectures Only** — Reasons: computation is the perfect scout: it kills false conjectures in an afternoon and points proof toward the true pattern; Gauss's hand-computed prime tables already worked this way — scouting, not proving Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where computational scouting systematically pointed proof efforts in wrong directions.
- **Full Experimental-Math Discovery Engine** — Reasons: PSLQ found the BBP formula for π, databases like the LMFDB and OEIS now generate theorems, not just conjectures; the laboratory model has already changed what pure mathematics is, and resisting it is nostalgia Statistic: The OEIS holds more than 350,000 integer sequences (OEIS Foundation, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that database-driven discovery produces identities without understanding, at a rate that clogs rather than feeds proof.

#### What Makes a Problem Worth Working On?
- **Crisp & Elementary** — Reasons: a two-line problem anyone can state concentrates difficulty honestly — no machinery to hide behind; Erdős's and Gowers's taste shows how much depth crisp problems hold Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a crisp elementary problem whose solution, once found, opened nothing and taught nothing.
- **Fertile & Theory-Forcing** — Reasons: the landmark problem is an excuse to build the machinery that outlives it — Fermat mattered less than what Wiles built; fertile problems reorganize mathematics for a century Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a theory-forcing programme whose machinery, once built, solved its problem and nothing else.
- **Beautiful & Serious** — Reasons: Hardy's criteria — depth, inevitability, economy, generality — judge mathematics as mathematics, uncontaminated by fashion or funding; beautiful serious problems are the ones still read in a hundred years Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a problem meeting all Hardy's criteria that the field rightly abandoned as sterile.
- **Physically & Geometrically Grounded** — Reasons: problems rooted in physics and geometry come with reality's guarantee of depth — Arnold's point that mathematics is the part of physics where experiments are cheap; grounded problems do not evaporate when fashion changes Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a physically grounded problem that proved shallower than the pure problems it displaced.

### 3.3 Branches & Territory


#### The Quadrivium of Pure Mathematics
- **Algebra & Symmetry** — Reasons: symmetry is the deepest organizing idea in mathematics and physics alike — Galois turned solvability into group theory; where there is transformation, algebra is the native language Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a domain where symmetry language demonstrably obscures the phenomenon it names.
- **Geometry & Topology** — Reasons: shape is what the mind grasps first — topology extracts what survives deformation, the most robust content a space has; geometry is also where pure mathematics keeps meeting physics Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a geometric intuition that misled a field for a generation where algebraic methods would not have.
- **Analysis & Continua** — Reasons: the continuum is where mathematics touches change itself — limits, measure, and variation are the grammar of the physical world; analysis also holds mathematics' hardest open analytic problems Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that continuous models are the wrong idealization for the holder's favourite phenomena.
- **Number Theory & Arithmetic** — Reasons: Gauss's queen: the integers hide the deepest patterns relative to their elementary statement; number theory has seeded algebra, analysis, and geometry for two centuries Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a verdict that number theory's central problems are deep only in the sense of being hard, not in what they connect.

#### Combinatorics
- **Affinity for Discrete Mathematics & Combinatorics** — Reasons: the finite is inexhaustible — Ramsey theory shows complete disorder is impossible, and extremal problems give exact answers; combinatorics is also mathematics' most democratic field, open to a beginner's good idea Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that discrete problems systematically yield less transferable structure than continuous ones.

#### Continuity
- **Discrete** — Reasons: discrete structures are what computation, and increasingly physics, actually run on; finite problems admit exact answers, not approximations Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a discrete model that failed precisely because the continuum limit carried the real phenomenon.
- **Continuous** — Reasons: the continuum carries calculus, geometry, and the mathematics of the physical world; continuous methods unify what discrete case-work fragments Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a continuous theory that dissolved once its discrete skeleton was understood.

#### Purpose
- **Pure** — Reasons: Hardy's defence: mathematics justified by application is justified by the wrong court — its value is intrinsic, like poetry's; pure work has also, repeatedly, become the applied work nobody foresaw Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that pure mathematics left entirely to itself drifts into sterility within a generation.
- **Use-Inspired Basic Research (Pasteur's Quadrant)** — Reasons: Pasteur's quadrant (Stokes) is where the deepest work happens: basic questions chosen because a use demands them; use-inspired choice disciplines taste without surrendering depth Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a use-inspired programme whose chosen use distorted the basic science it produced.
- **Applied** — Reasons: mathematics earns its keep in the world — bridges, codes, medicines, forecasts; applied problems also return the favour, generating pure theory (Fourier analysis from heat) no purist would have commissioned Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an applied field whose mathematics stagnated because application rewarded only the immediately usable.

#### Analytic vs. Algebraic Number Theory
- **Analytic Sieves, L-Functions & Estimates** — Reasons: the integers confess under analysis: zeta zeros govern primes, and sieves give bounds algebra cannot reach; analytic methods produce numbers — estimates, counts, error terms — not just existence Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a prime-distribution mystery that analysis circled for decades and algebra settled at once.
- **Arithmetic-Geometric Synthesis** — Reasons: BSD and Langlands show analytic L-functions and algebraic cohomology are two readings of one object; the synthesis is not a compromise but the discovery that the split was superficial Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where forcing the synthesis hid a phenomenon only one side could see.
- **Algebraic Galois, Cohomology & Motives** — Reasons: rational solutions are algebraic facts, and Galois representations expose their hidden symmetry; schemes and motives give the integers a geometry, which is where their secrets are kept Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an arithmetic question where geometric machinery has produced language but no theorem the analysts lacked.

#### Archimedean Real/Complex vs. p-Adic & Non-Archimedean Worlds
- **Classical Archimedean Continuum** — Reasons: real and complex analysis is the mathematics of the world we measure — manifolds, PDEs, and physical geometry live here; intuition built on the continuum transfers everywhere in applied work Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an arithmetic phenomenon invisible over ℝ and ℂ that only the p-adic places reveal.
- **Adelic Local–Global Balance** — Reasons: Hasse–Minkowski and Tate's thesis show ℝ is one prime among many — the adeles are the integers' true home; local–global thinking has settled questions no single place could Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major result the adelic view complicated without deepening.
- **p-Adic & Perfectoid Ultrametric Beauty** — Reasons: ultrametric geometry is not a curiosity: perfectoid tilting moves theorems between characteristic 0 and p, which classical geometry cannot do; Scholze's Fields-Medal work shows the p-adic world is a second, equal homeland Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that p-adic methods are a translation device whose theorems all have classical proofs of equal clarity.

#### Classical Algebraic Varieties vs. Derived & ∞-Categorical Geometry
- **Concrete Classical Varieties & Schemes** — Reasons: classical varieties and schemes already carry an enormous, working geometry; coherence data is a tax on every definition, paid whether or not the problem needs it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a classical moduli problem whose automorphisms make the classical treatment provably incoherent.
- **Pragmatic Derived Categories When Needed** — Reasons: derived tools are warranted exactly when intersections misbehave or moduli have automorphisms — use them surgically; surgical use keeps the theory answerable to the geometry that summoned it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a problem where partial derived methods failed and only the full ∞-categorical setting worked.
- **Full Higher Stacks & ∞-Category Paradigm** — Reasons: truncation loses theorems: gluing, duality, and deformation all live in the higher homotopies Lurie's programme keeps; derived geometry is not abstraction for its own sake but the native setting the problems themselves dictate Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a decade of higher-stack machinery that produced fewer theorems than the classical methods it replaced, on its own chosen problems.

#### Effective Diophantine Algorithms vs. Ineffective Finiteness Theorems
- **Explicit Computable Bounds Only** — Reasons: a finiteness theorem that cannot list its objects leaves the actual question — which solutions? — unanswered; Baker's bounds and Chabauty–Kim turn existence into answers a person can check Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an ineffective theorem whose later effectivization changed nothing about what the field did next.
- **Finiteness First, Effectivize Later** — Reasons: structural finiteness redraws the map first — Faltings changed Diophantine geometry before any bound existed; effectivization is a second, honourable industry that finiteness makes possible Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a finiteness result that misled the field because its ineffective bound hid how small the truth was.
- **Celebrate Pure Structural Finiteness** — Reasons: Mordell's conjecture proved through Arakelov geometry is a triumph of structural insight regardless of bounds; demanding algorithms confuses mathematics with computation, and some truths are structural in essence Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a structural finiteness proof whose lack of bounds concealed an error or an empty case for years.

#### Group Theory Ethos: Finite Classification vs. Geometric Group Theory
- **Finite Simple Groups & Representation Tables** — Reasons: the classification is a moon-landing of pure thought — decades, thousands of pages, and the sporadic groups nobody predicted; Monstrous Moonshine then tied the Monster to modular functions, proving the finite world is deep Statistic: Estimates put the original classification proof at roughly 10,000 or more journal pages across some 500 articles (Solomon, Notices of the AMS, 2001). What would move a holder: a demonstration that the classification's length makes it effectively unverifiable, and that the field should treat it as a conjecture.
- **Lie & Algebraic Group Symmetry** — Reasons: Lie groups are where algebra meets the continuum — root systems organize geometry, representations, and particle physics in one diagrammatic language; continuous symmetry is the symmetry nature actually uses Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a phenomenon of continuous symmetry that Dynkin-diagram thinking systematically misses.
- **Infinite Geometric & Hyperbolic Groups** — Reasons: Gromov's insight — a group *is* its Cayley graph's large-scale geometry — turned algebra into metric geometry and solved word problems with curvature; quasi-isometry sees what presentations hide Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two groups whose geometric similarity conceals an algebraic difference that matters decisively.

#### Homological Algebra & Cohomology as Universal Lens
- **Centrality of Cohomological & Homological Machinery** — Reasons: cohomology is the universal machine for turning local data into global obstructions — de Rham, sheaf, étale, and motivic versions run geometry, topology, and number theory; one engine, everywhere, is the strongest unity claim modern mathematics has Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major field whose central advances owe nothing to cohomological machinery.

#### Soft Functional Analysis vs. Hard Harmonic & PDE Analysis
- **Soft Banach/Hilbert & Operator Algebras** — Reasons: soft methods prove in a page what hard methods reach in fifty — spectral theory and Hahn–Banach organize infinite dimensions cleanly; structure first is also how the subject is taught, remembered, and extended Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a soft existence theorem whose non-quantitative character blocked the application that motivated it.
- **Dual Soft-Hard Synthesis** — Reasons: concentration-compactness extracts the enemy profile softly, then hard estimates destroy it — Kenig–Merle and Tao show the synthesis is a method, not a mood; each half covers the other's blindness Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a synthesis paper where the soft and hard halves each obscured what the other needed.
- **Hard Oscillatory Integrals & Quantitative PDEs** — Reasons: real waves blow up or scatter for quantitative reasons no compactness argument sees; Littlewood–Paley, Strichartz, and decoupling give numbers — thresholds, rates, sharp constants — which are the physics Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sharp estimate industry that produced constants nobody used while a soft argument settled the question.

#### Low-Dimensional Geometric Topology vs. High-Dimensional Surgery & Homotopy
- **Wild 3- & 4-Manifold Geometry (Thurston/Donaldson)** — Reasons: dimensions 3 and 4 are where topology is alive: hyperbolic manifolds, exotic ℝ⁴s, Ricci flow — phenomena with no higher-dimensional analogue; Thurston and Donaldson showed these dimensions defeat algebraic routine, which is exactly their fascination Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that low-dimensional richness is a temporary artefact of immature invariants.
- **Cross-Dimensional Gauge & Floer Bridges** — Reasons: Floer homology and Khovanov categorification are bridges, not borders — knots compute higher algebra and vice versa; the traffic across dimensions is where the new invariants are born Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a bridge theory that translated results without adding a single theorem to either side.
- **High-Dim Surgery & Stable Homotopy** — Reasons: above dimension 4 the Whitney trick unlocks systematic theory — surgery and stable homotopy have the regularity of a mature science; chromatic towers show pattern at a scale low dimensions cannot reach Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a stable-homotopy programme whose regularity turned out to be regularity of notation, not of phenomena.

#### Metric/Riemannian Geometry vs. Symplectic & Complex Geometry
- **Riemannian Curvature & Geometric Flows** — Reasons: curvature is measurable, local, and dynamic — flows deform spaces toward canonical forms, as Hamilton–Perelman proved for Poincaré; Riemannian geometry gives theorems you can compute with Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a canonical-shape problem that flow methods provably cannot reach.
- **Symplectic Flexibility & Pseudoholomorphic Curves** — Reasons: symplectic geometry's paradox — no local invariants, yet global rigidity (non-squeezing) — is the most surprising phenomenon in modern geometry; Floer homology and mirror symmetry keep it at the centre of physics-adjacent mathematics Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that symplectic rigidity is a small island in a generically flexible ocean.
- **Complex Kähler & Calabi–Yau Rigidity** — Reasons: Kähler geometry fuses the Riemannian, symplectic, and algebraic into one rigid world where Hodge theory and Yau's Calabi theorem interlock; rigidity is fertility — everything constrains everything, so theorems abound Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Kähler rigidity result that turned out to be an artefact of the very special metrics chosen.

#### Classical Manifolds vs. Noncommutative & Synthetic Metric Spaces
- **Smooth Classical Manifolds First** — Reasons: smooth manifolds are the laboratory where geometry was invented and where intuition is reliable; tensors and forms compute — generalization should earn its keep against this standard Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a degenerating family whose limit object is unavoidable in a mainstream problem and has no smooth model.
- **Singular Limits & Alexandrov/RCD Spaces** — Reasons: families of manifolds really do converge to singular spaces — refusing them refuses the compactification theorems geometry needs; synthetic curvature (Lott–Sturm–Villani) handles them with full rigour Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a synthetic-curvature result that dissolved when the approximating smooth geometry was examined closely.
- **Noncommutative Spectral Triples (Connes)** — Reasons: some spaces have no points to chart — foliation quotients, tilings, quantum phase spaces — and Connes's spectral triples capture their geometry in the only language that exists for them; points were a special case all along Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a noncommutative geometry whose spectral triple reproduced, at great cost, what a classical model already said.

#### Probabilistic Method & Random Structures vs. Explicit Deterministic Constructions
- **Explicit Deterministic Constructions Only** — Reasons: an existence proof without an example leaves the *why* untouched — explicit Ramanujan graphs show the mechanism, and mechanisms generalize; 'almost all are' is a statistic, not an understanding Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an explicit-construction programme that stalled for decades on objects the probabilistic method had long since secured.
- **Probabilistic Existence Then Derandomize** — Reasons: randomness first finds the truth's location — sharp thresholds tell constructors exactly what to aim at; derandomization then converts statistics into structure, each step earning the next Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a threshold where derandomization provably cannot follow, making the two-step a dead end.
- **Random Objects Reveal Typical Truth** — Reasons: random objects are the typical objects — Erdős–Rényi graphs and SLE reveal universal laws deterministic examples are too special to show; studying the typical is studying the truth, not evading it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'universal' random law that failed on every structured example the field cared about.

#### Dynamical Systems & Ergodic Theory vs. Static Structure
- **Time-Evolution & Ergodic Dynamics** — Reasons: watching a system move reveals what its statics hide — Furstenberg's correspondence and Ratner's theorems solved static problems by dynamics; orbits carry information no invariant list contains Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a static problem where dynamical methods circled for years and a direct invariant settled it.
- **Static Algebraic & Topological Invariants** — Reasons: invariants are the skeleton: compute them and the object is pinned, permanently, without following any orbit; static structure is also what can be tabulated, taught, and built on Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two objects with identical classical invariants that dynamics distinguishes at a glance.

#### Computability vs. Computational Complexity as Mathematical Foundation
- **Classical Turing Computability & Degrees** — Reasons: undecidability is the clean boundary of the knowable — the halting problem needs no arbitrary resource cutoff to state; Turing degrees organize the uncomputable into a real mathematical landscape Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that degree-theoretic distinctions have no consequence for any computation anyone attempts.
- **Bridging Logic & Complexity** — Reasons: descriptive complexity shows logic and resources are mirror images — Fagin's theorem is a discovery about both; the bridge disciplines each side with the other's precision Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mirror correspondence that turned out to be a coincidence of definitions with no transfer of theorems.
- **Feasible Complexity (P vs. NP) Is Primary** — Reasons: the difference between n² and 2ⁿ decides what cryptography, optimization, and physics can do — P versus NP is the boundary with consequences; halting oracles settle nothing anyone can use Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that feasible-complexity distinctions collapse at the scales that matter, leaving undecidability as the operative boundary.

#### Model Theory & O-Minimality vs. Classical Algebraic Geometry
- **Classical Core Algebra & Geometry** — Reasons: model-theoretic language often re-proves, in jargon, what geometers already knew — direct arguments are sharper, more explicit, and closer to the objects; a translation is not an advance Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mainstream theorem whose only known proof is model-theoretic, with classical attempts stalled for a decade.
- **Model-Theoretic Tame Bridge** — Reasons: o-minimality did what classical tools could not — Pila–Wilkie counting settled André–Oort cases that resisted geometry for decades; the tame/wild distinction is a real discovery, delivered as a bridge Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an o-minimal success later given a shorter classical proof, suggesting the bridge was scaffolding.
- **Model Theory & Stability Organize All Math** — Reasons: classification theory reveals the anatomy of tameness itself — stability, NIP, and definable groups recur across algebra, dynamics, and combinatorics; a theory of which mathematics can be classified is a theory of mathematics Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where stability-theoretic organization predicted structure that turned out not to be there.

#### Descriptive Set Theory & Determinacy vs. General Topology
- **Classical Point-Set & Algebraic Topology** — Reasons: mainstream mathematics lives in manifolds, CW complexes, and Zariski topology, and its theorems do not wait for the projective hierarchy; classification complexity is a logician's overlay, not the practitioner's need Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mainstream classification programme halted by a Borel-complexity obstruction it had not suspected.
- **Borel Equivalence & Polish Pragmatism** — Reasons: knowing a classification is Borel, smooth, or hyperfinite tells you whether complete invariants can exist at all — that is practical knowledge, bought cheaply; it explains failed invariant hunts across ergodic theory and operator algebras Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a classification declared Borel-impossible for which usable complete invariants were later found.
- **Descriptive Set Theory & Determinacy Paradigm** — Reasons: determinacy ties large cardinals to the regularity of every definable set of reals — logic, games, and analysis become one subject; Kechris's programme shows this is a paradigm, not a specialty Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that determinacy's consequences for mainstream analysis were obtainable from far weaker assumptions.

#### Extremal/Ramsey Combinatorics vs. Structural Graph Theory
- **Sharp Extremal & Ramsey Thresholds** — Reasons: thresholds are the facts: Turán densities, Ramsey numbers, Szemerédi bounds say exactly where order becomes inevitable; quantitative precision is what separates knowledge from impression Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a threshold industry computing bounds so loose they constrained nothing anyone studied.
- **Regularity & Pseudorandomness Bridge** — Reasons: the Regularity Lemma's structured + pseudorandom + error split is a universal anatomy of large discrete objects; graphons extend it to limits — the bridge turns extremal questions into structural ones and back Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a class of objects where the regularity decomposition is provably too lossy to recover the extremal truth.
- **Structural Minors & Decomposition Theory** — Reasons: Robertson–Seymour is discrete mathematics' great architecture theorem — every minor-closed world is finitely described; decomposition theory gives algorithms, not just bounds Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a decomposition theorem whose finite obstruction set is in principle unknowable, making the architecture unusable.

#### Category-Theoretic Categorification & Higher Algebra
- **Commitment to Categorification (Numbers → Vector Spaces → Categories)** — Reasons: Khovanov homology categorifies the Jones polynomial and sees more — functoriality under cobordisms is invisible downstairs; lifting numbers to spaces is how mathematics has always deepened, from sets to categories themselves Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a categorified invariant that computed, at great cost, exactly what its decategorified shadow already knew.

### 3.4 Proof & Verification


#### Computer Proof
- **Accept** — Reasons: the Four Color Theorem's proof has been checked, re-checked, and re-implemented — Appel–Haken's argument is a proof whose verification is distributed; refusing it while trusting 500-page human proofs is sentimentality about error rates Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a computer-assisted proof later found wrong in a way its checkers' process should have caught.
- **Accept, with audit** — Reasons: auditability is the honest middle: a computation whose code, data, and logic can be independently re-run meets the standard of a checkable proof; unaudited output is testimony, not proof Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an audited computer proof whose audit trail turned out to be as uncheckable as the original computation.
- **Reject** — Reasons: a proof is a reason a human mind can survey — a computation nobody can follow asks for trust, not understanding; mathematics loses its explanatory core if verification replaces comprehension Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a humanly unsurveyable proof, human or machine, that the field rightly treats as settled knowledge.

#### Proof verification
- **Distraction from Creative Discovery** — Reasons: formalization is a tax on the scarcest resource — research time — paid in syntax-wrangling; specialists and libraries can formalize landmarks without conscripting every discoverer Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a landmark whose informal proof harboured an error only formalization would have caught in time.
- **Targeted Tool for Complex Landmarks** — Reasons: formalize where error is likely and cost is shared: the Kepler conjecture and the Liquid Tensor Experiment show targeted formalization catching real gaps; routine exploration should stay at human speed Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a targeted formalization that consumed a landmark's community for years and found nothing the referees had missed.
- **Future Standard for All Research Proofs** — Reasons: proofs now exceed what refereeing can check — machine-checked libraries are the only scalable bedrock; Mathlib's growth shows the standard is coming whether or not this generation adopts it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that formal libraries introduce their own systematic errors at a rate comparable to human refereeing.

#### Axiom of Choice (AC) Stance
- **Unconditional Acceptance (ZFC)** — Reasons: without Choice, functional analysis loses Hahn–Banach's full power, Tychonoff fails, and vector spaces may lack bases; a century of mathematics is built on AC, and its 'paradoxes' are theorems, not refutations Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a contradiction, not merely a counterintuitive theorem, derived from AC in ordinary mathematics.
- **Skeptical Restriction (ZF + Dependent Choice)** — Reasons: dependent choice keeps analysis's working core — sequences, separability, Baire category — while refusing the non-measurable monsters full Choice manufactures; it is the axiom set that matches what proofs actually use Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mainstream theorem that needs full Choice and whose absence of proof under DC is a real loss, not a curiosity.
- **Defiant Rejection (Constructive / Determinacy)** — Reasons: Banach–Tarski is a reductio: an axiom that duplicates a ball has left mathematics for metaphysics; determinacy and constructivity give a reals with every set measurable — a cleaner world, honestly earned Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a body of indispensable mathematics that determinacy or constructivity provably cannot rebuild.

#### Conjecture orientation
- **Conjecture Solver** — Reasons: famous problems are the field's compass — Riemann and BSD concentrate a century of technique on one point; the hunt itself, win or lose, is how methods are forged Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a famous-problem hunt that consumed a generation and returned neither solution nor method.
- **Solve by building** — Reasons: Grothendieck's and Langlands's lesson: build the theory and the famous problem becomes a corollary or dissolves; solving-by-building wins the problem and the landscape around it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a built theory whose motivating problem survived it untouched, no clearer than before.
- **Theory Builder** — Reasons: worlds outlast problems: a new conceptual language (schemes, categories, distributions) serves a thousand theorems; the theory builder's work is still used when the conjecture hunter's target is forgotten Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a celebrated new language that, a decade on, had re-expressed its field without advancing it.

#### Proof
- **Rigor** — Reasons: formal proof is the only error-correction mathematics has never had to retract; 'it is clear' is where mistakes live, and rigour is the discipline that finds them Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rigorous literature shown to be systematically less reliable than the intuitive practice around it.
- **Intuition-guided rigor** — Reasons: rigour is the standard and intuition is the engine — Thurston's account of proof as communication describes real practice; intuition-guided rigour gets both the discovery and the check Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where the middle discipline decayed into intuition with rigour's branding.
- **Intuition** — Reasons: insight is the mathematics; formalism is its transcription, and transcriptions can be done by assistants and machines; the feel of a structure is what a mathematician actually contributes Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an intuition-led literature that accumulated errors only formalism could clear.

#### Primary Purpose of a Mathematical Proof
- **Ironclad Logical Certitude** — Reasons: certitude is proof's unique product — no other human activity delivers zero-gap deductive guarantee; understanding varies by reader, but the guarantee is the same for all Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proof whose certitude was complete and whose field gained nothing it used.
- **Human Conceptual Understanding** — Reasons: Thurston: a proof that verifies without illuminating has failed its readers — mathematics advances through upgraded mental models; certitude without understanding is a dead asset Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an illuminating 'proof' whose illumination survived the discovery that it had a gap.
- **Generating New Methods & Concepts** — Reasons: Rav's point: the machinery forged inside proofs — new concepts, new bridges — is routinely worth more than the theorem; judging proofs by their theorem alone undervalues the real product Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a method-generating proof whose methods were never used again, by anyone.

#### Informal Conceptual Rigor vs. Bourbaki Precision
- **High-Level Intuitive Fluency** — Reasons: ideas are the content; boilerplate is friction — expert readers fill routine steps faster than authors can write them; burying the idea under ε–δ sludge is a failure of communication, not a virtue Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'routine step' left to readers that concealed the paper's central difficulty for years.
- **Standard Peer-Reviewed Rigor** — Reasons: state hypotheses exactly, prove the non-trivial, compress the routine — this is the settlement refereeing actually enforces; it balances trust against verifiability at a sustainable cost Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the standard settlement's error rate exceeds what either stricter or looser practice achieves.
- **Airtight Axiomatic Completeness** — Reasons: 'left to the reader' is where the ABC-scale failures gestate — decades can pass inside an unexamined 'it is clear'; airtight completeness is expensive once and cheap forever after Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fully explicit literature whose completeness demands made it unreadable and therefore unchecked.

#### Speculative "Theoretical Mathematics" & Heuristics
- **Publish Only Complete Proofs** — Reasons: credit follows proof: publishing unproved claims as near-theorems taxes everyone who must sort the literature; Jaffe–Quinn's targets blurred exactly the line that keeps mathematics' credit system honest Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a speculative literature that policed its own labels so well no reader was ever misled.
- **Clearly Flagged Conjectural Program** — Reasons: flagged speculation is how revolutions start — mirror symmetry's conjectures were labelled and still reorganized two fields; the label, honestly kept, is the whole ethical requirement Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagged-conjecture programme whose flags were ignored by citers until the conjecture was quoted as a theorem.
- **Bold Speculative Synthesis Leads the Way** — Reasons: Ramanujan's formulas, Langlands's letters, mirror symmetry: the syntheses arrive by vision first, and rigour catches up over decades; throttling speculation throttles the supply of things worth proving Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a visionary synthesis that consumed a field's decade and dissolved under rigorous scrutiny.

#### Refereeing Duty & Verification of Ultra-Long Proofs
- **Strict Line-by-Line Public Verifiability Before Acceptance** — Reasons: a theorem nobody independent can follow is not yet proved, whatever its author's authority — the ABC controversy is the test case; public verifiability is not pedantry, it is the definition of proof the community actually uses Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a landmark accepted on insider authority that later verification fully vindicated, at no cost to the field's standards.

#### Do You Trust the Published Literature?
- **Broadly Reliable — Trust the Refereed Canon** — Reasons: peer review plus seminar scrutiny plus downstream use is a triple filter no other literature matches; most published mathematics is used, re-proved, and taught — errors surface Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an audit showing a large fraction of sampled published proofs contain gaps their users never noticed.
- **Trust, But Audit the Landmarks Case by Case** — Reasons: routine papers are sound and landmarks are where length and bespoke language concentrate risk; auditing case by case spends verification effort where the failure probability actually is Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that routine, short papers fail at the same rate as ultra-long landmarks.
- **Systematically Unreliable Until Formalized** — Reasons: referees check plausibility, not line-by-line truth, and complex proofs hide gaps for decades — Voevodsky's own error experience is the cautionary tale; only machine checking warrants full confidence Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a large formalization campaign whose machine-checked corpus proved no more reliable than the refereed one.

### 3.5 Beauty & Reality


#### Values
- **Elegance** — Reasons: a beautiful proof is reusable understanding — elegance signals the right concepts, and right concepts are what transfer; power without elegance is a fact, elegance is a method Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a beautiful proof whose concepts transferred nowhere, while an ugly powerful theorem reorganized a field.
- **Power** — Reasons: a powerful theorem changes what is possible — it is cited, applied, and built on for a century; beauty is private pleasure, power is public utility Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a powerful theorem that, lacking any elegant proof, remained unusable by the fields it should have served.

#### Results
- **Simplicity** — Reasons: simple results are the ones that travel — across fields, into textbooks, into other sciences; cleanliness is a result's carrying capacity Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a simple result whose simplicity concealed the depth that a richer formulation would have revealed.
- **Depth** — Reasons: depth is where the structure is — rich, complex results repay decades of study; simple results are often the visible tip, and mistaking the tip for the mathematics is shallowness Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a deep, complex theory that collapsed into a simple result once the right viewpoint arrived.

#### Beauty
- **Essential** — Reasons: beauty tracks truth too reliably to be accident — Dirac's and Hardy's experience: ugly theories and proofs keep turning out wrong; in a Platonic world, beauty is perception of the structure itself Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a census showing beautiful conjectures fail at the same rate as ugly ones in the holder's field.
- **Beauty as a clue** — Reasons: beauty is a clue with a known failure rate — it deserves weight in choosing problems and conjectures, and none in accepting proofs; weighed, not worshipped, it is a good instrument Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that beauty's signal is pure survivorship — that the failures were as beautiful as the successes.
- **Incidental** — Reasons: beauty is real pleasure and no evidence — the history of mathematics is littered with gorgeous falsehoods; treating taste as testimony confuses the mathematician with the mathematics Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where beauty, against the evidence available, correctly predicted a truth the field had rejected.

#### Hardy's stance
- **Proud Inutility (Hardy's Apology)** — Reasons: Hardy's pride: mathematics that cannot be used for harm or triviality stays clean — uselessness is the guarantee of purity; the Apology's standard judges mathematics as art, answerable only to itself Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pure field corrupted precisely by its uselessness — by having no external check on its fashions.
- **Pleased when it applies** — Reasons: purity is the value and application is a welcome accident — conic sections waited two millennia for Kepler; the holder keeps Hardy's hierarchy and enjoys Wigner's surprises as bonuses Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an application so transformative it retroactively became the pure field's reason for existing.
- **Unreasonable Effectiveness (Wigner)** — Reasons: Wigner's miracle is the datum: mathematics invented for beauty keeps describing physics unreasonably well; a holder takes the fit as the deepest fact about mathematics, not as a bonus Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a full census showing the celebrated fits are rare exceptions among pure structures that fit nothing.

#### Math-physics boundary
- **Autonomous Self-Reference** — Reasons: internal consistency is mathematics' only court — physical intuition has misled before and will again; autonomy protects the long, strange developments (non-Euclidean geometry) physics later needed Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an autonomous development that, cut off from physics, decayed into self-referential sterility.
- **Autonomous, physically nourished** — Reasons: autonomy with nourishment is the historical norm — Fourier, Riemann, and distribution theory all drank from physics and answered to logic; the membrane, not the wall, is the working arrangement Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that physical nourishment systematically biases which pure mathematics gets done, to its detriment.
- **Physical Symbiosis** — Reasons: string theory and QFT have generated conjectures (mirror symmetry, geometric Langlands) pure methods alone had not reached; symbiosis is not contamination, it is cross-fertilization with the only other exact science Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a physics-fertilized programme whose conjectures mostly dissolved, leaving pure mathematics to clean up.

#### Grand Unity vs. Healthy Archipelago
- **Grand Unification (Langlands Ideal)** — Reasons: Weil's Rosetta stone and the Langlands programme keep cashing the unity cheque — number theory, geometry, and representation theory translate into one another; the continent is real, and its map is the century's great project Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a celebrated bridge that turned out to be a formal analogy with no theorem traffic across it.
- **Web of Local Analogies** — Reasons: bridges are thrilling exactly because the territories differ — forcing unity where cultures genuinely differ produces translation, not insight; local analogies, honestly drawn, are the real network Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a unification so complete it showed two 'different cultures' were one subject all along.
- **Irreducible Archipelago of Subfields** — Reasons: combinatorics, PDE, logic, and geometry have different instincts, standards, and heroes, and thrive thereby; the archipelago's diversity is resilience — no single fashion can sink the whole discipline Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that an isolated subfield stagnated for want of a bridge its neighbours already had.

#### Assessing Bourbaki's Historical Legacy
- **Sterile Over-Abstraction** — Reasons: Bourbaki's diagram-free aridity taught generations that mathematics is definition-pushing, and 'New Math' exported the damage to schools; intuition and examples had to be re-imported by Arnold's generation at real cost Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that Bourbaki-era textbooks produced stronger geometric intuition than the ones that replaced them.
- **Essential Cleanup That Overshot** — Reasons: the cleanup was indispensable — terminology, structures, and rigour are Bourbaki's permanent gift — and the overshoot was real but corrigible; later pedagogy re-injected geometry and examples into a frame Bourbaki built Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Bourbaki-standardized area where the standardization itself, not its excess, caused the lasting harm.
- **Monumental Structural Triumph** — Reasons: modern algebraic geometry and representation theory are unthinkable without Bourbaki's architecture — Éléments is the century's load-bearing textbook; the triumph is measured by what stands on it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field that flourished specifically by rejecting Bourbaki's architecture and method.

#### Counterexamples & Mathematical "Monsters"
- **Distracting Pathologies** — Reasons: pathology-hunting is a distraction industry — the smooth, generic, well-behaved objects carry the mathematics and the physics; Poincaré's early complaint stands: monsters are manufactured, not found Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'monster' that became indispensable to a mainstream theory within a generation.
- **Necessary Boundary Fences** — Reasons: counterexamples are fences: they mark exactly where a theorem's hypotheses bite — compactness, separability, measurability; a field without its fences wastes years proving false generalizations Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fence-building literature that fenced off a region later found to contain the main theorem.
- **Vital Teachers of Structure** — Reasons: yesterday's monsters are tomorrow's foundations — Weierstrass functions became Brownian motion, Cantor sets became fractals, exotic spheres became differential topology; the monster is the message that our concepts were too narrow Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a celebrated monster that, fifty years on, remains a pure curiosity with no descendants.

### 3.6 The Mathematical Life


#### Social mode
- **Hyper-Collaborative / Itinerant (Erdős Mode)** — Reasons: Erdős's itinerant collaboration — hundreds of coauthors, problems carried from door to door — produced mathematics no solitary method matches; the network is the instrument Statistic: Erdős published roughly 1,500 papers, more than any other mathematician, most of them jointly (Erdős Number Project, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that hyper-collaboration produces breadth at the cost of the depth solitary workers reach.
- **Collaborative with retreats** — Reasons: collaboration supplies the sparks and retreat supplies the depth — the rhythm alternates by design; neither mode alone finishes a hard proof Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a body of work showing one of the two modes contributing nothing the other lacked.
- **Ascetic Monastic Solitude (Perelman / Grothendieck Mode)** — Reasons: Perelman and Grothendieck show the ceiling of solitude: a decade alone can rebuild a field; solitude protects a vision from the compromises collaboration imposes by default Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a solitary programme that failed for want of one collaborator's correction at the right moment.
- **Massively Open Polymath** — Reasons: Polymath proved mass collaboration works: Gowers's experiment solved problems in weeks by pooling dozens of specialists' tricks in the open; the scratch work being public is the method, not a by-product Statistic: The first Polymath project produced a new proof of the density Hales–Jewett theorem in about seven weeks of open online work (Gowers & Nielsen, Nature, 2009). What would move a holder: evidence that open mass collaboration succeeds only on problems pre-selected to suit it.

#### Authorship & Credit in Collaboration
- **Alphabetical & Egalitarian (Erdős)** — Reasons: alphabetical order is mathematics' egalitarian settlement: the conversation is indivisible, and contribution-ranking corrodes it; Erdős's practice scaled to hundreds of collaborations without a credit war Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a collaboration where alphabetical equality demonstrably cheated the person who did the work.
- **Contribution-Tracked (Mathlib / Polymath)** — Reasons: git logs do not lie: Mathlib and Polymath run on visible contribution, and credit follows the record; transparent tracking is what lets five hundred people trust one proof Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a contribution-tracked project where the metrics were gamed until the record meant less than reputation.
- **Single-Author Ownership (Monograph)** — Reasons: a unified vision needs one accountable mind — the monograph's author owns every error and every choice; committee mathematics blurs responsibility exactly where it matters Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a single-author monograph whose unchecked errors a collaborative process would have caught.

#### Instrument
- **Traditional Chalkboard** — Reasons: the chalkboard forces thought at speaking speed, in company, erasably — live seminars catch errors no notebook session does; dust on slate is a medium for thinking together Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that chalkboard practice systematically excludes results computation would have found.
- **Digital & Computational Experimentation** — Reasons: digital experimentation is exploration at machine speed — plots, searches, and symbolic checks find the pattern before the proof; refusing the instrument is refusing data Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a computation-led exploration that misled its field further than chalk ever did.

#### Reward
- **Private Aesthetic Epiphany** — Reasons: the private epiphany — understanding an eternal truth, alone, completely — is the reward no institution can give or take; Hardy wrote the Apology for exactly this Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mathematician's testimony that the private reward, honestly examined, was not what sustained the work.
- **Institutional Immortality** — Reasons: named theorems and medals are how the field remembers, and memory is the only immortality on offer; institutional honour also funds and protects the work itself Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that prize-chasing measurably deforms the problems a field chooses.

#### Output
- **Publish quickly** — Reasons: priority is real and results decay in drawers — publish, and let the community's scrutiny do the polishing; a result in the open can be used, cited, and corrected Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fast-published result whose errors cost its field more than a year's delay would have.
- **Publish, then perfect** — Reasons: publish the result, then perfect the exposition in the open — arXiv culture's working compromise; the record shows both the claim and its maturation Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the two-stage habit produces a literature of first drafts nobody finishes.
- **Polish forever** — Reasons: a perfected paper is a permanent gift and a rushed one is a permanent tax on readers; polish-forever honours the reader's time over the author's priority Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a polished-forever masterpiece that appeared after its field had re-derived it the hard way.

#### Academic life
- **Teaching** — Reasons: teaching is the transmission line — a field that does not teach itself dies with its discoverers; the lecture room is also where half-understood ideas get understood Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that teaching loads, as actually assigned, destroy the research they are meant to transmit.
- **Research-led teaching** — Reasons: research-led teaching keeps both alive: students meet live mathematics, researchers meet live questions; the settlement costs each side something and buys both more Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a department where research-led teaching measurably served neither students nor research.
- **Research** — Reasons: research is the frontier and the university's irreplaceable function — teaching can be done by many, a proof by one; the calendar should protect the scarce thing Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a research-first culture whose graduates could not reproduce the field they advanced.

#### AI & Automated Theorem Proving in Pure Math
- **Threat to Human Understanding** — Reasons: a proof nobody understands is a certificate, not mathematics — outsourcing search to opaque engines risks a literature of true, dead theorems; understanding is the discipline's actual product Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an AI-generated proof that, once studied, taught its readers a genuinely new idea.
- **Conjecture & Formalization Copilot** — Reasons: AI is a superb copilot for the routine — auto-formalization, literature search, pattern-spotting — freeing humans for framing and judgement; every previous instrument, from tables to CAS, settled at exactly this role Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that copilot use measurably erodes the skills it was meant to free up.
- **Co-Creative Discovery Partner** — Reasons: human–AI–verifier loops reach scales no brain reaches — the collaboration is a new kind of mathematical agent, not a tool; co-creation will be judged by the mathematics it produces, as every method is Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a co-creative programme whose output scaled in volume while its field's understanding measurably thinned.

#### Legacy Prestige Journals vs. Open Overlay Publishing
- **Flagship Journals Anchor Quality** — Reasons: flagship journals are a working filter: editors and referees at Annals-level scrutiny catch what open posting cannot; hiring committees need a signal somebody has vouched for Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an audit showing flagship-journal papers fail verification at the rate of unrefereed postings.
- **Hybrid arXiv + Traditional Review** — Reasons: arXiv already won distribution; reforming the society journals' pricing and speed keeps the filter and drops the paywall; the hybrid is the system actually emerging Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the hybrid's two tiers sort by prestige rather than quality, entrenching what it reformed.
- **Diamond-Open Overlay Journals** — Reasons: mathematicians write, edit, and referee for free, then buy the result back — overlay journals like Discrete Analysis prove the community can own the whole chain; commercial publishing is a toll, not a service Statistic: arXiv passed two million submitted e-prints in 2022 (arXiv.org, 2022). What would move a holder: an overlay journal that failed on the unglamorous work — copy-editing, archiving, dispute-handling — publishers actually do.

#### Mathematical Pedagogy: Intuition First vs. Axioms First
- **Concrete & Geometric Intuition First** — Reasons: motivation first is how concepts are actually learned — ε–δ answers a question the student should be helped to ask; pictures and problems make rigor arrive as rescue, not as ritual Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a cohort study showing intuition-first analysis teaching produced weaker proof skills than rigor-first, at equal effort.
- **Spiral from Intuition to Rigor** — Reasons: the spiral uses both honestly: intuition, then the paradox that breaks it, then rigor as the hero; each turn revisits the concept at a higher level, which is how mathematicians themselves learned it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that spiral courses produce students fluent in neither mode at the point of examination.
- **Clean Axiomatic Rigor from Day One** — Reasons: sloppy heuristics taught first must be unlearned at real cost — crisp definitions from day one build the reflexes analysis actually uses; mastery, once built cleanly, generates its own intuition Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rigor-first course whose survivors were fewer and no stronger than an intuition-first cohort's.

#### Stewardship of Mathematical Terminology & Notation
- **Preserve Classical Historical Names** — Reasons: the literature is a century deep and written in the old names — renaming taxes every reader of every classic paper forever; continuity is a feature readers use daily Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a classical term whose ambiguity demonstrably caused published errors a rename would have prevented.
- **Pragmatic Clarifying Tweaks** — Reasons: keep names unless a collision actively traps working researchers — pragmatic tweaks fix real traps without breaking the archive; the standard is harm, not tidiness Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'harmless' quirk that accumulated into a genuine barrier for an entering generation.
- **Systematic Structural Refactoring** — Reasons: Bourbaki's injective/surjective/bijective shows refactoring works and pays forever; self-consistent names lower every future reader's friction, which compounds across a century Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a systematic renaming that fragmented its field's literature into mutually unreadable eras.

## 4. Archetype research

All 18 samples in the skin have kind "archetype". Ratings in the skin rate advocacy per pole and openness per topic; the receptivity and intensity discussed below are the derived quantities (see §1), described in words, not re-rated here.


### Formalism — `theo-formalism.svg`

Formalism holds that mathematics is the manipulation of symbols according to formal rules — a game played with marks on paper, in Hilbert's image. Mathematical statements do not need to refer to abstract objects to be meaningful; their meaning lies in the rules that govern them. Why: It holds the §3 Formalism reasons on Foundations of Mathematics (topic 1), the Proof pole on how truths are known (§3, How do we know mathematical truths?), and the certitude-first reasons on the purpose of proof (§3, Primary Purpose of a Mathematical Proof). Receptive to: logicism and structuralism, its fellow anti-Platonist neighbours, and machine formalization, because a game of symbols is exactly what a proof assistant plays (§3, Proof verification). Closed to: Platonist and intuition-based epistemology, because private perception of abstract objects is precisely what the symbol-game was built to replace (§3, Intuition). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-formalism.svg

### Intuitionism — `theo-intuitionism.svg`

Intuitionism, founded by L. E. J. Brouwer, holds that mathematics is a mental construction: a statement is true only when we can construct a proof of it. Because truth requires construction, intuitionists reject the law of excluded middle for infinite domains — a claim is not true merely because its negation fails. Why: It holds the §3 Intuitionism reasons on Foundations of Mathematics, the Constructive Logic pole on Excluded middle, and the Construction pole on how truths are known. Receptive to: constructivist HoTT and finitist scruples, which share its demand for witnesses (§3, Infinity; Computer Proof's audit pole). Closed to: classical proof by contradiction over infinite domains and unconditional Axiom of Choice, because both assert existence without construction (§3, Excluded middle; Axiom of Choice). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-intuitionism.svg

### Platonism — `theo-platonism.svg`

Platonism in mathematics holds that numbers, sets, and other mathematical objects exist independently of us, in an abstract realm outside space and time. Mathematical truths are discovered, not invented — we find out what is true of these objects, much as we find out what is true of the physical world. Why: It holds the §3 Platonism reasons on Foundations of Mathematics, the Discovered pole on Mathematics, and the Truth Outruns Proof reading of Gödel (§3, What Do Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems Mean?). Receptive to: universist set theory and indispensability arguments, which supply its ontology with new axioms and scientific warrant (§3, Adopting New Axioms; Unreasonable Effectiveness). Closed to: formalism and invention-first accounts, because a made-up game cannot explain convergence or compulsion (§3, Foundations of Mathematics; Mathematics). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-platonism.svg

### Logicism — `theo-logicism.svg`

Logicism holds that mathematics reduces to logic: mathematical truths are logical truths, and mathematical concepts can be defined in purely logical terms. Associated with Frege and Russell, it treats arithmetic as a branch of logic rather than a separate science of its own objects. Why: It holds the §3 Logicism reasons on Foundations of Mathematics: arithmetic as logic, certainty without special intuition. Receptive to: formalism, its partner in anti-Platonism, and structuralism's relational turn (§3, Foundations). Closed to: indispensability naturalism, which makes mathematics empirical, and intuitionism, which makes it psychological — logicism wants it neither (§3, Unreasonable Effectiveness; How do we know mathematical truths?). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-logicism.svg

### Mathematical Structuralism — `theo-structuralism.svg`

Mathematical Structuralism holds that mathematical objects have no internal nature beyond their relational places inside abstract structures — groups, rings, categories, topoi — and that mathematics is the science of structure as such, not of isolated Platonic individuals. Benacerraf (1965) showed numbers could not be particular objects, Shapiro (1997) built the ontology of structures, and Mac Lane (1986) showed mathematics as form and function. Why: It holds the §3 Structuralism reasons on Foundations of Mathematics, the Pattern recognition pole on epistemology, and the One structure, many presentations pole on plurality. Receptive to: category-theoretic foundations and categorification, its natural machinery (§3, Foundations; Category-Theoretic Categorification). Closed to: object-Platonism about particular numbers, following Benacerraf's dilemma as recorded in its own about text, and to construction-only definitions that mistake a representative for the structure (§3, Concrete Classification vs. Universal Properties). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-structuralism.svg

### Quine–Putnam Indispensability & Quasi-Empiricism — `theo-quine-putnam.svg`

Quine–Putnam Indispensability holds that mathematics sits inside the same holistic web of belief as natural science, earning its ontological warrant from its indispensability to our best physical theories and evolving quasi-empirically through conjecture, counterexample and proof revision. Quine (1951) dissolved the analytic–synthetic boundary, Putnam (1971) drew the indispensability argument, and Lakatos (1976) showed proof itself as a fallible, revisable process. Why: It holds the §3 Quine–Putnam Indispensability pole on Foundations of Mathematics and on Unreasonable Effectiveness & Indispensability, with Lakatosian quasi-empiricism about proof revision (§3, Can mathematics be revised?). Receptive to: use-inspired and physically nourished positions, since science is its court of appeal (§3, Purpose; Math-physics boundary). Closed to: Hardyan proud inutility and autonomous self-reference, because indispensability makes mathematics answerable to physics (§3, Hardy's stance; Math-physics boundary). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-quine-putnam.svg

### The Grothendieckian Structuralist ("Rising Sea" Bird) — `theo-grothendieck.svg`

Dissolves hard problems by discovering their natural functorial, categorical, and cohomological universe where theorems become inevitable. Why: It holds the §3 Rising Sea pole on Method, the Fertile & Theory-Forcing pole on problem choice, and the Full Higher Stacks pole on derived geometry. Receptive to: universal-property definitions, categorification, and grand unification, all machinery-first positions (§3, Concrete Classification; Grand Unity). Closed to: hammer-and-chisel directness and pathology-hunting, which it reads as symptoms of missing concepts (§3, Method; Counterexamples & Monsters). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-grothendieck.svg

### The Erdős–Gowers Problem-Solver ("Frog") — `theo-erdos-gowers.svg`

Attacks concrete, stubborn conjectures directly with bare-handed ingenuity, sharp quantitative estimates, and razor-clean elementary arguments. Why: It holds the §3 Hammer & Chisel pole on Method, the Crisp & Elementary pole on problem choice, and the Random Objects / probabilistic poles in combinatorics (§3, Probabilistic Method; Extremal/Ramsey). Receptive to: experimental computation as scout and to mass open collaboration — Gowers founded Polymath (§3, Experimental Mathematics; Social mode). Closed to: abstraction for its own sake and ineffective finiteness, which give no checkable answer (§3, Abstraction level; Effective Diophantine Algorithms). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-erdos-gowers.svg

### The Hardy–Gödel Platonic Purist — `theo-hardy-godel.svg`

Views mathematics as the timeless, autonomous exploration of an objective realm of pure forms, transfinite infinities, and austere beauty. Why: It holds the §3 Proud Inutility pole on Hardy's stance, the Essential pole on Beauty, and the Truth Outruns Proof reading of incompleteness. Receptive to: actual infinity, large cardinals, and pure structural finiteness — the Platonic realm's furnishings (§3, Infinity; Effective Diophantine). Closed to: indispensability and applied purpose, because usefulness is the wrong court for mathematics (§3, Purpose; Unreasonable Effectiveness). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-hardy-godel.svg

### The Constructivist & Univalent Type Theorist — `theo-constructivist-hott.svg`

Rebuilds mathematics on constructive witnesses, computational content, and homotopy type theory where equivalence is literally equality. Why: It holds the §3 HoTT pole on Foundations, the Constructive Logic pole on Excluded middle, and the Future Standard pole on proof verification, since type theory is its native formalization. Receptive to: Lean-era verification and AI copilots working inside type theory (§3, AI & Automated Theorem Proving). Closed to: unconditional Choice and non-constructive existence, which its foundations refuse on principle (§3, Axiom of Choice; Excluded middle). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-constructivist-hott.svg

### The Lean-Era Mechanized Verifier — `theo-lean-verifier.svg`

Champions machine-checked formalization and human-AI-verifier workflows to eliminate silent errors and build a permanent, composable library of mathematics. Why: It holds the §3 Future Standard pole on Proof verification, the Systematically Unreliable pole on trust in the literature, and the Accept, with audit pole on Computer Proof. Receptive to: HoTT constructivists and AI co-creation, its toolchain partners (§3, Foundations; AI & Automated Theorem Proving). Closed to: intuition-first accounts of proof and publish-only-complete-proofs traditionalism that would leave the corpus unformalized (§3, Proof; Speculative heuristics). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-lean-verifier.svg

### The Arnoldian Physical-Geometric Intuitive — `theo-arnold-intuitive.svg`

Grounds mathematics in spatial vision, classical mechanics, rich concrete examples, and vivid human understanding—wary of sterile formalism. Why: It holds the §3 Physically & Geometrically Grounded pole on problem choice, the Visual pole on Thinking, and the Sterile Over-Abstraction verdict on Bourbaki. Receptive to: concrete examples first on the switchbox's axes, low-dimensional geometry, and physical symbiosis (§3, Examples vs. Axioms; Low-Dimensional Topology; Math-physics boundary). Closed to: pure universal-property definition and diagram-free formalism, which it blames for exiling intuition (§3, Concrete Classification; Bourbaki's Legacy). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-arnold-intuitive.svg

### The Langlands-Style Unifier & Physics-Bridge Builder — `theo-langlands-unifier.svg`

Follows deep structural analogies across arithmetic, geometry, representation theory, and quantum field theory to reveal mathematics' hidden unity. Why: It holds the §3 Grand Unification pole, the Arithmetic-Geometric Synthesis pole on number theory, and the Deep Structural Unity pole on proof aesthetics. Receptive to: physics-bridge positions and categorification, which supply its bridges' far ends (§3, Math-physics boundary; Categorification). Closed to: irreducible-archipelago pluralism and elementary-purity separatism, which deny the bridges exist (§3, Grand Unity; Purity of Methods). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-langlands-unifier.svg

### The Hard Analyst & Nonlinear PDE Tactician — `theo-hard-analyst.svg`

Masters oscillatory integrals, frequency decoupling, and sharp quantitative inequalities to tame wild nonlinear PDEs, dispersive waves, and analytic prime distributions. Why: It holds the §3 Hard Oscillatory Integrals pole on analysis, the Analytic Sieves pole on number theory, and the Explicit Computable Bounds pole on Diophantine effectivity. Receptive to: dual soft-hard synthesis as a tactic and to feasible-complexity thinking, since estimates are its currency (§3, Soft vs. Hard Analysis; Computability vs. Complexity). Closed to: pure structural finiteness and soft-only existence, which decline to give the numbers (§3, Effective Diophantine; Soft vs. Hard Analysis). Emblem: images/emblems/theo-hard-analyst.svg

## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype.** The map's poles are wider than its 18 archetypes. No archetype primarily embodies: finitism/ultrafinitism as a whole philosophy (touched only inside §3, Infinity); logicism as a *working style* beyond its school archetype; descriptive set theory and determinacy as a home territory; model theory as an identity; hard-nosed formalism about terminology and publishing (the overlay-journal and refactoring poles have no champion); or the teaching-first mathematical life (§3, Academic life). The five pure schools (Formalism, Intuitionism, Platonism, Logicism, Structuralism) are archetypes, but the hybrid positions most working mathematicians actually hold — e.g. pragmatic pluralism in set theory, dual soft-hard analysis as an identity — are embodied only partially, by the working-style archetypes.

**Thin topics.** Five topics are single-pole (monopole) topics — Reverse Mathematics & Axiom Economy, Combinatorics, Homological Algebra & Cohomology as Universal Lens, Category-Theoretic Categorification, and Refereeing Duty & Verification of Ultra-Long Proofs. Per the skin's design these are valid affinity topics: the one pole is the position, a positive rating is affinity and a negative rating is aversion, so §3 gives each one position entry. They are thin in one specific way: the indaba can list backing for the pole but cannot locate a disagreement on the topic itself. Several hovertip pole texts are empty in the JSON (e.g. Combinatorics' pole, parts of Hardy's stance, Social mode, Instrument), so §2 and §3 for those poles rest on the pole label and question alone.

**Skin-JSON problems (reported, not fixed).** (1) Three topics have an empty `question` field — Can mathematics be revised? (xth0032), How do we know mathematical truths? (xth0033), and One true mathematics or many? (xth0034). In each case `hovertip.issue` carries the de facto question, and §2/§3 use it as such. (2) Some poles have empty hovertip text (noted above), including the Defiant Rejection pole of Axiom of Choice and two poles of Hardy's stance. (3) The switchbox topic Examples vs. Axioms & Computation vs. Functoriality (xth0043) carries its two crossed axes as two poles whose labels each name both ends of an axis; §3 treats each pole as one axis-position, per the crossed-axes ruling, and does not treat the ends as separate poles. (4) Pole counts run from 1 to 6 (Foundations of Mathematics has six poles on a star-radar widget), wider than the 2–4 range the generic guide describes; §3 covers every pole as listed.

**Deliberately left out of this research.** Biography beyond what an archetype's own about text states; any statistic not listed in §6 — where §3 could not ground a number in a source named with confidence, it says so rather than estimating; and verdicts on which school is correct, since the skin's stance is comparative.
