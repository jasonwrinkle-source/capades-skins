# Praxi — Research

Skin: Praxicapades (`praxicapades.json`) · Domain: Applied Mathematics — how you model the world, how you compute, and what you value in applied work · Meter: practice
Coverage: 75 topics, 225 poles/positions, 13 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Praxi maps applied mathematics: how the world gets modelled, how models get computed, which arenas the work serves, how uncertainty and data are handled, what rigor and validation demand, and how an applied mathematician lives and works. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's questions — a pole a holder advocates for or against (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence), or, on the skin's single-pole topics, a strength of affinity for the one named practice, where a negative rating is aversion to it. What does not count as a position is mere tooling familiarity: the skin rates commitments about what makes a model good, trustworthy, and worth building, not whether someone can operate the software. The meter word is **practice**: the skin measures how strongly a profile stands for each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Two widget rulings apply throughout: the one xy-switchbox topic (Cognitive Working Style) crosses two independent yes/no axes that are not opposites — the status quo is both axes negative — and multi-pole poles are independent positions that need not be opposites of one another. Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness \u00d7 advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole, derived from the advocacy values. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance is pluralist and practice-internal: rival working cultures — Bayesian and frequentist, first-principles and data-driven, rigorous and pragmatic — are placed on one map in one vocabulary, with no culture treated as the default.

## 2. Topic rationale


### The Art of Modeling

**Levins' Grand Model Trilemma** — Levins' trilemma is the founding trade-off of modelling: generality, realism, and precision cannot all be maximized, so every modeller's vertex choice predicts their other answers. The Generality pole: Captures universal behavior of a whole class of systems; sacrifices precision. The Realism pole: Faithfully incorporates every known mechanism; sacrifices simplicity. The Precision pole: Exact quantitative fit to specific data; sacrifices generalizability.
**Predictability** — Predictability asks what kind of world the modeller believes they are modelling — lawful, noisy-lawful, or irreducibly stochastic. The Deterministic pole: Given the inputs, the outputs follow. The Laws with noise pole: Deterministic structure, stochastic detail. The Stochastic pole: You model distributions, not certainties.
**Models** — Continuous against discrete is the first representational choice, made before any equation is written. The Continuous pole: Differential equations, flows, fields. The Hybrid models pole: Continuous where the world flows, discrete where it jumps. The Discrete pole: Difference equations, networks, agents.
**Modeling philosophy** — Modeling philosophy poses derivation against learning: conservation laws first, data first, or the physics-informed hybrid between them. The Deductive First Principles pole: Conservation laws; white-box equations. The Physics-Informed Hybrid pole: First principles with learned components — physics-informed machine learning. The Inductive Data-Driven Surrogates pole: Neural networks, empirical fits; black-box.
**Linearity** — Linearity is the trust question for the oldest trick in applied mathematics — the approximation everything else is built on. The Linear Approximations pole: Superposition, eigenvalue spectra, closed-form perturbation. The Linearize, then check pole: Linear approximations, checked against the nonlinear reality. The Nonlinear Reality pole: Bifurcations, strange attractors, solitons, chaos.
**Breiman's Two Cultures: Generative Models vs. Algorithmic Prediction** — Breiman's two cultures name statistics' deepest split: model the mechanism, or judge by prediction alone. The Generative Stochastic Data Models pole: Specify an explicit stochastic data-generating mechanism whose parameters reflect nature. The Hybrid Interpretable Predictive Craft pole: Combine structured domain models with cross-validated predictive checks and modern regularization. The Algorithmic Black-Box Prediction First pole: Judge models strictly by out-of-sample predictive accuracy regardless of internal parametric form.
**Purpose of Modeling Compass** — The purpose compass forces a ranking of what a model is *for* — forecast, explanation, decision, or stress-testing — before any method is chosen. The Accurate Out-of-Sample Forecast pole: Anchor model evaluation on calibrated predictions of unseen future observations. The Mechanistic Insight & Explanation pole: Build models primarily to expose causal mechanisms and deep structural understanding. The Optimal Decision & Control pole: Evaluate models strictly by the quality of actions, policies, and control loops they enable. The Stress-Testing & Scenario Exploration pole: Use models as structured sandboxes to probe edge cases, tail risks, and counterfactual regimes.
**Asymptotics & Scaling Laws vs. Exact Numerical Precision** — Asymptotics against exact numerics is a mastery question: scaling insight first, computation first, or each checking the other. The Back-of-the-Envelope & Matched Asymptotics pole: True applied-math mastery lies in dimensional analysis, dominant balance, and scaling exponents. The Asymptotics Validated Numerically pole: Derive analytical scaling laws first, then verify prefactors and transition regimes numerically. The Exact Full-Scale Numerical Solution pole: Modern solvers make full high-precision numerical simulation superior to fragile asymptotic limits.
**Deterministic Chaos vs. Stochastic Modeling** — Chaos against stochasticity decides what irregular data *is* — hidden low-dimensional order, or genuine randomness with structure. The Low-Dim Deterministic Attractors pole: Seek low-dimensional nonlinear dynamics and strange attractors behind irregular time series. The Stochastic Perturbations on Dynamical Skeletons pole: Model irregular systems as deterministic dynamical skeletons driven by stochastic forcing. The Fundamentally Stochastic Processes pole: Treat jagged real-world trajectories directly as stochastic processes and SDEs rather than hidden chaos.
**Mean-Field & Continuum Equations vs. Network & Agent Complexity** — Mean-field against agents asks when averages lie: whether hubs, networks, and heterogeneity change the answer, not just the detail. The Analytic Mean-Field & PDE Limits pole: Clean compartmental ODEs and continuum limits capture core macroscopic transitions transparently. The Degree-Heterogeneous Network Theory pole: Extend analytical dynamics onto graph topologies where hubs and clustering alter thresholds. The Detailed Heterogeneous Agent Models pole: Individual heterogeneity, spatial contact, and adaptive behavior invalidate mean-field averages.
**Information Theory & MDL as Universal Organizing Principle** — Information theory as organizing principle is a single-pole affinity topic: it measures commitment to Shannon information and MDL as the north star of inference and model selection. The Minimum Description Length & Entropy as Modeling North Star pole: Ground statistical inference, regularization, and model selection in Shannon information, KL divergence, and compression.
**Sparsity & Compressed Sensing Worldview** — Sparsity is a single-pole affinity topic: it measures belief that high-dimensional signals are secretly low-dimensional, which compressed sensing turns into measurement practice. The Belief in Underlying Low-Dimensional Sparsity pole: High-dimensional signals and natural models possess sparse or low-rank structure recoverable from far fewer measurements.
**High-Dimensional Signal Processing: Wavelets & Harmonic Dictionaries vs. Learned Representations** — Designed wavelets against learned representations is the signal-processing version of the hand-crafted versus end-to-end divide. The Analytic Wavelets, Frames & Scattering pole: Mathematically designed multiscale bases provide stability, interpretability, and provable invariance. The Physics-Structured Learned Dictionaries pole: Embed symmetries, scattering priors, or unrolled sparsity into trainable signal dictionaries. The End-to-End Deep Learned Representations pole: Data-learned neural representations consistently outperform hand-crafted harmonic bases on complex real signals.

### Computation & Method

**The Computational Architecture Quartet** — The computational architecture quartet locates a practitioner's native weapon — mesh, particle, network, or neural surrogate. The Grid & Mesh PDE Solvers pole: FEM, FVM, adaptive mesh refinement, spectral methods. The Particle & Monte Carlo Methods pole: Molecular dynamics, SPH, MCMC, stochastic simulation. The Discrete Networks & Agent Simulators pole: Complex networks, kinetic Monte Carlo, agent-based frameworks. The Neural Operators & AI Surrogates pole: DeepONets, Fourier Neural Operators, PDE emulators.
**Solution method** — Solution method asks whether an answer should be exact and closed, or approximate and computed — and what is lost each way. The Analytic pole: Solve exactly; closed form, pen and paper. The Analytic where possible pole: Closed forms where they exist; numerics for the rest. The Numerical pole: Approximate; algorithms, computers, floating point.
**Approach** — Approach poses simulation against theory as rival arbiters, with the middle pole making each judge the other. The Simulation pole: Run it and see; computational experiments. The Each arbitrates the other pole: Simulation arbitrated by theory; theory tested by simulation. The Theory pole: Prove it; the math tells you what happens.
**Optimization** — Optimization asks whether the true optimum is worth its cost, or a verified heuristic, or a fast good-enough answer. The Exact pole: The true optimum; global, guaranteed. The Heuristic, verified pole: Heuristic search with exact checks where they matter. The Heuristic pole: Good-enough; fast, practical, approximate.
**Convex Guarantees vs. Non-Convex Empirical Power** — Convexity is a guarantee-versus-power trade: reformulate until global optimality is provable, or keep the non-convex expressiveness SGD exploits. The Formulate Convex Problems First pole: Reformulate and relax until convex so global optimality, duality, and reliable convergence are guaranteed. The Convex Relaxations + Local Refinement pole: Use convex relaxations for principled initialization and certificates, then refine non-convex objectives. The Embrace Non-Convex Landscape & SGD pole: Modern non-convex landscapes solved by SGD unlock expressiveness that convexification destroys.
**Solving Ill-Posed Inverse Problems** — Inverse problems are where applied mathematics meets hidden interiors; the three poles are three ways of taming ill-posedness. The Classical Variational Regularization (Tikhonov/TV) pole: Stabilize ill-posed reconstructions with transparent deterministic penalties like Tikhonov and total variation. The Full Bayesian Inverse Posterior pole: Treat reconstruction as posterior inference over function space to quantify uncertainty in unseen interiors. The Learned Plug-and-Play & Diffusion Priors pole: Use data-driven denoisers and diffusion priors paired with forward physics to achieve state-of-the-art reconstructions.
**Classical Optimal Control & MPC vs. Reinforcement Learning** — Control poses certified model-based design against learned policies, with safety as the contested ground. The Model-Based Optimal Control & Lyapunov Safety pole: Use dynamics models, Kalman filters, MPC, and Lyapunov certificates wherever physical safety matters. The Learning-Augmented MPC pole: Keep structured model-predictive control and safety filters while learning residual dynamics from data. The Model-Free Deep Reinforcement Learning pole: Let end-to-end reinforcement learning discover control policies directly in complex high-dimensional tasks.
**Worst-Case Minimax Robustness vs. Average-Case Expected Utility** — Worst-case against average-case is the decision-theoretic temperament question underneath robust optimization. The Optimize Average Expected Performance pole: Maximize expected utility under calibrated probabilistic forecasts without paying excessive worst-case insurance. The Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) pole: Optimize worst-case expected loss over an ambiguity ball of plausible distributions around the empirical data. The Strict Minimax Worst-Case Guarantees pole: Design estimators and decisions to survive adversarial worst-case shifts and tail failures.
**Optimal Transport & Geometric Data Analysis** — Optimal transport and TDA claim geometry and shape beat flat Euclidean statistics for modern data; the topic's two poles are the two claimant tools. The Optimal Transport & Wasserstein Geometry pole: Compare and interpolate distributions via Wasserstein distances and transport geometry rather than flat Euclidean norms. The Topological Data Analysis (Persistent Homology) pole: Extract robust multi-scale shape invariants and persistent homology features from complex high-dimensional point clouds.
**Exact Discrete Optimization (MIP/Branch-and-Bound) vs. Metaheuristics** — Exact discrete optimization against metaheuristics is the NP-hard practitioner's daily fork: certificates or speed. The Exact MIP & Certified Optimality Gaps pole: Exploit modern branch-and-cut MIP solvers to deliver provable optimality bounds and auditable schedules. The Matheuristics (Exact Core + Heuristic Search) pole: Combine exact integer programming subproblems with guided heuristic neighborhoods for massive scale. The Fast Metaheuristics (Simulated Annealing / Genetic / Local Search) pole: Deploy fast domain-tailored metaheuristics when NP-hard combinatorial scale outstrips exact solvers.
**Queueing Theory & Stochastic Networks vs. Fluid & Diffusion Limits** — Queueing theory's exact, limit, and simulation traditions give three different kinds of knowledge about congestion. The Exact Discrete Stochastic Queueing pole: Preserve exact arrival/service distributions, product-form networks, and discrete Markov transitions. The Heavy-Traffic Diffusion & Fluid Limits pole: Use fluid and Brownian heavy-traffic approximations to obtain clean analytical congestion laws. The Discrete-Event Simulation First pole: Real service networks violate tractable assumptions; simulate exact routing and priority rules directly.
**Multi-Objective Pareto Frontiers vs. Scalarized Utility Functions** — Pareto against scalarization asks whether value judgments should be hidden in weights or exposed as a frontier for stakeholders. The Explicit Scalar Cost / Utility Function pole: Force explicit trade-off weights into one scalar objective so optimization delivers a decisive choice. The Constrained Primary + Guardrail Objectives pole: Optimize one crisp primary metric subject to hard safety, equity, and resource constraints. The Full Multi-Objective Pareto Exploration pole: Never hide value judgments inside arbitrary weights—compute and present the full Pareto frontier to stakeholders.

### Domains & Arenas

**The Applied Domain Star** — The applied domain star locates the arena a practitioner's methods serve, against which the other domain topics are border crossings. The Physical Continua & Geophysical Fluids pole: Navier-Stokes, turbulence, geophysics, plasma. The Biological, Ecological & Epidemic Dynamics pole: Morphogenesis, SIR models, neuroscience. The Economic, Financial & Game Theoretic Flows pole: Stochastic calculus, Black-Scholes, systemic risk. The Engineering, Control & Cybernetics pole: Optimal control, Kalman filtering, robotics, MPC. The Information, Tensors & Machine Intelligence pole: Compressed sensing, tensor decomposition, graph algorithms.
**Purpose** — Purpose restates the skin-defining divide from the applied side: pure, use-inspired, or applied. The Pure pole: Math for its own sake. The Use-inspired pole: Pure questions chosen for what they unlock. The Applied pole: Math for what it does.
**Breadth** — Breadth asks whether a practitioner crosses fields, stands T-shaped between them, or goes deep in one. The Interdisciplinary pole: Borrow methods, cross boundaries. The T-shaped pole: Depth in one field, working fluency in the ones it touches. The Domain-Specific pole: Deep in one field; master its methods and literature.
**Rational Nash Equilibrium vs. Behavioral & Bounded-Rationality Modeling** — Nash rationality against behavioural realism decides what a strategic model is allowed to assume about people. The Exact Nash / Bayesian Equilibrium Analysis pole: Anchor strategic analysis in rational utility-maximizing equilibrium as a disciplined benchmark. The Evolutionary & Learning Dynamics pole: Model agents who adapt via no-regret learning and replicator dynamics rather than instant fixed points. The Behavioral Heuristics & Bounded Rationality pole: Ground strategic models in prospect theory, cognitive limits, and empirical human heuristics.
**Fixed Plant Dynamics vs. Strategic Adaptive Agents (Lucas Critique)** — The Lucas critique topic asks what a deployed policy does to the system it governs — fixed plant, adversarial shift, or strategic agents who game the rule. The Fixed Physical Dynamics & Feedback Stability pole: Model the system as an indifferent physical plant governed by known dynamics and design provably stable feedback controllers. The Adversarial & Distribution-Shift Robustness pole: Guard policies against worst-case disturbances, non-stationary environments, and performative distribution shift. The Strategic Incentive Compatibility (Beat Goodhart / Lucas) pole: Assume human or economic agents actively best-respond to your decision rule — design incentive-compatible mechanisms rather than static optimizations.
**Mechanism Design & Social Choice Paradoxes** — Mechanism design is a single-pole affinity topic: it measures how seriously impossibility theorems and incentive compatibility should constrain real institutions. The Taking Impossibility Theorems (Arrow / Gibbard–Satterthwaite) & Incentive Compatibility Seriously pole: Treat formal incentive compatibility and social-choice impossibility theorems as hard engineering constraints on institutions.
**Time-Series Forecasting: Classical State-Space / ARIMA vs. Deep Sequence Models** — Time-series forecasting's classical, hybrid, and deep traditions compete on the same public record of competitions. The Interpretable ARIMA, ETS & State-Space pole: Structured state-space and exponential-smoothing models remain unmatched in interpretability and small-sample robustness. The Hybrid Decomposition + Gradient Boosted Trees pole: Combine seasonal-trend decomposition and domain features with boosted trees across related series. The Deep Transformers & Foundation Time-Series Models pole: Cross-learning deep sequence architectures and foundation forecasters win at massive multi-series scale.
**Spatial Statistics & Geostatistics: Kriging/GPs vs. SPDE Mesh Models** — Spatial statistics is organized around one bottleneck — the O(N³) Gaussian-process cost — and three ways past it. The Exact Gaussian Processes & Classical Kriging pole: Preserve exact covariance kernels and classical kriging equations wherever sample size permits. The Sparse Local Approximations (Vecchia / NNGP) pole: Scale Gaussian processes to millions of sites via ordered conditional Vecchia and nearest-neighbor approximations. The Stochastic PDE & INLA Meshes pole: Represent Matérn spatial fields as sparse Markov random fields on finite-element meshes via SPDE/INLA.
**Quantitative Finance: No-Arbitrage Stochastic Calculus vs. Econophysics & Agent Markets** — Quantitative finance splits between no-arbitrage pricing discipline and the econophysics of real, crashing markets. The No-Arbitrage Martingale Pricing (Black–Scholes / HJM) pole: Anchor derivatives pricing and hedging in martingale equivalence and no-arbitrage stochastic calculus. The Rough Volatility & Regime-Aware Stochastic Finance pole: Retain arbitrage discipline while incorporating rough volatility, jumps, and liquidity frictions. The Econophysics, Microstructure & Agent Markets pole: Reject frictionless equilibrium pricing; model order books, endogenous feedback, and crash cascades directly.
**Causal Inference Framework Allegiance** — Causal inference has three formal languages; allegiance to one shapes what counts as an answer. The Potential Outcomes (Neyman–Rubin) pole: Formalize treatment effects through counterfactual potential outcomes, assignment mechanisms, and design-based randomization. The Structural Causal Graphs (Pearl DAGs) pole: Encode qualitative causal structure in directed acyclic graphs with do-calculus and graphical identification rules. The Dynamical & Structural Econometric Systems pole: Represent causality through coupled structural equations, equilibrium behavior, and dynamical feedback.
**Reduced-Form Identification vs. Structural Modeling** — Reduced-form against structural modelling is empirics' identification-versus-counterfactual trade, stated plainly. The Design-Based Reduced-Form Clarity pole: Prioritize transparent natural experiments (IV, DiD, RDD) with minimal functional-form assumptions. The Sufficient-Statistics Bridge pole: Combine clean reduced-form elasticities with compact structural formulas to evaluate welfare and policy. The Full Structural Counterfactual Models pole: Only full behavioral and equilibrium models can predict counterfactual policies never seen before.

### Uncertainty & Data

**The Epistemic Uncertainty Quartet** — The uncertainty quartet names the four sources a modeller can blame — chance, parameters, structure, or deep ambiguity — and the blame chosen decides the remedy. The Aleatoric Uncertainty pole: Inherent, irreducible randomness (thermal noise, quantum fluctuations). The Epistemic Parameter Uncertainty pole: Inexact coefficients; reducible with more sensors. The Structural Model Misspecification pole: The equations themselves are wrong; unmodeled physics. The Knightian Ambiguity / Extreme Regimes pole is one rated position on this topic.
**The Inverse Problem** — The inverse problem topic places a practitioner's work on the forward-assimilation-inversion spectrum. The Forward Simulation pole: Given equations and initial conditions, calculate the future. The Real-Time Data Assimilation pole: Continuous Bayesian updating; blending sensor data with simulation. The Inverse Problem & Parameter Identification pole: Infer hidden physics from sparse, noisy measurements.
**Statistics** — Statistics in the diamond sense is the probability-philosophy question: Bayesian, frequentist, likelihood, or pragmatic pluralism. The Bayesian Updating pole: Priors, posteriors, MCMC; quantifying subjective confidence. The Pragmatic Pluralism pole: Draw pragmatically from Bayesian, frequentist, and pure-likelihood toolkits as each problem demands. The Frequentist / Objective Likelihood pole: Confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, objective frequencies. The Pure Likelihood pole: Evaluate statistical evidence directly through likelihood ratios without subjective priors or long-run sampling spaces.
**Data** — Data asks the volume question: more data, right-sized data, or small data with better models. The Big Data pole: More data; let the data speak; machine learning, scale. The Right-sized data pole: As much data as the model can honestly use. The Small Data pole: Better models; careful design; insight over volume.
**Significance testing** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is p < 0.05 the gatekeeper of a result, a broken ritual, or one tool among others?” Significance testing is the gatekeeping ritual whose status — gatekeeper, broken, limited, tightened, or replaced — every empirical practitioner must settle. The The standard (gatekeeper) pole: A result clears p < 0.05 or it does not clear. The Broken (abandon it) pole: Significance testing is a broken ritual. Stop using it as the gate. The Useful but limited pole: A p-value is one tool, read with effect sizes and intervals. The Tighten Threshold (p < 0.005) pole: Keep frequentist significance testing but raise the default discovery bar to p < 0.005 to slash false-positive rates (Benjamin et al. 2018). The Switch to Bayes Factors & Posteriors pole: Replace p-values with Bayes factors and hierarchical posteriors that directly weigh competing hypotheses. The Effect Sizes & Estimation Intervals pole: Replace dichotomous significance gates with point estimates, effect sizes, and confidence or compatibility intervals (Cumming 2014; Amrhein et al. 2019).
**Multiple Testing & Selective Inference Discipline** — Multiple testing discipline decides what a thousand simultaneous claims owe the reader: family-wise control, false-discovery control, shrinkage, or held-out proof. The Strict Family-Wise Error Control (FWER) pole: Control the probability of even a single false positive via Bonferroni, Holm, or confirmatory hierarchical gatekeeping. The False Discovery Rate & Selective Inference (FDR / Knockoffs) pole: Control the expected proportion of false discoveries (Benjamini–Hochberg, knockoffs, post-selection adjustment) in high-dimensional screening. The Hierarchical Bayesian Partial Pooling pole: Shrink noisy estimates toward a shared multilevel prior rather than inflating p-value thresholds. The Held-Out Validation & Predictive Error pole: Skip formal multiplicity tables and judge discovered features strictly by out-of-sample held-out predictive performance.
**Bayesian Prior Philosophy** — Bayesian prior philosophy asks where priors may legitimately come from — invariance, regularization, or elicited knowledge. The Objective / Reference Non-Informative Priors pole: Derive priors via invariance, maximum entropy, or reference rules to let the likelihood dominate. The Weakly Informative Regularizing Priors pole: Use domain-scaled regularizing priors to rule out absurd parameter values while staying responsive to data. The Elicited Subjective Domain Priors pole: Encode genuine prior scientific knowledge and expert beliefs explicitly as coherent subjective probabilities.
**Intractable Posteriors: MCMC vs. Variational/Laplace vs. Simulation-Based Inference** — Intractable posteriors force a computational allegiance: exact MCMC, fast approximation, or likelihood-free simulation. The Asymptotically Exact MCMC (HMC / NUTS) pole: Pay the compute cost for asymptotically exact Hamiltonian Monte Carlo and rigorous convergence diagnostics. The Fast Variational & Laplace / INLA Approximations pole: Trade exact tail geometry for orders-of-magnitude speed via variational inference, Laplace, or integrated nested Laplace approximations. The Likelihood-Free Simulation-Based Inference (ABC / Neural SBI) pole: When realistic forward simulators have intractable likelihoods, infer posteriors directly from simulated rollouts via ABC or neural posterior estimation.
**High-Dimensional Regime (p ≫ n): Non-Asymptotic vs. Classical Asymptotics** — The high-dimensional regime asks which asymptotic story, if any, tells the truth when p rivals n. The Classical Fixed-p Large-n Asymptotics pole: Standard large-sample normality and Fisher-information limits remain the cleanest core of inference. The Proportional High-Dim Limits (p/n → γ) pole: Analyze modern ML and statistics in the random-matrix proportional regime where dimension scales with sample size. The Strict Finite-Sample Non-Asymptotic Bounds pole: Demand explicit concentration inequalities and finite-sample guarantees that hold at actual n and p.
**Parametric Structure vs. Nonparametric & Distribution-Free Guarantees** — Parametric structure against distribution-free guarantees is the efficiency-versus-insurance trade in inference. The Structured Parametric Models pole: Explicit parametric families maximize statistical efficiency and interpretability when checked carefully. The Semiparametric Targeted Efficiency pole: Target low-dimensional scientific estimands while estimating nuisance functions flexibly (TMLE / double ML). The Distribution-Free & Conformal Guarantees pole: Demand bootstrap, permutation, and conformal coverage guarantees that survive arbitrary model misspecification.
**Gaussian Thin-Tail Convenience vs. Heavy-Tail Extreme Risk** — Gaussian convenience against heavy tails is the risk temperament question: workhorse or trap. The CLT Gaussianity Is Sound Default pole: Central-limit behavior makes Gaussian models and variance-based tools reliable workhorses across most domains. The Robust Heavy-Tail Diagnostics pole: Use Student-t likelihoods, Huber losses, and tail checks to safeguard standard models against outliers. The Extremistan Power-Law & EVT First pole: Assume heavy tails, power laws, and extreme-value theory first—Gaussian averages fatally hide ruinous tail risk.
**Design-Based vs. Model-Based Survey & Sampling Inference** — Survey inference under collapsing response rates asks whether design weights or models carry the credibility now. The Strict Probability Sampling (Horvitz–Thompson) pole: Credible population inference rests on known random selection probabilities and design-based estimators. The Model-Assisted Calibration & MRP pole: Pair probability frames with multilevel regression and poststratification to repair nonresponse bias. The Superpopulation Model-Based Adjustment pole: When response rates collapse below 10%, strong hierarchical modeling outperforms noisy design weights.
**The "Big Data Paradox": Sample Size vs. Data Quality** — The Big Data Paradox is a single-pole topic: it measures how uncompromising a practitioner is that unbiased provenance beats massive biased volume, in Meng's sense. The Prioritizing Unbiased Small Probability Samples Over Massive Biased Big Data pole: Recognize that tiny selection bias collapses the effective sample size of millions of records to dozens.
**Missing Data & Censoring Philosophy** — Missing data philosophy asks how much invented data is too much — prevention, principled imputation, or worst-case bounds. The Complete-Case Caution & Design Prevention pole: Prevent missingness through study design and avoid aggressive imputation models that invent data. The Multiple Imputation Under MAR pole: Propagate uncertainty cleanly via principled multiple imputation and full-information likelihood under MAR. The Explicit MNAR Sensitivity & Tipping-Point Bounds pole: Never trust unverifiable MAR assumptions—compute Manski worst-case bounds and MNAR tipping points.
**Non-Stationarity & Structural Breaks vs. Stationary Ergodicity** — Non-stationarity asks whether drift is a nuisance to transform away, a regime structure to model, or the permanent condition. The Transform to Stationary Ergodic Core pole: Difference, detrend, and cointegrate until classical stationary ergodic theory applies cleanly. The Online Changepoint & Regime-Switching pole: Model discrete structural breaks explicitly with Bayesian online changepoint detection and hidden regimes. The Assume Perpetual Non-Stationary Drift pole: Real-world distributions never hold still—rely on online adaptive conformal and continual recalibration.
**Forecast Evaluation: Proper Scoring Rules vs. Point-Error Metrics** — Forecast evaluation asks what a forecast is graded *by* — point errors stakeholders grasp, interval coverage, or strictly proper scores. The Business-Aligned Point Errors (RMSE / MAE) pole: Evaluate forecasts with intuitive point-error metrics tied directly to operational cost and stakeholder comprehension. The Quantile & Interval Coverage Checks pole: Require prediction intervals and pinball quantile losses alongside point accuracy. The Strictly Proper Probabilistic Scoring (Log / CRPS) pole: Only strictly proper scoring rules incentivize honest, well-calibrated full predictive distributions.
**Handling Model Misspecification ("All Models Are Wrong")** — Misspecification is universal ('all models are wrong'); the topic asks what protection a holder buys against it. The Iterative Posterior Predictive Checking pole: Cycle through Box's loop—fit, criticize against posterior predictive checks, and expand the model where it fails. The Sandwich & Misspecification-Robust Inference pole: Keep simple working models and protect standard errors with Huber–White sandwich and robust variance estimators. The Model Averaging & Stacking Ensembles pole: Never bet on one parametric family—combine diverse candidate models via Bayesian stacking and cross-validated ensembles.
**Statistical Data Visualization: Minimalist Integrity vs. Exploratory Interactivity** — Data visualization poses the chart as static instrument, grammatical construction, or interactive canvas. The Tufte–Cleveland Minimalist Static Precision pole: Maximize data-ink integrity and perceptual accuracy in disciplined, publication-grade static figures. The Layered Grammar of Graphics (Wilkinson/Wickham) pole: Build transparent, reproducible statistical graphics through declarative grammatical mappings of data to aesthetics. The Interactive Exploratory Dashboards & Linked Views pole: Empower analysts and stakeholders with linked brushing, zooming, and interactive visual exploration.

### Rigor & Validation

**Modeling values** — Modeling values asks how much rigor applied work owes — proof-grade, decision-grade, or flight-grade. The Pure Rigor (Analysis of PDEs) pole is one rated position on this topic. The Rigorous enough to trust pole: As much rigor as the decision riding on it requires. The Pragmatic Engineering Utility pole: If the plane flies and the bridge stands, the model is valid.
**Values** — Values in this skin's sense: the beautiful model against the effective one, with the middle pole claiming effectiveness is the beauty. The Elegance pole: A beautiful model; clean, spare, insightful. The Elegant because effective pole: Elegance valued where it produces effectiveness. The Effectiveness pole: An effective model; it predicts, it controls, it delivers.
**Scope** — Scope asks whether the deliverable is a general theory, general machinery fitted bespoke, or one tailored solution. The General pole: A theory covering many problems; reusable, abstract. The General method, bespoke fit pole: General machinery, fitted to the specific case. The Specific pole: A solution tailored to one problem; bespoke, concrete.
**Model complexity** — Model complexity spans toy models to double descent: how much machinery a phenomenon is owed. The Occam's Minimalist Toy Model pole: A 2-variable ODE revealing the essential mechanism. The As complex as needed pole: The simplest model that still captures the phenomenon. The Multi-Physics High-Fidelity Ensemble pole: A 50-million variable coupled simulation. The Overparameterized Double Descent pole: Embrace massive overparameterization beyond the interpolation threshold as a modern generalization strategy.
**Modeling goal** — Modeling goal separates understanding mechanisms from forecasting outcomes, with the middle pole claiming prediction reveals mechanism. The Mechanistic Understanding pole: Explain why; uncover causality and feedback loops. The Understanding through prediction pole: Forecast well enough and the mechanism has to show itself. The Predictive Supremacy pole: Forecast what happens next; minimize error.
**Randomized Experiments vs. Observational Causal Identification** — Randomized experiments against observational identification is the causal credibility ladder, with three rungs practitioners actually stand on. The No Causation Without Manipulation — RCT Gold Standard pole: Physical randomization is the gold standard; observational correlations rarely justify confident causal claims. The Quasi-Experimental Design-Based Pluralism pole: Combine RCTs with credible natural experiments (DiD, RDD, IV) when randomization is infeasible. The Observational Adjustment & Sensitivity Analysis pole: Target-trial emulation, g-methods, and quantitative sensitivity analyses unlock vital causal questions RCTs cannot touch.
**Preregistration & Multiverse Analysis vs. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)** — Preregistration against exploration is the forking-paths dispute: Tukeyan craft, a sealed split, or mandatory audits. The Tukeyan Open Exploratory Craft pole: Rich interactive exploration uncovers unexpected patterns and anomalies that rigid preregistration misses. The Strict Split: Exploratory vs. Confirmatory pole: Explore freely on training/discovery data, but seal confirmatory hypotheses on fresh holdout samples. **Classical Factorial DoE vs. Adaptive Bayesian Optimization & Bandits** — Experimental design poses Fisher's orthogonal craft against adaptive bandit allocation, with a batch-sequential settlement. ### 3.1 The Art of Modeling


#### Levins' Grand Model Trilemma
- **Generality** — Reasons: a general model teaches the whole class at once — Levins's point that generality is what makes a model a theory rather than a fit; sacrificing precision is the honest price of saying something about systems never measured Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a general model whose qualitative predictions failed in the very class it claimed to cover.
- **Realism** — Reasons: a model that omits a known mechanism will be wrong for a knowable reason — realism keeps faith with the system's actual causal structure; when the model fails, the failure localizes to a mechanism, not to a fiction Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a realistic model so parameter-hungry that its forecasts lost to a two-parameter caricature across a whole domain.
- **Precision** — Reasons: decisions are made on numbers, so the model that nails the specific case is the one that earns its keep; precision is checkable in a way generality and realism are not Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a precise model that collapsed the moment it was carried one step outside its calibration data.

#### Predictability
- **Deterministic** — Reasons: deterministic structure is what modelling has repeatedly found underneath apparent randomness — celestial mechanics to fluid equations; noise is usually unmodelled determinism, and treating it as real randomness surrenders explanation Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mature domain where the residual randomness proved irreducible in principle, not merely unmodelled.
- **Laws with noise** — Reasons: most successful working models are exactly this: a deterministic skeleton carrying stochastic detail — state-space models, SDEs; the hybrid matches how nature presents: law in the large, chance in the small Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where the hybrid's noise term turned out to contain the entire phenomenon.
- **Stochastic** — Reasons: distributions are the honest output: ensemble weather forecasting beats any single trajectory because the world, or our access to it, is stochastic at the level decisions care about; modelling the distribution *is* modelling the system Statistic: Numerical weather prediction has gained roughly one day of predictive skill per decade (Bauer, Thorpe & Brunet, Nature, 2015). What would move a holder: a stochastic model outperformed everywhere by a deterministic one in the holder's own arena.

#### Models
- **Continuous** — Reasons: flows are the native mathematics of continua — fluids, fields, populations at scale — and differential equations carry two centuries of theory; discretization is an implementation detail, not the model Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a continuum model whose discrete microstructure reversed its qualitative prediction.
- **Hybrid models** — Reasons: the world itself is hybrid — continuous flow punctuated by discrete events, births, failures, trades; hybrid models refuse to flatten either regime into the other Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hybrid model whose interface between regimes became its largest source of error.
- **Discrete** — Reasons: agents, networks, and events are what many systems actually consist of — epidemics spread person to person, not density to density; discrete models keep the individuals the continuum averages away Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a discrete model at a scale where its continuum limit was provably the whole story.

#### Modeling philosophy
- **Deductive First Principles** — Reasons: a model derived from conservation laws extrapolates: it is right outside its data because it is right about the world; fitted models interpolate beautifully and fail silently at the boundary Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a first-principles model beaten out-of-sample by a fitted surrogate in a regime both claimed to cover.
- **Physics-Informed Hybrid** — Reasons: physics-informed learning takes the law where it is known and learns only the residual — the hybrid dominates its parents on real problems, as physics-informed neural networks show; neither purity is a virtue in itself Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hybrid whose learned component quietly violated the physics it was supposed to respect, undetected.
- **Inductive Data-Driven Surrogates** — Reasons: modern data volumes changed the economics: a surrogate that predicts superbly and trains in hours beats a derivation that takes a career; mechanism is a luxury when the forecast is the product Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a black-box surrogate whose silent extrapolation failure caused a real-world loss.

#### Linearity
- **Linear Approximations** — Reasons: linearization is the most successful approximation in science — superposition, spectra, and perturbation theory built engineering; most systems, most of the time, are operated near a point where linear is true enough Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship linear design that failed because the operating point wandered into the nonlinear regime it ignored.
- **Linearize, then check** — Reasons: linearize for insight, then check against the nonlinear reality — the check is cheap and the insight is real; this is the working settlement of every mature modelling shop Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the checking step is systematically skipped in practice, making the settlement fictional.
- **Nonlinear Reality** — Reasons: bifurcation, chaos, and solitons are not decorations — they are what nonlinear systems *do*, and linear models cannot see them at all; insisting on the full dynamics is insisting on the phenomenon Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a nonlinear analysis that produced chaos-theoretic language where a linear model predicted everything observed.

#### Breiman's Two Cultures: Generative Models vs. Algorithmic Prediction
- **Generative Stochastic Data Models** — Reasons: Breiman's first culture: a model that names the data-generating mechanism can be interrogated, extrapolated, and believed; parameters that mean something are the difference between statistics and curve-fitting Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a generative model whose named mechanism was decisively rejected while its predictions stayed excellent.
- **Hybrid Interpretable Predictive Craft** — Reasons: structured models checked predictively get both virtues — the mechanism disciplines the fit, held-out accuracy disciplines the mechanism; Efron's and Shmueli's reconciliations describe what good shops already do Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that hybrid practice inherits both cultures' failure modes rather than neither.
- **Algorithmic Black-Box Prediction First** — Reasons: held-out accuracy is the only court that cannot be lobbied — random forests and boosting won Breiman's argument empirically; a mechanism story that predicts worse is a story, not a model Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a black-box champion that failed catastrophically under a distribution shift a generative model survived.

#### Purpose of Modeling Compass
- **Accurate Out-of-Sample Forecast** — Reasons: forecasting is the falsifiable purpose: a model that cannot predict unseen data has not been tested at all; calibration on the future is the anchor that keeps every other purpose honest Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a beautifully explanatory model that predicted nothing, yet rightly changed its field's decisions.
- **Mechanistic Insight & Explanation** — Reasons: Epstein's case: most models are built to understand, and a model that exposes the mechanism serves every future decision, not just the next forecast; insight compounds, forecasts expire Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mechanistic insight, widely believed, that a forecasting test would have killed a decade earlier.
- **Optimal Decision & Control** — Reasons: a model that improves the decision has done the whole job — control and policy are where models meet consequences; judging by decision quality aligns the modeller's incentive with the user's Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a decision-optimal model whose opacity caused the organization to misuse it at scale.
- **Stress-Testing & Scenario Exploration** — Reasons: sandboxes protect against the forecast's blind spot: the regime that matters is the one with no data; stress-testing is the only purpose that takes tail regimes seriously before they arrive Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a stress-testing programme whose scenarios were all drawn from the same imagination that built the model.

#### Asymptotics & Scaling Laws vs. Exact Numerical Precision

#### Deterministic Chaos vs. Stochastic Modeling

#### Mean-Field & Continuum Equations vs. Network & Agent Complexity

#### Information Theory & MDL as Universal Organizing Principle
- **Minimum Description Length & Entropy as Modeling North Star** — Reasons: Shannon gave inference a currency: bits — MDL turns Occam's razor into arithmetic, and compression is the one model-selection principle that cannot be gamed by parametrization; Rissanen's programme unifies estimation, prediction, and code length in a single ledger Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a model-selection problem where MDL's chosen model was decisively worse by every predictive measure.

#### Sparsity & Compressed Sensing Worldview
- **Belief in Underlying Low-Dimensional Sparsity** — Reasons: sparsity is an empirical discovery, not a hope: natural signals *are* compressible, and Candès–Romberg–Tao turned that into measurement — fewer samples, exact recovery; low-rank structure is the hidden simplicity that makes high-dimensional inference possible at all Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a structured learned model whose embedded prior turned out to be the wrong symmetry, silently.

What would move a holder: a learned representation that failed out-of-distribution where a wavelet baseline degraded gracefully.

What would move a holder: a simulation study whose warm-up and replication choices manufactured its headline result.

#### Multi-Objective Pareto Frontiers vs. Scalarized Utility Functions
- **Explicit Scalar Cost / Utility Function** — Reasons: a decision needs a number: scalarization forces the trade-off into the open where it can be argued, signed, and owned; a frontier handed to a committee is a decision deferred, not supported Statistic: No reliable statistic found. - **Constrained Primary + Guardrail Objectives** — Reasons: optimize the primary metric and fence the rest with hard constraints — safety and equity as guardrails, not trade goods; this matches how regulated decisions are actually legitimated Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a guardrail constraint that quietly became the binding objective, distorting the primary metric it fenced.
- **Full Multi-Objective Pareto Exploration** — Reasons: weights are value judgments wearing arithmetic — Pareto exploration keeps them with the stakeholders, where they belong; computing the frontier is the analyst's job, choosing on it is not Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Pareto presentation so wide that decision-makers picked by politics exactly as before, with extra steps.

### 3.3 Domains & Arenas

- **Biological, Ecological & Epidemic Dynamics** — Reasons: life is the growth arena: morphogenesis, epidemics, and neuroscience generate new mathematics as fast as physics did; biological dynamics is where modelling meets systems that evolved, not designed Statistic: No reliable statistic found. - **Economic, Financial & Game Theoretic Flows** — Reasons: markets are mathematics with consequences priced in real time — stochastic calculus and systemic-risk models are tested by money itself; game-theoretic flows are where models change the system they model Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a financial model whose widespread adoption manufactured the crash it failed to foresee.
- **Engineering, Control & Cybernetics** — Reasons: control is applied mathematics with a feedback loop and a safety case — Kalman filtering landed spacecraft; engineering arenas grade models by whether the machine works Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a control-theoretic success that turned out to owe more to engineering margin than to the model.
- **Information, Tensors & Machine Intelligence** — Reasons: information is the newest arena and the fastest-moving: tensors, compressed sensing, and graph algorithms are the mathematics of the data century; intelligence, natural and artificial, is an applied-math problem now Statistic: No reliable statistic found. #### Purpose
- **Pure** — Reasons: pure mathematics is the seed corn — today's number theory is tomorrow's cryptography; an applied skin that forgets the pure pole forgets where its tools come from Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pure field that, after a century, has yielded nothing any application could use.
- **Use-inspired** — Reasons: Stokes's quadrant again: basic questions chosen for the uses they unlock produce both depth and impact; use-inspired choice is a taste-discipline, not a compromise Statistic: No reliable statistic found. - **Applied** — Reasons: applied mathematics is judged in the world: the model ships, the bridge stands, the trial reads out; usefulness is not a lower standard, it is a different and harder one Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an applied field captured by its clients' deadlines until no reusable mathematics remained.

#### Breadth
- **Interdisciplinary** — Reasons: methods migrate: the modeller who crosses fields carries tools no native has — interdisciplinarity is a research strategy, not a dilution; most new applied fields are border crossings that stuck Statistic: No reliable statistic found. - **T-shaped** — Reasons: the T-shape is the employable, effective form: one deep root, working fluency around it; depth without breadth cannot talk to its users, breadth without depth cannot deliver Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a domain specialist blind-sided by a method from a neighbouring field that solved their open problem in a year.

#### Rational Nash Equilibrium vs. Behavioral & Bounded-Rationality Modeling
- **Exact Nash / Bayesian Equilibrium Analysis** — Reasons: equilibrium is the disciplined benchmark: Nash analysis forces every assumption about incentives into the open; departures from rationality should be measured against it, not instead of it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an equilibrium analysis whose benchmark status protected it from a decade of contrary evidence.
- **Evolutionary & Learning Dynamics** — Reasons: agents learn: replicator and no-regret dynamics model the adaptation equilibrium assumes away; evolutionary dynamics explains which equilibria are ever reached, which is the operative question Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a learning-dynamics model whose adaptation story could fit any outcome and therefore predicted none.
- **Behavioral Heuristics & Bounded Rationality** — Reasons: people use heuristics, frame effects, and prospect-theoretic valuations — Kahneman and Tversky measured it; a strategic model of humans that assumes rationality is a model of nobody Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a behavioural model so parameter-rich it explained the experiment it was fitted to and no other.

#### Fixed Plant Dynamics vs. Strategic Adaptive Agents (Lucas Critique)
- **Fixed Physical Dynamics & Feedback Stability** — Reasons: most plants are indifferent: a bridge, a reactor, a circuit does not read your policy — model the physics, prove stability, ship it; feedback design on fixed dynamics is control theory's validated core Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'fixed plant' deployment where the plant's operators adapted to the controller and voided its proof.
- **Adversarial & Distribution-Shift Robustness** — Reasons: the environment shifts and adversaries probe: robust design against distribution shift and performative effects is the price of deployment in open systems; Doyle's robust control was built for exactly this Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a robustness programme so preoccupied with adversaries that it underperformed a naive controller in the actual, benign environment.
- **Strategic Incentive Compatibility (Beat Goodhart / Lucas)** — Reasons: Lucas and Goodhart are laws of social systems: agents best-respond to the rule, so the rule must be designed as a mechanism, not an optimization; incentive compatibility is the difference between a policy and a wish Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an incentive-compatible mechanism whose strategic sophistication priced out the honest behaviour it wanted.

#### Mechanism Design & Social Choice Paradoxes
- **Taking Impossibility Theorems (Arrow / Gibbard–Satterthwaite) & Incentive Compatibility Seriously** — Reasons: Arrow and Gibbard–Satterthwaite are not curiosities — they are engineering constraints, like thermodynamics: institutions designed against them fail predictably; Roth's market design shows impossibility-aware design working in kidneys and schools Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a successful institution that violated incentive compatibility flagrantly and thrived for decades anyway.

#### Time-Series Forecasting: Classical State-Space / ARIMA vs. Deep Sequence Models
- **Interpretable ARIMA, ETS & State-Space** — Reasons: state-space and exponential-smoothing models are interpretable, robust at small samples, and still win their share of competitions — Hyndman's textbook tradition is a technology of trust; a forecaster should know what the model thinks the level is Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a classical forecaster beaten across a whole public benchmark by methods with no interpretable state at all.
- **Hybrid Decomposition + Gradient Boosted Trees** — Reasons: decomposition plus boosted trees is the competition-proven hybrid: structure where structure exists, learning where it does not; M-competition leaderboards are full of exactly this architecture Statistic: The M4 competition compared forecasting methods on 100,000 time series (Makridakis, Spiliotis & Assimakopoulos, International Journal of Forecasting, 2020). What would move a holder: a hybrid whose hand-built features capped its accuracy below the end-to-end baseline.
- **Deep Transformers & Foundation Time-Series Models** — Reasons: cross-learning at scale is a phase change: deep sequence models and foundation forecasters exploit thousands of related series no classical fit can pool; the M5 generation showed learned models taking the top of the board Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a foundation forecaster that lost to seasonal naïve on the holder's own production series.

#### Spatial Statistics & Geostatistics: Kriging/GPs vs. SPDE Mesh Models
- **Exact Gaussian Processes & Classical Kriging** — Reasons: exact Gaussian processes are the principled article: the kernel *is* the model, kriging's equations are sixty years validated; where n permits, approximation is a self-inflicted wound Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship exact-GP analysis whose n forced a subsample that threw away the phenomenon's scale.
- **Sparse Local Approximations (Vecchia / NNGP)** — Reasons: Vecchia and nearest-neighbour approximations keep the GP's semantics and break the cubic wall — millions of sites, principled conditioning; the approximation error is itself understood Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Vecchia ordering whose artefacts were mistaken for spatial structure in a published map.
- **Stochastic PDE & INLA Meshes** — Reasons: the SPDE move is the elegant one: a Matérn field *is* a Markov random field on a mesh, so INLA delivers full Bayesian spatial inference at sparse-matrix speed; Lindgren–Rue–Lindström changed what spatial statistics can attempt Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an SPDE mesh whose boundary effects contaminated the region the study existed to estimate.

#### Quantitative Finance: No-Arbitrage Stochastic Calculus vs. Econophysics & Agent Markets
- **No-Arbitrage Martingale Pricing (Black–Scholes / HJM)** — Reasons: no-arbitrage is finance's conservation law: martingale pricing disciplines every model that touches a traded price; abandoning it abandons the difference between pricing and storytelling Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a no-arbitrage model whose hedges failed in the crash its assumptions excluded.
- **Rough Volatility & Regime-Aware Stochastic Finance** — Reasons: keep the arbitrage discipline and repair the dynamics — rough volatility and jumps fit the smile the classical model cannot; this is normal science: same court, better physics Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rough-volatility calibration that fit the smile and mispriced every hedge it was bought for.
- **Econophysics, Microstructure & Agent Markets** — Reasons: real markets are order books, feedback, and cascades — econophysics models the mechanism that actually generates prices, including the crashes equilibrium treats as exogenous; Bouchaud's programme takes the data's fat tails seriously Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an agent-market model that generated realistic crashes and predicted none.

#### Causal Inference Framework Allegiance
- **Potential Outcomes (Neyman–Rubin)** — Reasons: potential outcomes make the causal question precise — what would have happened, to this unit, otherwise — and tie identification to the assignment mechanism, which is checkable; Rubin's framework is design-first, and design is where credibility lives Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a potential-outcomes analysis whose assignment story was clean and whose answer was still wrong for reasons only a graph would have shown.
- **Structural Causal Graphs (Pearl DAGs)** — Reasons: graphs make assumptions visible: a DAG states what the analyst believes about the world's structure, and do-calculus derives what is identifiable from it; Pearl's framework turns hand-waving about confounding into a theorem Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a DAG whose elegant identification rested on an arrow whose absence nobody could defend.
- **Dynamical & Structural Econometric Systems** — Reasons: real systems feed back: structural equations and dynamical systems represent the equilibrium and adjustment that static frameworks freeze; econometrics' structural tradition models the mechanism policy actually moves Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a structural system whose beautiful feedback story was unidentified by its own equations.

#### Reduced-Form Identification vs. Structural Modeling
- **Design-Based Reduced-Form Clarity** — Reasons: a clean natural experiment beats a beautiful model: IV, DiD, and RDD state their assumptions in plain sight and need no functional-form faith; Chetty's credibility revolution moved whole fields by design, not by specification Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a design-based literature whose local estimates, each impeccable, summed to no answer to the policy question asked.
- **Sufficient-Statistics Bridge** — Reasons: sufficient statistics are the honest bridge: take the credibly estimated elasticity, insert it in the compact welfare formula, and say exactly what was assumed; Heckman's and Chetty's middle is where policy numbers should come from Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sufficient-statistics calculation whose 'compact formula' hid the general-equilibrium effect that dominated.
- **Full Structural Counterfactual Models** — Reasons: only a structural model can price a policy nobody has tried — reduced forms are local by construction; counterfactuals are the questions governments actually ask, and behaviour plus equilibrium is the only machinery that answers Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a structural counterfactual that failed out-of-sample against the reduced-form benchmark it dismissed.

### 3.4 Uncertainty & Data


#### The Epistemic Uncertainty Quartet
- **Aleatoric Uncertainty** — Reasons: some randomness is the world's, not ours: thermal and quantum noise do not shrink with more sensors; naming aleatoric uncertainty correctly stops the waste of trying to measure it away Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an 'irreducible' noise floor that better instrumentation quietly lowered by an order of magnitude.
- **Epistemic Parameter Uncertainty** — Reasons: most working uncertainty is parameter uncertainty — coefficients known loosely and knowable better; it is the reducible kind, and the remedy is measurement, which makes it the optimistic pole Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a parameter-estimation programme that converged beautifully on the parameters of the wrong equations.
- **Structural Model Misspecification** — Reasons: the equations are the risk: structural misspecification is the uncertainty that survives infinite data; Box's warning lives here — the model class, not its coefficients, is where big errors hide Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a misspecification panic that dissolved when the 'wrong' equations predicted the new regime correctly.
- **Knightian Ambiguity / Extreme Regimes** — Reasons: Knight's ambiguity is the honest name for regimes with no stable distribution at all — crises, novel technologies, tipping points; pretending a probability exists here is the deepest false precision Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an 'unknowable' regime that a boring actuarial table priced correctly for a decade.

#### The Inverse Problem
- **Forward Simulation** — Reasons: forward simulation is the validated direction: given laws and initial conditions, compute — weather, structures, circuits; it is where applied mathematics' public successes live Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a forward programme whose initial-condition sensitivity made its long-horizon output a coin flip.
- **Real-Time Data Assimilation** — Reasons: assimilation is the living loop: sensors correct the simulation continuously, so the model never drifts far from the world; operational weather forecasting is this pole, industrialized Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an assimilation system that fit its sensors so eagerly it chased their noise into the forecast.
- **Inverse Problem & Parameter Identification** — Reasons: the inverse problem is where the value hides: inferring the hidden interior — the tumour, the reservoir, the parameter — from sparse surface data; it is ill-posed, essential, and the field's hardest honest work Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an inverse solution whose regularization, not the data, supplied the headline structure.

#### Statistics
- **Bayesian Updating** — Reasons: Bayes is the complete logic of belief revision: priors state what you know, posteriors what you should now think, and MCMC computes it; uncertainty stated as probability is the deliverable decision-makers can use Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Bayesian workflow whose posterior was driven by a prior nobody would defend once it was plotted.
- **Pragmatic Pluralism** — Reasons: the toolkit is larger than any church: likelihood for evidence, frequentist calibration for error rates, Bayes for coherent updating — Gelman's pluralism is what strong practitioners actually do; allegiance is a tax on judgement Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pluralist workflow that chose its method after seeing which answer it preferred.
- **Frequentist / Objective Likelihood** — Reasons: long-run error rates are the public standard: a 95% interval should cover 95% of the time, whoever computes it; frequentist objectivity is what lets strangers trust each other's numbers Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a confidence procedure whose stated coverage failed by ten points on the data it was sold for.
- **Pure Likelihood** — Reasons: the likelihood ratio is the evidence, full stop — it needs no prior anyone can dispute and no imaginary repetitions; pure likelihood inference reports what the data said, not what the analyst believed or the procedure promises Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a likelihood analysis paralysed at the exact decision point where a prior or a procedure was needed.

#### Data
- **Big Data** — Reasons: data volume changed what is learnable: phenomena invisible at n=100 — rare events, fine structure, personalization — are plain at n=10⁸; 'let the data speak' built the models that now win Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a big-data model that learned its collection process instead of its phenomenon, at scale.
- **Right-sized data** — Reasons: the right size is set by the model's honest appetite: enough data to estimate what is claimed, no more collected than can be curated; right-sizing is respect for both statistics and subjects Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a right-sizing judgement that starved a model of the data its own diagnostics begged for.
- **Small Data** — Reasons: small, designed data beats volume: Fisher's century — a careful experiment answers the question a million scraped rows circle; insight per observation is the metric volume cannot game Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a small-data study whose elegant design answered a question subtly different from the one asked.

#### Significance testing
- **The standard (gatekeeper)** — Reasons: a gate keeps the literature's floor: p < 0.05 is arbitrary the way a blood-alcohol limit is arbitrary — a line, enforced, beats a judgement, negotiated; fields without gates fill with noise Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an audit showing gatekept literatures no more replicable than ungated ones.
- **Broken (abandon it)** — Reasons: the ritual is broken: p-hacking, publication bias, and the replication crisis are its fruits — Amrhein and colleagues' call to retire significance is a diagnosis, not a mood; stop using a broken gate as a gate Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field that abandoned the gate and filled measurably faster with false claims.
- **Useful but limited** — Reasons: a p-value read with effect sizes and intervals is one honest tool among several — the abuse is dichotomous thinking, not the statistic; reform the reading, keep the tool Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that 'read with effect sizes' is advice the literature demonstrably does not follow.
- **Tighten Threshold (p < 0.005)** — Reasons: Benjamin et al.'s proposal is the conservative repair: keep the frequentist gate, raise it to 0.005 for new discoveries, and watch false positives fall; it is cheap, enforceable, and already costed Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 0.005 regime whose main effect was to halve a field's discovery rate without improving its replication rate.
- **Switch to Bayes Factors & Posteriors** — Reasons: Bayes factors answer the question actually asked — how much more likely is this hypothesis than that one — and posteriors carry the uncertainty forward; replacing the gate with evidence-weighing is the principled exit Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Bayes-factor literature whose prior sensitivity made its verdicts as negotiable as the p-values it replaced.
- **Effect Sizes & Estimation Intervals** — Reasons: estimation is the grown-up format: a point estimate with a compatibility interval says everything a p-value says and more — Cumming's 'new statistics' is a reporting standard any field can adopt tomorrow Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an estimation-first journal whose intervals were read as significance tests by its own authors.

#### Multiple Testing & Selective Inference Discipline
- **Strict Family-Wise Error Control (FWER)** — Reasons: in confirmatory work one false positive can license a drug or a policy — family-wise control is the ethics of the claim, not pedantry; Bonferroni and gatekeeping hierarchies are how regulated science keeps its promises Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a FWER regime so strict it screened out a generation of real effects along with the false ones.
- **False Discovery Rate & Selective Inference (FDR / Knockoffs)** — Reasons: screening thousands of hypotheses with family-wise control is statistical self-harm: Benjamini–Hochberg prices discoveries in proportion, and knockoffs extend the discipline to selection itself; FDR is the error rate discovery science actually means Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an FDR-screened literature whose realized false-discovery proportion ran far above its nominal level.
- **Hierarchical Bayesian Partial Pooling** — Reasons: partial pooling is the Bayesian answer to multiplicity: shrink the noisy extremes toward the group, and the multiple-comparisons problem dissolves rather than being policed — Gelman's multilevel argument is that the adjustment should be a model, not a penalty Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pooled model whose shrinkage erased the one subgroup effect that was real and large.
- **Held-Out Validation & Predictive Error** — Reasons: a discovery that cannot predict held-out data is not a discovery — predictive validation is the multiplicity discipline that cannot be argued with; it also grades the whole pipeline, not just the test Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a held-out regime gamed by repeated peeking until its validation meant as little as the p-values it replaced.

#### Bayesian Prior Philosophy
- **Objective / Reference Non-Informative Priors** — Reasons: objective priors keep Bayes honest between strangers: invariance and reference rules make the prior a convention, not a belief, so the data dominate; Jaynes's programme is Bayesianism with the subjectivity engineered out Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two equally 'objective' prior rules giving opposite posteriors on the same small sample.
- **Weakly Informative Regularizing Priors** — Reasons: weakly informative priors are regularization with a Bayesian accent: they rule out the absurd values that destabilize fits while letting data speak; Gelman's practice is the working standard of applied Bayes Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a weakly informative prior that, on inspection, was doing the decisive work in a headline result.
- **Elicited Subjective Domain Priors** — Reasons: knowledge exists before the study: eliciting it honestly as a prior is more scientific than pretending ignorance; subjective Bayes is the only version that takes 'prior information' literally Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an elicited prior that encoded the sponsor's hoped-for conclusion and survived every sensitivity check offered.

#### Intractable Posteriors: MCMC vs. Variational/Laplace vs. Simulation-Based Inference
- **Asymptotically Exact MCMC (HMC / NUTS)** — Reasons: HMC/NUTS is the gold standard because it is asymptotically exact with diagnostics that complain — Hoffman and Gelman's sampler made full posteriors routine; paying compute for exactness is paying for the right answer Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an MCMC analysis whose diagnostics passed and whose posterior was still wrong in the tail that mattered.
- **Fast Variational & Laplace / INLA Approximations** — Reasons: variational and Laplace methods trade a known approximation for orders of magnitude of speed — most decisions do not need the tail's third decimal; INLA built a whole spatial-statistics practice on the trade Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a variational posterior whose understated variance licensed a decision the exact posterior would have forbidden.
- **Likelihood-Free Simulation-Based Inference (ABC / Neural SBI)** — Reasons: some simulators have no likelihood to write down — SBI and ABC infer from the forward model itself, which is the only honest route for much of physics and biology; neural posterior estimation made it fast enough to use Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an SBI posterior that fit the simulator's artefacts more faithfully than the phenomenon's.

#### High-Dimensional Regime (p ≫ n): Non-Asymptotic vs. Classical Asymptotics
- **Classical Fixed-p Large-n Asymptotics** — Reasons: classical asymptotics built statistics and still anchor its cleanest results — Fisher information and normality at large n are not approximations to apologize for; most real n are larger than the theory needs Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a classical interval whose coverage collapsed at exactly the moderate n its paper claimed.
- **Proportional High-Dim Limits (p/n → γ)** — Reasons: the proportional regime is where modern practice lives: p/n → γ explains double descent and random-matrix behaviour that fixed-p theory calls paradox; Wainwright's programme reads the present correctly Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proportional-regime prediction that failed at the finite sizes practitioners actually use.
- **Strict Finite-Sample Non-Asymptotic Bounds** — Reasons: finite-sample bounds hold at the n and p on the desk, not in a limit nobody reaches — concentration inequalities are the guarantees a regulator can audit; asymptotics of any flavour is a promise about infinity Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a non-asymptotic bound so loose it certified nothing any decision could use.

#### Parametric Structure vs. Nonparametric & Distribution-Free Guarantees
- **Structured Parametric Models** — Reasons: a correct parametric family is worth orders of magnitude of data — efficiency is not a luxury when samples are expensive; structured models also extrapolate, which flexible fits cannot promise Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a parametric efficiency triumph reversed when the family was wrong by one component.
- **Semiparametric Targeted Efficiency** — Reasons: target the estimand, flex the nuisance: TMLE and double ML buy parametric-grade answers for the quantity that matters with machine-learned nuisances; semiparametrics is the professional's allocation of assumptions Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a double-ML estimate whose nuisance fits were so poor the 'robustness' was nominal.
- **Distribution-Free & Conformal Guarantees** — Reasons: conformal prediction delivers coverage with no distributional assumptions at all — a guarantee that survives misspecification is the only kind a deployment should quote; distribution-free is not a fallback, it is a standard Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a conformal deployment whose exchangeability assumption failed silently under drift, voiding the guarantee it advertised.

#### Gaussian Thin-Tail Convenience vs. Heavy-Tail Extreme Risk
- **CLT Gaussianity Is Sound Default** — Reasons: the central limit theorem is not a convenience, it is a phenomenon: sums of small effects really are near-Gaussian across most of science; variance-based machinery is reliable precisely where its conditions are checked Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Gaussian workhorse model implicated in a real-world blow-up its thin tails had priced as impossible.
- **Robust Heavy-Tail Diagnostics** — Reasons: robust diagnostics keep the workhorse and fence the cliff: Student-t likelihoods, Huber losses, and tail checks catch the outliers Gaussianity would swallow; this is maintenance, not apostasy Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a robustified pipeline whose diagnostics flagged tails so often the team learned to ignore them — including the real one.
- **Extremistan Power-Law & EVT First** — Reasons: Mandelbrot was right where it matters most: in finance, networks, and extremes, power laws rule and Gaussian averages are ruinous fictions; EVT first is the only posture that prices the event that ends the firm Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an EVT-first analysis that fitted a power law to lognormal data and insured against the wrong catastrophe.

#### Design-Based vs. Model-Based Survey & Sampling Inference
- **Strict Probability Sampling (Horvitz–Thompson)** — Reasons: probability sampling is the only design whose bias is zero by construction — Horvitz–Thompson needs no model of the nonrespondents; every model-based repair is a bet, and design is the refusal to bet Statistic: The Literary Digest's roughly 2.3 million returned ballots still predicted the wrong winner in 1936 — the standing lesson that volume does not cure selection bias (Squire, Public Opinion Quarterly, 1988). What would move a holder: a probability sample whose 6% response rate made its 'design' a fiction its weights could not repair.
- **Model-Assisted Calibration & MRP** — Reasons: frames rot and response collapses, so repair them with the best available model: MRP turned a biased Xbox sample into accurate state estimates — Wang, Rothschild, Goel and Gelman's demonstration is the pole's founding exhibit; calibration plus modelling is the working standard of modern polling Statistic: US telephone survey response rates fell to about 6% by 2018 (Pew Research Center, 2019). What would move a holder: an MRP estimate whose poststratification cells rested on population counts as stale as the frame it repaired.
- **Superpopulation Model-Based Adjustment** — Reasons: below 10% response, the design is a polite fiction and the weights are noise multipliers — a strong hierarchical model at least states its assumptions where they can be argued; superpopulation modelling is honesty about what now carries the inference Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a model-based survey triumph reversed when its model's key assumption was checked against a rare probability sample.

#### The "Big Data Paradox": Sample Size vs. Data Quality
- **Prioritizing Unbiased Small Probability Samples Over Massive Biased Big Data** — Reasons: Meng's identity is an arithmetic fact: a whiff of selection bias in millions of records leaves an effective sample of dozens — the 1936 Literary Digest is the permanent exhibit; provenance is not hygiene, it is the whole inference Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a massive convenience dataset whose bias was measured, modelled, and shown to be negligible for the estimand at hand.

#### Missing Data & Censoring Philosophy
- **Complete-Case Caution & Design Prevention** — Reasons: imputation invents data, and invented data has a way of becoming findings — prevent missingness by design, analyse what was actually observed, and let the gaps show; complete-case caution is the analyst refusing to fabricate Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a complete-case analysis whose missingness was so patterned that its 'caution' was the largest bias in the paper.
- **Multiple Imputation Under MAR** — Reasons: multiple imputation under MAR is the principled middle: fill in multiply, propagate the uncertainty between imputations, and the standard errors confess what was invented; Little and Rubin's framework is forty years validated Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an MAR imputation in a setting where missingness plainly depended on the missing values themselves.
- **Explicit MNAR Sensitivity & Tipping-Point Bounds** — Reasons: MAR is unverifiable by construction, so report the bounds: Manski's worst cases and tipping points tell the reader exactly how much of the conclusion the assumption is carrying; a result that survives its tipping point needs no faith Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: bounds so wide, honestly computed, that the study's funders concluded the data could not answer the question at all.

#### Non-Stationarity & Structural Breaks vs. Stationary Ergodicity
- **Transform to Stationary Ergodic Core** — Reasons: differencing, detrending, and cointegration exist because stationary ergodic theory is powerful — Engle and Granger won a Nobel for finding the stationary core inside wandering series; transform first is craft, not denial Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a differencing that destroyed the long-run relationship the study existed to estimate.
- **Online Changepoint & Regime-Switching** — Reasons: breaks are real events with dates — changepoint and regime-switching models name them, date them, and let each regime be estimated cleanly; Adams and MacKay's online detection makes it operational Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a changepoint model that found a regime for every wiggle and explained the series by renaming it.
- **Assume Perpetual Non-Stationary Drift** — Reasons: drift is the condition, not the episode: adaptive conformal and continual recalibration assume the world moves and keep the guarantees honest anyway; stationarity is the special case that occasionally visits Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an adaptive system that recalibrated itself into chasing noise its static predecessor had ridden out.

#### Forecast Evaluation: Proper Scoring Rules vs. Point-Error Metrics
- **Business-Aligned Point Errors (RMSE / MAE)** — Reasons: stakeholders fund forecasts in currency, not log scores: RMSE and MAE map to cost, inventory, and pain, and a metric nobody understands governs nothing; business alignment is what gets forecasts used Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a point-metric regime that rewarded forecasts whose uncertainty, unmeasured, bankrupted the plan built on them.
- **Quantile & Interval Coverage Checks** — Reasons: intervals are the least a forecaster owes: pinball losses and coverage checks catch the overconfident point forecast; a forecast without a stated uncertainty is half a forecast Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an interval regime whose 90% bands covered 60% for a year before anyone audited them.
- **Strictly Proper Probabilistic Scoring (Log / CRPS)** — Reasons: only strictly proper scores pay for honesty: log score and CRPS make the calibrated true distribution the optimal report — Gneiting's principle is the ethics of forecasting stated as mathematics; anything weaker invites gaming Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proper-score leaderboard won by a model whose distributions were honest and useless for every decision on the table.

#### Handling Model Misspecification ("All Models Are Wrong")
- **Iterative Posterior Predictive Checking** — Reasons: Box's loop is the scientific method for models: fit, check against posterior predictive replications, expand where the check fails; misspecification is managed by iteration, not denied by assumption Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a checking loop that expanded the model at every failure until it fit the data and nothing else.
- **Sandwich & Misspecification-Robust Inference** — Reasons: keep the simple working model and armour its standard errors: Huber–White sandwich estimators deliver honest inference under misspecification without a modelling arms race; White's result is the pragmatist's charter Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sandwich-corrected result whose point estimate, not its error bar, was the misspecification's victim.
- **Model Averaging & Stacking Ensembles** — Reasons: never bet the conclusion on one family: stacking and model averaging spread the risk across the model space itself; Yao et al.'s stacking is misspecification insurance with a cross-validated premium Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an ensemble whose averaged prediction was worse than its best member on every regime that mattered.

#### Statistical Data Visualization: Minimalist Integrity vs. Exploratory Interactivity
- **Tufte–Cleveland Minimalist Static Precision** — Reasons: a chart is an instrument: Tufte's data-ink discipline and Cleveland's perceptual rankings make static figures accurate, comparable, and citable; print is where results are archived, and archives should not animate Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a minimalist figure whose discipline stripped the context that would have reversed its reading.
- **Layered Grammar of Graphics (Wilkinson/Wickham)** — Reasons: the grammar of graphics makes figures reproducible objects: Wilkinson's and Wickham's declarative mappings mean the chart can be rebuilt, audited, and varied systematically; a figure as code is a figure with a methods section Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a grammar-built figure whose elegant defaults hid a scale choice that reversed the comparison.
- **Interactive Exploratory Dashboards & Linked Views** — Reasons: exploration needs interaction: linked brushing and zoom find the structure static views flatten; dashboards put the data in the stakeholder's hands, which is where applied work is supposed to end up Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an interactive dashboard whose explorability let every viewer leave with the pattern they came for.

### 3.5 Rigor & Validation


#### Modeling values
- **Pure Rigor (Analysis of PDEs)** — Reasons: some applied questions are theorems: PDE analysis with proof-grade rigor is what separates a model from a simulation habit; where the mathematics can be settled, settling it is the job Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proof-grade programme that polished its PDE while the client shipped a heuristic.
- **Rigorous enough to trust** — Reasons: rigor is a resource allocated by stakes: the bridge calculation and the exploratory plot do not owe the same standard; decision-grade rigor is professionalism, not laxity Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a stakes judgement that under-allocated rigor to the one calculation that failed.
- **Pragmatic Engineering Utility** — Reasons: the plane flies or it does not: engineering utility is a validation no proof can substitute for, because the proof is about the model and the flight is about the world; pragmatic success is evidence, honestly come by Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flying artefact whose success concealed a model error that a rigorous analysis would have caught before the expensive failure.

#### Values
- **Elegance** — Reasons: elegance is information: a beautiful model has found the phenomenon's own variables, and such models extrapolate; Box's beautiful models were beautiful because they were right-shaped Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a beautiful model whose elegance was the modeller's projection onto a phenomenon with a different shape.
- **Elegant because effective** — Reasons: the middle dissolves the dichotomy: models are elegant *when* their form produces their effectiveness — parsimony that predicts is the beauty applied work can defend in public Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the elegance-effectiveness correlation is a story survivors tell.
- **Effectiveness** — Reasons: effectiveness is the applied court: a model that predicts, controls, and delivers has passed the only examination the world sets; beauty is a private pleasure the client should not be billed for Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an effective model so ugly nobody could maintain it, whose replacement cost dwarfed its wins.

#### Scope
- **General** — Reasons: general theories are capital: built once, they pay out on every problem in their class; the generalist's machinery is what a field's practitioners actually share Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a general theory whose class turned out to contain one real problem.
- **General method, bespoke fit** — Reasons: general machinery, bespoke fit is the consulting reality and the scientific one: the method transfers, the parameters and structures are earned per problem; this is what 'methodology' means when it works Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a bespoke fit so thorough the general machinery underneath was never actually tested.
- **Specific** — Reasons: the tailored solution respects the problem's individuality — bespoke, concrete, and done; generality is often the procrastination of people who prefer building tools to finishing jobs Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a shop that rebuilt the same bespoke solution eleven times, refusing the general tool each time.

#### Model complexity
- **Occam's Minimalist Toy Model** — Reasons: a two-variable toy model that reveals the mechanism teaches more than a simulation that reproduces the data — Occam's models are how fields think; the essential phenomenon, isolated, is the result Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a beloved toy model whose mechanism turned out to be an artefact of its two variables.
- **As complex as needed** — Reasons: complexity is owed to the phenomenon, not to taste: the simplest model that still captures it — and no simpler, because the omitted term is usually the one that bites Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'needed complexity' judgement that added the term that sank the model's calibration.
- **Multi-Physics High-Fidelity Ensemble** — Reasons: high-fidelity ensembles are how real systems — weather, climate, structures — are actually known now: fifty million variables is not excess, it is resolution; the coupled ensemble is the instrument, and its spread is part of the answer Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a high-fidelity ensemble whose resolution manufactured detail its validation data could not check.
- **Overparameterized Double Descent** — Reasons: double descent broke the old bias-variance catechism: past the interpolation threshold, bigger models generalize *better*; massive overparameterization is not a sin to confess but a regime to understand and use Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an overparameterized model whose double-descent gains vanished under the distribution shift its deployment met.

#### Modeling goal
- **Mechanistic Understanding** — Reasons: mechanism is the durable product: forecasts expire with the regime, but a causal feedback structure transfers to the next regime; understanding is what modelling adds to curve-fitting Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mechanistic consensus that a regime change exposed as a fitted story.
- **Understanding through prediction** — Reasons: prediction is mechanism's examination: a model that forecasts well has captured *something* real about the generating process; understanding through prediction keeps explanation honest by grading it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a superb forecaster whose implied mechanism, once extracted, was physically impossible — and whose forecasts then failed.
- **Predictive Supremacy** — Reasons: the forecast is the deliverable the world grades: minimize error, calibrate, ship; mechanism-talk that does not improve the number is commentary, and clients are not buying commentary Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a predictive champion whose total opacity made its one catastrophic miss unexplainable and unrepeatable-by-design.

#### Randomized Experiments vs. Observational Causal Identification
- **No Causation Without Manipulation — RCT Gold Standard** — Reasons: Holland's dictum is the credibility standard: no causation without manipulation — randomization kills confounding by construction, and every observational alternative is an argument where the RCT is a fact; the gold standard is gold because nothing else mints trust like it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a policy domain where randomization is impossible and the observational evidence, honestly weighed, was the only guide there will ever be.
- **Quasi-Experimental Design-Based Pluralism** — Reasons: the quasi-experimental toolkit earns causal claims where randomization cannot go: DiD, RDD, and IV with transparent designs — Angrist and Pischke's pluralism covers most of the questions economics actually faces Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a quasi-experimental literature whose parallel-trends assumptions failed in concert when finally tested.
- **Observational Adjustment & Sensitivity Analysis** — Reasons: target-trial emulation and g-methods answer the questions trials cannot reach — long horizons, rare harms, real populations; Hernán and Robins show observational data, handled with trial discipline, is evidence and not merely correlation Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an emulation whose unmeasured confounding, later quantified, reversed its headline effect.

#### Preregistration & Multiverse Analysis vs. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
- **Tukeyan Open Exploratory Craft** — Reasons: Tukey's craft finds what preregistration cannot imagine: anomalies, interactions, and new questions live in exploration; a science that only confirms is a science that stops discovering Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an exploratory literature whose garden of forking paths filled a journal with unreplicable flowers.
- **Strict Split: Exploratory vs. Confirmatory** — Reasons: the split is the honest settlement: explore with total freedom on discovery data, confirm with total discipline on sealed data; each half protects the other's virtue Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a split-sample regime whose 'sealed' half leaked through a dozen innocent-looking decisions.
- **Mandatory Preregistration & Multiverse Audits** — Reasons: Gelman and Loken's garden is real, so fence it in public: preregister the plan, report the multiverse of defensible analyses — Steegen's audits turn one fragile result into a robustness map; mandatory transparency is the price of believing anything Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a preregistration so vague its multiverse contained every result the authors could have wanted.

#### Classical Factorial DoE vs. Adaptive Bayesian Optimization & Bandits
- **Classical Orthogonal & Response-Surface DoE** — Reasons: Fisher's designs are optimal in a proved sense: balanced, blocked factorials estimate effects unconfounded with known variance; a century of agriculture and industry runs on them because they work Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a factorial programme that spent its budget estimating interactions nobody acted on.
- **Batch-Sequential Hybrid Design** — Reasons: space-filling first, adaptive after: the batch-sequential hybrid buys global coverage and local refinement in one budget; it is how modern simulation experiments are actually run Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hybrid whose adaptive phase chased an early artefact its initial batch had manufactured.
- **Online Bandits & Bayesian Optimization** — Reasons: adaptivity is information used at full speed: Bayesian optimization and bandits spend each trial where the surrogate says it pays, minimizing regret by construction; fixed designs spend trials the data already answered Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a bandit allocation that starved the arm that was, in truth, best — early noise, permanent sentence.

#### Fixed-Horizon Clinical Trials vs. Adaptive Platform & Sequential Designs
- **Classic Fixed-Sample Parallel RCT** — Reasons: the fixed parallel RCT protects the two things trials exist for: type-I error and freedom from time trends; Pocock's discipline is why a trial result can license a drug Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fixed trial that read out, correctly and uselessly, three years after practice had moved on.
- **Group-Sequential Alpha-Spending** — Reasons: alpha-spending is the ethical middle: look early under Lan–DeMets discipline, stop for overwhelming efficacy or futility, keep the error budget honest; patients outside the trial deserve its answer sooner Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a group-sequential trial stopped early on a random high that its full sample would have reversed.
- **Bayesian Adaptive Platform Trials (I-SPY / RECOVERY)** — Reasons: platform trials are the pandemic's methodological legacy: RECOVERY's master protocol tested many therapies against a shared control and changed practice in months; response-adaptive designs treat participants as patients, not as sample size Statistic: In RECOVERY, dexamethasone cut 28-day mortality by about one-third among patients on ventilation (RECOVERY Collaborative Group, New England Journal of Medicine, 2021). What would move a holder: an adaptive platform whose shifting allocation ratios confounded its later arms with calendar time.

#### Anytime-Valid Inference (e-Values) vs. Classical Fixed-Horizon Testing
- **Fixed-Horizon Classical Testing** — Reasons: pre-specification is the contract that makes a p-value mean anything: fixed-horizon Neyman–Pearson testing has maximum power at its design point; monitoring is a design choice, priced in advance Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fixed-horizon trial ruined by the interim peek everyone took and nobody priced.
- **Pragmatic Sequential Corrections** — Reasons: sequential corrections are the working compromise: group-sequential boundaries when monitoring is genuinely needed, classical power the rest of the time; the correction is small when the looks are few Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'pragmatic' correction applied so often it became optional stopping with paperwork.
- **Game-Theoretic e-Values & Safe Testing** — Reasons: optional stopping is not a sin to police but a practice to make valid: e-values and confidence sequences (Shafer–Vovk, Ramdas) keep inference honest under continuous monitoring; safe testing fits how science is actually watched now — live Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an e-value analysis so conservative at realistic sample sizes that it could not detect effects its classical twin found routinely.

#### Reproducibility, Data Hygiene & Computational Workflows
- **End-to-End Scripted Reproducible Pipelines** — Reasons: a result nobody can recompute is a claim, not a finding: version-controlled, containerized, scripted pipelines with zero manual spreadsheet edits are the reproducibility floor — Donoho's and Peng's standard is achievable today by any shop that wants it Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fully scripted pipeline that reproduced, perfectly, an error coded into its first commit.

#### Open Benchmark Competitions vs. Real-World Deployment Validation
- **Standardized Benchmarks Drive Progress** — Reasons: the Common Task Framework is machine learning's engine: shared benchmarks made progress measurable, comparable, and fast — Donoho's own history credits the framework, not any single algorithm; leaderboards are how a field keeps score honestly Statistic: ImageNet's roughly 14 million labelled images powered a decade of benchmark-driven progress in computer vision (Deng et al., CVPR, 2009). What would move a holder: a benchmark whose leaderboard rose for years while deployment performance on the same task quietly fell.
- **Rotating Blind & Real-World Audits** — Reasons: benchmarks rot by Goodhart's law, so rotate them: blind test sets and distribution-shift audits keep the score honest; the framework survives by distrusting its own numbers Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rotating-benchmark regime whose churn made year-on-year progress unmeasurable.
- **Live Deployment & Decision Outcomes First** — Reasons: deployment is the only court with jurisdiction: live decision outcomes — money saved, patients helped, systems steadied — are what applied models are for; Hardt and Recht's warning is that leaderboard skill and field skill are different skills Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a deployment-first shop whose live metrics were gamed by the teams being measured, faster than any leaderboard ever was.

## 4. Archetype research

All 13 samples in the skin have kind "archetype". Ratings in the skin rate advocacy per pole and openness per topic; the receptivity and intensity discussed below are the derived quantities (see §1), described in words, not re-rated here.


### The Bayesian — `pra-bayesian.svg`

Treats probability as a degree of belief and updates it with evidence using Bayes' theorem: start with a prior, fold in new data, and get a posterior. The stance is that all inference is belief revision, and uncertainty should be stated as a probability. Why: It holds the §3 Bayesian Updating pole on Statistics, the Full Bayesian Inverse Posterior pole on inverse problems, and the Hierarchical Bayesian Partial Pooling pole on multiple testing. Receptive to: weakly informative prior practice and MCMC craft from any camp, and to likelihood arguments it can absorb as prior-free special cases (§3, Bayesian Prior Philosophy; Intractable Posteriors). Closed to: gatekeeping significance rituals and purely technical accuracy mandates in fairness, because a posterior, not a threshold, is its unit of honesty (§3, Significance testing; Algorithmic Fairness). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-bayesian.svg

### The Frequentist — `pra-frequentist.svg`

Treats probability as the long-run frequency of an outcome if an experiment were repeated many times. Inference runs on significance tests, p-values, and confidence intervals, with no prior beliefs in the calculation. Why: It holds the §3 Frequentist pole on Statistics, the gatekeeper and Tighten Threshold poles on Significance testing, and the Fixed-Horizon pole on anytime-valid inference. Receptive to: design-based survey inference and FWER discipline, its fellow long-run-error cultures (§3, Design-Based vs. Model-Based; Multiple Testing). Closed to: elicited subjective priors, because a prior one analyst chooses is not a property strangers can verify (§3, Bayesian Prior Philosophy). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-frequentist.svg

### The First-Principles Theorist — `pra-first-principles.svg`

Derives results from the most fundamental laws available rather than from empirical fitting or rules of thumb: start from basic principles and work upward. The stance is that a model is only as trustworthy as the foundations it is built on. Why: It holds the §3 Deductive First Principles pole on Modeling philosophy, the Mechanistic Insight pole on the Purpose compass, and the Pure Rigor pole on Modeling values. Receptive to: asymptotics-first mastery and physics-informed hybrids that keep the law in charge (§3, Asymptotics; Modeling philosophy's hybrid pole). Closed to: black-box surrogates and algorithmic prediction-first cultures, which abandon the mechanism it exists to derive (§3, Breiman's Two Cultures; Modeling philosophy). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-first-principles.svg

### The Simulation-First Computationalist — `pra-simulation-first.svg`

Builds understanding through computation first: simulate the system, watch it behave, and let the numbers guide theory and experiment. For systems too complex or costly to solve on paper or in the lab, the simulation is the primary instrument. Why: It holds the §3 Simulation pole on Approach, the Discrete-Event Simulation First pole on queueing, and the Detailed Heterogeneous Agent Models pole on mean-field. Receptive to: neural operators and agent architectures as new simulation engines (§3, Computational Architecture Quartet). Closed to: closed-form-only solution standards, because the systems it studies confess only when run (§3, Solution method). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-simulation-first.svg

### The Pragmatic Engineer — `pra-engineer.svg`

Optimizes for solutions that work under real constraints: budget, materials, deadlines, and safety margins. The stance is that a good-enough design that ships and holds up beats a perfect design that never leaves the drawing board. Why: It holds the §3 Pragmatic Engineering Utility pole on Modeling values, the Heuristic pole on Optimization, and the Deliver Crisp Actionable Point Advice pole on communicating uncertainty. Receptive to: verified heuristics and prototype-then-port workflows, the other deadline-honest positions (§3, Optimization; Computational craft). Closed to: proof-grade rigor demands and full-uncertainty foregrounding that would keep a shippable design on the drawing board (§3, Modeling values; Communicating Deep Uncertainty). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-engineer.svg

### The Breimanite Predictive & Common-Task Empiricist — `pra-breimanite.svg`

Measures models ruthlessly by held-out predictive calibration, Common Task benchmarks, and double-descent expressiveness rather than parametric dogma. Why: It holds the §3 Algorithmic Black-Box Prediction First pole on Breiman's Two Cultures, the Standardized Benchmarks pole on benchmark competitions, and the Overparameterized Double Descent pole on model complexity. Receptive to: deep learned representations and foundation forecasters, its fellow held-out winners (§3, High-Dimensional Signal Processing; Time-Series Forecasting). Closed to: generative-mechanism requirements for model legitimacy, the demand Breiman's 2001 argument was written against (§3, Breiman's Two Cultures). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-breimanite.svg

### The Causal-Inference Architect — `pra-causal-architect.svg`

Refuses to confuse correlation with intervention; insists on explicit potential outcomes, causal DAGs, clean natural experiments, and transparent identification assumptions. Why: It holds the §3 Potential Outcomes and Structural Causal Graphs poles on Causal Inference Framework Allegiance, the Design-Based Reduced-Form pole on identification, and the Causal Identification Clarity pole on the aesthetic profile. Receptive to: quasi-experimental pluralism and target-trial emulation, which share its design-first discipline (§3, Randomized Experiments vs. Observational). Closed to: prediction-first cultures that report correlations in causal language (§3, Breiman's Two Cultures; Reduced-Form vs. Structural). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-causal-architect.svg

### The Convex Optimization & Control Strategist — `pra-convex-control.svg`

Turns complex engineering, sensing, and decision problems into tractable convex programs, sparse recovery guarantees, and safety-certified robust control architectures. Why: It holds the §3 Formulate Convex Problems First pole, the Model-Based Optimal Control pole, and the Belief in Sparsity pole that its compressed-sensing guarantees rest on. Receptive to: distributionally robust optimization and convex-relaxation-then-refine settlements (§3, Worst-Case vs. Average-Case; Convex Guarantees). Closed to: model-free deep RL for safety-critical control, because an uncertified policy is precisely what its guarantees exist to prevent (§3, Classical Optimal Control vs. RL). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-convex-control.svg

### The Complexity, Fat-Tail & Societal-Risk Modeler — `pra-complexity-risk.svg`

Skeptical of thin-tailed equilibrium assumptions and false precision; focuses on heavy-tail extremes, heterogeneous networks, model humility, and ethical accountability in society. Why: It holds the §3 Extremistan Power-Law pole on tails, the Knightian Ambiguity pole on the uncertainty quartet, and the Ethical Impact & Refusal pole on fairness. Receptive to: stress-testing purposes, minimax postures, and MNAR bounds — its fellow distrust-the-average positions (§3, Purpose compass; Worst-Case; Missing Data). Closed to: CLT-Gaussian defaults and crisp point advice, which it reads as the false precision its whole stance opposes (§3, Gaussian vs. Heavy-Tail; Communicating Deep Uncertainty). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-complexity-risk.svg

### The Experimental-Design & Clinical-Trial Methodologist — `pra-trial-methodologist.svg`

Insists clean evidence starts before data are ever collected; masters randomization, blocking, adaptive clinical trials, anytime-valid testing, and vigilance against selection bias. Why: It holds the §3 Classic Fixed-Sample and Group-Sequential poles on trial design, the Tukeyan-versus-preregistration discipline in its confirmatory form, and the End-to-End Scripted Reproducible Pipelines pole. Receptive to: adaptive platform trials under formal error control — RECOVERY is its own exhibit (§3, Fixed-Horizon vs. Adaptive Trials). Closed to: unpreregistered exploration reported as confirmation, the forking-paths practice its methodology exists to exclude (§3, Preregistration & Multiverse). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-trial-methodologist.svg

### The Game Theorist & Operations-Research Optimizer — `pra-game-or.svg`

Designs incentive-compatible institutions, certified discrete schedules, and congestion-proof stochastic networks, fusing equilibrium theory with exact mathematical programming. Why: It holds the §3 Exact Nash pole on strategic modelling, the Exact MIP pole on discrete optimization, and the Taking Impossibility Theorems Seriously pole on mechanism design. Receptive to: incentive-compatible deployment thinking (the Lucas/Goodhart pole) and queueing's exact tradition (§3, Fixed Plant vs. Strategic Agents; Queueing Theory). Closed to: purely behavioural strategic models with no equilibrium discipline, which it reads as storytelling about agents (§3, Rational Nash vs. Behavioral). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-game-or.svg

### The Information Theorist & High-Dimensional Signal Analyst — `pra-info-signal.svg`

Views inference, compression, and sensing through Shannon entropy, MDL, wavelets, compressed sensing, and exact high-dimensional random-matrix phase transitions. Why: It holds the §3 MDL pole on information theory, the Analytic Wavelets pole on signal processing, and the Strict Finite-Sample / Proportional-regime poles on high-dimensional inference. Receptive to: optimal transport and TDA as fellow geometry-of-data programmes (§3, Optimal Transport & Geometric Data Analysis). Closed to: classical fixed-p asymptotics as a universal warrant, which its phase-transition results show failing exactly where modern signals live (§3, High-Dimensional Regime). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-info-signal.svg

### The Spatial, Time-Series & Probabilistic Forecaster — `pra-prob-forecaster.svg`

Obsesses over calibrated out-of-sample probabilistic forecasts across space and time: state-space models, SPDE/Gaussian-process fields, changepoint awareness, and strictly proper scoring rules. Why: It holds the §3 Strictly Proper Probabilistic Scoring pole on forecast evaluation, the SPDE & INLA Meshes pole on spatial statistics, and the Online Changepoint pole on non-stationarity. Receptive to: hybrid time-series architectures that submit to proper scoring, from any tradition (§3, Time-Series Forecasting; Forecast Evaluation). Closed to: point-error-only evaluation, because a forecast graded without its uncertainty is, for this archetype, not yet a forecast (§3, Forecast Evaluation). Emblem: images/emblems/pra-prob-forecaster.svg

## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype.** The map's poles are wider than its 13 archetypes. No archetype primarily embodies: the frequentist's mirror-image in survey work (strict Horvitz–Thompson design purism, §3, Design-Based vs. Model-Based); econophysics and agent-market finance (§3, Quantitative Finance's third pole); topological data analysis as an identity (§3, Optimal Transport & TDA); the Tufte–Cleveland visualization minimalist (§3, Statistical Data Visualization); or the diamond-open / industry-habitat applied life as a whole persona. Several strong poles — e-values and safe testing, conformal guarantees, MNAR bounds — are held inside archetypes (chiefly the Trial Methodologist and the Complexity-Risk Modeler) but have no archetype of their own.

**Thin topics.** Five topics are single-pole (monopole) topics — Information Theory & MDL, Sparsity & Compressed Sensing, Mechanism Design & Social Choice, the Big Data Paradox, and Reproducibility. Per the skin's design these are valid affinity topics: the one pole is the position, a positive rating is affinity and a negative rating is aversion, so §3 gives each one position entry. They are thin in one specific way: the indaba can list backing for the pole but cannot locate a disagreement on the topic itself. A few hovertip pole texts are empty in the JSON (e.g. the Knightian Ambiguity pole of the Epistemic Uncertainty Quartet, the Pure Rigor pole of Modeling values), so §2 and §3 for those poles rest on the pole label and question alone.

**Skin-JSON problems (reported, not fixed).** (1) One topic has an empty `question` field: Significance testing (xpr0027). Its `hovertip.issue` carries the de facto question; §2 quotes it as the recovered question and §5.1 collects it. (2) The Cognitive Working Style switchbox (xpr0069) carries its two crossed axes as two poles whose labels each name both ends of an axis; §3 treats each pole as one axis-position, per the crossed-axes ruling. (3) Optimal Transport & Geometric Data Analysis (xpr0040) has two poles on a linear-sliders widget that are not ends of one dimension but two different tools; §3 treats them as independent positions, per the multi-pole ruling. (4) Pole counts run from 1 to 6 (Significance testing has six poles on a star-radar widget), wider than the 2–4 range the generic guide describes; §3 covers every pole as listed.

**Deliberately left out of this research.** Software documentation and vendor claims; any statistic not listed in §6 — where §3 could not ground a number in a source named with confidence, it says so rather than estimating; and verdicts on which working culture is correct, since the skin's stance is comparative.
