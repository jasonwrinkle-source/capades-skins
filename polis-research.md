# Polis — Research

Skin: Poliscapades (`poliscapades.json`) · Domain: Political Economy, Civic Allocation & Governance · Meter: conviction
Coverage: 110 topics, 327 poles, 38 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Polis maps politics: how a community should allocate resources, organise power, protect or limit freedom, and act in the world. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's 110 questions — a pole a holder advocates for, against, or toward which they are averse (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence). Holding no stance is not a position; a 0 rating is. The meter word is **conviction**: the skin measures how strongly a profile stands for each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness × advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance is pluralist and comparative: US party platforms (2000–2024) and four ideal types are placed on one map in one vocabulary, with no pole treated as the default.

## 2. Topic rationale

### The Economy

**Economic Structure** — The organising choice beneath all other economic topics. The State pole trusts public planning and redistribution; The Market trusts private ownership, prices and competition; The Commons trusts cooperatives, civil society and mutual aid.

**Theories of Distributive Justice Triad** — What makes an outcome fair, before any policy is chosen. Need distributes by need; Merit / Contribution by what a person produces; Strict Equality gives each the same share regardless.

**Macroeconomic Policy** — How the state handles money, slumps and debt over the cycle. MMT says a currency-issuing state is constrained by inflation, not revenue; Keynesian policy spends against slumps; Sound Money prioritises a stable currency; Austerity prioritises balanced budgets.

**Civic Resource Allocation Pentad** — A forced ranking of five claims on one finite budget. Each pole names the claim put first: Healthcare & Social Welfare; Infrastructure & Clean Energy; Education, Science & Frontier R&D; Defense, Border Security & Resilience; Ecology, Climate & Biodiversity.

**Who owns what** — The property regime itself. Private Absolute makes ownership near-complete; State Owned puts assets in public hands; Common Pool Resource governs shared assets by Ostrom-style rules; Usufruct ties possession to use; Open-Access / Copyleft makes digital goods free to use and build on.

**Income tax rates** — How steeply earnings should be taxed. Higher taxes on the wealthy steepens progressivity; Moderate, flatter rates simplifies and broadens; Lower taxes across the board shrinks the take at every level.

**Corporate Taxes** — How business profit should be taxed. Close loopholes, tax more raises effective rates; Competitive rate, fewer loopholes trades rate for base; Cut corporate taxes lowers the statutory burden to attract investment.

**Federal Spending** — The size of the federal effort and whether deficits matter. Invest generously spends for public goods; Spend, but bend the curve invests while slowing long-run growth of spending; Cut spending to balance makes balance the binding goal.

**National debt** — What to do about accumulated federal borrowing. Manage through growth & fair tax outgrows the debt; Balance by cutting spending retires it by retrenchment.

**Wealth Distribution** — Whether the rich–poor gap is itself a policy problem. Reduce inequality acts on the gap directly; Ease inequality at the margins softens its edges; Market outcome treats the distribution markets produce as legitimate.

**The income tax** — Whether the income tax should exist at all. Keep it; raise rates on the wealthy keeps and steepens it; Keep it; replace with a flat or consumption tax swaps the base; Abolish it entirely ends it with no named replacement.

**Trade Policy** — How open US markets should be. Free trade removes barriers; Fair trade conditions access on labour and environmental standards; Protective tariffs shield domestic producers.

**Manufacturing** — Whether bringing factories and supply chains home is a public task. Industrial policy & reshoring does it deliberately; Targeted support helps strategic sectors only; Leave it to the market lets location follow cost.

**Economic rivalry** — How to compete economically with China. Managed competition competes inside continuing ties; Selective decoupling separates strategic sectors; Economic decoupling separates broadly.

**Monetary Policy** — Who controls the money supply. An Independent central bank insulates it from politics; Constrain the Fed adds rules and oversight; Abolish the Fed ends the institution.

**Digital Currency** — What digital money should be. Private crypto keeps issuance and holding outside the state; Government coin (CBDC) issues a state digital currency.

**Inflation** — What government should do about rising prices. Act to bring prices down intervenes directly; Guard the currency, hands off prices defends sound money only; Leave prices to the market treats price-setting as no public business.

**The monetary standard** — What backs money itself. Fiat money under a central bank rests on state credit; A gold or commodity standard anchors money to a commodity; Free banking lets competing currencies circulate with no legal tender.

**Minimum wage** — Whether a wage floor should be set federally, and how high. A Higher federal floor raises it substantially; A modest federal floor keeps a low backstop; No federal floor leaves wages to contract and states.

**Labor Rights** — The rules of organising and bargaining. Strong labor protections ease unionising and protect strikes; Basic protections, voluntary association protects organising without compelling membership; Voluntary association only removes state support and compulsion alike.

**Social Security** — The future of the federal retirement programme. Protect and expand raises benefits or revenue to secure it; Preserve with reforms adjusts ages, taxes or formulas; Phase it out winds the programme down toward private provision.

**Welfare** — Whether Washington should run anti-poverty programmes. A Strong safety net guarantees broad support; A limited safety net helps narrowly and conditionally; No federal welfare leaves relief to states, charity and family.

**Housing affordability** — Why homes cost so much and what to do. Build and subsidize more adds public money and supply; Deregulate & build (YIMBY) removes zoning and permitting barriers; Leave it to the market opposes intervention in either form.

**Universal basic income** — Whether cash should come without conditions. A universal payment to every adult pays all alike; A targeted guaranteed income pays only those in need; No guaranteed income rejects a state income floor.

### Health & Care

**Health insurance system** — Who finances care and whether coverage is a right. Private financing keeps insurance a market product; Guaranteed coverage makes coverage universal by public guarantee.

**Prescription drug prices** — Whether the state may set or bargain over medicine prices. Negotiate and cap prices does both; Negotiate the worst excesses bargains only outliers; Leave prices to the market protects patent pricing.

**Medicare** — The future of public insurance for older Americans. Protect and expand extends it; Replace with universal folds it into a universal system; Leave to the market retires public provision.

**Vaccines** — Whether public-health requirements can bind individual choice. Mandates legitimate allows requirements; Mandates with exemptions allows them with conscience and medical outs; Mandates violate autonomy rejects compulsion in the body.

**Behavioral Health** — How to answer mental illness and the opioid epidemic. Treatment and investment funds care and recovery; Enforcement and interdiction attacks supply and trafficking.

**Child care** — Who pays for care of young children. Guaranteed affordable care makes it a public guarantee; Subsidize, don't guarantee helps with cost without a guarantee; Families pay themselves treats care as a household expense.

**Paid Leave** — Whether time off for birth, care or illness should be guaranteed. A National paid-leave program creates a public scheme; Incentivize employer leave rewards firms that offer it; Leave it to employers makes leave a contract term.

### Climate, Energy & Environment

**Climate Change** — Whether warming is a crisis requiring state action. Urgent action treats it as an emergency; Gradual, market-led action prices and incentivises over time; No government action denies a public role.

**Fossil fuels** — The place of oil, gas and coal. Drill and expand grows production; All of the above, in transition uses fossil fuels while building replacements; Phase out ends production on a schedule.

**Nuclear power** — Whether fission belongs in the mix. Keep and expand builds new capacity; Keep the fleet, build selectively runs existing plants and adds cautiously; Phase it out retires nuclear power.

**Renewable energy** — How fast to build wind, solar and other renewables. Massive public investment funds the build-out directly; Incentives, not mandates subsidises without requiring; Let the market decide leaves adoption to price.

**Electric vehicles** — Whether the vehicle fleet should be electrified by policy. Incentivize and mandate EVs does both; Incentives, no mandates subsidises purchase only; Cancel EV mandates removes requirements and treats EVs as one option among many.

**Growth vs. Limits** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Can technological innovation sustain endless material growth, or must rich societies stabilize throughput?” Abundance & Acceleration says innovation outruns limits; Green Decoupling says growth can be separated from emissions and extraction; Post-Growth Sufficiency says enough is a goal in itself.

**Environmental Justice** — Who bears pollution's costs. Strict rules, polluter-pays charges those who pollute and protects overburdened places; Rules with property remedies compensates harmed owners; Property rights & courts only leaves redress to owners suing in court.

**Land stewardship** — Who manages public land and nature. Federal conservation keeps national management; Shared stewardship divides it among federal, state, local and private hands; Return to states / private transfers land out of federal control.

**Agriculture** — The state's role in farming and food. Support farmers & reform funds and reshapes the system; A safety net, market-led insures against disaster while markets lead; Leave it to the market ends farm support.


### Immigration & Borders

**Border Enforcement** — How the southern border should be secured. Wall and hard enforcement builds physical barriers and maximises enforcement; Technology & agents (the label is truncated in the skin) pairs surveillance and staffing with legal pathways, as its key implies.

**Deportation** — Removal of unauthorised immigrants. Mass deportation seeks removal at scale; Prioritized removal targets serious offences and recent arrivals first; Removal after fair process keeps removal but subordinates it to hearings and due process.

**Sanctuary cities** — Whether localities may limit cooperation with federal enforcement. Let cities decide protects local discretion; Cooperate on serious crimes shares information for grave offences only; Cut federal funding punishes non-cooperation financially.

**Legal Immigration** — How many should be admitted lawfully, and by what test. Expand admissions raises numbers and channels; Merit-based selection allocates places by skills and points (the skin's second pole is truncated in display; its key is merit-selection).

**Safe haven** — How asylum claims should be handled. A Fair, humane process centres protection and hearings; Orderly, screened asylum centres capacity and vetting; Strict vetting & deterrence centres discouraging claims.

**Immigration legalization** — The status of long-term unauthorised residents and Dreamers. A Path to citizenship offers eventual citizenship; Legal status without citizenship offers residence and work rights only; Enforcement only, no amnesty offers neither.

**Birthright citizenship** — Whether US birth alone confers citizenship. Keep birthright citizenship retains the Fourteenth Amendment rule; Restrict it at the edges narrows it for some births; End it abolishes it.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 The Economy

#### Economic Structure
- **The State (Command & Redistribution)** — Reasons: only public power can guarantee essentials no market will supply to the poor; planning can direct investment to national needs instead of profitable wants. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a market or commons system delivered essentials more reliably to the worst-off.
- **The Market (Capital & Price Discovery)** — Reasons: prices aggregate knowledge no planner can collect; ownership and competition discipline producers through failure and choice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that planning matched market living standards without shortages or coercion.
- **The Commons (Civil Society & Mutual Aid)** — Reasons: people govern shared resources well under rules they make themselves; mutual aid meets needs without either profit or bureaucracy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that commons institutions failed at scale where state or market provision succeeded.

#### Theories of Distributive Justice Triad
- **Need (Marxian / Welfare)** — Reasons: resources matter only as they meet human need; a distribution leaving basic needs unmet while luxuries abound is unjust on its face. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: proof that need-based distribution destroyed the production that makes meeting needs possible.
- **Merit / Contribution (Productivist)** — Reasons: those who create value deserve its return, which rewards effort and skill; severing reward from contribution invites free-riding and resentment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that most large fortunes owed little to their holders' contribution.
- **Strict Equality (Egalitarian)** — Reasons: equal moral worth implies equal shares unless a difference can be justified to everyone; luck of birth and talent is morally arbitrary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a justification for inequality that the worst-off could reasonably accept as improving their own position.

#### Macroeconomic Policy
- **Modern Monetary Theory (MMT)** — Reasons: a sovereign currency issuer cannot run out of its own money, so the real limits are inflation and resources; deficits are a policy choice about employment, and job guarantees can stabilise both. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a currency-issuing state hitting a financing (not inflation) limit, or a job guarantee producing runaway inflation.
- **Keynesian Counter-Cyclical Intervention** — Reasons: slumps are demand failures the private sector cannot self-correct quickly; public spending in downturns and restraint in booms shortens recessions and protects jobs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: repeated cases where stimulus arrived too late or only raised prices without employment gains.
- **Sound Money / Monetarism** — Reasons: steady, predictable money growth prevents both inflation and policy whiplash; discretionary intervention is systematically mistimed by politics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that discretionary stabilisation reliably beat a money-growth rule across cycles.
- **Fiscal Austerity / Balanced Budget** — Reasons: debt service eventually crowds out public services and binds future voters; living within revenue is the household discipline a state should share. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that austerity in slumps shrank economies so much that debt ratios rose rather than fell.

#### Civic Resource Allocation Pentad
- **Healthcare & Social Welfare** — Reasons: health and a floor under income are preconditions for every other good; a society is judged by whether illness means ruin. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that welfare spending at the margin bought less wellbeing than the same funds elsewhere.
- **Infrastructure & Clean Energy** — Reasons: roads, grids and water are the platform all private activity stands on; deferred maintenance compounds like debt. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: audits showing infrastructure funds systematically wasted relative to other uses.
- **Education, Science & Frontier R&D** — Reasons: knowledge compounds across generations and markets underfund research whose returns no firm can capture; education is the main ladder of mobility. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that public R&D and education spending had stopped raising productivity or mobility.
- **Defense, Border Security & Resilience** — Reasons: safety is the first public good, without which all others are hostage; deterrence is cheaper than war. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that current security spending already exceeded any plausible threat by a wide margin.
- **Ecology, Climate & Biodiversity** — Reasons: ecosystems are irreplaceable capital whose loss is irreversible; future generations cannot bid in today's markets. Statistic: human-caused warming reached about 1.1 °C in 2011–2020 relative to 1850–1900 (IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report, 2023). What would move a holder: evidence that ecological decline was reversing without public priority.

#### Who owns what
- **Private Absolute (Allodial / Capitalist)** — Reasons: secure, complete ownership makes people invest, maintain and trade assets; fragmenting ownership rights makes every use a negotiation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that strong private title mainly entrenched extraction rather than investment.
- **State Owned (Nationalized / Public)** — Reasons: natural monopolies and commanding industries serve the public only under public ownership; profits from public assets should return to the public. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: durable evidence that nationalised industries served citizens worse than regulated private ones.
- **Common Pool Resource (Ostromian Commons)** — Reasons: communities with clear boundaries, monitoring and graduated sanctions sustain shared resources for centuries; neither privatisation nor central control is necessary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that Ostrom's design principles failed outside small, stable communities.
- **Usufruct (Possession by Use)** — Reasons: ownership should follow use, so land and tools held idle while others need them is waste; use-rights keep assets working and accessible. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that use-based tenure destroyed incentives to build or improve long-lived assets.
- **Open-Access / Copyleft (Digital Commons)** — Reasons: digital goods cost nothing to copy, so exclusion is artificial scarcity; open licensing produced the shared software and knowledge everyone builds on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that open access collapsed production of digital goods compared with proprietary models.

#### Income tax rates
- **Higher taxes on the wealthy** — Reasons: the wealthy gain most from the legal and market order taxes fund, and can pay without sacrificing necessities; progressive rates are the main lever against runaway top incomes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that higher top rates raised little revenue while measurably reducing investment and wages.
- **Moderate, flatter rates** — Reasons: a broad base with fewer brackets and deductions is simpler, harder to game, and keeps incentives intact; predictability itself is fairness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that flattening shifted the burden downward without simplifying compliance.
- **Lower taxes across the board** — Reasons: money is spent better by those who earned it than by government programmes; lower rates reward work and expand the economy that funds everything else. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that broad cuts mainly produced deficits, not growth or wage gains.

#### Corporate Taxes
- **Close loopholes, tax more** — Reasons: profitable corporations use public infrastructure, courts and educated workers, and should not engineer near-zero rates; loopholes reward lobbying over production. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that higher effective corporate taxes fell mainly on workers' wages rather than shareholders.
- **Competitive rate, fewer loopholes** — Reasons: a moderate, internationally competitive rate with a clean base raises stable revenue without driving headquarters abroad; complexity, not the rate, is the scandal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that rate competition was a race the US could not win and base-broadening failed.
- **Cut corporate taxes** — Reasons: corporate taxes are ultimately paid by workers and consumers through lower wages and higher prices; lower rates keep investment and jobs at home. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that cuts flowed overwhelmingly to buybacks and dividends with no investment response.

#### Federal Spending
- **Invest generously** — Reasons: public investment in health, infrastructure and research returns more than it costs and markets underprovide it; deficits in a growing economy are manageable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that marginal federal programmes returned less than their financing cost over time.
- **Spend, but bend the curve** — Reasons: investment today and restraint tomorrow are compatible; the long-run drivers (health costs, interest) must slow without abandoning public purposes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that promised future restraint never materialised, making present generosity a ratchet.
- **Cut spending to balance** — Reasons: persistent deficits transfer costs to future taxpayers who never voted for them; a state that cannot balance will eventually be disciplined by markets. Statistic: gross federal debt passed $36 trillion in November 2024 (US Department of the Treasury, Fiscal Data, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that rapid cuts harmed growth and revenue enough to worsen the balance.

#### National debt
- **Manage through growth & fair tax** — Reasons: debt is sustainable when the economy grows faster than interest accrues; fair taxation plus growth shrinks the ratio without cutting services people rely on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sustained period where interest rates exceeded growth and the ratio spiralled despite fair-tax effort.
- **Balance by cutting spending** — Reasons: interest payments are now a major budget item buying nothing; only spending restraint is arithmetically certain to close a gap revenue promises keep missing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that growth and revenue measures alone had closed comparable gaps without cuts.

#### Wealth Distribution
- **Reduce inequality** — Reasons: extreme gaps convert into unequal political power, health and opportunity, corrupting democracy itself; shared prosperity is more stable than concentrated wealth. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that high-inequality societies matched low-inequality ones on mobility, health and democratic quality.
- **Ease inequality at the margins** — Reasons: the worst harms of inequality — destitution, exclusion — can be relieved without restructuring the whole distribution; targeted help respects both fairness and enterprise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that marginal measures left the underlying gap and its political effects untouched.
- **Market outcome** — Reasons: a distribution produced by voluntary exchange is not an injustice to correct; attempts to engineer outcomes punish success and require coercive power. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that market distributions mainly reflected rent, inheritance and monopoly rather than exchange and contribution.

#### The income tax
- **Keep it; raise rates on the wealthy** — Reasons: the income tax is the fairest broad revenue source because it tracks ability to pay; raising top rates funds public goods without taxing necessities. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a replacement that raised the same revenue more fairly and simply.
- **Keep it; replace with a flat or consumption tax** — Reasons: taxing consumption rather than earnings stops penalising work and saving; a single rate with a clean base ends most avoidance and compliance cost. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: modelling showing the replacement shifted the burden onto low and middle earners.
- **Abolish it entirely** — Reasons: taxing income punishes production and requires an intrusive apparatus that audits private life; government should be small enough to fund by narrower means. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that no alternative funded even a minimal state without worse distortions or intrusion.

#### Trade Policy
- **Free trade** — Reasons: open trade lowers prices, widens choice and lets each country specialise where it is strongest; protection is a tax on one's own consumers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that openness produced permanent, uncompensated ruin for whole regions rather than transition costs.
- **Fair trade** — Reasons: trade rules should not reward labour repression or pollution havens; conditioning access on standards keeps competition honest rather than a race to the bottom. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that standards conditionality mainly served as disguised protection for domestic lobbies.
- **Protective tariffs** — Reasons: strategic industries and the jobs and towns around them are national assets markets will not preserve; tariffs buy time and bargaining power. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that tariffs raised consumer prices and invited retaliation without reviving the protected industries.

#### Manufacturing
- **Industrial policy & reshoring** — Reasons: supply chains for chips, medicines and energy are security assets, and private cost-minimising will offshore them; deliberate policy rebuilt capacity before and can again. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that reshoring programmes produced expensive Potemkin plants without resilient supply.
- **Targeted support** — Reasons: a few sectors are genuinely strategic while most manufacturing competes fine unaided; targeted help limits cost and capture. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that 'strategic' designations expanded by lobbying until targeting meant everything.
- **Leave it to the market** — Reasons: firms locate production best when following cost and skill, and consumers gain from global efficiency; industrial policy is government picking winners with public money. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a crisis in which dependence on a rival for essentials caused concrete national harm.

#### Economic rivalry
- **Managed competition** — Reasons: rivalry with China is long-term and must be competed in without tipping into war or self-harming rupture; guardrails keep competition bounded. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that Beijing treated guardrails as weakness and exploited every opening.
- **Selective decoupling** — Reasons: dependence on a rival for strategic goods (chips, rare earths, medicines) is a vulnerability, while ordinary trade benefits both sides; separate the strategic, keep the rest. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that selective separation inevitably widened into general rupture.
- **Economic decoupling** — Reasons: trade with a strategic rival funds its military and technology base; separation, though costly, is cheaper than dependence in a conflict. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that decoupling harmed US capacity more than China's, or pushed China to self-sufficiency faster.

#### Monetary Policy
- **Independent central bank** — Reasons: politicians facing elections will always prefer cheap money now and inflation later; insulation lets the bank take the long view voters actually want. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that independent banks performed no better on inflation and growth than politically directed ones.
- **Constrain the Fed** — Reasons: an unelected institution with vast power needs rules, audits and a narrower mandate to stay legitimate; discretion has produced bailouts and asset inflation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that rules-based constraint would have forced catastrophic tightening in a genuine crisis.
- **Abolish the Fed** — Reasons: central banking socialises money, enables government debt and boom–bust cycles, and rescues the financial sector at public expense; money should not be a state monopoly project. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that pre-Fed or non-central-bank systems were more stable, not less.

#### Digital Currency
- **Private crypto** — Reasons: money outside state control protects savers from inflation, surveillance and account freezes; open networks innovate faster than official money. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that private crypto mainly served speculation and crime while failing as money.
- **Government coin (CBDC)** — Reasons: a public digital currency gives everyone direct access to central-bank money, faster payments and a public alternative to private payment monopolies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a CBDC could not be built without transaction surveillance or disintermediation of banks.

#### Inflation
- **Act to bring prices down** — Reasons: inflation is a tax falling hardest on those least able to hedge; government has tools — supply, competition, targeted relief — and a duty to use them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that interventions produced shortages worse than the price rises they targeted.
- **Guard the currency, hands off prices** — Reasons: inflation is a monetary phenomenon, and defending the currency's value is the state's whole job here; interfering with individual prices creates shortages and black markets. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cases where price rises were driven by monopoly markups that sound money could not touch.
- **Leave prices to the market** — Reasons: prices are information, and suppressing them hides scarcity rather than curing it; high prices are themselves the signal that calls forth supply. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that essential-goods pricing in emergencies systematically produced exploitation markets could not correct.

#### The monetary standard
- **Fiat money under a central bank** — Reasons: flexible money lets policy respond to crises and growth; every modern economy runs on managed fiat without returning to commodity limits. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a long-run record showing fiat regimes systematically debased savings compared with anchored ones.
- **A gold or commodity standard** — Reasons: anchoring money to something governments cannot print disciplines spending and protects savers; fiat's short history is a history of debasement. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that commodity standards transmitted shocks and deflations worse than fiat crises.
- **Free banking; competing currencies, no legal tender** — Reasons: competition disciplined private note issuers historically, and no single issuer should be trusted; people should choose the money that holds value. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that competing-currency systems collapsed into confusion or monopoly in practice.

#### Minimum wage
- **Higher federal floor** — Reasons: full-time work should not leave a family in poverty, and employers should not be subsidised by food stamps for their own staff; a floor restores bargaining power monopsonistic employers erode. Statistic: 1.0% of hourly-paid US workers earned at or below the $7.25 federal minimum in 2024 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025 report on 2024 data). What would move a holder: credible evidence that a substantially higher floor caused large job losses among the low-paid it aimed to help.
- **A modest federal floor** — Reasons: a low national backstop prevents the worst exploitation without pricing marginal workers out of jobs in low-cost regions; states can go higher where costs justify it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a modest floor was either useless against poverty or as harmful as a high one.
- **No federal floor** — Reasons: a single national wage cannot fit Mississippi and Manhattan, and floors price the least-skilled out of first jobs; wages should be set by agreement and local law. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that abolishing floors left low-wage workers no worse off was false — i.e. that floors raised pay without the predicted job losses at scale.

#### Labor Rights
- **Strong labor protections** — Reasons: individual workers cannot bargain evenly with firms, so collective organisation is the counterweight that built middle-class wages and safety; union membership was 9.9% of US wage and salary workers in 2024, down from 20.1% in 1983 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). Statistic: see reasons (BLS, 2025). What would move a holder: evidence that strong protections mainly protected insiders while raising unemployment for outsiders.
- **Basic protections, voluntary association** — Reasons: workers must be free to organise, and equally free not to join or pay; protection of the right, without compulsion either way, is the liberal settlement. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that voluntariness alone collapsed organising wherever employers resisted.
- **Voluntary association only** — Reasons: unions are private associations and should persuade, not be armed with state-enforced bargaining and dues; labour markets work when contracts are free. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that purely voluntary systems left systematic coercion by employers unanswered.

#### Social Security
- **Protect and expand** — Reasons: Social Security is the most effective anti-poverty programme in US history, especially for the elderly, and its shortfall is fixable by revenue; benefits are earned, not welfare. Statistic: Social Security paid benefits to about 68 million people in 2024 (US Social Security Administration, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that expansion could not be financed without harming the workers who fund it.
- **Preserve with reforms** — Reasons: the programme's promise must be kept, and honest adjustments to taxes, ages or formulas now are kinder than abrupt cuts later; preservation requires arithmetic. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that proposed 'reforms' were cuts in disguise that broke the programme's universality.
- **Phase it out** — Reasons: a pay-as-you-go system is a chain letter demography has broken, and workers would do better owning real assets; transition, though hard, ends an unsustainable promise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that privatised systems left typical retirees poorer or exposed to market ruin in old age.

#### Welfare
- **Strong safety net** — Reasons: poverty is usually bad luck — illness, job loss, birth — not bad character, and a rich society can insure its members against it; children should not pay for parents' fortunes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that generous nets trapped recipients in dependency across generations at scale.
- **A limited safety net** — Reasons: help should be a trampoline, not a hammock — targeted, time-aware and tied to work or training where possible; limits keep the net affordable and legitimate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that conditions and limits mainly excluded the eligible through bureaucracy rather than encouraging work.
- **No federal welfare** — Reasons: relief belongs to families, churches, charities and local communities that know the person, not distant programmes; federal welfare crowds out exactly those bonds. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that local and charitable relief demonstrably failed to reach whole classes of need.

#### Housing affordability
- **Build and subsidize more** — Reasons: housing is too scarce and too dear for wages, so public money must both build and help people pay; homelessness and rent burdens are policy outcomes, not fate. Statistic: 22.4 million US renter households were cost-burdened in 2022, a record high (Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that subsidies mainly bid up prices where supply stayed fixed.
- **Deregulate & build (YIMBY)** — Reasons: zoning, permitting and vetoes by incumbents are the binding constraint on supply; freeing builders to build densely near jobs lowers prices without public money. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that deregulated cities still failed to build, or that new supply did not moderate prices.
- **Leave it to the market** — Reasons: housing interventions — rent control, subsidies, public building — have long records of distortion and shortage; prices signal where building is wanted. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unaided markets systematically failed to house working people in productive cities.

#### Universal basic income
- **A universal payment to every adult** — Reasons: universality ends stigma, bureaucracy and benefit traps, and gives every citizen a share of common wealth and automation gains; freedom requires an unconditional floor. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a large, long trial in which a universal payment reduced work and wellbeing rather than raising them.
- **A targeted guaranteed income for those in need** — Reasons: guaranteeing income to those who lack it delivers UBI's security at a fraction of the cost; universality wastes payments on people who do not need them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that targeting recreated the traps and stigma that universality was designed to escape.
- **No guaranteed income** — Reasons: income should come from work, family and voluntary support; a state guarantee severs income from contribution and must be financed by taxing producers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unconditional payments raised work, enterprise and family stability rather than reducing them.


### 3.2 Health & Care

#### Health insurance system
- **Private financing** — Reasons: private insurance competes on price and plan design, and keeps medical decisions between patient, doctor and insurer rather than the state; public systems ration by queue. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that private financing left comparable countries with worse coverage at higher cost than public systems.
- **Guaranteed coverage** — Reasons: illness is not a consumer choice, and a right to care means coverage cannot depend on income or employment; 8.0% of the US population had no health insurance in 2023 (US Census Bureau, 2024). Statistic: see reasons (Census Bureau, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that a guarantee produced rationing so severe that effective access fell.

#### Prescription drug prices
- **Negotiate and cap prices** — Reasons: medicines are monopolies by patent, so the largest buyer should bargain like one; people ration insulin and cancer drugs at prices other countries do not pay. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that negotiation materially reduced new-drug development for serious disease.
- **Negotiate the worst excesses** — Reasons: a few outlier prices are indefensible while most pricing funds research; targeted negotiation fixes abuse without a general price regime. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that 'worst excesses' could not be defined without becoming general price control.
- **Leave prices to the market** — Reasons: high prices are the prize that draws capital into risky drug development; caps today mean missing cures tomorrow. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that high US prices bought little extra innovation relative to other countries' spending.

#### Medicare
- **Protect and expand** — Reasons: Medicare works — near-universal coverage for the elderly at lower administrative cost than private insurance — and expanding it extends a proven model. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that expansion undermined the programme's finances or care quality for current beneficiaries.
- **Replace with universal** — Reasons: a patchwork by age is arbitrary; one universal system ends gaps at 65, job-lock, and duplicate bureaucracies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that folding Medicare into a new system put current beneficiaries' care at risk.
- **Leave to the market** — Reasons: public insurance crowds out private provision and loads costs onto taxpayers; older people, like everyone, are better served by competing plans they choose. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that private markets would not insure the old and sick at any affordable price.

#### Vaccines
- **Mandates legitimate** — Reasons: vaccination protects others, not only the vaccinated, so it is a classic case where individual choice imposes risk on the community; school and workplace requirements ended whole diseases. Statistic: kindergarten MMR coverage in the US was 92.7% in the 2023–24 school year, below the 95% target (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that a mandated vaccine's risks to individuals outweighed its protection of others.
- **Mandates with exemptions** — Reasons: public health and conscience can both be honoured — require vaccination, but exempt medical need and sincere belief; exemptions keep mandates legitimate and compliance high. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that exemptions were exploited so widely that community protection collapsed.
- **Mandates violate autonomy** — Reasons: the body is the first property, and compelled medical treatment crosses a line no benefit calculation should cross; persuasion, not compulsion, is the free society's tool. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an outbreak in which voluntary uptake demonstrably failed and only compulsion could protect the vulnerable.

#### Behavioral Health
- **Treatment and investment** — Reasons: addiction and mental illness are health conditions that punishment does not cure; treatment, housing and crisis care reduce deaths, crime and emergency costs together. Statistic: about 80,000 US drug overdose deaths occurred in 2024 on provisional counts, down sharply from 2023 (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2025). What would move a holder: evidence that treatment investment did not reduce deaths or disorder where it was tried at scale.
- **Enforcement and interdiction** — Reasons: supply drives epidemics — fentanyl precursors, trafficking networks and open dealing create the casualties treatment then chases; without interdiction, demand-side work is overwhelmed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that interdiction raised prices and purity-adjusted availability so little that it could not affect deaths.

#### Child care
- **Guaranteed affordable care** — Reasons: child care is infrastructure for work — parents cannot work without it — and early years shape lifetime outcomes; guaranteeing affordability raises employment and child development together. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that public guarantee raised prices and lowered quality or parental choice.
- **Subsidize, don't guarantee** — Reasons: helping families with cost through subsidies or credits preserves diverse provision — family, church, market — without a state system; guarantees invite bureaucracy and queues. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that subsidies were captured in higher prices while access barely moved.
- **Families pay themselves** — Reasons: child care is a normal cost of the family life people choose, like food and housing; subsidising it taxes the childless and single-earner families to favour one arrangement. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unaffordable care was driving parents — especially mothers — out of work at large economic cost.

#### Paid Leave
- **National paid-leave program** — Reasons: no one should lose a job or income for a birth, a dying parent or chemotherapy; a social-insurance programme spreads a universal risk the way pensions do. Statistic: 27% of US private-industry workers had access to paid family leave in 2023 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2023). What would move a holder: evidence that national programmes mainly benefited higher earners or damaged small employers.
- **Incentivize employer leave** — Reasons: tax credits and encouragement spread leave provision without a new bureaucracy or mandate; firms compete on benefits when nudged. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that incentives left most low-wage workers — those least able to take unpaid leave — uncovered.
- **Leave it to employers** — Reasons: leave is one term of a job contract among wages and hours, best varied by workplace; mandates raise the cost of hiring the very workers they aim to help. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that voluntary provision systematically failed workers at moments of family crisis.

### 3.3 Climate, Energy & Environment

#### Climate Change
- **Urgent action** — Reasons: warming is human-caused, its harms compound, and delay locks in infrastructure and temperatures; the cost of acting is smaller than the cost of unmitigated damage. Statistic: human-caused warming reached about 1.1 °C in 2011–2020 relative to 1850–1900 (IPCC AR6 Synthesis Report, 2023). What would move a holder: evidence that climate sensitivity and damages were far lower than assessed, making rapid action wasted effort.
- **Gradual, market-led action** — Reasons: a rising carbon price and clean-technology competition cut emissions at least cost without picking winners or shocking energy prices; haste wastes capital on immature fixes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that gradual paths overshot temperature targets while faster action was demonstrably affordable.
- **No government action** — Reasons: climate models and policy costs are too uncertain to justify restructuring energy, and adaptation has historically outpaced predictions of harm; energy poverty is a present, certain evil. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: direct, repeated evidence of severe harms that adaptation could not answer at reasonable cost.

#### Fossil fuels
- **Drill and expand** — Reasons: oil, gas and coal supply most energy people actually use, and restricting domestic production hands supply to worse regimes without cutting demand; abundance keeps prices and industry competitive. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that clean alternatives could replace fossil energy at scale and price without shortages.
- **All of the above, in transition** — Reasons: an honest transition uses gas as a bridge, keeps energy affordable and reliable, and avoids stranding the economy before replacements are ready. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that 'all of the above' was a pretext for never transitioning at all.
- **Phase out** — Reasons: every new well and plant locks in decades of emissions incompatible with climate targets; a clear phase-out date is what makes investment move. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that phase-out schedules caused energy crises that discredited climate policy itself.

#### Nuclear power
- **Keep and expand** — Reasons: nuclear is the only proven source of large, reliable, zero-carbon power, and modern designs answer the safety objections of the 1970s; climate goals without nuclear require heroic assumptions. Statistic: nuclear supplied about 19% of US electricity in 2023 (US Energy Information Administration, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that renewables plus storage could match nuclear's reliability at lower total system cost.
- **Keep the fleet, build selectively** — Reasons: existing plants are paid-for clean power worth keeping, while new builds should proceed where designs and costs are proven; neither worship nor phobia. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that selective building became a de facto ban, or that new designs were ready for general build-out.
- **Phase it out** — Reasons: waste remains unsolved over geological time, accidents are low-probability but catastrophic, and the money buys more carbon reduction in renewables and efficiency. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a permanent waste solution and a long record of the newest designs operating safely and cheaply.

#### Renewable energy
- **Massive public investment** — Reasons: clean energy has the network and learning-curve character that public investment historically unlocked (rail, electrification, the internet); speed is the whole point under a carbon budget. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that public build-outs produced stranded, low-quality capacity compared with incentive-led growth.
- **Incentives, not mandates** — Reasons: credits and prizes let the cheapest clean technologies win and keep innovation private; mandates freeze today's technology into law. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that incentives alone were too slow to meet targets the holder accepts.
- **Let the market decide** — Reasons: wind and solar now compete on cost where they work, and subsidy regimes distort siting and reward lobbying; if renewables are cheapest, they need no help. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unpriced pollution meant the 'market' comparison was rigged from the start.

#### Electric vehicles
- **Incentivize and mandate EVs** — Reasons: transport is a major emissions source and fleet turnover is slow, so standards plus incentives are needed to move it in time; mandates give industry the certainty to invest. Statistic: electric cars were 18% of all cars sold worldwide in 2023 (International Energy Agency, Global EV Outlook, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that mandates outran charging, grids and affordability, producing backlash without emissions gains.
- **Incentives, no mandates** — Reasons: helping buyers choose EVs speeds adoption while respecting that rural drivers, trades and budgets differ; compulsion breeds resentment against the technology itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that incentives without standards left fleet emissions essentially unchanged.
- **Cancel EV mandates** — Reasons: mandates force a product many buyers do not want and grids are not ready for, and amount to banning the affordable cars working people rely on; let EVs win on merit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that mandates were the decisive factor in rapid, popular adoption elsewhere.

#### Growth vs. Limits
- **Abundance & Acceleration** — Reasons: scarcity is a technology problem, and energy, housing and food abundance have repeatedly been unlocked by building more, not rationing; limits talk becomes austerity for the poor. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence of a hard physical limit that innovation repeatedly failed to move.
- **Green Decoupling** — Reasons: many rich economies have grown while emissions fell, showing output and impact can separate with clean technology and pricing; decoupling keeps prosperity and climate goals compatible. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that apparent decoupling was mostly offshored emissions and extraction.
- **Post-Growth Sufficiency** — Reasons: infinite material growth on a finite planet is arithmetic denial, and wellbeing saturates — beyond sufficiency, more consumption buys little health or happiness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: durable absolute decoupling of growth from all major material impacts, not carbon alone.

#### Environmental Justice
- **Strict rules, polluter-pays** — Reasons: pollution burdens fall on communities with least power to refuse them, which is an injustice, not an accident; making polluters pay internalises costs they now dump on neighbours. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that strict regimes mainly closed plants and jobs in the same communities they aimed to protect.
- **Rules with property remedies** — Reasons: environmental harm is real, and its cleanest remedy is compensating and empowering the owners and residents harmed; property remedies avoid a vast discretionary bureaucracy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that property remedies left non-owners — renters, children, the unborn — unprotected.
- **Property rights & courts only** — Reasons: nuisance and trespass law handled pollution for centuries without agencies; courts tie remedy to proven harm to identifiable people, which disciplines both industry and regulators. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that diffuse, cumulative harms could not be litigated effectively case by case.

#### Land stewardship
- **Federal conservation** — Reasons: ecosystems and watersheds cross state lines, and only national management protects them from local development pressure; public lands belong to all citizens, including future ones. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that federal management produced worse ecological outcomes than state or private stewardship.
- **Shared stewardship** — Reasons: federal, state, tribal, local and private stewards each know part of the land; sharing authority combines national standards with local knowledge. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that shared arrangements dissolved accountability when damage occurred.
- **Return to states / private** — Reasons: distant federal management mismanages land it cannot know, and blocks the people who live on it; states and owners have stronger incentives to care for what is theirs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that transferred lands were promptly sold, fenced or degraded at scale.

#### Agriculture
- **Support farmers & reform** — Reasons: food security is national security, farming is hostage to weather and world prices, and support should be reformed toward soil, resilience and small producers rather than volume alone. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that support mainly enriched large agribusiness while food security was never at risk.
- **A safety net, market-led** — Reasons: insurance against catastrophic crop and price failure keeps farmers farming, while ordinary decisions should follow markets; a net, not a direction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the safety net had become permanent income support for the largest operations.
- **Leave it to the market** — Reasons: subsidies distort planting, raise land prices for new farmers, and pay for overproduction of the wrong crops; food is produced abundantly where support is low, as in New Zealand. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that removing support caused food insecurity or dependence on imports for staples.

### 3.4 Immigration & Borders

#### Border Enforcement
- **Wall and hard enforcement** — Reasons: a border that cannot be physically controlled is not a border, and barriers plus enforcement change the economics of crossing; sovereignty begins with deciding who enters. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that barriers merely displaced crossings while legal pathways and enforcement at workplaces did the real work.
- **Technology & agents** — Reasons: sensors, drones and well-staffed ports cover terrain walls cannot, and enforcement works best paired with legal channels that drain pressure from the illegal route. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that technology-led enforcement repeatedly failed to detect or deter crossings a barrier would have stopped.

#### Deportation
- **Mass deportation** — Reasons: law that is not enforced against the many invites more violation, and a large unauthorised population is itself the product of selective enforcement; equal enforcement is the rule of law. Statistic: the unauthorised immigrant population in the US was 11.0 million in 2022 (Pew Research Center, 2024). What would move a holder: credible costing showing mass removal would devastate industries and families at a scale outweighing its legal gains.
- **Prioritized removal** — Reasons: enforcement resources are finite, so removing serious criminals and recent arrivals first protects the public and keeps the system legitimate; priorities are how all law enforcement works. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that prioritisation became de facto non-enforcement for most violations.
- **Removal after fair process** — Reasons: even non-citizens are owed hearings, evidence and appeal before the state expels them, because errors deport the lawful and the endangered; process is what separates law from roundup. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that full process for every case made the system so slow it ceased to function at all.

#### Sanctuary cities
- **Let cities decide** — Reasons: local police need community trust to solve crime, and conscripting them into federal enforcement destroys it; federalism lets localities judge their own safety. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that non-cooperation repeatedly released people who committed serious, preventable crimes.
- **Cooperate on serious crimes** — Reasons: a middle settlement — local discretion for minor contact, cooperation for grave offences — honours both community trust and public safety. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the 'serious crimes' line could not be held in practice by either side.
- **Cut federal funding** — Reasons: jurisdictions cannot take federal money while obstructing federal law; funding conditions are the lawful lever Washington has over defiance. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a ruling or record showing funding cuts punished unrelated services without changing cooperation.

#### Legal Immigration
- **Expand admissions** — Reasons: immigrants grow the economy, fill labour gaps, found businesses and offset demographic decline; legal expansion also shrinks illegal immigration by giving it a lawful route. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that expanded admissions depressed wages for the lowest-paid citizens persistently.
- **Merit-based selection** — Reasons: admission should serve the admitting country's needs — skills, language, self-sufficiency — as Canada and Australia do; selection by contribution is fairer to those already here. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that points systems mainly selected credentials without improving integration or earnings.

#### Safe haven
- **Fair, humane process** — Reasons: asylum is a legal and moral duty to people fleeing persecution, and most failures — destitution, delay, detention — are choices, not necessities. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that humane processing at scale acted as a magnet overwhelming genuine protection.
- **Orderly, screened asylum** — Reasons: protection works only if claims are screened quickly and the unfounded are returned; order is what keeps public consent for asylum alive. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that screening speed was bought by refusing large numbers of genuine refugees.
- **Strict vetting & deterrence** — Reasons: an asylum system that rewards arrival becomes an immigration channel outside the law; deterrence protects the system's integrity and discourages lethal journeys. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that deterrence mainly punished the persecuted without reducing overall flows.

#### Immigration legalization
- **Path to citizenship** — Reasons: millions who work, pay taxes and raise citizens here cannot live permanently in shadow; earned citizenship closes the gap between law and reality. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that legalisation triggered new unauthorised flows larger than the population it regularised.
- **Legal status without citizenship** — Reasons: residence and work rights end exploitation and limbo without conferring the vote, which fairly distinguishes joining a society from joining its citizenry. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that permanent second-class status created an enduring, resentful underclass.
- **Enforcement only, no amnesty** — Reasons: legalising past violation rewards queue-jumping over legal applicants and teaches that immigration law is negotiable; enforcement first, and only, restores credibility. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that decades of enforcement-only policy left the unauthorised population unchanged while its costs compounded.

#### Birthright citizenship
- **Keep birthright citizenship** — Reasons: the Fourteenth Amendment's rule is clear, bright-line and equality-making — no hereditary caste of the US-born non-citizen; it has integrated generations of newcomers' children. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that birthright citizenship had become a major driver of unlawful immigration rather than a marginal one.
- **Restrict it at the edges** — Reasons: citizenship by birth to parents with no lawful tie at all — birth tourism, very recent arrival — stretches the rule past its purpose; narrow restriction preserves the core. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that edge cases were trivial in number and restriction would entangle ordinary births in proof burdens.
- **End it** — Reasons: nearly every other country ties citizenship to parentage or lawful residence; automatic citizenship for any birth on soil is an anomaly that subsidises unlawful entry. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that ending it created stateless children or a permanent hereditary non-citizen class.


### 3.5 Democracy & Government

#### The Classic Political Compass Tetrad
- **Authoritarian Left (State Socialism)** — Reasons: equality requires a state strong enough to plan production and break private power; liberty without material security is formal only. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that state-socialist economies delivered equality only by generalising poverty and repression.
- **Authoritarian Right (Traditionalist / Fascist)** — Reasons: a nation needs hierarchy, shared tradition and a state willing to defend them against dissolution; order and belonging precede individual choice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that such regimes reliably destroyed the traditions and communities they claimed to protect.
- **Libertarian Right (Anarcho-Capitalism / Minarchism)** — Reasons: taxation and regulation are coercion, and markets plus property rights coordinate society without rulers; the state should shrink to courts, defence and contract, or vanish. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that minimal states were captured by private power worse than public power.
- **Libertarian Left (Democratic Confederalism / Anarcho-Syndicalism)** — Reasons: both state and capital are hierarchies to be replaced by federated workplaces and communes; freedom and equality stand or fall together. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that federated communes could not coordinate defence, infrastructure or welfare at scale.

#### Decision Ecology
- **Executive Mandate (Decisive Sovereign)** — Reasons: someone must be able to decide and be blamed; assemblies diffuse responsibility until no one answers for outcomes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that decisive executives systematically produced worse long-run decisions than slower bodies.
- **Technocratic Meritocracy (Rule by Expertise)** — Reasons: monetary, epidemiological and engineering questions have right answers expertise can find; putting them to majorities or patrons wastes knowledge. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that expert rule repeatedly served the expert class's interests over the public's.
- **Direct Deliberative Democracy (Assembly)** — Reasons: decisions are legitimate when made by those who live with them, and ordinary citizens deliberating with good information judge well; representation is a compromise, not an ideal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that citizen assemblies were captured by whoever set their information and agenda.
- **Consensus Federation (Veto / Unanimity)** — Reasons: no unit should be governed against its will on fundamentals, so federation by consent protects diversity and minorities; slow agreement beats imposed uniformity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that veto systems reliably produced paralysis in the face of common threats.

#### Macro Political Matrix Hexad
- **Free-Market Capitalism** — Reasons: markets, property and contract have produced the largest rises in living standards in history; a regime should organise around what demonstrably enriches ordinary life. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that its prosperity depended on exclusions — colonies, monopolies, unpriced damage — it could not survive without.
- **Technocratic Meritocracy** — Reasons: complex societies are engineering problems at scale, and rule by the competent, selected by examination and record, beats rule by inheritance, wealth or applause. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that merit selection entrenched a closed caste calling itself merit.
- **Authoritarian Order** — Reasons: when societies fragment, order — one authority, one law, enforced — is the precondition of every other good, including eventual liberty. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that authoritarian order reliably consumed itself in succession crises and corruption.
- **Democratic Socialism** — Reasons: democracy should extend into the economy — workplaces, housing, health — not stop at the factory gate; public ownership under elected control reconciles equality with liberty. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that economic democracy destroyed the productivity that funds social goods.
- **Communitarian Localism** — Reasons: people flourish in thick local communities of family, faith and place, and a regime should protect those against both central state and global market. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that localism mainly protected local tyrannies and exclusion.
- **Libertarian Voluntaryism** — Reasons: every legitimate human arrangement is consensual, so the regime ideal is no regime — only voluntary association, contract and exit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that purely voluntary orders could not supply defence, justice or relief in catastrophe.

#### Seven Theories of Political Legitimacy
- **Divine Right / Cosmic Order** — Reasons: authority mirrors a moral order higher than human preference, which is why it can bind consciences; a ruler accountable to God is limited by a law he did not make. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that claims to divine warrant served only to immunise rulers from correction.
- **Social Contract (Hobbes / Locke / Rousseau)** — Reasons: government is legitimate as the people's own act — they trade obedience for protection of life and rights, and may reclaim authority when the bargain is broken. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that contract language merely disguised conquest or inheritance in every real state.
- **Democratic Majoritarianism** — Reasons: the only non-arbitrary source of authority is the counted will of equals; a majority's decision binds because every citizen's vote weighed the same. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: repeated cases where majorities voted away the rights that made them a people at all.
- **Institutional Functionalism (Output Legitimacy)** — Reasons: institutions earn authority by working — prosperity, safety, competent administration — and lose it by failing, whatever their origin story. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that output-legitimate regimes collapsed the moment outputs faltered, lacking any deeper loyalty.
- **Technocratic Epistocracy** — Reasons: political decisions have better and worse answers, so authority should track knowledge; weighting rule toward the competent is not elitism but responsibility. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that no test of political knowledge could avoid encoding the test-makers' interests.
- **Revolutionary Vanguard** — Reasons: an oppressed class cannot free itself through institutions built to contain it; a disciplined vanguard earns authority by making the revolution and defending it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that vanguards reliably became new ruling classes rather than dissolving into the people's rule.
- **Anarchist Voluntaryism** — Reasons: no one has a right to rule another, so all claimed political authority is usurpation; legitimacy exists only in consent, continuously given and revocable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that stateless orders reliably fell to the first organised armed minority.

#### The nature of the Union
- **The Union is indissoluble** — Reasons: the Constitution created a people, not a league, and the Civil War settled that exit is rebellion; indissolubility protects every citizen's rights against local majorities. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where a region's people were held in a union that systematically stripped their fundamental rights.
- **States may leave by mutual agreement** — Reasons: a union of consent should allow negotiated divorce, as federations and marriages do; consent that cannot be withdrawn is not consent. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that negotiated-exit clauses destabilised every federation that adopted them.
- **States may secede unilaterally** — Reasons: states entered as sovereigns and retain the right to leave, which is the ultimate check on federal overreach; a voluntary union must be voluntary in fact. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unilateral secession was invoked historically to protect slavery and Jim Crow rather than liberty.

#### Voting Access
- **Expand access, no ID** — Reasons: voting is a right, not a licence, and documented impersonation is vanishingly rare while ID and registration barriers fall hardest on the poor, young and mobile. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence of impersonation fraud at a scale capable of changing results.
- **Easy voting with ID** — Reasons: broad access and a basic identity check are compatible — most democracies do both — and ID reassures losers that results are clean, which is itself democratic infrastructure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that ID requirements, even with free provision, excluded significant numbers of eligible voters.
- **Require ID & same-day voting** — Reasons: elections must be verifiably one person, one vote, decided on the day under uniform rules; late and loose processes corrode confidence in outcomes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that strict rules measurably disenfranchised eligible citizens while fraud stayed negligible.

#### Campaign finance
- **Limit and publicly fund** — Reasons: elections bought by large donors are auctions, not arguments; public funding and caps let candidates answer to voters instead of funders. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that public funding entrenched incumbents or failed to reduce donor influence.
- **Disclosure without caps** — Reasons: sunlight is the proportionate remedy — voters can judge who funds whom — while caps entrench incumbents and drive money into darker channels. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that disclosed influence was just as corrupting as hidden influence.
- **Unrestricted private financing** — Reasons: spending on speech is speech, and restricting it restricts criticism of government itself; voters, not regulators, should discount funded messages. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unrestricted spending converted directly into policy outcomes against clear majorities.

#### Electoral Systems
- **Abolish the College** — Reasons: the president should be chosen by the people counted equally, and a system that can install the popular-vote loser corrodes legitimacy; every other US office works by direct vote. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a national popular vote would concentrate campaigning and recount risk intolerably.
- **Proportional representation** — Reasons: seats should mirror votes, ending manufactured majorities and wasted votes; PR gives minorities representation and forces coalition government by negotiation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that PR systems produced chronic instability or extremist kingmakers worse than two-party distortion.

#### Voting Systems
- **Reform the ballot** — Reasons: ranked-choice voting elects candidates with broad support and ends spoiler dynamics, and open primaries give unaffiliated voters a voice; plurality rules distort real preferences. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that ranked ballots confused voters and produced exhausted, unrepresentative counts in practice.
- **Modest reforms** — Reasons: the system's basics are sound and legitimacy rests on familiarity; small fixes — ballot access, counting speed, primary timing — capture most of the gain without risk. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that modest fixes left the core distortions untouched.
- **Keep the current system** — Reasons: plurality voting produces clear winners, stable parties and accountable single representatives; reforms trade known virtues for theoretical ones. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: repeated elections in which plurality winners were opposed by large, demonstrable majorities.

#### Redistricting
- **Independent, fair maps** — Reasons: politicians choosing their voters inverts democracy; independent commissions under fairness criteria restore competition and representation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that commissions were captured or produced maps as skewed as legislatures'.
- **Legislatures with fairness rules** — Reasons: map-drawing is political and should stay with accountable elected bodies, bound by enforceable rules on compactness and competitiveness that courts can police. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that fairness rules were routinely gamed by the legislatures they bound.
- **Leave to state legislatures** — Reasons: redistricting has always been ordinary politics, and voters who dislike maps can elect new legislators; 'independent' bodies merely hide the politics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that self-interested map-drawing had locked in unrepresentative delegations for decades.

#### Political status
- **Statehood / self-determination** — Reasons: millions of US citizens in DC and Puerto Rico lack voting representation in the government that taxes and conscripts them; self-determination is the democratic remedy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a free vote in the affected territory clearly rejecting statehood.
- **Free association** — Reasons: a sovereign partnership — own citizenship and government, negotiated compact with the US — respects a people's distinct nationhood better than absorption as a state. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that free-association compacts left the associated people dependent and voiceless in practice.
- **Keep current status** — Reasons: current arrangements bring benefits — tax treatment, autonomy, US citizenship without full federal burdens — that majorities in the territories have not clearly voted to surrender. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a clear, repeated territorial vote for a different status.

#### Choosing Senators
- **Popular election of Senators** — Reasons: senators wield national power and should answer to the people, not to statehouse bargains that historically produced corruption and deadlocked vacancies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that direct election destroyed the Senate's federal character without improving its quality.
- **State legislatures choose Senators** — Reasons: the original design made the Senate the states' house, giving state governments a check inside Washington; direct election nationalised the Senate and weakened federalism. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that legislative selection in the modern party era would reproduce the bribery and deadlock that ended it.

#### Supreme Court
- **Change the size** — Reasons: the Court's size is set by statute and has changed before; when a majority entrenches itself against democratic correction, expanding or restructuring is a lawful democratic answer. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that size changes triggered endless retaliation that destroyed the Court's authority.
- **Term limits** — Reasons: life tenure made sense when lives and tenures were shorter; staggered fixed terms regularise appointments, lower the stakes of each vacancy, and end strategic retirements. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that term-limited judges became more political as they auditioned for post-court careers.

#### Judicial appointments
- **Protect rights and freedoms** — Reasons: courts exist chiefly to defend rights against majorities, so judges should be chosen for willingness to strike down rights-violating laws; deference abandons the vulnerable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that rights-expanding courts mainly protected the powerful's preferred liberties.
- **Judicial restraint** — Reasons: judges are unelected and should resolve cases narrowly, deferring to democratic branches on policy; restraint keeps courts courts. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cases where restraint left grave rights violations permanently unremedied.
- **Apply the law as written** — Reasons: the judge's job is fidelity to enacted text and original meaning, not personal values; textualism constrains judges of every ideology equally. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that textualist method concealed discretionary choices as often as it constrained them.

#### Levels of government
- **Strong federal standards** — Reasons: civil rights, clean air and fair markets cannot depend on state lines, and states have historically been the violators national standards exist to correct. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that uniform national rules crushed beneficial state experimentation without improving outcomes.
- **Federal floors, state freedom** — Reasons: national minima guarantee rights and safety everywhere while states remain free to innovate above the floor; this is federalism working as designed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that floors reliably became ceilings, or ratcheted into full national control.
- **Powers to the states** — Reasons: decisions made closest to the people are most accountable and best fitted to local conditions; the Constitution reserved undelegated powers to states and people for this reason. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that state control systematically produced rights violations national action had to correct.

#### Government Size
- **Active federal government** — Reasons: modern problems — pandemics, financial crises, climate — exceed any state's capacity, and an active state built the prosperity and security Americans take for granted. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that federal expansion had passed the point where marginal programmes did more harm than good.
- **Lean but capable** — Reasons: a state should do fewer things excellently rather than everything badly; competence, not size, is what citizens actually experience. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that 'lean' was reliably a pretext for disabling functions the public wanted kept.
- **Minimal government** — Reasons: government is coercion financed by taxation, so every function beyond protecting rights and enforcing contracts needs extraordinary justification it rarely survives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that minimal states failed to protect their own citizens' rights and safety.

#### Regulation
- **Regulate to protect** — Reasons: markets left alone produced poisoned food, crashed banks and lethal workplaces, and regulation is how the public answers harms no individual can litigate alone. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a given regulatory regime cost far more than the harm it prevented.
- **Smarter regulation** — Reasons: the goals of protection are right and the machinery is often dumb — rules should be cost-aware, reviewed and sunset, keeping benefits without the deadweight. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that 'smarter' review processes were captured to strangle protection itself.
- **Deregulate** — Reasons: regulation accumulates into a barrier protecting incumbents, raising prices and criminalising ordinary business; removing it frees enterprise and lowers costs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that deregulated sectors promptly recreated the harms the rules had prevented.

#### Administrative State
- **Expert Agency Autonomy** — Reasons: agencies staffed by scientists, economists and engineers should apply statutes on evidence, insulated from day-to-day politics; expertise without insulation is decoration. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that autonomous agencies systematically pursued their own agendas against elected policy.
- **Elected Executive Control** — Reasons: power exercised in the people's name must answer to the person the people elected; an unaccountable fourth branch is a democratic defect whatever its expertise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that executive control produced purges of expertise and policy whiplash worse than autonomy.

#### Term limits for Congress
- **Necessary** — Reasons: incumbency advantages have made most seats safe, turning representatives into careerists; limits rotate power, weaken lobbyist relationships and restore citizen-legislators. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence from state legislatures that limits mainly empowered lobbyists, staff and executives.
- **Harmful** — Reasons: limits throw out experience on a schedule, handing power to unelected staff, agencies and lobbyists who stay; voters already hold the only legitimate term limit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that limited legislatures stayed expert and independent of lobbyists.
- **Unnecessary (elections suffice)** — Reasons: if voters keep returning a legislator, that is their right, and overriding it by rule treats citizens as incompetent; competition should be fixed directly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that incumbency advantage had become so structural that elections no longer functioned as a check.

#### Privacy
- **Strong privacy protections** — Reasons: surveillance chills speech, association and belief, and collected data is inevitably misused, breached or repurposed; privacy is a precondition of a free citizenry. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that strong protections prevented the solving of serious crimes at scale.
- **Warrants and oversight** — Reasons: the Fourth Amendment settlement — search with judicial warrant, oversight of programmes — balances security and liberty, and the abuse is warrantless collection, not collection itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that warrant systems had become rubber stamps providing no real check.
- **Security first** — Reasons: the state's first duty is protecting life, and modern threats — terrorism, trafficking, cyberattack — are found in data or not at all; privacy claims must yield to prevention. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that mass collection contributed little to security while enabling abuse.

#### Free Speech
- **No government censorship** — Reasons: the state is the last institution to trust with deciding which ideas may be heard, and every censorship power built for good targets is inherited by bad governments. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence of speech harms so direct and grave that no counterspeech or private remedy could answer them.
- **Protect speech, narrow limits** — Reasons: the First Amendment tradition already holds this line — protect speech fiercely, with narrow, proven exceptions for incitement, true threats and fraud. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the narrow exceptions had expanded in practice into general speech policing.
- **Act against obscenity** — Reasons: a community may defend its shared moral environment, and obscenity has never been protected speech; public decency is a legitimate public good. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that obscenity enforcement mainly became a tool against unpopular minorities and art.

#### Artificial intelligence
- **Regulate AI** — Reasons: powerful AI systems affect hiring, credit, information and weapons, and voluntary ethics by the firms selling them is not accountability; rules set floors before harms scale. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that binding rules froze beneficial uses while risky development moved offshore unchanged.
- **Light-touch guardrails** — Reasons: targeted safeguards on the highest-risk uses, with room to learn, beat comprehensive regimes written for a technology still changing monthly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that light-touch regimes were ignored by the largest developers until harms occurred.
- **Minimal regulation** — Reasons: AI is a general technology like electricity or computing, and existing law already covers fraud, discrimination and harm; new bureaucracies would entrench incumbents who can afford compliance. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major AI harm that existing law demonstrably could not reach.

#### Constitutional Design
- **Counter-Majoritarian Checks** — Reasons: rights and stability require institutions that can say no to temporary majorities — courts, senates, amendment hurdles; pure majoritarianism consumes its own foundations. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the checks had become minority rule, blocking durable majorities indefinitely.
- **Direct Popular Mandate** — Reasons: legitimacy flows from the people's present will, and structures that frustrate it — however venerable — are democratic defects; a constitution should transmit, not filter, the mandate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unfiltered mandates repeatedly destroyed the rights of minorities and the constitution itself.

#### Section 230 and platform liability
- **Repeal it; platforms are liable for user content** — Reasons: platforms exercise editorial power while claiming a publisher's immunity, a combination no print publisher enjoys; liability would force genuine responsibility for what they amplify. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that liability produced mass over-removal and killed small platforms while giants absorbed the cost.
- **Reform it; condition immunity on neutrality or moderation standards** — Reasons: immunity was granted to encourage good-faith moderation, so conditioning it on transparent, even-handed standards restores the original bargain. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that any neutrality condition was unworkable or itself became government speech control.
- **Keep it; platforms set their own rules** — Reasons: without immunity, every platform faces a choice between censoring everything and drowning in lawsuits; private moderation under property rights is what makes diverse platforms possible. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a few platforms had become the public square itself, making private rule a public problem.

#### Net neutrality
- **Essential** — Reasons: broadband is a bottleneck monopoly in most places, and without neutrality rules providers can tax, slow or favour content, converting infrastructure power into editorial power. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a long record after repeal in which blocking and paid prioritisation did not occur.
- **Harmful** — Reasons: classifying broadband as a utility chilled the investment that builds networks, while the predicted abuses were hypothetical; heavy-handed rules protect yesterday's internet. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: documented cases of providers exploiting gatekeeper power against competitors or speech.
- **Unnecessary** — Reasons: competition, transparency rules and antitrust already police real abuses, and the internet developed fastest without neutrality mandates. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that local broadband monopolies made competition and antitrust remedies illusory.


### 3.6 Rights & Freedoms

#### Abortion
- **Pro-choice** — Reasons: bodily autonomy means the state may not compel pregnancy and birth, and the decision belongs to the pregnant person with her doctor and conscience; bans drive abortion underground, not away. Statistic: an estimated 1,038,000 abortions occurred in the US formal health-care system in 2023 (Guttmacher Institute, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence they accepted that the foetus is a person with a right to life overriding bodily autonomy from conception.
- **Legal with limits** — Reasons: both the woman's autonomy and the developing life's claims are real and change over gestation, so law may protect choice early and life later; this is where most citizens actually stand. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that gestational limits were unworkable in practice, or that one side's claim wholly displaced the other's.
- **Pro-life** — Reasons: the unborn child is a human life with a right not to be killed, and a society is judged by protecting its most defenceless members; abortion ends that life as a matter of definition. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence they accepted that personhood begins later than conception, or that bans increased total deaths.
- **Leave it to the states** — Reasons: on a question the Constitution does not settle in text, federalism lets diverse communities live by their own moral judgments, and state decisions are closer and more revisable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that state variation produced rights harms so grave that a national rule was required either way.

#### Reproductive Care
- **Guaranteed access** — Reasons: contraception and IVF are ordinary medicine, and access should not depend on an employer's or insurer's theology; guaranteeing access prevents the abortion question from being recreated by the back door. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that guaranteed provision coerced objecting providers rather than protecting patients.
- **Protected, with conscience carve-outs** — Reasons: patients' access and providers' conscience are both rights; carve-outs with referral duties let both survive without either being absolute. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that carve-outs in practice denied whole regions any willing provider.
- **Restricted or unprotected** — Reasons: some reproductive technologies involve moral questions — embryo destruction in IVF, abortifacient claims — on which the state may legislate, and there is no right to state-guaranteed provision of contested medicine. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that restrictions mainly blocked ordinary contraception rather than the contested practices.

#### Religious liberty
- **Freedom to worship & act** — Reasons: faith that cannot be lived in work, dress, diet and speech is not free exercise but private toleration; the First Amendment protects exercise, not merely belief. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cases where acting on belief directly denied others their own legal rights or access to services.
- **Accommodation in a neutral square** — Reasons: neutral laws of general application with reasonable accommodations balance believers and everyone else; special exemptions for religion alone privilege one class of citizen. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that 'neutral' rules were designed or applied to burden unpopular faiths.
- **Strict separation** — Reasons: separation protects both state and church — government entanglement corrupts faith and privileges majority religions; equal citizenship requires a secular public square. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that strict separation had become hostility, excluding religious citizens from public benefits open to all others.

#### Neutrality vs. Common Good
- **Procedural State Neutrality** — Reasons: citizens disagree about the good life, so a legitimate state keeps fair procedures and lets people pursue their own conceptions of the good; a state with a moral programme has subjects, not citizens. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that procedural neutrality itself smuggled in a substantive moral order favouring some citizens.
- **Substantive Moral Formation** — Reasons: every state forms citizens through schools, law and honour whether it admits it or not; doing so deliberately toward virtue and common goods is more honest and better than formation by markets and media. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that state moral formation reliably became oppression of those outside the official good.

#### Federal funding of abortion
- **Allowed (repeal Hyde)** — Reasons: funding restrictions make a legal right meaningless for poor women while wealthier women buy access privately; equal citizenship forbids rights rationed by income. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that public funding significantly increased abortion numbers rather than equalising access.
- **Banned (Hyde)** — Reasons: taxpayers who believe abortion is killing should not be compelled to pay for it; the Hyde settlement has held across decades and parties as a fair compromise on a contested act. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the ban's burden fell as punishment on the poor rather than as protection of conscience.
- **Shifted over time** — Reasons: this pole records a holder whose position has moved — for example with party or jurisprudence — rather than a fixed principle; its holders' reasons are those of whichever settled position they currently hold. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: settling the underlying abortion question, which is what the shift tracks (see §5).

#### Marriage & Family
- **Marriage equality** — Reasons: civil marriage carries hundreds of legal rights, and excluding same-sex couples makes them second-class citizens for no public purpose; equal treatment under law is the whole claim. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence of a public harm from equal marriage beyond private disapproval.
- **Government out** — Reasons: marriage is a religious and social institution the state should neither define nor license; privatising it ends the culture war by removing the prize. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that state exit left spouses — especially the economically weaker — without needed legal protections.

#### LGBTQ+ rights
- **Full equality and protection** — Reasons: explicit protection in employment, housing, schools and care is needed because general equality has repeatedly failed LGBTQ+ people in practice; dignity requires enforceable rights. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that specific mandates produced serious conflicts with others' rights that general law could not resolve.
- **Equal treatment, limited mandates** — Reasons: LGBTQ+ citizens are owed exactly the same treatment as everyone else, while specific mandates in contested areas — sport categories, school policy, medicine for minors — should be decided case by case, not by blanket rule. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that case-by-case treatment collapsed into systematic denial of equal treatment.
- **Restrictions** — Reasons: some contested practices — sex-segregated sport, medical transition for minors, school materials — involve competing rights and child welfare, on which legislatures may restrict in good faith. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that restrictions were driven by animus rather than the stated welfare concerns, or harmed the people they claimed to protect.

#### Civil Rights
- **Active equity programs** — Reasons: formal equality after centuries of legal discrimination leaves accumulated disadvantage untouched; programmes aimed at disparities — in wealth, schooling, health — are how a society repairs what it did. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that equity programmes entrenched bureaucracy and stigma without narrowing the disparities.
- **Equal treatment, targeted help** — Reasons: the state should treat citizens by the same rules while aiming help at need — poverty, failing schools, illness — which reaches disadvantaged minorities without racial classification. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that need-based targeting systematically missed racially specific harms.
- **Equal treatment only** — Reasons: racial classification by the state is the original sin of civil-rights law, and repeating it for benign purposes keeps race at the centre of public life and corrodes equal citizenship. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that formally equal rules left large, specifically racial harms permanently unaddressed.

#### Disability rights
- **Disability rights** — Reasons: disability is produced as much by barriers — steps, formats, attitudes — as by bodies, so accessibility and anti-discrimination law are civil rights, not charity; support and income protection let disabled people live as citizens, not dependents. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: this pole has no opposing pole in the skin (see §5); within it, holders are moved by evidence about which instruments — mandates, incentives, direct support — actually deliver access.

#### Women's equality
- **Women's equality** — Reasons: equal citizenship requires explicit guarantee — in constitutional text, pay, representation and protection from violence — because general equality clauses have repeatedly been read down for women. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: this pole has no opposing pole in the skin (see §5); within it, holders are moved by evidence about whether explicit guarantees change outcomes beyond existing law.

#### Tribal nations
- **Tribal nations** — Reasons: Tribal Nations are sovereigns by treaty, and treaties are supreme law, not favours; honouring sovereignty, land and self-government is a legal obligation and the minimum repair for a history of broken agreements. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: this pole has no opposing pole in the skin (see §5); within it, holders are moved by evidence about which federal arrangements best serve tribal self-determination.

#### Reparations for slavery
- **Full monetary reparations** — Reasons: slavery and the century of legal discrimination after it transferred wealth on a vast scale whose effects compound in today's wealth gap; a debt does not expire because paying it is hard. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the present gap owed little to slavery and its legal aftermath, or that payment could not reach those harmed.
- **A commission to study reparations** — Reasons: the factual record — who was harmed, how effects transmit, what forms repair could take — should be established officially before a nation commits; study is how democracies prepare large moral decisions. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that commissions functioned purely as delay, with findings predetermined to be ignored.
- **No reparations** — Reasons: no living person enslaved or enslaved another, and present taxpayers cannot justly be billed for ancestors' crimes; repair should be forward-looking help for all disadvantaged citizens. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence of specific, traceable, uncompensated harms to identifiable living people.

#### Guns
- **Strict regulation** — Reasons: firearms are uniquely lethal consumer products, and licensing, registration and limits on military-style weapons are how other democracies keep both ownership and far lower death rates. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that strict regimes did not reduce gun deaths, or were enforced mainly against lawful owners.
- **Background checks, broadly legal** — Reasons: the settlement most Americans endorse — lawful ownership for the law-abiding, universal checks to keep guns from people already barred by law. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that checks were either useless against crime guns or a step to registration and confiscation.
- **No regulation at all** — Reasons: the Second Amendment protects an individual right precisely against government permission schemes; a right you must register to exercise is a privilege. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that completely unregulated access produced harms no rights-respecting society could accept — which holders weigh against the amendment's text.

#### Policing
- **Fund and protect police** — Reasons: police are the thin line in high-crime neighbourhoods whose residents — disproportionately poor — demand more protection, not less; demoralised, defunded forces mean abandoned streets. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that additional funding did not improve clearance rates or safety.
- **Reform and accountability** — Reasons: policing's legitimacy depends on answerability — training, standards, body cameras, consequences for abuse — and unaccountable force loses the community cooperation crime-solving needs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that accountability regimes mainly produced risk-averse withdrawal from lawful enforcement.

#### Criminal Justice
- **Reform, reduce incarceration** — Reasons: the US imprisons far more people than comparable nations without proportional safety, and prison often manufactures worse offenders; rehabilitation and alternatives work for most non-violent crime. Statistic: about 1.9 million people were incarcerated in the US in 2024 (Prison Policy Initiative, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that decarceration at scale produced sustained crime increases.
- **Firm and fair** — Reasons: punishment should be certain, proportionate and fairly applied — neither a warehousing system nor a revolving door; fairness is what makes firmness legitimate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the system's failures were structural, not correctable within a firm-and-fair frame.
- **Tougher enforcement & sentencing** — Reasons: incapacitation works mechanically — a jailed offender commits no crimes outside — and leniency signals that crime pays, which the law-abiding poor pay for. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that longer sentences added no deterrent or incapacitation benefit beyond shorter ones.

#### Death penalty
- **Abolish it** — Reasons: the state inevitably executes some innocent people under an irreversible sentence, and capital punishment falls unevenly by race, geography and quality of counsel. Statistic: 25 people were executed in the US in 2024 (Death Penalty Information Center, 2024). What would move a holder: a system demonstrably incapable of executing an innocent person — which holders doubt is achievable.
- **Keep it, narrowly** — Reasons: a small class of murders — mass killing, murder of officers, torture-murder — forfeits the offender's life, and reserving execution for them under strict safeguards expresses society's ultimate condemnation justly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that even narrow systems could not exclude innocence or arbitrariness.
- **Keep it** — Reasons: retribution is a legitimate purpose of punishment, and some crimes deserve death as a matter of justice, not deterrence; abolition tells victims' families their loss ranks below the murderer's life. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that executions were applied so arbitrarily that the retributive claim collapsed.

#### Drug Policy
- **Legalize, treat as health** — Reasons: prohibition created the illicit market, its violence and its adulterated supply, while criminalisation punishes a health condition; legal regulation plus treatment is the model that worked for alcohol. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that legalisation substantially increased addiction and public disorder.
- **Decriminalize use, fight supply** — Reasons: users should not be criminals, but traffickers should be; removing penalties for possession while fighting supply targets the market's harmful end. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that decriminalisation without legal supply left the illicit market — and its harms — intact.
- **Keep criminal, enforce hard** — Reasons: drugs destroy families and neighbourhoods, and law expresses society's refusal to normalise that destruction; enforcement raises price and stigma, both of which suppress use. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that hard enforcement increased deaths (through unsafe supply) without reducing use.

#### Crime
- **Crack down** — Reasons: crime is committed by a small number of repeat offenders, and visible enforcement, prosecution and incapacitation reduce it quickly — order is the first service owed to law-abiding, usually poorer, citizens. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that crackdowns displaced rather than reduced crime while damaging community trust.
- **Prevent — root causes** — Reasons: crime concentrates where jobs, schooling, housing and family stability have collapsed, so prevention is the only strategy that reduces the supply of offenders rather than processing them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that root-cause programmes failed to reduce offending even when well funded and given time.

### 3.7 Education & Culture

#### School Choice
- **Universal choice & vouchers** — Reasons: parents, not districts, should direct the public money for their child's education, and competition forces every school to serve families or lose them; a poor child should not be assigned to a failing school by address. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that universal choice mainly subsidised families already in private schools while draining public ones.
- **Charter schools** — Reasons: publicly funded, independently run schools combine public accountability for results with freedom from district bureaucracy, and successful models can be replicated. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that charter sectors as a whole performed no better while creaming easier students.
- **Invest in public schools** — Reasons: public schools are the common institution that educates everyone, and diverting funds to private alternatives abandons the children left in them; improvement comes from investment, not exit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that sustained investment without structural change failed to improve outcomes.

#### Curriculum
- **Honest, inclusive curriculum** — Reasons: a curriculum that omits slavery, dispossession and excluded voices teaches a false country; students trusted with the full record become citizens, not myths' consumers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that inclusive curricula displaced core literacy and numeracy rather than enriching them.
- **Fundamentals, honestly taught** — Reasons: reading, writing, mathematics and honest history are the school's non-negotiable core, and both propaganda and omission betray it; mastery of fundamentals is the equity issue. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a fundamentals focus was being used as cover to exclude contested history.
- **Fundamentals & patriotic civics** — Reasons: schools exist also to form citizens who know and value their country's institutions and story; without shared civic loyalty, the polity itself dissolves. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that patriotic requirements became compelled orthodoxy suppressing honest history.

#### Federal role in education
- **Active federal role** — Reasons: local funding ties school quality to local wealth, and only Washington can equalise opportunity and protect students' civil rights against local majorities. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that federal involvement raised costs and paperwork without narrowing opportunity gaps.
- **Shrink the Department** — Reasons: education is a state and local function the federal department has grown into by funding leverage; a smaller department returning functions would restore accountability to communities. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that shrinkage mainly removed civil-rights enforcement and data the public needs.
- **Close the Department** — Reasons: the Department of Education (created 1979) is a constitutional trespasser — education is nowhere a federal power — and its budget passes through bureaucracy before reaching a classroom. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that states could not or would not protect equal educational opportunity without it.

#### College Affordability
- **Free college & debt cancellation** — Reasons: a degree has become a job requirement while its price tripled, indebting a generation for what earlier generations got cheaply; public college was once near-free and can be again. Statistic: US student loan debt stood at about $1.77 trillion in 2024 (Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that free college mainly subsidised the affluent or inflated prices further.
- **Cheaper, not free** — Reasons: costs should fall through funding, completion and price discipline, and targeted relief should fix real hardship — without writing blank cheques to universities or cancelling debts owed by high earners. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that incremental cost measures had repeatedly failed against tuition inflation.
- **Market-priced, no forgiveness** — Reasons: loans are contracts voluntarily taken for an asset that raises lifetime earnings, and forgiveness taxes non-graduates to relieve graduates; price signals discipline bloated institutions. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the loan system had become predatory in structure — debts that repayment under contracted terms could not retire.

#### Arts Funding
- **Fund them** — Reasons: arts and humanities are public goods markets underproduce — regional theatre, archives, public media — and small public sums leverage large private and civic value. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that public arts money flowed mainly to elite metropolitan audiences.
- **Modest public funding** — Reasons: a small public stake preserves national collections, broadcasting and access schemes without making the state the arts' patron-in-chief. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that modest funding was either a foothold for politicised content control or too small to matter.
- **Leave to private support** — Reasons: art funded by the state is art answerable to the state, and America's philanthropic and market arts ecology is the world's richest without ministries of culture. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that private support left whole regions and art forms without any provision.

#### English as official language
- **Official** — Reasons: a shared public language is the medium of common citizenship, and designating English official encourages acquisition while simplifying government; immigrants themselves benefit most from English fluency. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that official designation mainly served to deny services rather than promote English.
- **Not official** — Reasons: the US has thrived without an official language, English needs no legal protection, and designation is a symbolic exclusion of citizens who speak other languages. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that absence of designation fragmented public services or civic life.
- **Official with accommodation** — Reasons: designating English as the language of record while guaranteeing translation where rights and safety are at stake combines a common civic language with practical fairness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that accommodation swallowed the designation, or that the designation swallowed accommodation.


### 3.8 Foreign Policy & Defense

#### Rodrik's Trilemma
- **Hyperglobalization** — Reasons: deep global integration has lifted hundreds of millions from poverty and binds nations in mutual dependence; where it conflicts with national policy, integration's gains are worth the constraint. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that hyperglobalisation's gains accrued so narrowly that democratic consent for it collapsed.
- **National Sovereignty** — Reasons: a people who cannot set their own economic rules are governed from outside whatever their elections say; sovereignty is the container democracy needs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that sovereignty-first economies stagnated and their citizens demanded integration anyway.
- **Democratic Politics** — Reasons: when global rules and democratic choices conflict, democracy must win, because legitimacy cannot be outsourced; markets and treaties are instruments, not masters. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that democratic majorities, unconstrained, chose policies destroying the prosperity integration provided.

#### Grand Strategy
- **Realpolitik / Hegemony** — Reasons: international politics is a contest of power without a referee, and a preponderant America deters challengers and keeps sea lanes and order; sentiment does not stop armies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that hegemony provoked balancing coalitions and overextension costing more than primacy bought.
- **Liberal Internationalism** — Reasons: rules, alliances and institutions multiply US power and make it legitimate, and democracies allied together have kept a long peace among themselves. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that institutions constrained the US while its rivals ignored them freely.
- **Civilizational Realignment** — Reasons: the coming contests are between civilisational blocs with incompatible values, and strategy should consolidate one's own bloc — its culture, demography and industry — rather than chase universal rules. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that bloc thinking created the very civilisational conflict it predicted.
- **Anti-Imperialist Solidarity** — Reasons: US power has repeatedly overthrown governments, armed dictators and extracted wealth, and solidarity with the peoples resisting empire is both justice and the only honest foreign policy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that anti-imperialist alignments mainly delivered peoples to rival empires.
- **Strategic Neo-Isolationism** — Reasons: America is a continental, resource-rich power separated by oceans, and most foreign entanglements have cost blood and treasure for other nations' quarrels; restraint is strength husbanded. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that withdrawal created vacuums filled by hostile powers at greater eventual cost.
- **Cosmopolitan Borderless Globalism** — Reasons: humanity's problems — climate, pandemics, poverty — are planetary, and loyalty to humanity outranks loyalty to one state; borders are administrative conveniences, not moral boundaries. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that cosmopolitan institutions lacked any democratic accountability to the people they governed.

#### Civilizational Resilience
- **Energy Density & Grid Security** — Reasons: civilisations run on energy, and dense, reliable power plus a hardened grid underwrite everything else in a crisis; blackouts cascade into every system. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that energy systems were already resilient and other foundations were the binding weakness.
- **Food & Water Sovereignty** — Reasons: a society that cannot feed itself or secure its water is hostage in any blockade, drought or war; staple self-sufficiency is the oldest resilience. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that trade-diversified food systems proved more resilient than self-sufficiency drives.
- **Demographic Vitality & Family Support** — Reasons: below-replacement fertility hollows out workforces, armies, tax bases and cultures within two generations; supporting families is long-horizon national survival. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that automation and immigration durably replaced demographic decline without social cost.
- **Technological Innovation & Industrial Capacity** — Reasons: the capacity to invent and manufacture at scale decides wars, pandemics and economic shocks alike; a service economy without industry is brittle. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that industrial capacity no longer translated into resilience in modern crises.
- **Ecological Stability & Planetary Boundaries** — Reasons: soil, climate, oceans and biodiversity are the substrate every other system sits on, and crossing planetary boundaries produces civilisational shocks no army can repel. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that ecological systems were recovering or that technological substitutes covered their functions.
- **Social Trust & Civic Cohesion** — Reasons: in genuine crises — war, pandemic, disaster — what holds is whether citizens trust each other and their institutions enough to cooperate; cohesion is the multiplier on every other asset. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that low-trust societies performed equally in crises through coercion or wealth.
- **Strategic Deterrence & Institutional Defense** — Reasons: resilience requires enemies to calculate that attack fails — credible deterrence and institutions able to withstand subversion, disinformation and lawfare. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that deterrence spending provoked the threats it aimed to prevent.

#### Defense Spending
- **Bigger, stronger military** — Reasons: deterrence fails when rivals perceive weakness, and the US faces peer and near-peer challengers simultaneously for the first time in a generation; strength is cheaper than the wars weakness invites. Statistic: US military expenditure was $997 billion in 2024, the world's largest (Stockholm International Peace Research Institute, 2025). What would move a holder: evidence that added spending bought little capability — absorbed by cost inflation and contractor overhead.
- **Hold the line** — Reasons: current spending already dwarfs any rival's, and the task is reforming procurement and strategy within a stable budget, not feeding an unaudited growth claim. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence of a specific, funded capability gap a rival was exploiting.
- **Cut the defense budget** — Reasons: the US outspends the next several militaries combined while domestic needs go unmet, and a vast military invites its use; cutting forces strategy to prioritise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that cuts produced deterrence failures or forced costlier later rebuilds.

#### Compelled service
- **Keep the draft** — Reasons: a citizen army distributes war's burden across classes, restrains leaders who must conscript voters' children, and supplies mass in a true emergency. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that modern war's skill demands made conscript mass militarily useless.
- **Dormant draft** — Reasons: keeping registration and a standby mechanism is cheap insurance for existential war, while an all-volunteer force serves peacetime needs; abolishing the machinery in calm weather is the risk. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that standby systems could not actually be activated fairly or in time.
- **Abolish it** — Reasons: conscription is involuntary servitude, and a free country should defend itself with volunteers it pays properly or not at all; the draft's history is one of class-biased evasion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a genuine existential threat a volunteer force demonstrably could not meet.

#### Veterans
- **Veterans** — Reasons: those who served — especially the wounded, in body or mind — hold a first claim on the society they defended; care, benefits, housing and honour for veterans are a debt, not a programme to be trimmed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: this pole has no opposing pole in the skin (see §5); within it, holders are moved by evidence about delivery — whether public or contracted care actually reaches veterans in time.

#### Nuclear Weapons
- **Modernize the deterrent** — Reasons: the arsenal's delivery systems date to the Cold War, and deterrence depends on adversaries believing weapons will work; a decaying deterrent invites miscalculation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that modernisation triggered an arms race making all sides less secure.
- **Minimum deterrent** — Reasons: a small, survivable force able to retaliate is enough to deter, at a fraction of the cost and risk of a war-fighting arsenal; more warheads buy no more security. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that adversaries' missile defences or first-strike capacity could neutralise a minimum force.
- **Disarm** — Reasons: nuclear weapons threaten civilisation's existence, their use would be mass murder, and every year of possession is a year of accident and miscalculation risk; someone must move first toward abolition. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unilateral disarmament invited coercion or attack by remaining nuclear powers.

#### Alliances
- **Strengthen alliances** — Reasons: alliances multiply US power, deter aggression collectively and have kept great-power peace for decades; 23 of NATO's 32 allies met the 2%-of-GDP defence spending guideline in 2024 (NATO, 2024). Statistic: see reasons (NATO, 2024). What would move a holder: evidence that allies had become permanent free-riders whose defence the US subsidised without return.
- **Alliances with limits** — Reasons: alliances are instruments, not sacraments — worth keeping where interests genuinely overlap, with real burden-sharing and no blank cheques for allies' own quarrels. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that conditional commitment destroyed the deterrence alliances exist to provide.
- **Avoid entangling alliances** — Reasons: permanent alliances drag nations into wars over interests not their own — the founders' warning — and allies who expect rescue take risks they would otherwise avoid. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a world without US alliances produced more, larger wars reaching America anyway.

#### China
- **Managed competition** — Reasons: China is a permanent peer, so rivalry must be competed hard inside guardrails that prevent miscalculation; neither rupture nor accommodation is available at acceptable cost. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that guardrails were exploited to prepare aggression while the US restrained itself.
- **Compete without decoupling** — Reasons: broad trade with China benefits US consumers and firms and gives both sides a stake in peace, while competition in technology and security proceeds; decoupling would injure the US economy severely. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that continued trade materially funded military capabilities aimed at the US.
- **Confrontation & decoupling** — Reasons: China is an adversary building power to displace the US and coerce its neighbours, and interdependence is a weapon it will use; confrontation and separation now are cheaper than later. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that confrontation accelerated China's self-reliance and coalition-building more than it constrained it.

#### The Middle East
- **Stand with Israel** — Reasons: Israel is a democratic ally in a hostile region, a partner in intelligence and technology, and a people whose state arose from genocide; abandoning allies teaches every partner that US commitments expire. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unconditional support damaged US interests and Israel's own security more than conditionality would.
- **Palestinian self-determination** — Reasons: Palestinians are owed the same national self-determination claimed for everyone else, and permanent occupation without citizenship or statehood is unjust and a perpetual source of war. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a Palestinian state under foreseeable leadership would become a platform for attacks rather than a settlement.

#### Foreign Aid
- **Foreign aid** — Reasons: aid buys health, stability and goodwill at a small fraction of the cost of the wars and crises it prevents, and wealthy nations owe assistance to people in catastrophe. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that aid mainly enriched corrupt intermediaries without reaching its stated purposes.
- **Military intervention** — Reasons: some evils — genocide, aggression, terrorism with sanctuary — are stopped only by force, and a power that can intervene and refuses owns the consequences of refusal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that interventions reliably produced outcomes worse than the evils they answered.

#### Iran
- **Confront and pressure** — Reasons: Iran's regime pursues nuclear capability, missiles and proxy wars, and responds to pressure, not goodwill; sanctions and credible force are the levers that have ever slowed it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that maximum pressure accelerated the nuclear programme and empowered hardliners.
- **Pressure with an open channel** — Reasons: pressure creates the leverage, but only a kept-open channel converts leverage into agreement and prevents miscalculation; pure confrontation leaves no exit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the open channel was used to stall while the programme advanced.
- **Engage and negotiate** — Reasons: negotiation produced the only verifiable limits ever placed on Iran's nuclear programme, and engagement strengthens Iranian factions favouring normal relations; isolation has a forty-year record of failure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that negotiated limits were systematically cheated while sanctions relief funded regional aggression.

#### International institutions
- **Full participation in the UN, NATO, and WTO** — Reasons: these institutions are the operating system of a rules-based order the US built and benefits from, and leaving rooms does not empty them — rivals fill them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the institutions had become net constraints on US action while rivals used them freely.
- **Participate, with limits and burden-sharing** — Reasons: membership is valuable and its costs and vetoes are real; staying while demanding reform, funding discipline and fair shares gets the benefits without the blank cheque. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that conditional participation forfeited the influence that made membership worthwhile.
- **Withdraw from them** — Reasons: these bodies bind US sovereignty to the votes of states that do not share its interests, and fund bureaucracies hostile to it; a sovereign nation can cooperate case by case without membership. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that withdrawal left the US excluded from rules it still had to obey in practice.

#### Military aid to allies
- **Provide military aid freely** — Reasons: arming partners lets them defend themselves without US troops, deters common enemies, and binds allies to US systems and strategy; hesitation in a crisis costs more than generosity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that freely provided arms were turned on civilians, resold, or used for wars the US opposed.
- **Condition military aid on human rights and conduct** — Reasons: arms carry the giver's responsibility, and unconditional supply implicates the US in partners' abuses while removing its best leverage over their conduct. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that conditions pushed partners to rival suppliers without improving their conduct.
- **End military aid** — Reasons: arms transfers fuel arms races and wars by proxy, and 'aid' that is really weapons exports makes the US armourer to conflicts worldwide; ending it is the only clean hand. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that ending aid left partners to be conquered by aggressors the aid would have deterred.


## 4. Archetype research

All 38 samples in the skin have kind "archetype": five party-platform stacks (current plus dated layers, 2000–2024) and four ideal types. Archetype ids covered in this section, in skin order: pol-democrat, pol-democrat-2020, pol-democrat-2016, pol-democrat-2012, pol-democrat-2008, pol-democrat-2004, pol-democrat-2000, pol-republican, pol-republican-2020, pol-republican-2016, pol-republican-2012, pol-republican-2008, pol-republican-2004, pol-republican-2000, pol-libertarian, pol-libertarian-2020, pol-libertarian-2016, pol-libertarian-2012, pol-libertarian-2008, pol-libertarian-2004, pol-libertarian-2000, pol-green, pol-green-2020, pol-green-2016, pol-green-2010, pol-green-2004, pol-green-2000, pol-constitution, pol-constitution-2020, pol-constitution-2016, pol-constitution-2012, pol-constitution-2008, pol-constitution-2004, pol-constitution-2000, pol-classical-liberal, pol-social-democrat, pol-communitarian-populist, pol-revolutionary-socialist. Ratings in the skin rate advocacy per pole and openness per topic; the receptivity and intensity discussed below are the derived quantities (see §1), described in words, not re-rated here.

### Democratic Party platform — `pol-democrat.svg`
The 2024 Democratic platform: active federal government growing the economy "from the middle out and bottom up." Why: it holds the §3 State and Keynesian reasons on economic structure and macro policy, the guaranteed-coverage reasons on health, urgent-action reasons on climate, and rights-expansion reasons on abortion, voting and civil rights — public power as the counterweight to market inequality. Receptive to: Commons and social-democratic positions, targeted versions of its own programmes, and market mechanisms offered as instruments of public goals (§3, Economic Structure; Health insurance). Closed to: phase-outs of Social Security and Medicare, no-federal-floor labour positions, and no-government-action climate positions, because its whole case is that public action works (§3, Social Security; Climate Change). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-democrat.svg

### Republican Party platform — `pol-republican.svg`
The 2024 Republican platform ("Make America Great Again"): lower taxes, deregulation, energy dominance, sealed border and large-scale deportation, gun and religious-liberty protections, protecting Social Security and Medicare, peace through strength, abortion left largely to the states. Why: it holds the §3 Market reasons on structure, lower-tax and deregulation reasons, drill-and-expand energy reasons, hard-enforcement immigration reasons, and deterrence reasons on defence. Receptive to: state-level variation (Leave it to the states), market instruments inside public programmes, and burden-sharing versions of alliances (§3, Abortion; Alliances). Closed to: higher taxes on the wealthy, phase-out fossil-fuel positions, and path-to-citizenship legalisation, because each contradicts a load-bearing plank (§3, Income tax rates; Immigration legalization). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-republican.svg

### Libertarian Party platform — `pol-libertarian.svg`
The Libertarian platform: self-ownership, sharply limited government, free markets, strong civil liberties, non-intervention abroad. Why: it generalises the §3 Market, minimal-government, no-government-censorship, abolish-the-Fed and avoid-entangling-alliances reasons into one principle — no forcible interference with equal rights. Receptive to: any pole that removes a compulsion, including left poles on drugs, speech, privacy and war (§3, Drug Policy; Privacy; Grand Strategy). Closed to: mandates, guarantees and industrial policy from either major party, because the instrument, not the goal, is the objection (§3, Vaccines; Manufacturing). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-libertarian.svg

### Green Party platform — `pol-green.svg`
The Green platform, built on ecological wisdom, social justice, grassroots democracy and nonviolence: rapid climate action, universal health care, economic equality, decentralised participatory democracy. Why: it holds the §3 urgent-action, phase-out, guaranteed-coverage and reduce-inequality reasons, and adds the ecological-stability reasons of Civilizational Resilience as a first principle. Receptive to: Commons ownership, democratic-socialist and post-growth positions (§3, Who owns what; Growth vs. Limits). Closed to: drill-and-expand energy, bigger-military spending and market-outcome distribution, each of which denies a pillar (§3, Fossil fuels; Defense Spending). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-green.svg

### Constitution Party platform — `pol-constitution.svg`
The Constitution Party: strict original-intent constitutionalism, federal power limited to express authorisations, traditional moral values including protection of life from conception, non-intervention and American sovereignty. Why: it holds the §3 powers-to-the-states, apply-the-law-as-written, pro-life, abolish/constrain-the-Fed and withdraw-from-institutions reasons, grounded in the constitutional text as a binding compact. Receptive to: state-level decision-making, originalist judging, and restraint abroad (§3, Levels of government; Alliances). Closed to: active-federal-government and full-participation-in-institutions positions, and any reading of the Constitution as a living grant of new federal powers (§3, Government Size; International institutions). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-constitution.svg

### The Classical Liberal — `pol-classical-liberal.svg`
An ideal type, not a platform: distrust of concentrated power in boardrooms and ministries alike; constitutional rules, open markets, robust civil liberties. Why: the §3 social-contract legitimacy, counter-majoritarian-checks, market and strong-privacy reasons — power is the problem, whoever holds it. Receptive to: smarter regulation, disclosure-based campaign finance, and commons arrangements that are genuinely voluntary (§3, Regulation; Campaign finance). Closed to: both state command and corporate capture — it resists poles that concentrate power even for ends it shares (§3, Economic Structure). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-classical-liberal.svg

### The Social Democrat — `pol-social-democrat.svg`
An ideal type: democratic politics as the counterweight to market inequality, universal public goods as the substance of real freedom for working people. Why: the §3 need-based justice, guaranteed-coverage, strong-safety-net and protect-and-expand reasons (§3, Theories of Distributive Justice; Welfare). Receptive to: market instruments that demonstrably serve universal goals, and Green ecological priorities (§3, Renewable energy). Closed to: market-outcome distribution and phase-outs of universal programmes, because universality is the point (§3, Wealth Distribution). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-social-democrat.svg

### The Communitarian Populist — `pol-communitarian-populist.svg`
An ideal type: national solidarity, family stability, local roots and economic protection for domestic workers over global efficiency and abstract autonomy. Why: the §3 protective-tariff, communitarian-localism, substantive-moral-formation and demographic-vitality reasons (§3, Trade Policy; Civilizational Resilience). Receptive to: state economic action that protects domestic workers and families, including from the left (§3, Manufacturing). Closed to: hyperglobalisation, cosmopolitan globalism and government-out positions on family and community questions (§3, Rodrik's Trilemma; Marriage & Family). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-communitarian-populist.svg

### The Revolutionary Socialist — `pol-revolutionary-socialist.svg`
An ideal type: structural class struggle, public ownership of the commanding heights, planned redistribution, anti-imperialist internationalism; rejects laissez-faire and liberal reformism alike. Why: the §3 State (command), need-based justice, state-owned and revolutionary-vanguard reasons, taken as a system rather than a programme (§3, Economic Structure; Seven Theories of Political Legitimacy). Receptive to: social-democratic measures only as transitions, and anti-imperialist positions from any quarter (§3, Grand Strategy). Closed to: market poles in every form and to reformist accommodation, which it reads as stabilising the system it aims to replace (§3, Macro Political Matrix Hexad). Emblem: images/emblems/pol-revolutionary-socialist.svg

## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype.** The map's poles are far wider than its 38 archetypes. No archetype primarily embodies: MMT or austerity as a macro identity; CBDC or free-banking monetary positions; Ostromian commons ownership as a whole politics (the Classical Liberal and Greens touch it only); epistocracy / technocratic meritocracy as a regime (no party platform holds it); anarchist voluntaryism in either direction (Libertarians approach, but keep a state); secession; post-growth sufficiency as a governing programme (Greens approach it); or a pro-choice Republican / pro-life Democratic cross-pressure profile, the two combinations real electorates contain in large numbers.

**Thin topics.** Four topics carry a single pole — Disability rights (xpo0077), Women's equality (xpo0078), Tribal nations (xpo0079), Veterans (xpo0087) — so they register conviction but no disagreement; the indaba can list backing for the sole pole (done in §3) but cannot locate a meeting point. Two topics carry two unlike poles that are not opposites: Electoral Systems (abolish the College vs. proportional representation — two different reforms), Foreign Aid (aid vs. military intervention — two different instruments), and The Middle East (two positions that are not exhaustive of the space, e.g. a two-state negotiated settlement as such is not a pole). Supreme Court likewise carries two reform poles and no status-quo pole.

**Skin-JSON problems (reported, not fixed).** (1) Eight topics have an empty `question` field: Growth vs. Limits (xpo0097), Administrative State (xpo0094), Constitutional Design (xpo0095), Term limits for Congress (xpo0107), Net neutrality (xpo0109), Neutrality vs. Common Good (xpo0096), Federal funding of abortion (xpo0110), English as official language (xpo0108) — §2 above now includes the recovered question for each (see §5.1), taken verbatim from that topic's hovertip issue; the skin JSON itself is unchanged. (2) Federal funding of abortion's third pole, Shifted over time, is descriptive (a holder who moved), not a position a holder advocates in the same sense as Allowed/Banned. (3) Display labels appear truncated for Border Enforcement's second pole and Legal Immigration's merit pole; §2 relies on their keys. (4) Green dated layers skip 2008 and 2012 while other stacks include them. (5) Every sample is kind "archetype," including dated party platforms that the repo's house guidance treats as real documents/samples — flagged here as the skin's own classification choice, not changed.

**Deliberately left out of this research.** Candidate personalities and election forecasting (the skin maps positions, not persons or odds); state-level politics except where a pole is explicitly about federalism; and any statistic not listed in §6 — where §3 could not ground a number in a source the author could name with confidence, it says so rather than estimating.
