# Dharma — Research

Skin: Dharmacapades (`dharmacapades.json`) · Domain: Eastern Religions — Dharmic & Taoic Traditions · Meter: practice
Coverage: 43 topics, 147 poles, 11 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Dharmacapades maps the Eastern religions of the Dharmic and Taoic worlds — Buddhism in its Theravada, Zen, Pure Land and Tibetan forms, Hindu traditions from Advaita Vedanta to Vaishnava bhakti, Jainism, Sikh Gurmat, classical Confucianism, philosophical Daoism, and the Cārvāka materialist school that argues with all of them. The domain is how a person walks the path: what karma and samsara are taken to be, what liberation is and who it is for, which practices carry weight, what the divine is, how one should act, and where one stands in community and lineage.

The meter word is **practice**. What is measured is not assent to a creed but the shape of a lived way — which practices, disciplines and devotions a profile actually keeps, and with what understanding. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's questions: advocacy for a pole of that question, where the poles are the real positions the traditions themselves hold — literal against psychological karma, self-power against other-power, sudden against gradual awakening. What does not count as a position is mere affiliation (a census label with no practice behind it) or a scholarly fact about a tradition (a date, a dynasty); the skin rates the walk, not the label or the trivia.

**Rating contract.** The inputs a profile supplies are **advocacy per pole** (a signed number, −100 to +100, for each pole of each topic; positive is for the pole, negative is against it) and **openness per topic** (0–100, how willing the profile is to hear other positions on that topic). Everything else is derived, never rated directly: **receptivity** is `round-half-even(openness × advocacy)`, computed with exact fractions rather than floating-point arithmetic, and **intensity** is the signed largest-|value| pole — the strongest pole, carrying its sign. Aversion therefore lives only in a rating's sign; it is never encoded anywhere else, and no sign is ever flipped or folded. On xy-switchbox topics the two poles are crossed, independent yes/no axes — a holder may affirm either, both, or neither — not opposite ends of one scale, and the both-negative position is the status-quo middle.

The skin's stance is respectful parity: every tradition on the map, including the materialist school that rejects the others' metaphysics, is stated in its adherents' own strongest terms, and no tradition is treated as the yardstick for the rest. The Scribe for this skin is an **indaba** — a meeting of the recorded ways that works toward consensus by naming where paths meet, where they part, and what would move each holder — and the research below is written to be lifted into that meeting: reasons and numbers per position, and, for each archetype, what it can hear and what it cannot.

## 2. Topic rationale

### Dharma & The Way

**Karma** — The load-bearing doctrine of every tradition on the map — what action does to the actor, across this life or beyond it. Literal: Karma is a real mechanism of rebirth; actions literally determine your next life. Psychological: Karma is an ethical principle; actions shape character and consequences in this life. No Karma: Karma isn't real; actions carry no metaphysical weight.

**Samsara** — What one does about the wheel itself — leave it, serve within it, or live well on it. Escape: Samsara is suffering; the point is to get out. Engage: Stay in the cycle to help others (the bodhisattva vow). Accept: Samsara is fine; live well within it.

**Buddhism** — What kind of thing Buddhism is taken to be, which quietly decides what practicing it means. Religion: Devotion, ritual, sangha, rebirth; Buddhism is a religion. Philosophy: Reason, analysis, ethics; no god needed. Way of Life: Practice, meditation, living well; neither religion nor philosophy.

**The Four Purusharthas** — The Hindu map of a whole life — duty, prosperity, pleasure and liberation held in one frame. Dharma: Moral order, righteousness, spiritual duty. Artha: Material prosperity, wealth, security. Kama: Sensual pleasure, erotic desire, love. Moksha: Spiritual liberation, release from samsara.

**The Three Teachings / San Jiao** — The classical Chinese settlement — three teachings, one civilisation, and the practitioner's own balance among them. Confucianism: Social harmony, filial piety, moral duty, ritual propriety. Taoism: Natural flow, spontaneity, simplicity, harmony with the Dao. Buddhism: Emptiness, compassion, liberation from suffering.

**Archetypal energies** — The eight trigrams as a temperament map — which energies a practitioner cultivates and balances. Heaven (Qian): Creative, strong, initiating. Lake (Dui): Joyful, open, receptive. Fire (Li): Clinging, bright, illuminating. Thunder (Zhen): Arousing, moving, shocking. Wind (Xun): Gentle, penetrating, dispersing. Water (Kan): Abysmal, dangerous, flowing. Mountain (Gen): Still, keeping, resting. Earth (Kun): Receptive, yielding, nourishing.

**The Three Gunas** — The Bhagavad Gita's three strands of nature, read as temperament and as a diagnosis of practice. Sattva: Purity, clarity, harmony, wisdom. Rajas: Passion, activity, restlessness, drive. Tamas: Inertia, darkness, ignorance, heaviness.

**The Five Mahabhutas** — The five great elements as body, practice and cosmos at once — the practitioner's working element. Earth (Prithvi): Solidity, stability, form; the sense of smell. Water (Apas): Fluidity, cohesion, adaptability; the sense of taste. Fire (Tejas): Transformation, energy, digestion; the sense of sight. Air (Vayu): Movement, breath, circulation; the sense of touch. Ether (Akasha): Space, the medium of all; the sense of hearing.

**Scripture and authority** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “What is scripture — a living Guru, a provisional guide, revealed truth, or a layered authority?” What a text is — Guru, guide, revelation or layered authority — the question underneath every appeal to scripture. Living Guru: The book itself is the Guru, enthroned and alive. Provisional (test it): Scripture is a guide to be tested, not believed on arrival. Revealed (heard truth): Scripture is truth that was heard, not composed. Authoritative but layered: Scripture binds, but in layers, with commentary and difference.

**Can the truth be spoken?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Can the ultimate truth be said at all — or is it unspeakable, layered, or many-sided?” Whether the ultimate can be said — the apophatic question that divides the map's mystics from its dogmatists. Speakable: The truth can be said, though it may be hard. Unspeakable: What can be told is not the ultimate truth. Layered: Different truths are spoken for different levels. Many-sided: No single statement captures the truth.

### Liberation & Enlightenment

**Liberation** — What the end of the path actually is — release, cessation, or return to the Way. Moksha: Hindu liberation; union with Brahman, the self realized. Nirvana: Buddhist cessation; extinction of craving, the end of suffering. Union with the Dao: Taoist return; becoming one with the Way.

**Enlightenment** — The tempo of awakening — one moment, or a lifetime of cultivation. Sudden: Instant awakening, Zen satori. Sudden awakening, gradual cultivation stands for the position that the synthesis many schools actually live: insight breaks in suddenly, habits dissolve gradually, as the sun rises at once and melts the frost by degrees. Gradual: Slow cultivation, step by step.

**Selfhood** — The map's deepest metaphysical divide — whether there is a self to liberate. Atman: Eternal self, Hindu. Pudgalavada Person: The Buddhist Pudgalavada 'person' — a real entity neither identical to nor different from the aggregates, the inexpressible bearer of karma. Anatta: No-self, Buddhist.

**Innate Purity vs. Gradual Purification** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Are beings already fundamentally awakened beneath obscuring clouds, or weighed down by real karmic stains that must be burned away?” A switchbox of two crossed claims — whether awakening uncovers an innate purity, and whether the soul bears real stains to cleanse; the axes are independent (see §5). Original Buddha-Nature / Inner Light: Awakening is uncovering the luminous perfection already whole inside every being right now. Defiled Soul Requiring Cleansing: Treating liberation as already accomplished breeds complacency; heavy karmic residue requires patient, strenuous purification.

**Self-Power vs. Other-Power** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Does liberation rest on the practitioner's own disciplined exertion, or on total trust in divine grace and compassionate vows?” A switchbox of two crossed claims — whether liberation is won by one's own effort, and whether it is received by surrender to grace; the axes are independent (see §5). Self-Reliant Effort (Jiriki): Nobody else can walk the path for you; diligent mindfulness, study, and restraint cut the roots of delusion. Surrender to Grace (Tariki): Relying on egoic willpower only strengthens pride; humble surrender opens the heart to saving grace.

**Liberation for whom?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Who is liberation for — all beings, you alone, your soul, or the whole community?” The scope of liberation — whose release the path is for. All beings: Liberation is vowed for every being, not only for oneself. The individual: Liberation is a person's own release. The soul: What is freed is the soul, from the matter that binds it. The community: Liberation is walked together, in the assembly.

**Liberation in this life or beyond it?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is liberation for this life, for a life beyond, at the end of a long path, or both?” When liberation lands — in this body, after death, or at a long path's end. In this life: Awakening is for now, in this body. Beyond this life: Release comes after death, not before. At the end of the path: Liberation comes after a long purification, life after life. Both: Union is possible now, and it continues after death.

### Practice & Discipline

**Yoga: Five Dimensions** — The five dimensions of yoga as actually practised — body, breath, mind, heart and understanding. Physical: Asana, the body, posture and exercise. Breath: Pranayama, the life-force, breathing practice. Meditative: Dhyana, stillness, concentration of the mind. Devotional: Bhakti, surrender, the heart. Philosophical: Jnana, study, understanding the teachings.

**Meditation** — The two classical aims of meditation — calm the mind, or see through it. Samatha: Concentration, calm. Vipassana: Insight, seeing clearly.

**Tantra** — Tantra's two hands — transgression enacted, internalised, or refused. Left-hand: Transgressive, embraces the forbidden. Internalized transgression: The forbidden is crossed in visualization and symbol, not in outward act. Right-hand: Orthodox, symbolic.

**Kundalini** — The coiled energy at the spine's base — awakening's engine or its hazard. Awakening: The coiled energy rising to liberation; central, transformative. Danger: Destabilizing, risky; something to approach with caution or avoid.

**The Noble Eightfold Path** — The Noble Eightfold Path limb by limb — the practitioner's own weighting of the Buddha's complete programme. Right View: Seeing things as they are (Wisdom). Right Intention: Resolve, renunciation, goodwill (Wisdom). Right Speech: Truthful, kind, useful speech (Ethics). Right Action: Acting without harm (Ethics). Right Livelihood: Earning a living without harm (Ethics). Right Effort: Cultivating wholesome states, abandoning unwholesome ones (Meditation). Right Mindfulness: Clear awareness of body, feeling, mind (Meditation). Right Concentration: Deep absorption, jhana (Meditation).

**Action** — The Daoist spectrum of action — from effortless alignment to deliberate intervention. Wu Wei: Effortless action, natural alignment, non-forcing. Act when needed: Striving laid down; action taken when the situation calls for it. You Wei: Deliberate striving, active intervention, intentional control.

**The Six Classical Yogas** — The six classical yogas as complete paths — which door the practitioner actually enters. Raja Yoga: The royal path: meditation, the eight limbs of Patanjali. Jnana Yoga: The path of knowledge: wisdom, self-inquiry. Bhakti Yoga: The path of devotion: love, surrender, worship. Karma Yoga: The path of action: selfless service without attachment to results. Hatha Yoga: The path of force: postures, breath, physical discipline. Tantra Yoga: The path of weaving: energy, ritual, mantra, transgression.

**Ritual & Ancestral Efficacy** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Do precise mantras, offerings, and ancestral rites carry real cosmic power, or is inner ethical clarity all that counts?” A switchbox of two crossed claims — whether rite and ancestral duty carry real power, and whether inner intention is what finally counts; the axes are independent (see §5). Sacred Rite & Ancestral Duty: Reverent ritual harmonizes visible and invisible worlds and honors the debt owed to forebears. Interior Ethical Intention: Outward ceremonies without pure motive and active kindness are empty shells.

**Somatic & Yogic Subtle Body** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Are breath control, posture, and subtle energy channels vital levers of awakening, or secondary distractions from wisdom?” A switchbox of two crossed claims — whether the subtle body is a vital lever of awakening, and whether inquiry alone is the path; the axes are independent (see §5). Asana, Pranayama & Kundalini: Body, breath, and mind form one continuum; refining prana unlocks states pure thought never reaches. Purely Mental & Ethical Inquiry: Chasing energetic experiences or physical feats can inflate subtle pride instead of uprooting greed and delusion.

**The body** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “What do you do with the body — mortify it, cultivate it, accept it, or honour it?” What the path does with the body — the ascetic, the cultivator, the middle way and the honouring of the gift. Mortify: Wear the body down. Austerity burns off what binds the soul. Cultivate: Train the body. It is a vehicle for the path. Accept (middle way): Neither punish the body nor indulge it. Honour: The body is a gift. Do not harm it or flee it.

### Deity & The Divine

**The Divine: Four Quadrants** — The four quadrants of the divine — personal, impersonal, none, and nothing. Personal Theism: Gods exist and are personal; devotional Hinduism, bhakti, prayer. Impersonal Theism: The divine exists but is impersonal; Brahman as principle. Non-theistic Spirituality: No gods, but spiritual reality; Buddhism, Jainism. Atheism / Materialism: No gods, no spiritual reality; Carvaka, secular materialism.

**Brahman** — How the ultimate is conceived in the Hindu frame — with attributes, without, or both at once. Saguna: Personal, with attributes. Both: The personal God is the face of the impersonal absolute: with attributes as the appearance of the attributeless. Nirguna: Impersonal, without attributes.

**Buddha** — Who the Buddha is — a man, a cosmic principle, or the nature of every mind. Historical Buddha: Siddhartha, a man who woke up. Cosmic Buddha: Eternal, universal, always present. Buddha-nature: Everyone is Buddha already; awakening is remembering.

### Ethics & Conduct

**Ahimsa: Three Schools** — Three schools of non-violence, differing in absoluteness — the map's sharpest ethical comparison. Jain: Absolute non-violence; no harm to any living being. Buddhist: Contextual non-violence; intention matters, with practical exceptions. Hindu: Dharma-based; non-violence as a virtue with exceptions for duty.

**Precepts** — How binding the precepts are — law, discipline, or compass. Strict rules: The precepts are commandments to follow. Firm but flexible: Precepts held firmly, and read by their spirit when they collide. Flexible guidelines: The precepts are compass points to steer by.

**Two wings** — The bodhisattva's two wings — compassion and wisdom, and which one the practitioner feeds. Karuna: Compassion. Prajna: Wisdom.

**Confucian Social Order vs. Taoist Withdrawal** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Does human flourishing depend on cultivating ritual harmony in society, or stepping back from titles and conventions?” A switchbox of two crossed claims — whether flourishing needs ritual order in society, and whether it needs withdrawal from that order; the axes are independent (see §5). Filial & Civic Propriety (Li): Reverence for elders, reciprocal duties, and moral education humanize power and sustain peace. Rusticated Natural Simplicity: Elaborate status rules breed hypocrisy; living simply close to mountains and streams keeps the spirit whole.

**When is force justified?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “When, if ever, is force justified — never, to defend dharma, only in extremis, or against tyranny?” The hardest ethical case on the map — when, if ever, force may be used. Never: Force is not justified. Harm is the thing to refuse. In defence of dharma: Force is the warrior's duty when order and dharma are at stake. Only in extremis: Force is a last resort, and even then it is regretted. Against tyranny: Force is justified to defend the oppressed from a tyrant.

**Food and non-violence** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “What does non-violence mean for what you eat — strict rules, mindfulness, purity, or sharing?” Non-violence at the table — what ahimsa means for eating. Strictly non-violent: Even tiny lives count: no roots, no eating after dark. Mindful: Eat with care. Vegetarian is preferred, and alms are accepted. Sattvic: Pure food makes a pure mind. Shared: The point is eating together. The menu is not the vow.

**Where does right come from?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Where does right come from — consequences, duty, non-violence, or the Guru's word?” Where rightness itself comes from — the meta-ethical root each tradition plants. Consequences: Right is what does not cause suffering. Karma is the measure. Dharma: Right is duty and the order of things. Non-violence: Ahimsa is the first principle. The rest follows from it. The Guru's word: Right is what the Guru's word and the assembly teach.

### Community & Lineage

**Guru** — Whether the path needs a living teacher — and how much of one. Essential: You need a teacher. Helpful, not essential: A guru helps greatly; the path can still be walked without one. Optional: You can find the way alone.

**Sangha** — Where practice lives — the monastery, the retreat rhythm, or the marketplace. Monastic: Renounce, join the sangha. Retreatant householder: A lay life punctuated by regular retreats. Lay: Practice in the world.

**Tradition** — Fidelity to one lineage against borrowing across them. Orthodox: Follow one lineage strictly. Rooted but borrowing: One home lineage, with practices borrowed from others as needed. Eclectic: Mix traditions freely.

**Way of life** — The ashrama arc — householder, forest-dweller, renouncer — as a life's shape. Grasthi: The active householder; family, honest labor, social responsibility. Vanaprastha: The forest-dweller: halfway out of the world, household ties loosened, not yet renounced. Sannyasa: The ascetic renouncer; monastic isolation, world-denial.

**Caste, Birth & Spiritual Eligibility** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Should access to sacred texts, priesthood, and leadership ignore birth entirely, or preserve traditional hereditary duties?” A switchbox of two crossed claims — whether spiritual eligibility ignores birth entirely, and whether hereditary order has a sacred function; the axes are independent (see §5). Radical Equality of All Seekers: Divine light dwells equally in every human being regardless of birth, gender, or social station. Traditional Varnashrama Order: Inherited roles and life-stage duties preserve specialized sacred learning and social stability across generations.

**Women's spiritual equality** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Can women attain liberation — equally, with restrictions, or does it depend on the sect?” Whether women can attain the goal — the question the traditions answer unevenly. Equal (can attain): Women can attain the goal. The path is not closed to them. Restricted (traditional): Women's spiritual standing is traditionally limited. Sect-dependent: Whether women can attain it depends on which sect is speaking.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 Dharma & The Way

#### Karma
- **Literal** — Reasons: the traditions' own texts teach that intention and deed ripen as rebirth, and practitioners order whole lives by it; a moral universe in which no good or evil act is finally lost is, for its holders, simply what the teachings and the testimony of the awakened report. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration, accepted within the holder's own tradition, that the rebirth teachings were intended only as skilful means and not as fact.
- **Psychological** — Reasons: karma is verifiable where it matters — in this life, acts form character, character forms circumstance, and the teaching's practical force survives translation into psychology; holders prize that it asks for no belief beyond experience. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence from within practice that the psychological reading could not carry the teaching's weight — for instance, that it fails the dying or the wronged innocent.
- **No Karma** — Reasons: no mechanism linking deed to rebirth has ever been observed, and the Cārvāka holder adds that a doctrine explaining fortune by past lives blames the suffering for their suffering; moral responsibility stands firmer without metaphysical bookkeeping. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: reliable evidence of the kind the holder accepts — direct perception or sound inference from it — for post-mortem consequences of action.

#### Samsara
- **Escape** — Reasons: samsara, however pleasant in stretches, is marked by impermanence and suffering, and every rebirth re-rolls the dice; the Theravada holder's aim is the far shore, not a better seat on the raft that sinks. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that continued rebirth could be finally secure against falling back into suffering.
- **Engage** — Reasons: the bodhisattva's vow answers the escape with a greater compassion: to leave while others remain bound is a private peace, and Mahayana holders hold that awakening itself is completed only in returning for all beings. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument, from within Mahayana sources, that the vow delays rather than deepens awakening.
- **Accept** — Reasons: the householder traditions answer that the world is not a prison but a field of duty, love and enjoyment rightly ordered by dharma; samsara embraced under dharma is not bondage but life. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that worldly life, however ordered, cannot finally satisfy — the first noble truth pressed home in the holder's own experience.

#### Buddhism
- **Religion** — Reasons: devotion, ritual, sangha, heavens and rebirth are what most Buddhists across Asia have actually lived; calling Buddhism a religion honours the tradition as practised, not only as theorised. Statistic: Buddhists numbered about 488 million, roughly 7% of the world's population, in 2010 (Pew Research Center, The Global Religious Landscape, 2012). What would move a holder: evidence that the devotional and communal forms were late additions foreign to the earliest strata — and that this should change present practice.
- **Philosophy** — Reasons: the Buddha's own method is analytic — dependent origination, the aggregates, the refusal of unanswerable questions; holders prize a path that asks for reasoned conviction and no creator god. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that analysis alone, without devotion or ritual, leaves practitioners unable to sustain the path.
- **Way of Life** — Reasons: the proof of the path is a life — meditation, ethics, daily attention; classifying it as religion or philosophy matters less than whether it ends suffering, which is the Buddha's own pragmatic test. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where the classification question had real consequences the way-of-life framing could not settle.

#### The Four Purusharthas
- **Dharma** — Reasons: duty is the frame that makes the other three aims safe: wealth and pleasure pursued under dharma build a life and a society, and the holder ranks the frame first because without it the aims corrupt. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that duty-first ordering, in practice, crushed the legitimate claims of the other aims.
- **Artha** — Reasons: prosperity is not greed but capacity — security for family, generosity for community, patronage for learning; the Kauṭilyan tradition treats material order as the precondition of every other good. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that artha pursued as the first aim reliably deformed the holder's dharma rather than funding it.
- **Kama** — Reasons: pleasure, love and beauty are named aims of life in the tradition itself, not concessions to weakness; the holder resists readings of dharma that treat embodiment as an embarrassment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing from the holder's own texts that kama's place is strictly subordinate in a way that changes practice.
- **Moksha** — Reasons: the other three aims, however well pursued, end at death; liberation alone does not, and the renouncer traditions rank it first because it is the only aim that answers the final question. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument the holder accepts that liberation is attained precisely through the other aims fulfilled, not by ranking them last.

#### The Three Teachings / San Jiao
- **Confucianism** — Reasons: social harmony is an achievement of ritual, filial piety and moral cultivation, not a default; the holder holds that persons are made by their duties well performed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that ritual order, in the holder's own setting, produced hypocrisy faster than character.
- **Taoism** — Reasons: what is forced breaks and what is named falsifies; the holder trusts the Dao's unforced order over every human scheme, including pious ones. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where spontaneity failed a community that ritual order would have carried.
- **Buddhism** — Reasons: neither social duty nor natural flow answers suffering and death; the Buddhist teaching on emptiness and compassion does, and the holder gives it the final word among the three. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that the Buddhist analysis dissolves questions the holder still needs answered by the other teachings.

#### Archetypal energies
- **Heaven (Qian)** — Reasons: the creative, initiating force — pure yang — is the energy of beginnings; holders cultivate it because without initiative nothing else on the wheel turns. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that initiative, in their practice, repeatedly needed yielding to complete it.
- **Lake (Dui)** — Reasons: joy and openness are spiritual capacities, not distractions; the Lake holder holds that a path without delight in it will not be walked for long. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that openness, unguarded, dissipated what stillness would have gathered.
- **Fire (Li)** — Reasons: clarity and illumination — seeing and being seen truly; the Fire holder cultivates brightness of understanding, accepting its clinging as the price of light. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that illumination without grounding burned the holder's practice into restlessness.
- **Thunder (Zhen)** — Reasons: the arousing shock that starts movement and breaks stagnation; holders value the jolt that wakes a settled life into practice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that arousal, cultivated, became agitation rather than awakening.
- **Wind (Xun)** — Reasons: the gentle that penetrates everywhere precisely because it does not force; the Wind holder trusts gradual, pervasive influence over heroic effort. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where gentleness failed and only direct force served.
- **Water (Kan)** — Reasons: the abysmal — depth, danger and flow; the Water holder learns from the element that finds the lowest place and is stopped by nothing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that dwelling in depth became avoidance of the surface duties of life.
- **Mountain (Gen)** — Reasons: stillness and knowing where to stop are attainments, not absences; the Mountain holder cultivates rest as the root of right movement. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that stillness, in their case, had become refusal to act when action was owed.
- **Earth (Kun)** — Reasons: receptivity, yielding and nourishment — the power that bears all things by not competing with them; holders rank it as strength misread as passivity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that yielding, in a concrete case, enabled harm that resistance would have prevented.

#### The Three Gunas
- **Sattva** — Reasons: clarity, harmony and knowledge bind the soul to happiness and light, the Gita says — and even that bond is to be understood; holders cultivate sattva as the temperament from which liberation is visible. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that their sattva had become its own attachment — purity as pride.
- **Rajas** — Reasons: passion and activity build the world — families, works, kingdoms; the rajasic holder does not apologise for drive, but aims it, as the Gita aims Arjuna's. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that drive, however aimed, kept generating the restlessness it promised to satisfy.
- **Tamas** — Reasons: inertia and darkness are named honestly because every practitioner meets them; holders who name tamas in themselves treat recognition as the first loosening of its hold — and some traditions add that rest and dissolution have their own necessary place in the cycle. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that what they called necessary rest was, in their teacher's judgement, avoidance.

#### The Five Mahabhutas
- **Earth (Prithvi)** — Reasons: solidity and stability ground every other practice; the Earth holder works with form, posture and patience, and trusts what can be stood on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that grounding had become rigidity their practice needed to melt.
- **Water (Apas)** — Reasons: fluidity and cohesion teach adaptation without loss of nature; the Water holder's practice bends so that it does not break. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where adaptability dissolved a commitment that should have held firm.
- **Fire (Tejas)** — Reasons: transformation and digestion — of food, of experience, of karma; the Fire holder trusts the element that converts one thing into another. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that their fire was consuming the practitioner rather than the fuel.
- **Air (Vayu)** — Reasons: movement and breath carry life through the body; the Air holder works with prana's motion as the most tangible handle on the subtle. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that chasing breath-states had replaced the insight the breath was meant to serve.
- **Ether (Akasha)** — Reasons: space is the medium in which all the others move and the subtlest element; the Ether holder attends to the container rather than the contents. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that spaciousness became vagueness, losing the forms practice needs.

#### Scripture and authority
- **Living Guru** — Reasons: for the Sikh holder the Guru Granth Sahib is not a book about the Guru but the Guru — enthroned, consulted and obeyed as a living presence; its authority is personal, not archival. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument from within the Panth that a passage's application required the historical reading over the enthroned presence — changing practice, not just theory.
- **Provisional (test it)** — Reasons: the Buddha's advice in the Kalama Sutta — test teachings in experience as a goldsmith tests gold — makes scripture a raft and a hypothesis; holders prize a canon that invites its own verification. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a teaching that could not be tested in one lifetime yet demanded assent — rebirth itself — forcing the holder to qualify the test.
- **Revealed (heard truth)** — Reasons: the Veda is śruti, heard by the seers, not composed by authors; for the holder its authority rests precisely on having no human author to err, and the whole edifice of dharma stands on that hearing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing, from within the tradition's own hermeneutics, that a contested passage bears a different sense than the holder's school reads.
- **Authoritative but layered** — Reasons: scripture binds through its commentaries, schools and living teachers, in layers that sometimes differ; the holder trusts the layered whole more than any single flat reading, including their own. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where the layers contradicted each other so directly that one had to be set aside.

#### Can the truth be spoken?
- **Speakable** — Reasons: the traditions do speak — hymns, sutras, the Name — and speech guides real practitioners to real liberation; the holder holds that unspeakability, pressed too far, dissolves the teaching it claims to protect. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience in their own practice of a truth that every formulation they tried betrayed.
- **Unspeakable** — Reasons: 'the Dao that can be told is not the eternal Dao'; neti neti — not this, not this; holders hold that every positive statement about the ultimate is a finger taken for the moon. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a formulation that the holder's own awakened teachers endorsed as adequate — making silence a refusal rather than a reverence.
- **Layered** — Reasons: the two-truths doctrine and its cousins let both be right: conventionally spoken, ultimately unspeakable; holders value a scheme that protects beginners' guidance and adepts' silence at once. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where the layering became an evasion — every hard question reassigned to the other level.
- **Many-sided** — Reasons: the Jain doctrine of anekantavada: reality is many-sided, every statement is 'in some respect', and contradiction between schools is often just partial views colliding; holders prize the intellectual humility built into the logic itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a question on which the many-sided method yielded no guidance where a decision was unavoidable.

### 3.2 Liberation & Enlightenment

#### Liberation
- **Moksha** — Reasons: liberation is the Self recognised as Brahman — not a place one goes but a bondage one wakes from; the holder's traditions describe it as freedom from rebirth through knowledge of what one already is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument from a sister school — that recognition without devotion or grace leaves liberation cold and incomplete — that the holder finds unanswered.
- **Nirvana** — Reasons: nirvana is the blowing-out of craving, aversion and delusion — the end of suffering, not the soul's relocation; holders resist every picture that turns it into a heaven, because the teaching's precision is its compassion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that cessation language fails the tradition's own descriptions of the awakened life that follows it.
- **Union with the Dao** — Reasons: the Daoist end is not escape from the world but unobstructed accord with it — the sage returns to the uncarved block; holders prize a liberation that looks like naturalness rather than departure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that accord with things as they are left suffering unaddressed that another path's discipline relieved.

#### Enlightenment
- **Sudden** — Reasons: Zen's satori and the Platform Sutra's teaching: awakening is not manufactured by steps, because what is sought is already the seeker's nature; a gradual path can become one more postponement. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sudden experience that faded, leaving the holder's defilements intact — and needing the cultivation they had scorned.
- **Sudden awakening, gradual cultivation** — Reasons: the synthesis many schools actually live: insight breaks in suddenly, habits dissolve gradually, as the sun rises at once and melts the frost by degrees. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence in their own case that the two halves belonged to different events entirely — that the synthesis papers over a real choice.
- **Gradual** — Reasons: the Theravada path of purification and the Confucian making of the sage are step-ladders for a reason: character and concentration are built, and claims of instant awakening most often mark its beginning mistaken for its end. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a genuine, teacher-confirmed breakthrough that no ladder predicted.

#### Selfhood
- **Atman** — Reasons: the Upanishadic teaching — the Self is not the body, the mind or the biography, and it is identical with Brahman; holders hold that meditation's witness, unchanging through every state, is exactly this. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the Buddhist analysis pressed home: a witness examined and found to be one more conditioned process.
- **Pudgalavada Person** — Reasons: the historical Pudgalavada school held the person is real — neither identical to the aggregates nor different from them — because karma and rebirth need a bearer that anatta, flatly read, dissolves; holders prize the honesty of refusing both easy answers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that the inexpressible person does no explanatory work the aggregates do not already do.
- **Anatta** — Reasons: whatever is examined — body, feeling, perception, formations, consciousness — is impermanent and not-self, and clinging to any of it as 'I' is the root suffering; the holder finds liberation in the analysis itself, not despite it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience of the witness that the fivefold analysis could not accommodate, reported by a teacher the holder trusts.

#### Innate Purity vs. Gradual Purification
- **Original Buddha-Nature / Inner Light** — Reasons: the tathagatagarbha teaching and the Jain soul's innate omniscience agree across traditions: awakening uncovers what was never lost; holders hold that practice removes clouds, and never manufactures the sun. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where presuming purity licensed complacency that a teacher had to break.
- **Defiled Soul Requiring Cleansing** — Reasons: karmic residue is real weight, in Jain teaching literally matter clinging to the soul; holders warn that 'already awakened' talk, taken early, is the defilements defending themselves. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience of the underlying purity so direct that the cleansing model had to be re-read as uncovering.

#### Self-Power vs. Other-Power
- **Self-Reliant Effort (Jiriki)** — Reasons: 'you yourselves must strive; the Tathagatas only point the way' — no one can meditate, renounce or see for another; holders hold that grace-talk, taken early, is effort avoiding itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience of being carried — in devotion or in crisis — that their own effort demonstrably did not produce.
- **Surrender to Grace (Tariki)** — Reasons: Shinran's teaching: the ego that strives to save itself is the obstacle, and Amitabha's vow was made precisely for those who cannot save themselves; surrender is not passivity but the end of self-power's illusion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that surrender, in their community, had become an excuse for ethical laxity the teaching itself condemns.

#### Liberation for whom?
- **All beings** — Reasons: 'sentient beings are numberless; I vow to free them all' — the bodhisattva and the Jain reverence for every jiva agree that a private liberation is an unfinished one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument from within their school that one's own release must come first to be of any use — the life-jacket principle — pressed to a real change of practice.
- **The individual** — Reasons: each soul's karma is its own, and no one can be liberated by proxy; the holder's traditions aim the path at the one who walks it, and call collective language a noble metaphor. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience of communal practice — sangha, langar, congregation — that demonstrably carried them further than solitary effort.
- **The soul** — Reasons: in Jain and Samkhya framings what is freed is precisely the soul or pure consciousness, from the matter that binds it; naming the patient correctly is, for holders, the beginning of the cure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the anatta analysis applied to their own case with a force they could not answer.
- **The community** — Reasons: the Sikh Panth and the Confucian order agree that liberation-talk that leaves the community unchanged is incomplete; the holder walks in the assembly, and the assembly is part of the goal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that communal forms had become the obstacle — that the holder needed the forest, not the congregation.

#### Liberation in this life or beyond it?
- **In this life** — Reasons: jivanmukti — liberation while living — is attested across the traditions, and Zen adds that awakening delayed to another life is awakening refused; holders prize the teachings that point at this body, this moment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their own teacher's judgement that what they took for attainment in this life was a stage, not the end.
- **Beyond this life** — Reasons: rebirth in the Pure Land, or release at death for the soul still bound at life's end — holders hold that honesty about one's present capacity makes the beyond not a failure but the plan. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an attainment in this life, witnessed and confirmed, that the beyond-framing said should not happen yet.
- **At the end of the path** — Reasons: the Jain and Theravada ladders count lives the way geology counts strata; holders find the long view humbling and steadying — it removes the panic from practice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a breakthrough that compressed the ladder — sudden where the map said gradual.
- **Both** — Reasons: the Vaishnava and Sikh framings: union and loving service begin now, in the householder's kitchen and the congregation, and continue beyond death without a break in kind. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument that the two halves pull apart — that now-language and beyond-language answer different questions.

### 3.3 Practice & Discipline

#### Yoga: Five Dimensions
- **Physical** — Reasons: asana is where most practitioners actually begin, and the body is the one instrument every other dimension plays; holders hold that a path ignoring the body will be ambushed by it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that physical practice had become the whole path — fitness wearing yoga's name.
- **Breath** — Reasons: pranayama works the hinge between body and mind — breath is the one autonomic function also under voluntary control; holders prize it as the most direct lever a beginner can actually pull. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that breath-states, however refined, left the holder's conduct untouched.
- **Meditative** — Reasons: dhyana is the dimension the others serve: posture and breath exist to make sustained stillness possible, and stillness is where the teachings are verified rather than believed. Statistic: 14.2% of US adults reported using meditation in the previous 12 months in 2017, up from 4.1% in 2012 (National Center for Health Statistics, NCHS Data Brief 325, 2018). What would move a holder: evidence that meditation without ethics and devotion produced calm without transformation.
- **Devotional** — Reasons: bhakti within yoga turns practice from self-improvement into love; holders hold that the heart's surrender accomplishes what technique alone keeps postponing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where devotion curdled into dependency on the object or the teacher.
- **Philosophical** — Reasons: jnana asks the questions the other dimensions presuppose — who is practising, and for what liberation; holders hold that uninstructed practice builds on unexamined ground. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that study had become a substitute for the practice it described.

#### Meditation
- **Samatha** — Reasons: a scattered mind can see nothing, including its own nature; concentration is not a lesser goal but the instrument's sharpening, and the jhanas are attainments in their own right in the early texts. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a plateau of calm that insight practice, once begun, showed to be a refined hiding place.
- **Vipassana** — Reasons: calm that never turns to insight leaves the root defilements untouched; seeing impermanence, suffering and not-self in direct experience is, for holders, the meditation the Buddha called liberating. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that insight pursued without a calm base destabilised a practitioner — the tradition's own warnings made concrete.

#### Tantra
- **Left-hand** — Reasons: vamachara uses precisely what orthodoxy forbids because the forbidden carries the charge: if liberation cannot survive contact with desire, meat, wine and death, it is a fair-weather freedom; holders insist the practices are disciplined, initiated and anything but indulgence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing, from within a lineage, that a community's transgressions had become ordinary harm with ritual excuses.
- **Internalized transgression** — Reasons: the crossing done in visualisation and symbol takes the teaching — nothing is inherently impure — without the acts' casualties; holders hold this is the mature reading most traditions themselves adopted. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument from a left-hand teacher that internalisation is avoidance wearing sophistication.
- **Right-hand** — Reasons: dakshinachara keeps tantra within orthodox bounds — mantra, yantra, deity yoga without the literal transgressions; holders hold that the energy is real and the guardrails are why lineages survive. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where orthodoxy had drained the practice of the force it was built to channel.

#### Kundalini
- **Awakening** — Reasons: in the tantric physiologies kundalini's rise through the chakras is the mechanism of transformation itself, described in detail by lineages that mapped it from experience; holders treat the phenomenology as data, not metaphor. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case, witnessed by the holder, of forced awakening producing the breakdown the cautious schools predict.
- **Danger** — Reasons: the same texts warn that unprepared awakening destabilises body and mind, and modern teachers document the casualties; holders counsel the slow paths precisely because they respect the energy's reality. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their own or a trusted student's safe, guided awakening that the danger-framing would have forbidden.

#### The Noble Eightfold Path
- **Right View** — Reasons: seeing things as they are — karma, impermanence, the four truths — is the forerunner of the whole path, the texts say; a wrong map wastes every later effort. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that correct views, held as opinions, changed nothing until effort and mindfulness moved.
- **Right Intention** — Reasons: renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness are the volitions that steer conduct before conduct happens; holders cultivate the intention because the act follows it as surely as a cart its ox. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where intention was pure and the act, unexamined, still harmed.
- **Right Speech** — Reasons: speech is where most people do most of their harm and most of their good; the holder treats truthful, kind, timely speech as daily practice available in every conversation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a situation where the speech rules collided — truth against kindness — and the holder had to rank them.
- **Right Action** — Reasons: the precepts in motion: no killing, stealing or misconduct, embodied rather than believed; holders hold that liberation built on harmful acts is a contradiction the path refuses. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a dilemma in which every available act broke one precept to keep another.
- **Right Livelihood** — Reasons: a living earned by harm — weapons, intoxicants, trafficking — taxes every other limb; holders accept real economic cost to keep the path's foundation clean. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that their livelihood scruples, in context, protected purity more than people.
- **Right Effort** — Reasons: the gardener's limb: abandon unwholesome states, prevent their arising, cultivate and maintain the wholesome; holders prize it because it makes practice a daily craft rather than an event. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that effort itself became striving — the defilement wearing the remedy's clothes.
- **Right Mindfulness** — Reasons: clear awareness of body, feeling, mind and phenomena is the path's laboratory — the one limb where every teaching can be tested in first person, moment by moment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that bare attention, without concentration and view, plateaued into observation without release.
- **Right Concentration** — Reasons: the jhanas are not decorations on the path but its culmination in the early texts — collectedness deep enough that insight has something unshakable to work with. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a teacher's correction that their absorption had become a refined refuge from insight.

#### Action
- **Wu Wei** — Reasons: effortless action is not inaction but action so aligned with the situation's own grain that it leaves no strain and no residue; the Daodejing's ruler and Zhuangzi's cook Ding are its portraits, and holders find forced action reliably self-defeating. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where non-forcing, in their hands, was indistinguishable from neglect.
- **Act when needed** — Reasons: the middle the holder actually lives: striving laid down as a default, action taken cleanly when the situation calls — neither forcing nor hiding behind the Dao. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a decision in which 'when needed' proved to be exactly the contested question.
- **You Wei** — Reasons: deliberate striving built the dikes, the schools and the clinics; the Confucian-leaning holder holds that much of what wu wei enjoys was made by people who intervened on purpose. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: experience that their interventions kept creating the problems the next intervention had to solve.

#### The Six Classical Yogas
- **Raja Yoga** — Reasons: Patanjali's eight limbs are the most complete operating manual any tradition produced — ethics, posture, breath, concentration, absorption in one ladder; holders trust its engineering. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that the ladder's later limbs were being used to bypass its first ones — absorption without ethics.
- **Jnana Yoga** — Reasons: self-inquiry — 'who am I?', neti neti, the discrimination of the seer from the seen — cuts the root rather than pruning branches; holders hold the other yogas prepare a question only knowledge answers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience in which knowledge was complete as doctrine and absent as realisation.
- **Bhakti Yoga** — Reasons: love of God needs no prerequisites of learning or strength, and the Gita itself calls the devotee dearest; holders find surrender warmer and surer than self-reliance. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where devotion depended so wholly on its object that doubt became catastrophe.
- **Karma Yoga** — Reasons: act without attachment to fruits and the world itself becomes the monastery; the Gita's answer to Arjuna is the householder's charter, and holders prize a path that sanctifies necessary work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unattached action, in their workplace, was exploited as uncomplaining labour.
- **Hatha Yoga** — Reasons: the body forced, held and breathed into an instrument — holders hold that the subtle is reached through the gross, and that philosophies ignoring embodiment stay theoretical. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that physical mastery had become its own goal, displacing the liberation it served.
- **Tantra Yoga** — Reasons: weaving energy, mantra, ritual and body into one technology of awakening — holders value tantra's refusal to exile any part of life from the path. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a lineage scandal or harm that made the holder qualify their trust in tantric institutions.

#### Ritual & Ancestral Efficacy
- **Sacred Rite & Ancestral Duty** — Reasons: mantra pronounced correctly, offerings made, ancestors honoured — the Confucian li and the Vedic rite agree that form is not packaging but the act itself, and that the debt to forebears is real whether or not it is felt. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rite performed perfectly that the holder's own tradition judged empty for want of the heart.
- **Interior Ethical Intention** — Reasons: the Buddha relocated sacrifice from the altar to intention — cetana is karma; holders hold that a rite without kindness is choreography, and that the traditions' own reformers said so first. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience in which the rite itself — its form, its repetition — produced the inner change intention alone had not.

#### Somatic & Yogic Subtle Body
- **Asana, Pranayama & Kundalini** — Reasons: body, breath and mind are one continuum in the yogic physiologies, and prana refined in the channels reaches states discursive thought cannot; holders treat the subtle body as anatomy learned from inside. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where energetic pursuit inflated exactly the pride and grasping the path exists to end.
- **Purely Mental & Ethical Inquiry** — Reasons: the Buddha's and the jnani's caution: experiences are not insight, and chasing them is samsara with better scenery; holders keep practice where liberation is decided — view, conduct, understanding. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience in which a bodily practice broke a knot that years of inquiry had only described.

#### The body
- **Mortify** — Reasons: Jain and early ascetic practice: the body is where karma sticks, and austerity — fasting, exposure, stillness — burns it off; holders point to the mahavratas' fruits and call comfort the subtlest chain. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where austerity damaged the instrument of practice itself, and a teacher ordered it stopped.
- **Cultivate** — Reasons: the yogic and Daoist answer: the body is the vehicle, and a vehicle is maintained — trained, breathed, strengthened for the long road; neglect is not holiness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that cultivation had quietly become vanity's practice.
- **Accept (middle way)** — Reasons: the Buddha's own discovery, between the palace and the forest: neither indulgence nor mortification, but a body fed and rested enough to sit; holders prize the refusal of both theatres. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a situation in which the middle was the evasion — where their tradition demanded the ascetic extreme.
- **Honour** — Reasons: the Sikh and bhakti answer: the body is God's gift and the site of service — fed at the langar, defended as the saint-soldier's duty; fleeing it is ingratitude, harming it is harm. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument from an ascetic tradition that honouring, in their case, rationalised attachment.

### 3.4 Deity & The Divine

#### The Divine: Four Quadrants
- **Personal Theism** — Reasons: God can be loved, addressed and obeyed — Vishnu, Krishna, the Guru's Waheguru; bhakti's holders hold that a principle cannot return love, and that devotion's fruits across millennia are its evidence. Statistic: Hindus numbered about 1.03 billion, roughly 15% of the world's population, in 2010 (Pew Research Center, The Global Religious Landscape, 2012). What would move a holder: an experience of the divine as formless that their personal language could not carry.
- **Impersonal Theism** — Reasons: Brahman without attributes is not a lesser God but God without limits — every attribute a boundary; Advaita holders hold the personal to be the impersonal's face for minds that need one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a devotional encounter whose personal character the holder could not honestly reduce to a face.
- **Non-theistic Spirituality** — Reasons: Buddhism and Jainism run complete soteriologies with no creator — liberation by insight and effort, in a universe no one made; holders find the absence not a lack but the teaching's honesty. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that their practice relied, in fact, on devotion to figures functioning as gods.
- **Atheism / Materialism** — Reasons: the Cārvāka holder: perception shows no gods, inference from perception reaches none, and the priestly economy built on them is visible enough; this life, this body and its flourishing are the whole of the evidence and the whole of the task. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence meeting the holder's own standard — perception, or inference the holder accepts — for a reality beyond the material.

#### Brahman
- **Saguna** — Reasons: God with attributes can be worshipped, narrated and loved — Ramanuja's Brahman is perfect with qualities, not despite them; holders hold that nirguna language, taken alone, describes an absolute no one can reach or love. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a contemplative experience of attributelessness their saguna frame had to make room for.
- **Both** — Reasons: the personal God as the face of the impersonal absolute — the tradition's own reconciliations, from the Gita's two natures onward; holders refuse to amputate either pole of their inheritance. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a teacher's ruling that, on a contested point, the two readings gave different duties and one had to govern.
- **Nirguna** — Reasons: whatever can be attributed can be denied — neti neti leaves Brahman as pure being-consciousness; Sankara's holders hold that attributes belong to the level of appearance, however useful there. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument that the attributeless absolute cannot be the object of the knowledge that liberates — pressed by a Ramanujan the holder respects.

#### Buddha
- **Historical Buddha** — Reasons: Siddhartha Gautama, a man who woke up under a tree in the fifth century BCE — the Theravada holder's Buddha needs no mythology, and his humanity is the encouragement: what one man did, others can. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: texts and practices within their own tradition that treat the Buddha as more — and demand a reckoning.
- **Cosmic Buddha** — Reasons: Amitabha, Vairocana, the dharmakaya — the Mahayana expansion in which Buddhahood is the universe's own nature and its Buddhas are present tense; holders find the historical-only reading too small for the sutras they chant. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that cosmic readings were historical developments rather than the founder's teaching — and that this should matter.
- **Buddha-nature** — Reasons: the tathagatagarbha teaching: awakening is not acquired but uncovered, because the nature was never absent; holders prize the confidence this gives practice — one polishes a mirror, one does not manufacture light. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a teacher's warning, from inside the tradition, that Buddha-nature language was becoming a self by another name.

### 3.5 Ethics & Conduct

#### Ahimsa: Three Schools
- **Jain** — Reasons: ahimsa without remainder: every jiva, down to the one-sensed beings in water and soil, is a soul like one's own, and the vow's strictness — filtering water, sweeping the path — is its logic lived; holders call every exception the beginning of the slide. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a situation in which absolute non-harm proved impossible — the tradition's own casuistry of unavoidable himsa — forcing finer distinctions than the slogan allows.
- **Buddhist** — Reasons: intention is karma, so Buddhist ahimsa weighs the mind and the context — the monk accepts alms-meat not seen, heard or suspected to be killed for him, and lay life keeps its tragic margins; holders prize a non-violence that survives contact with reality. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where contextual flexibility licensed what the stricter schools would have prevented.
- **Hindu** — Reasons: ahimsa as the highest dharma — and dharma includes the warrior's duty and the householder's necessities, as the Gita's battlefield setting itself declares; holders hold the tension openly rather than dissolving either side. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument, from the Jain or Buddhist side, that duty-exceptions had swallowed the virtue in the holder's own community.

#### Precepts
- **Strict rules** — Reasons: a vow that bends under pressure is a preference; the Jain and monastic holders keep precepts as commandments precisely so that the hard case — where breaking them is tempting — is already decided. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a collision of strict precepts in which obedience to one required breaking another.
- **Firm but flexible** — Reasons: precepts held firmly and read by their spirit when they collide — the holder's mainstream: the rule against lying meets the murderer at the door, and the tradition's casuistry exists for exactly this. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where spirit-reading became the habitual solvent of every inconvenient rule.
- **Flexible guidelines** — Reasons: the precepts as training directions rather than law — compass points for a mind being educated, in several modern and Zen readings; holders hold that guilt-driven rigidity obstructs the very clarity the precepts serve. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that guideline-framing, in their community, dissolved a standard that strictness would have kept.

#### Two wings
- **Karuna** — Reasons: compassion is the motive without which wisdom becomes a private acquisition; the holder feeds the wing that answers suffering first, trusting wisdom to be sharpened by use in the world. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a compassionate act that wisdom would have prevented — help that harmed — changing the holder's balance.
- **Prajna** — Reasons: compassion without wisdom burns out, misfires and attaches; seeing emptiness is what lets help be given without a giver's grasping, so the holder feeds the wing that makes the other sustainable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an encounter with suffering in which analysis arrived too late and only the heart would have served.

#### Confucian Social Order vs. Taoist Withdrawal
- **Filial & Civic Propriety (Li)** — Reasons: li is civilisation's technology: reverence for elders, reciprocal duties and rites that teach the heart its place; the Confucian holder holds that harmony is manufactured by form, or not at all. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the forms, in the holder's own family or state, had become theatre over cruelty.
- **Rusticated Natural Simplicity** — Reasons: the Daoist suspicion: every title and rite is a place hypocrisy can live, and the uncarved life near mountains and streams keeps the spirit whole; holders withdraw not from people but from the machinery of status. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where withdrawal abandoned dependants whom the order, for all its faults, would have obliged them to serve.

#### When is force justified?
- **Never** — Reasons: the Jain vow and the early Buddhist refusal agree: harm done by one's own hand is karma one has chosen, and no outcome launders it; holders accept the costs of refusal as the vow's meaning. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a concrete case — defence of the helpless in the moment — in which the holder's own tradition judged refusal the greater harm.
- **In defence of dharma** — Reasons: the Gita is spoken on a battlefield to a warrior who would rather renounce: Krishna's answer is that the kshatriya's duty can require the sword, and the Sikh saint-soldier takes the same stand — force in defence of dharma and the oppressed is not a fall from the path but part of it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a war claimed as dharma's defence that the holder's own teachers judged adharma in dharma's clothing.
- **Only in extremis** — Reasons: force as the last resort, regretted even when justified — the mainstream Buddhist and modern Hindu settlement; holders keep the door nearly shut and count the regret as part of the act's morality. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case in which 'last resort' arrived, by their own account, too late to prevent the greater harm.
- **Against tyranny** — Reasons: the Sikh tradition's kirpan and the doctrine of miri-piri: when all other means fail, taking up the sword against a tyrant is righteous; holders name tyranny, not disorder, as the trigger. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a claimed tyranny that proved, on examination, to be a lawful order the holder disliked.

#### Food and non-violence
- **Strictly non-violent** — Reasons: the Jain table: no meat, no root vegetables whose harvest kills the plant and its soil-beings, no eating after dark lest insects be harmed unseen; holders call the strictness the vow's honesty, not its exaggeration. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that a rule's observance, in a modern setting, caused harms the rule existed to prevent.
- **Mindful** — Reasons: the Buddhist table: vegetarian by preference and conscience, alms accepted as given, attention on gratitude and consequence rather than purity; holders prize a practice a lay life can actually keep. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that mindfulness at their table had become a blessing pronounced over unchanged habits.
- **Sattvic** — Reasons: the yogic table: food as guna — fresh, light, pure food for a clear mind, in the Gita's own classification; holders eat for the quality of consciousness the meal builds. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where sattvic purity became social exclusion at a shared table.
- **Shared** — Reasons: the Sikh langar's answer: the point of the table is that everyone sits in one line and eats the same food — equality enacted; holders hold that who eats together matters more than the menu's refinements. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument that the shared table's own rules quietly excluded — and needed the stricter schools' challenge.

#### Where does right come from?
- **Consequences** — Reasons: in the Buddhist analysis an act is right as it does not sow suffering — karma is consequence woven into character and world; holders judge rules by their fruits because the teaching itself does. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an act with good fruits that the holder's conscience, and tradition, still called wrong.
- **Dharma** — Reasons: right is what accords with the order of things and one's place and stage within it — duty is not invented by preference but discovered; holders trust the given structure over private calculation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a conflict of dharmas — the tradition's own hardest cases — in which the structure gave no single answer.
- **Non-violence** — Reasons: ahimsa as the first principle from which the rest follows — the Jain and Gandhian root: if an act harms, the burden of proof is total, and every other virtue is commentary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case in which non-violence to one being required harm to another, and the first principle alone could not arbitrate.
- **The Guru's word** — Reasons: for the Sikh holder, right is learned from the Guru — the Granth's word and the Panth's lived discipline; a revealed and communal standard stands above private reasoning, which the tradition regards as the ego's home ground. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case in which the holder had to interpret the Guru's word against a settled communal reading.

### 3.6 Community & Lineage

#### Guru
- **Essential** — Reasons: the guru traditions are blunt: the subtlest obstacles are invisible to the one who has them, texts cannot correct your posture of mind, and transmission is person to person; holders point to their own turning points, which all have a teacher in them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a teacher's failure — ethical or doctrinal — that forced the holder to rebuild the claim on a narrower base.
- **Helpful, not essential** — Reasons: teachers accelerate and correct, and the holder uses them gratefully — but the Buddha's own 'be lamps unto yourselves' keeps the final authority in the practice itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a plateau the holder could not leave until a teacher's single sentence moved it.
- **Optional** — Reasons: the Cārvāka and self-reliant readings: books, reason and experience suffice, and guru-dependence has its own well-documented casualties; the holder keeps authority where it can be audited. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the holder's unaided reading had entrenched exactly the error a teacher exists to catch.

#### Sangha
- **Monastic** — Reasons: renunciation buys the one resource practice consumes — undivided life; the sangha's holders hold that the Buddha founded an order, not a hobby, and that its full-time form remains the path's engine. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that their monastery sheltered the defilements it existed to end.
- **Retreatant householder** — Reasons: the working synthesis of much modern practice: a lay life punctuated by real retreats, each feeding the other; holders hold the rhythm more honest than either pure form for most people now. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a retreat whose fruits dissolved within weeks, repeatedly — suggesting the rhythm was tourism, not training.
- **Lay** — Reasons: the householder paths — Sikh, Confucian, bhakti — deny that liberation requires leaving the world; the marketplace is not the path's obstacle but its examination hall. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a showing that the holder's worldly duties had, in fact, consumed the practice they were meant to examine.

#### Tradition
- **Orthodox** — Reasons: a lineage is a tested vessel — its sequence of practices assumes its whole; mixing methods mid-stream, holders warn, produces practitioners deep in nothing and answerable to no teacher. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a borrowing that demonstrably completed what their own lineage lacked, acknowledged by their own teacher.
- **Rooted but borrowing** — Reasons: one home, open windows: the holder's root tradition governs, and borrowings are guests that must answer to it; most living traditions, examined closely, were built this way. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a borrowing that quietly contradicted the root — discovered late.
- **Eclectic** — Reasons: the teachings are fingers pointing at one moon, and the modern practitioner's situation — many traditions available, none inherited whole — makes honest eclecticism more truthful than performed orthodoxy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that their eclecticism had become consumption — breadth as a way of never being claimed by any path.

#### Way of life
- **Grasthi** — Reasons: the householder stage carries the world — children, elders, guests, the poor at the door; its holders hold that the other stages exist because this one feeds them, and that its duties are a complete yoga. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experience that the householder's web, however honoured, was the bondage the later stages exist to loosen.
- **Vanaprastha** — Reasons: the forest-dweller stage is the tradition's wisdom about endings: loosen the ties gradually, hand over, withdraw by degrees; holders prize a culture that scripts the second half of life. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where gradual withdrawal was, in fact, abandonment of duties still owed.
- **Sannyasa** — Reasons: the renouncer takes the final stage whole — no property, no home, no name from before; holders hold that liberation pursued part-time is a negotiation the ego always wins. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sannyasi's fall, witnessed closely, that taught the holder the stage's dangers as well as its heights.

#### Caste, Birth & Spiritual Eligibility
- **Radical Equality of All Seekers** — Reasons: the Sikh langar's seating, the Buddha's admission of all castes to the sangha, and bhakti's poet-saints from every station agree: the divine light is not distributed by birth; holders call hereditary spiritual rank an injustice the traditions' best selves condemn. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an argument from within their own tradition for a function of inherited role that equality-language cannot describe.
- **Traditional Varnashrama Order** — Reasons: the classical defence, in its holders' terms: varna by aptitude and duty, ashrama by stage — a division of sacred labour that preserved the Veda through millennia of recitation lineages, and a social order in which every station has dignity and obligation; holders distinguish this ideal sharply from birth-rigidity and oppression practised in its name. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the ideal, in the holder's own society, could not be practised without the rigidity they disown.

#### Women's spiritual equality
- **Equal (can attain)** — Reasons: women attain awakening throughout the record — the Therigatha's elder nuns, the bhakti women saints, Gargi debating in the Upanishads, the Sikh Gurus' explicit teaching; holders hold the path's own evidence settles it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a text in their own canon that resists the equal reading and must be honestly faced.
- **Restricted (traditional)** — Reasons: the traditional restrictions, stated as their holders state them: certain lineages historically required male rebirth for full ordination or the highest attainments, on grounds of the traditions' reading of karma and of monastic precedent — a position its holders present as textual fidelity, and which the map records without endorsing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence from within the tradition — the founding texts or the founder's acts — that the restriction was circumstance, not doctrine.
- **Sect-dependent** — Reasons: the honest census: the traditions genuinely differ, and within them schools differ; the holder maps the variation rather than forcing a verdict the sources do not give. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case in which 'sect-dependent' functioned as a refusal to judge a restriction the holder's own principles condemned.

## 4. Archetype research

All 11 samples in the skin have kind "archetype": eleven traditions stated as ideal types, each with its own canon, path and account of liberation. Archetype ids covered in this section, in skin order: dharma-theravada, dharma-zen, dharma-pure-land, dharma-tibetan, dharma-advaita-vedanta, dharma-vaishnava-bhakti, dharma-confucian, dharma-jain, dharma-sikh, dharma-daojia, dharma-carvaka. Ratings in the skin rate advocacy per pole and openness per topic; the receptivity and intensity discussed below are the derived quantities (see §1), described in words, not re-rated here.

### Theravada Buddhism — `dharma-theravada.svg`
Theravada, the 'Teaching of the Elders', is the oldest surviving school of Buddhism, rooted in the Pali Canon and practised mainly in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. Its path centres on the Four Noble Truths, the Noble Eightfold Path and meditation leading to awakening. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for gradual purification, self-reliant effort, samatha-then-vipassana and the historical Buddha — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: gradual purification, self-reliant effort, samatha-then-vipassana and the historical Buddha (§3, Enlightenment; Self-Power vs. Other-Power; Meditation), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: other-power surrender and cosmic-Buddha devotion, because its canon and its commentaries stake liberation on effort guided by the Pali texts. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-theravada.svg

### Zen Buddhism — `dharma-zen.svg`
Zen is the school of Buddhism that emphasizes direct experience over doctrine, with meditation (zazen), koans and the guidance of a teacher at its centre. It holds that awakening is found in the present moment, here and now. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for sudden awakening, unspeakable truth, wu wei and direct teacher transmission — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: sudden awakening, unspeakable truth, wu wei and direct teacher transmission (§3, Enlightenment; Can the truth be spoken?; Action), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: scriptural gradualism and merit-by-recitation, because Zen's founding gesture is a special transmission outside the scriptures. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-zen.svg

### Pure Land Buddhism — `dharma-pure-land.svg`
Pure Land Buddhism centres on devotion to Amitabha Buddha and the aspiration to be reborn in his Pure Land, where awakening is easier to attain. Its central practice is the sincere recitation of Amitabha's name (nembutsu), open to ordinary people of any capacity. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for surrender to grace, liberation beyond this life for ordinary people, and devotional practice open to every capacity — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: surrender to grace, liberation beyond this life for ordinary people, and devotional practice open to every capacity (§3, Self-Power vs. Other-Power; Liberation in this life or beyond it?), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: self-power exclusivism — the claim that only the spiritually athletic can be saved is precisely what Amitabha's vow answers. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-pure-land.svg

### Tibetan Buddhism — `dharma-tibetan.svg`
Tibetan Buddhism, also called Vajrayana, is the form of Buddhism that developed in Tibet, combining Mahayana philosophy with tantric practices, guru devotion and meditation on compassion. It is best known in the West through the Dalai Lama and the Gelug tradition. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for tantra, the subtle body, guru devotion and compassion for all beings — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: tantra, the subtle body, guru devotion and compassion for all beings (§3, Tantra; Somatic & Yogic Subtle Body; Guru), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: the dismissal of ritual, deity yoga and lineage as superstition, because Vajrayana's methods are its identity. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-tibetan.svg

### Advaita Vedanta (Non-Dual Hindu Jnana) — `dharma-advaita-vedanta.svg`
Advaita Vedanta holds that the innermost Self (Atman) is identical with ultimate reality (Brahman), and that liberation (moksha) comes through discriminative knowledge (jnana) dissolving the illusion (maya) of separation. Its canon is the Principal Upanishads — Brhadaranyaka, Chandogya, Mandukya — read through the Brahma Sutras and Adi Sankara's commentaries, above all the Vivekacudamani, the 'Crest-Jewel of Discrimination'. The path is inquiry, not devotion or ritual: the guru and the scripture point, but only direct recognition — 'I am Brahman' — frees. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for nirguna Brahman, atman, jnana and liberation in this life — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: nirguna Brahman, atman, jnana and liberation in this life (§3, Brahman; Selfhood; Yoga: the path of knowledge), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: dualist devotion as a final position and caste-by-birth, because non-duality dissolves both the distance devotion presupposes and every hierarchy of souls. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-advaita-vedanta.svg

### Vaishnava Bhakti & Karma-Yoga (Devotional Householder) — `dharma-vaishnava-bhakti.svg`
Vaishnava Bhakti fulfills dharma inside active worldly and family life, offering every action without attachment to its fruits (karma-yoga) in loving personal devotion (bhakti) to the supreme Personal Divine. Its canon is the Bhagavad Gita and the Bhagavata Purana, read through Ramanuja's Sri Bhasya, which holds that the soul is real and eternally distinct from God, and reaches Him by surrender (prapatti) and grace. The householder's kitchen, work, and family are the temple; liberation is eternal loving service, not merger. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for saguna personal theism, karma-yoga in householder life, and liberation as loving service — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: saguna personal theism, karma-yoga in householder life, and liberation as loving service (§3, The Divine; The Six Classical Yogas; Way of life), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: impersonal absolutism as the last word and world-flight as the ideal, because its God is a person to be served in the world, not an abstraction beyond it. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-vaishnava-bhakti.svg

### Classical Confucianism (Ruism — Ritual & Civic Virtue) — `dharma-confucian.svg`
Classical Confucianism (Ruism) cultivates humane benevolence (ren) and moral character through filial piety (xiao), sacred ritual propriety (li), lifelong learning, and responsible stewardship of family and civic order. Its canon is Confucius's Analects with the Mencius and Xunzi, gathered by Zhu Xi into the Four Books. There is no escape from the world and no grace from above: the sage is made here, gradually, by studying the classics, rectifying the heart-mind, and serving well. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for ritual and ancestral duty, filial and civic propriety, gradual self-cultivation — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: ritual and ancestral duty, filial and civic propriety, gradual self-cultivation (§3, Ritual & Ancestral Efficacy; Confucian Social Order; Scripture and authority), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: withdrawal as an ideal and sudden-awakening claims, because the sage is made slowly, in society, or not at all. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-confucian.svg

### Jain Dharma (Ascetic Path) — `dharma-jain.svg`
Jain Dharma practices absolute non-violence (ahimsa) toward all living souls (jivas), non-possessiveness (aparigraha), and multi-perspectival truth (anekantavada), purging karmic matter from the soul through self-effort alone, without a creator deity. Its canon is the Acaranga Sutra and Umasvati's Tattvartha Sutra, the 'That Which Is' that systematizes the soul, karma, and liberation. The monk's five great vows are the strictest ethics in the Dharmic world; the soul is innately omniscient, and only the karmic dust it has gathered keeps it from seeing so. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for absolute ahimsa, the defiled soul cleansed by effort, many-sided truth and strict precepts — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: absolute ahimsa, the defiled soul cleansed by effort, many-sided truth and strict precepts (§3, Ahimsa; Innate Purity vs. Gradual Purification; Can the truth be spoken?), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: duty-exceptions to non-violence and grace without effort, because the soul frees itself or is not freed. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-jain.svg

### Sikh Gurmat (Khalsa Saint-Soldier & Householder Devotion) — `dharma-sikh.svg`
Sikh Gurmat rejects caste and empty ascetic withdrawal in favor of remembering the One Formless Creator (Naam Japna), earning an honest householder living (Kirat Karni), sharing with the community (Vand Chhako), and defending the oppressed as saint-soldiers (Sant-Sipahi). Its canon is the Guru Granth Sahib, compiled by Guru Arjan in 1604 and enthroned as eternal Guru in 1708, lived by the Sikh Rehat Maryada. The langar — free food for all, seated together regardless of birth — is the doctrine made visible. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for the living Guru, radical equality, the shared table and force against tyranny — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: the living Guru, radical equality, the shared table and force against tyranny (§3, Scripture and authority; Caste, Birth & Spiritual Eligibility; When is force justified?), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: ascetic withdrawal and birth-based rank, both of which the Gurus explicitly rejected in favour of the householder's engaged life. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-sikh.svg

### Philosophical Daoism (Daojia — Lao-Zhuang Tradition) — `dharma-daojia.svg`
Philosophical Daoism (Daojia) aligns life with the spontaneous, uncontrived flow of the Dao through effortless action (wuwei), naturalness (ziran), and simplicity, skeptical of rigid moralizing and artificial social hierarchy. Its canon is Laozi's Daodejing and the Zhuangzi — the uncarved block, the butterfly dream, the sage who does nothing and leaves nothing undone. Where Confucius builds the ritual order, the Daoist walks out of the city gate and lets the river carry him. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for wu wei, the unspeakable Dao, rusticated simplicity and acceptance within samsara — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: wu wei, the unspeakable Dao, rusticated simplicity and acceptance within samsara (§3, Action; Can the truth be spoken?; Confucian Social Order), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: ritual propriety as a final answer and deliberate striving as a virtue, because forcing is the disease the Dao is the cure for. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-daojia.svg

### Cārvāka / Lokāyata (Classical Indian Materialist) — `dharma-carvaka.svg`
The classical Indian materialist and empiricist school (Lokāyata — 'prevalent in the world'), attributed to Bṛhaspati and preserved through the Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha and Jayarāśi Bhaṭṭa. Holds direct sensory perception (pratyakṣa) as the sole reliable source of knowledge, recognizes only the four observable elements (earth, water, fire, air), views consciousness as an emergent property of the living body, rejects karma, rebirth, Vedic ritualism, and caste hierarchy, and affirms embodied flourishing (kāma and artha) in this life. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for no-karma, atheist-materialist and perception-only positions — those reasons are the tradition's own account of itself, drawn from its canon as summarised in its `about` text. Receptive to: no-karma, atheist-materialist and perception-only positions (§3, Karma; The Divine), because those positions share its account of what the path is. Closed to: every appeal to rebirth, revelation or priestly authority — not from hostility to the traditions' ethics of flourishing, which it shares, but because its epistemology admits no evidence for their metaphysics. Emblem: images/emblems/dharma-carvaka.svg

## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype.** No archetype primarily embodies: Shaktism or Shaivism as distinct Hindu paths (the Hindu archetypes are a non-dualist jnana school and a Vaishnava bhakti school); Daoist religious practice (Daojiao) as distinct from philosophical Daoism; Confucian practice in its modern or state forms; a Sufi, Sikh-missionary or diaspora-convert identity; or a secular mindfulness practitioner — the fastest-growing real-world holder of several of this map's meditation poles, who appears in §3's statistics but has no archetype. The Pudgalavada pole (Selfhood) is a position no surviving tradition holds, retained as the map's honest record of a lost school.

**Thin topics.** No topic on this skin carries a single pole; the thinnest coverage is Deity & The Divine, the smallest category at three topics, where the map's largest metaphysical questions are carried by comparatively few, very wide poles. Six topics are xy-switchboxes — Innate Purity vs. Gradual Purification (xdh0029), Self-Power vs. Other-Power (xdh0030), Ritual & Ancestral Efficacy (xdh0031), Confucian Social Order vs. Taoist Withdrawal (xdh0032), Caste, Birth & Spiritual Eligibility (xdh0033), and Somatic & Yogic Subtle Body (xdh0034): on each, the two poles are crossed, independent yes/no axes, not opposites, and the both-negative position is the status-quo middle; they are not flagged as defects.

**Skin-JSON problems (reported, not fixed).** (1) 15 topics have an empty `question` field; §2 above includes the recovered question for each (see §5.1), taken verbatim from that topic's hovertip issue, and the skin JSON itself is unchanged. (2) The four Buddhist archetypes — Theravada Buddhism, Zen Buddhism, Pure Land Buddhism and Tibetan Buddhism — each carry ratings on only 38 of the skin's 43 topics: all four are missing the same five topics, The Four Purusharthas (xdh0004), Archetypal energies (xdh0006), The Three Gunas (xdh0007), The Five Mahabhutas (xdh0008) and Brahman (xdh0020). The pattern is intelligible — those five are Hindu- and Chinese-framed topics — but it breaks the rule that every archetype rates every topic (a genuine no-stance would be a 0 rating), and it is reported here rather than repaired. The other seven archetypes rate all 43 topics.

**Deliberately left out of this research.** Abrahamic traditions except where a pole explicitly reaches them (the skin's domain is Dharmic and Taoic); modern political uses of these traditions, including nationalist movements, except where a pole is itself about social order; and any statistic not listed in §6 — where §3 could not ground a number in a source named with confidence, it says so rather than estimating. Doctrinal summaries follow each archetype's own `about` text and canon; contested readings are flagged inside §3 at the pole, not adjudicated here.
