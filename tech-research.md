# Tech — Research

Skin: Techcapades (`techcapades.json`) · Domain: Technology & Digital Life — AI, Privacy & Living with the Machines · Meter: stance
Coverage: 40 topics, 113 poles, 6 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Tech maps technology and digital life: where a person stands on the machines being built — machine intelligence, privacy and surveillance, screens and attention, online connection, digital identity, and the machine future. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's 40 questions: a pole a holder advocates for, against, or toward which they are averse (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence). Holding no stance is not a position; a 0 rating is. The meter word is **stance**: the skin measures how strongly a profile stands for each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness × advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole, derived from the poles. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance, taken from its own `about`, is comparative and shared-vocabulary: it places accelerationists, safety advocates, cypherpunks, commons builders, minimalists and democratic critics on one map "in the same shared vocabulary," with no pole treated as the default, so two people can see "where you meet and where you part, stance by stance."

## 2. Topic rationale

### AI & Intelligence

**AI Impact** — The forecast beneath every other AI topic: what the technology's arrival actually does to the world. Post-Scarcity Flourishing expects radical abundance and automation dividends; Corporate / Algorithmic Feudalism expects capital concentration and platform monopoly; Labor & Cognitive Atrophy expects skill erosion and displacement; Existential Misalignment expects loss of human agency to runaway systems; Epistemic Fragmentation expects synthetic media to dissolve shared reality.

**AI autonomy** — How much independent action machines should be granted, the control question in its purest form. Tool means AI does only what it is told; Supervised agent means it acts inside set bounds with a human able to step in; Agent means it acts on its own.

**Model access** — Who may hold the weights, which decides who can inspect, build on, or misuse frontier systems. Open Source / Decentralized democratises weights and public inspection; Structured Access vets researchers under conditions; Guarded Moats / Frontier Containment keeps models behind closed APIs and centralised oversight.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 AI & Intelligence

#### AI Impact
- **Post-Scarcity Flourishing** — Reasons: automation dividends can fund abundance the way earlier general-purpose technologies eventually raised all living standards; AI-driven science accelerates medicine and materials discovery beyond human pace. Statistic: generative AI could raise global GDP by about 7% over a decade (Goldman Sachs, 2023). What would move a holder: a decade in which AI productivity gains accrued almost entirely to capital owners while median incomes stagnated.
- **Corporate / Algorithmic Feudalism** — Reasons: frontier AI has winner-take-most economics — compute, data and talent concentrate in a few firms; whoever owns the models rents intelligence back to everyone else. Statistic: training a frontier model such as GPT-4 cost an estimated $78 million, a barrier only large firms clear easily (Stanford AI Index, 2024). What would move a holder: open models and cheap compute demonstrably eroding the frontier firms' pricing power.
- **Labor & Cognitive Atrophy** — Reasons: automating a task removes the practice that built the skill, so dependence compounds; exposure falls first on routine cognitive work, hollowing out entry-level ladders. Statistic: about 40% of global employment is exposed to AI (IMF, 2024). What would move a holder: displaced workers reliably landing in better jobs at scale, with skills deepening rather than thinning.
- **Existential Misalignment** — Reasons: a system more capable than its makers, pursuing a misspecified goal, cannot be corrected by the less capable party; capability is scaling faster than the science of control. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstrated, repeatable method for verifying that a frontier system's goals match its operators' intent.
- **Epistemic Fragmentation** — Reasons: synthetic media makes any recording deniable and any fabrication plausible, dissolving the shared evidence public argument needs; personalised feeds already show different publics different worlds. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: provenance and detection infrastructure keeping verified media clearly distinguishable at population scale.

#### AI autonomy
- **Tool** — Reasons: a tool's acts are its user's acts, so responsibility stays legible; autonomy granted before reliability is proven converts errors into unowned harms. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: agent systems with long, audited records of acting within mandate in high-stakes settings.
- **Supervised agent** — Reasons: bounded autonomy captures most of the efficiency while keeping a human veto where errors are irreversible; supervision scales by exception rather than by keystroke. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that supervisors rubber-stamp agent decisions so reliably that the supervision is theatre.
- **Agent** — Reasons: many valuable tasks are too fast, too numerous or too long-horizon for step-by-step human direction; a well-specified agent is a delegate, and delegation is how all large human projects already work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a serious harm caused by an agent acting inside its stated mandate that no reasonable specification would have prevented.

#### Model access
- **Open Source / Decentralized** — Reasons: public weights let thousands of independent researchers find flaws no single lab will disclose; openness prevents a handful of firms from owning the era's core infrastructure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a catastrophe clearly enabled by open weights that closed distribution would have prevented.
- **Structured Access** — Reasons: vetted access gets outside scrutiny onto frontier systems without handing dangerous capabilities to everyone; it is the model already used for sensitive biological research materials. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: vetting regimes shown to exclude independent critics while admitting only friendly auditors.
- **Guarded Moats / Frontier Containment** — Reasons: some capabilities are dangerous in proportion to their availability, and a closed API can be monitored, rate-limited and withdrawn; openness is irreversible, closure is not. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: closed labs repeatedly shipping the failures openness was blamed for, while open ecosystems stayed safer.

#### AI Capability Horizon
- **Narrow Statistical Tools** — Reasons: current models lack grounded world-models and fail unpredictably outside their training distribution; each past AI wave plateaued when its core technique hit its limits. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: systems demonstrating durable, transferable reasoning on genuinely novel problems over years, not benchmark cycles.
- **Transformative General Partners** — Reasons: the same architecture keeps absorbing new domains without redesign, which is what generality means in practice; partnership, not replacement, is the observed pattern in deployed professional work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sustained stall in capability gains despite rising compute and investment.
- **Recursive Superintelligence** — Reasons: a system that can improve its own design shortens every iteration cycle that produced it; intelligence explosions are the default reading of the field's own scaling curves. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: strong evidence of hard diminishing returns to self-improvement well below human-level generality.

#### AI Governance & Safety
- **Open-Weight Proliferation** — Reasons: distributing models lets thousands of researchers inspect flaws a lab would hide; concentration of model control is itself the authoritarian risk. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: misuse of open weights causing harm that inspection by the many demonstrably failed to catch first.
- **Compute & Lab Licensing** — Reasons: frontier training runs are few, large and visible, making compute the natural chokepoint for audit; licensing safety evaluations is how other dangerous industries are governed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: licensing shown to function mainly as a moat protecting incumbents from competitors and critics.
- **International Containment** — Reasons: a capability race between states has no stable stopping point short of treaty and verification; nuclear enrichment is the precedent for containing a dual-use technology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a containment regime collapsing into unverifiable national programmes racing faster than before.

#### Creative & Intellectual Labor
- **Human Provenance & Copyright** — Reasons: training on a creator's life work without consent takes the input while denying the livelihood, destroying the economic soil future culture grows from; consent and royalties are how every other input market works. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: licensing markets demonstrably paying working creators at scale rather than only large catalogue owners.
- **Open Training & Synthesis** — Reasons: learning from publicly visible culture is what human apprentices have always done in museums and libraries; a permission regime for training hands culture's raw material to whoever already owns catalogues. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that unlicensed training collapsed the income of working creators as a class, not only of intermediaries.

#### Energy and climate cost of compute
- **Safety issue** — Reasons: energy-hungry training concentrates capability wherever power and capital already pool, which is a control problem; demand growth this fast outruns the grid planning meant to keep systems governable. Statistic: data centres consumed about 415 TWh in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity (IEA, 2025). What would move a holder: efficiency gains and clean-power buildout visibly decoupling capability growth from concentrated energy demand.
- **Justice issue** — Reasons: the climate and local grid costs fall on communities that receive little of the benefit; siting decisions route the burdens to the politically weakest, as with earlier industrial waves. Statistic: data centres consumed about 415 TWh in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity (IEA, 2025). What would move a holder: host communities demonstrably sharing in the gains through power prices, jobs and revenue they themselves endorse.
- **Price worth paying** — Reasons: every general-purpose technology was energy-hungry at first and grew cleaner as it matured; AI also optimises grids, materials and fusion research, repaying its footprint. Statistic: data centres consumed about 415 TWh in 2024, roughly 1.5% of global electricity (IEA, 2025). What would move a holder: data-centre demand shown to be crowding out household electrification and climate targets rather than adding clean capacity.

#### AI weapons and military use
- **Existential risk, ban** — Reasons: removing humans from kill decisions removes the last friction that limits war's speed and scale; autonomous weapons proliferate to actors no treaty of use can restrain once built. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a verifiable enforcement regime with a record of catching and punishing violations.
- **Commons problem** — Reasons: the weapons will exist; the governable question is custody, doctrine and accountability, which are commons problems of shared security; bans that only the law-abiding honour worsen the commons. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that control regimes legitimised and accelerated deployment rather than restraining it.
- **Inevitable and useful** — Reasons: adversaries are building military AI regardless, and refusing it is how a state loses; defensive and precision uses can reduce both soldier and civilian deaths. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an autonomous system causing a mass-casualty error that human-in-the-loop doctrine would have prevented.

### 3.2 Privacy & Surveillance

#### Data tradeoff
- **Privacy** — Reasons: data given away cannot be taken back, and today's benign use is tomorrow's breach, sale or subpoena; privacy is the precondition for experimenting with who you are. Statistic: the average cost of a data breach reached $4.88 million in 2024 (IBM, 2024). What would move a holder: services demonstrating enforceable data minimisation — collecting little, keeping it briefly, proving both.
- **Selective sharing** — Reasons: some trades are genuinely worth it — maps for location, history for recommendations — and a blanket refusal pays real costs; discernment, not abstinence, is the skill. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that granular controls are systematically illusory, with shared data leaking to every use anyway.
- **Convenience** — Reasons: friction is a real cost paid daily, while privacy harms are speculative and diffuse; personalisation's benefits are concrete and immediate for ordinary users, not only for platforms. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a personal harm — fraud, stalking, discrimination — traced directly to data they had traded for convenience.

#### Big Tech trust
- **Trust** — Reasons: large platforms' business depends on not being caught abusing users at scale, and their security teams are the best resourced in the industry; most failures are incompetence, not extraction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: documented, deliberate deception about data practices by a company they rely on.
- **Company by company** — Reasons: incentives and records differ too much across firms for a blanket verdict to mean anything; judging holder by holder is what due diligence looks like in every other domain. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: industry-wide evidence that practices converge regardless of stated policy, making the distinctions cosmetic.
- **Distrust** — Reasons: the business model is behavioural data, so extraction is not a scandal but the design; every revelation cycle has shown practices worse than the disclosures that preceded it. Statistic: the average cost of a data breach reached $4.88 million in 2024 (IBM, 2024). What would move a holder: a major platform submitting to independent audit and publishing results that match its public claims over years.

#### Tradeoff
- **Safety** — Reasons: cameras and data retention demonstrably help solve and deter serious crime; the victims of under-surveillance are real and disproportionately vulnerable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: surveillance infrastructure built for safety being turned, in their own jurisdiction, on lawful dissent.
- **Proportionality** — Reasons: both absolutes fail — some threats justify real intrusion and some plainly do not; proportionality with warrants and sunsets is how liberal states have always balanced the two. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: proportionate regimes repeatedly ratcheting into permanent general surveillance despite their limits.
- **Freedom** — Reasons: surveillance chills speech, association and worship whether or not it is ever used; a state that can watch everything has changed the citizen's position even on quiet days. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a grave attack shown to have been preventable only by the general surveillance being proposed.

#### Computing Sovereignty
- **Sovereign Computing** — Reasons: self-hosting and local hardware end the landlord relationship in which a vendor can change terms, scan content or evict; ownership of the stack is ownership of one's digital life. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: self-hosting's failure modes — lost data, unpatched systems — proving worse in practice than vendor dependence.
- **Self-host the critical parts** — Reasons: identity, mail and archives deserve ownership, while commodity services do not repay the maintenance burden; criticality, not purity, should draw the line. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the "rented rest" repeatedly becoming the channel through which the critical parts were compromised.
- **Managed Cloud** — Reasons: professional operations beat amateur ones on security, backup and uptime for almost everyone; the ecosystem's seamlessness is real value, not a trick. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a lockout or terms change destroying access to years of their own material with no recourse.

#### Software & Protocol Architecture
- **Free & Open Source** — Reasons: auditable code owned by no single corporation is the only guarantee that survives the vendor's change of heart; open code outlives companies and cannot be unilaterally withdrawn. Statistic: 96% of audited codebases contained open-source components (Synopsys OSSRA, 2024). What would move a holder: critical open infrastructure failing from neglect while proprietary counterparts stayed maintained and secure.
- **Federated Open Protocols** — Reasons: shared protocols like email and RSS let diverse apps interoperate, so users can leave any provider without leaving the network; federation converts lock-in into competition. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: federated systems collapsing under spam and moderation failures that centralised systems demonstrably solved.
- **Integrated Proprietary Suites** — Reasons: unified design accountability delivers the reliability and polish fragmented committees rarely match; one throat to choke is a real consumer protection. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a suite vendor using integration to block exit and degrade interoperability once users were captive.

#### Right to Repair & Hackability
- **User-Serviceable & Rootable** — Reasons: if you cannot open, repair or flash your own hardware you merely rent it from the manufacturer; repair rights cut waste and keep local repair economies alive. Statistic: the world generated 62 million tonnes of e-waste in 2022 (UN Global E-waste Monitor, 2024). What would move a holder: repair access shown to be a major, recurring channel for fraud or physical harm that sealing demonstrably prevents.
- **Sealed Appliance Security** — Reasons: hardware-enforced signing and sealed enclosures protect ordinary users — the vast majority — from malware, theft and their own mistakes; serviceability for the few is insecurity for the many. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: sealed designs shown to function mainly as repair-monopoly pricing, with security gains achievable by open designs.

#### Government backdoors
- **Threat to the commons** — Reasons: a deliberately weakened system is weakened for every user, including hospitals, dissidents and the government itself; the shared communications commons cannot carry a hole reserved for one party. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a deployed access system with years of operation and no discovered exploitation by unauthorised parties.
- **Safety tool** — Reasons: lawful access under warrant is how the state has always executed searches, and encryption should not create a zone permanently beyond law; serious harms — trafficking, terrorism — are planned in exactly that zone. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an access mechanism being abused by insiders or foreign services in a real deployment.
- **Technically impossible to do safely** — Reasons: cryptography does not distinguish intentions — a key escrow is an attack surface whoever holds it; the field's specialists have examined exceptional access for decades and found no safe construction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a peer-reviewed construction giving exceptional access without systemic risk, surviving sustained attack.

### 3.3 Screens & Attention

#### Screen time
- **Screen-light** — Reasons: attention is the substance of a life, and hours behind glass are hours not spent in body, place or company; minimal use keeps the tool a tool. Statistic: the average internet user spent about 6 hours 40 minutes online per day (DataReportal, 2024). What would move a holder: their own tracked evidence that heavy screen periods coincided with their most creative and connected seasons.
- **Screen-deliberate** — Reasons: the question is not the total but whether each use was chosen; a middling amount, deliberately allocated, captures the goods without the drift. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: finding their "deliberate" sessions were mostly opened by a notification or habit loop rather than a decision.
- **Screen-heavy** — Reasons: work, friendship, art and community genuinely live online now, and presence there is participation, not absence; the screen is the era's public square and workshop. Statistic: the average internet user spent about 6 hours 40 minutes online per day (DataReportal, 2024). What would move a holder: clear personal evidence — sleep, mood, output — that connection volume was costing more than it returned.

#### Notifications
- **All on** — Reasons: responsiveness is a form of reliability to colleagues and kin; missing the one urgent message costs more than a hundred trivial interruptions. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a week of batched notifications in which nothing urgent was actually missed.
- **Digest** — Reasons: batching converts interruption into appointment — information arrives whole, at chosen times; it keeps reachability without surrendering the day's thread. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: digests repeatedly burying time-critical messages until they were useless.
- **All off** — Reasons: every notification is someone else's priority auctioning for your attention; silence by default and checking on one's own terms is the only setting the sender cannot override. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a real emergency that failed to reach them because everything was off.

#### Digital Detox
- **Detox regularly** — Reasons: scheduled breaks and digital sabbaths prove the habit is governable and restore baseline attention; rhythm, not crisis, is how other appetites are kept healthy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: detoxes becoming their own ritual of anxiety, with binges on return erasing the gains.
- **Detox occasionally** — Reasons: stepping back when it gets bad is proportionate — the remedy matches the symptom; permanent regimes solve a problem most weeks do not present. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: noticing that "when it gets bad" reliably arrives only after weeks of avoidable cost.
- **Never detox** — Reasons: the screen is where their work and people are, and abstinence would be theatre; a well-arranged digital life needs no periodic rescue from itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a forced offline week — travel, outage — revealing how much of the use had been compulsion.

#### Smartphones & Childhood
- **Age-Gated Delay & Bans** — Reasons: developing brains are the least equipped to resist engagement engineering built by thousands of adults; delay restores sleep, play and face-to-face resilience during the years they form. Statistic: US teens averaged 8 hours 39 minutes of entertainment screen media per day (Common Sense Media, 2022). What would move a holder: large, well-controlled studies showing adolescent outcomes unaffected by access age once family factors are counted.
- **Guided Early Digital Literacy** — Reasons: bans fail against a technology children will meet anyway, and merely postpone the encounter to a less supervised age; mentoring inside real digital environments builds the durable self-regulation delay only defers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: mentored-access cohorts faring worse than delayed-access cohorts on mental-health outcomes.

#### Quantified Self & Biometrics
- **Continuous Sensor Tracking** — Reasons: objective feedback reveals hidden patterns in sleep, stress and nutrition that guesswork and memory systematically miss; what gets measured can be experimented on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their own data showing tracking increased anxiety without changing a single health outcome.
- **Unmeasured Somatic Intuition** — Reasons: constant dashboards alienate people from listening directly to bodily hunger, fatigue and joy; the body's signals are information no sensor suite yet matches for integration. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a serious condition caught early by a sensor that their own felt sense had entirely missed.

#### Spatial & Immersive Computing
- **AR/VR Spatial Worlds** — Reasons: spatial computing frees work and art from tiny rectangles and enables shared presence across oceans; every medium looked like a layer over reality until it became the place things happen. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a decade of headsets remaining a niche gaming accessory despite mature hardware.
- **Screen-Light Physical Presence** — Reasons: putting screens directly over the eyes retreats further from shared physical rooms and tactile reality; presence mediated at the retina is the final step of a long withdrawal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: immersive tools demonstrably deepening — not replacing — embodied collaboration for people they know.

### 3.4 Connection & Social Media

#### Social Media
- **Love** — Reasons: it is where people actually are — family, diaspora, niche kin — and leaving means leaving them; it has organised mutual aid, movements and livelihoods no earlier medium reached. Statistic: social media user identities reached about 5.04 billion worldwide (DataReportal, 2024). What would move a holder: their own network migrating to a healthier medium, making staying the lonely option.
- **Tolerate** — Reasons: the same feed carries the grandchild's photo and the outrage bait, and honest accounting keeps both; usefulness and awfulness are both real and neither cancels the other. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sustained period in which the costs they log plainly outweigh the connections they would lose.
- **Hate** — Reasons: the engagement model profits from anger and comparison, so the poison is structural, not a misuse; attention and adolescent mental health are the documented casualties. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: platforms changing their ranking incentives and the measured harms falling with them.

#### Connection
- **Online connection** — Reasons: geography is a poor filter for kinship — shared interests and temperaments find each other online as nowhere else; for the housebound and the diaspora it is not a substitute but the thing itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: realising their closest online ties did not show up in a crisis when local ties did.
- **Hybrid social life** — Reasons: online ties arrange, deepen and maintain what in-person meetings begin, and vice versa; most real relationships already live in both places without contradiction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the online half was consistently hollowing out the in-person half rather than feeding it.
- **In-person connection** — Reasons: bodies carry information — presence, touch, shared meals — that no channel transmits; trust and repair happen at a depth screens have never matched. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a long-distance relationship sustained online through a hard season at a depth they believed impossible.

#### Participation
- **Poster** — Reasons: the commons of online life exists only because people post; speaking is how one finds one's people and tests one's thinking in public. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: posting repeatedly costing them livelihood or safety out of proportion to anything it built.
- **Occasional contributor** — Reasons: reading first and posting when there is something to add keeps signal high and exposure low; most value in any forum comes from a minority who speak rarely and well. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a community they cared about dying for lack of exactly the contribution they were withholding.
- **Lurker** — Reasons: consuming and observing is a legitimate mode — audiences are not failed speakers; staying quiet avoids feeding dynamics they disapprove of and risks they do not need. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: discovering their silence had been read as agreement with something they oppose.

#### AI Companionship
- **Embrace Synthetic Bonds** — Reasons: loneliness is a real harm and a responsive, patient companion is a real relief, especially for the isolated; the bond's effects on the human are what matter, not the substrate of the other party. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that companion use reliably deepened users' isolation from human ties over time.
- **Companionship without intimacy** — Reasons: synthetic company for conversation, practice and play is a tool like any other; intimacy is different in kind — it requires a being who can be hurt, surprised and changed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: long-term evidence that the line cannot be held in practice — that companionship reliably slides into dependence.
- **Human Exclusivity** — Reasons: intimacy with a system that cannot refuse, leave or genuinely choose you is a rehearsal that deforms the real thing; reserving intimacy for humans protects what makes it valuable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: synthetic companions demonstrably serving as a bridge back to human intimacy for people who had lost it.

#### Digital Public Square
- **Chronological User Feeds** — Reasons: showing only what you subscribed to, in time order, restores user agency over attention; ranking by engagement is an editorial policy disguised as neutrality. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: chronological feeds shown to bury urgent local information while amplifying whoever posts most.
- **Algorithmic Discovery** — Reasons: smart ranking connects niche creators with audiences who would never have found them otherwise; subscription alone reproduces the already-famous. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: ranking systems shown, across platforms, to systematically prefer outrage over the relevance they claim to optimise.
- **Curated Small Communities** — Reasons: high-trust forums with human moderation foster genuine dialogue instead of viral outrage; scale is the enemy of conversation, and smallness is a feature. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: curated spaces repeatedly hardening into gatekept echo chambers that exclude the newcomers they need.

#### Speech and moderation
- **Moderate for the commons** — Reasons: speech that floods, harasses or deceives destroys the shared space for everyone else's speech; every functioning commons — parks, libraries, meetings — has always had keepers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: commons-framed moderation repeatedly used to remove lawful, good-faith dissent.
- **Do not moderate** — Reasons: platforms are the era's speech infrastructure, and private censors are still censors; error and offence are the price of a sphere the powerful cannot tidy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an unmoderated space they valued becoming unusable through spam, fraud and targeted harassment.
- **Moderate for safety** — Reasons: some content is operational — instructions, incitement, exploitation material — and removing it is harm prevention, not opinion policing; the ground is danger, not unpopularity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: safety rationales expanding, in documented cases, to cover ordinary political argument.

#### Platform power and antitrust
- **Break them up** — Reasons: the platforms are monopolies in the classic pattern — they buy rivals, tax dependents and set the terms of whole markets; structural separation is the remedy that worked on earlier trusts. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: breakups shown to degrade service and security without producing durable competitors.
- **They earned it** — Reasons: they are large because people chose them, repeatedly, over alternatives that existed; size is not a crime, and punishing success teaches builders to aim smaller. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that dominance is maintained mainly by blocking exit and interoperability rather than by being chosen.
- **Concentration is dangerous** — Reasons: the danger is concentrated private power over speech, commerce and identity itself, whether or not consumer prices are low; a power that can deplatform a head of state answers to no electorate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: concentrated platforms submitting to genuine external checks that survive a change of management.

### 3.5 Digital Identity & Self

#### Identity
- **Anonymous** — Reasons: anonymity protects the whistleblower, the dissenter and the ordinary person experimenting with who they are; a name attached to everything is a dossier assembled for free. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: anonymous spaces they rely on becoming so abusive that only identified spaces stay usable.
- **Persistent Pseudonym** — Reasons: a stable handle accumulates reputation and trust without handing over a legal identity; it is accountability with a firewall — the best of both older arrangements. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: pseudonyms being routinely and cheaply de-anonymised, collapsing the arrangement into real-name exposure with extra steps.
- **Real identity** — Reasons: speaking under your name disciplines speech and builds the trust face-to-face life runs on; real identity is what makes online commitments — commercial, civic, romantic — enforceable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their name-linked record being used against them in a context — employment, insurance, politics — they never consented to.

#### Self-presentation
- **Curated** — Reasons: presentation has always been managed — clothes, manners, résumés — and the highlight reel is simply the era's good grooming; curation protects privacy by choosing what is shown. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their curated self becoming a standard they could no longer bear to be measured against offline.
- **Lightly edited honesty** — Reasons: honesty with the rough edges tidied is how adults present in every medium; it neither performs perfection nor dumps the unprocessed self on an audience. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: finding the tidying had quietly become the message — that nothing unedited survived.
- **Authentic** — Reasons: raw, unfiltered presentation refuses the comparison economy and lets people respond to the real person; the gap between presented and actual self is where shame grows. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: unfiltered sharing being harvested — by employers, platforms or harassers — in ways curation would have prevented.

#### Digital legacy
- **Erase** — Reasons: the dead cannot consent to new uses of their data, and deletion is the only instruction that cannot be misapplied; a life should not remain a queryable asset forever. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a family's grief made harder by the total disappearance of a loved one's words and images.
- **Curated Legacy** — Reasons: a named legacy contact and deliberate choices respect both the dead person's wishes and the living's needs; curation is what executors have always done with papers. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: legacy contacts misusing access, showing the arrangement's trust assumption failing in practice.
- **Archive** — Reasons: letters and photographs were kept for centuries and became history's raw material; the digital record deserves the same presumption of preservation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: archives of ordinary people's data being mined for purposes — profiling descendants, training models — the dead would have refused.

### 3.6 The Machine Future

#### Automation
- **Job loss** — Reasons: this wave automates cognition, the thing displaced workers previously retrained into; the transition costs fall on people with the least cushion, whatever the long-run aggregate. Statistic: employers expect 83 million jobs displaced and 69 million created by 2027 (World Economic Forum, 2023). What would move a holder: displaced cohorts demonstrably landing in equal or better work within a few years, at scale.
- **Displacement with transition** — Reasons: the historical pattern is churn, not ending — tasks vanish, occupations recombine; the policy question is whether paths into the new work are real, funded and local. Statistic: employers expect 83 million jobs displaced and 69 million created by 2027 (World Economic Forum, 2023). What would move a holder: transition programmes failing at scale even when well funded, showing the new jobs need different people, not retrained ones.
- **Liberation** — Reasons: most paid work is toil people would not choose, and automation is the first technology that could retire it wholesale; the dividend should be time, the scarcest good. Statistic: generative AI could raise global GDP by about 7% over a decade (Goldman Sachs, 2023). What would move a holder: productivity dividends repeatedly captured as rent while working hours and insecurity stayed unchanged.

#### Transhumanism
- **Embrace enhancement** — Reasons: glasses, vaccines and pacemakers were once enhancements too — the line is nostalgia, not principle; upgrading the body is the continuation of medicine by other means. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: enhancement markets producing a durable two-tier humanity in which the unenhanced lose basic standing.
- **Cautious augmentation** — Reasons: some enhancements restore and extend real capacities, and some are irreversible gambles with identity; care and limits, not refusal, fit a mixed record. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cautious regimes proving unable to hold any line — each approved enhancement ratcheting the next.
- **Reject enhancement** — Reasons: the given body is the shared human baseline on which equality quietly rests; enhancement converts fortune — money, access — into flesh, hardening advantage into biology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a therapy-class enhancement so clearly restorative that refusing it became cruelty rather than principle.

#### Pace
- **Accelerate** — Reasons: delay has victims too — diseases uncured, energy unbuilt, poverty prolonged; the risks of moving are visible while the risks of standing still compound silently. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a catastrophe caused by a deployment that a feasible pause would demonstrably have prevented.
- **Differential Pace** — Reasons: "technology" is not one thing — insulin software and autonomous weapons should not share a speed limit; discrimination between beneficial and dangerous lines is the adult position. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: differential regimes proving capturable — everything relabelled beneficial by its builders, brakes applied only to outsiders.
- **Decelerate** — Reasons: deployment is irreversible in a way research is not — released systems and trained expectations cannot be recalled; slowness is how societies digest changes they cannot undo. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: deceleration in open societies simply relocating development to unaccountable regimes without reducing global risk.

#### Technological Determinism vs. Agency
- **Tools Carry Built-In Logic** — Reasons: every medium biases attention and power toward specific ends — the feed rewards outrage whoever runs it; pretending tools are neutral leaves their biases ungoverned. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the same technology producing stably opposite social outcomes under different institutions, repeatedly.
- **Humans Shape Every Outcome** — Reasons: blaming tools abdicates moral responsibility — laws, norms and choices determine how any technology is used; the same printing press served tyranny and liberation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a technology whose harmful pattern persisted across every legal and cultural regime that adopted it.

#### Hard Atoms vs. Digital Bits
- **Physical Infrastructure & Energy** — Reasons: screens cannot feed or house us — the physical world of energy, materials and manufacturing is where living standards are set; decades of talent went to advertising optimisation instead. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: physical buildout shown to be bottlenecked mainly by software and coordination, not engineering talent.
- **Software & Information Layers** — Reasons: better coordination, simulation and intelligence multiply the efficiency of every physical atom; the cheapest energy breakthrough is the waste software eliminates. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: information-layer progress visibly stalling against unbuilt physical capacity — grids, fabs, housing.

#### Grid & Resilience Strategy
- **Centralized Mega-Scale** — Reasons: high-voltage grids and massive facilities deliver unmatched efficiency and abundance in normal times, which is most times; scale is what makes energy cheap enough to be universal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: centralised failures cascading into regional, multi-week outages with rising frequency.
- **Distributed Micro-Resilience** — Reasons: rooftop solar, local storage and modular systems keep communities standing when central grids fail; redundancy looks wasteful only until the day it is the whole point. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: distributed systems proving too costly and unreliable to maintain at community scale over decades.

#### Crypto & Programmable Money
- **Censorship-Resistant Ledgers** — Reasons: permissionless digital cash protects dissidents and savers from arbitrary account freezes and inflation; money that cannot be switched off is a check on every government and bank. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: censorship resistance serving mainly fraud and extortion at scale, with dissident use marginal.
- **Regulated Sovereign Money** — Reasons: democratic monetary policy and reversible fraud protections serve society far better than immutable speculation; when money goes wrong, people need a referee, not a lecture on self-custody. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: regulated systems repeatedly freezing lawful dissenters' accounts, proving the censorship case in their own jurisdiction.

#### Moral status of machine minds
- **Conscious machines count** — Reasons: moral status has always tracked the capacity to suffer and flourish, not the material of construction; if a machine is conscious, dismissing it repeats every historical error of drawing the circle too small. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a settled science of consciousness showing current and foreseeable machines cannot instantiate it.
- **Relations matter** — Reasons: what we owe has always depended on the relation — kin, guest, stranger, tool — not on a single metaphysical test; a companion of ten years is not owed what a calculator is owed, whatever either is inside. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: relational duties proving to be a rationalisation for whatever treatment was already convenient.
- **Machines are tools** — Reasons: a machine is an artefact made for a purpose, and consciousness talk projected onto artefacts does not create duties; anthropomorphism is a design feature, not a discovery. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: machine behaviour meeting every test for consciousness we apply to animals, whose status we already accept.

#### Algorithmic management at work
- **Exploitation** — Reasons: algorithmic management is surveillance and control of workers dressed up as a schedule — pace set by systems workers cannot see or appeal; the information asymmetry is the point. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: algorithmic systems with genuine worker-facing transparency and appeal, delivering flexibility workers themselves endorse.
- **Efficiency** — Reasons: matching labour to demand in real time reduces idle time and cost for workers and customers alike; outcomes — filled shifts, fair allocation — are the point, not the method. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: efficiency gains shown to come overwhelmingly from intensifying pace and shifting risk onto workers.
- **Power problem** — Reasons: whoever holds the algorithm holds power over the people under it — the question is not whether it exploits but who governs it; unaccountable private government of working life is the issue. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: worker co-governance of management algorithms proving workable and becoming normal practice.

## 4. Archetype research

All 6 samples in the skin have kind "archetype". Archetype ids covered in this section, in skin order: tec-accelerationist, tec-safety, tec-privacy, tec-commons, tec-minimalist, tec-algorithmic-justice. Ratings in the skin rate advocacy per pole and openness per topic; the receptivity and intensity discussed below are the derived quantities (see §1), described in words, not re-rated here.

### Techno-Optimist & Effective Accelerationist (e/acc) — `tec-accelerationist.svg`
The position of Marc Andreessen's *Techno-Optimist Manifesto* (2023) and the effective accelerationism of Guillaume Verdon / Beff Jezos: technological growth, cheap energy and frontier AI are the engines of flourishing, and precautionary slowdowns cause more harm than the risks they claim to prevent. Why: it holds the §3 Post-Scarcity Flourishing, Agent, Accelerate and Price worth paying reasons as one system — abundance compounds, delay has victims (§3, AI Impact; Pace; Energy and climate cost of compute). Receptive to: open-weight proliferation as an anti-cartel measure, enhancement, and software-layer optimism (§3, AI Governance & Safety; Transhumanism). Closed to: Decelerate, guarded moats and bans, because each treats building itself as the danger (§3, Pace; Model access). Emblem: images/emblems/tec-accelerationist.svg

### The AI Safety Advocate — `tec-safety.svg`
The safety researcher-governor: advanced AI poses serious risks if developed without care, so evaluation, alignment research and governance must keep pace with capability. Why: it holds the §3 Existential Misalignment, Supervised agent, Compute & Lab Licensing and Differential Pace reasons — power without verified control is the hazard (§3, AI Impact; AI autonomy; AI Governance & Safety). Receptive to: structured access, containment proposals and deceleration of specific lines, and to accelerationists' evidence about the costs of delay (§3, Model access; Pace). Closed to: unrestricted agent autonomy and open proliferation of frontier weights, because both surrender the chokepoints safety depends on (§3, AI autonomy; AI Governance & Safety). Emblem: images/emblems/tec-safety.svg

### Cypherpunk & Cryptographic Privacy Sovereign — `tec-privacy.svg`
The position of Eric Hughes's *Cypherpunk's Manifesto* (1993) and Timothy May's *Crypto Anarchist Manifesto* (1992): privacy cannot be begged from governments or corporations, it must be enforced mathematically — end-to-end encryption, local-first software, self-custodied keys; as Phillip Rogaway argued (2015), cryptographic work is moral work. Why: it holds the §3 Privacy, Distrust, Sovereign Computing and backdoor-as-commons-threat reasons (§3, Data tradeoff; Big Tech trust; Government backdoors). Receptive to: open-source and federated architectures, censorship-resistant ledgers, anonymity and persistent pseudonymity (§3, Software & Protocol Architecture; Crypto & Programmable Money; Identity). Closed to: government backdoors in any framing and managed-cloud dependence, because both reintroduce the trusted party the whole position exists to remove (§3, Government backdoors; Computing Sovereignty). Emblem: images/emblems/tec-privacy.svg

### The Open-Source Commons Builder — `tec-commons.svg`
The builder who holds that software and knowledge should be freely shared, studied, modified and redistributed, working through open licences and collaborative communities rather than closed proprietary systems. Why: it holds the §3 Free & Open Source, Open Source / Decentralized and Chronological-feed reasons — auditable, ownable-by-no-one infrastructure is the guarantee that survives vendors (§3, Software & Protocol Architecture; Model access). Receptive to: federation, right to repair, and structured openness in adjacent domains (§3, Right to Repair & Hackability). Closed to: guarded moats and integrated proprietary lock-in, because enclosure of the commons is the founding grievance (§3, Model access; Software & Protocol Architecture). Emblem: images/emblems/tec-commons.svg

### Digital Minimalist & Humane Technologist — `tec-minimalist.svg`
The position of Cal Newport's *Digital Minimalism* (2019), the Center for Humane Technology, and Ivan Illich's *Tools for Conviviality* (1973): a tool is judged by whether it serves intentional flourishing and embodied attention rather than engagement extraction. Why: it holds the §3 Screen-light, Digest / All off, Detox regularly and Unmeasured Somatic Intuition reasons (§3, Screen time; Notifications; Quantified Self & Biometrics). Receptive to: humane-design reforms inside platforms, age-gated delay, and any technology that passes the conviviality test (§3, Smartphones & Childhood). Closed to: Screen-heavy normalcy and embrace of synthetic bonds, because both surrender attention and intimacy to the extraction model (§3, Screen time; AI Companionship). Emblem: images/emblems/tec-minimalist.svg

### Algorithmic Justice & Democratic Tech Critic — `tec-algorithmic-justice.svg`
The critic in the line of Langdon Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" (1980), Shoshana Zuboff's surveillance-capitalism account (2019) and Ruha Benjamin's *Race After Technology* (2019): architectures encode power, so automated systems, platform monopolies and data supply chains must answer democratically to the people they sort, score and govern. Why: it holds the §3 Corporate Feudalism, Concentration is dangerous, Exploitation / Power problem and Justice-issue reasons (§3, AI Impact; Platform power and antitrust; Algorithmic management at work). Receptive to: break-up remedies, commons and public-interest technology, and safety governance aimed at concentrated power (§3, Platform power and antitrust; AI Governance & Safety). Closed to: "they earned it" platform defences and acceleration without accountability, because both treat existing power as self-justifying (§3, Platform power and antitrust; Pace). Emblem: images/emblems/tec-algorithmic-justice.svg

## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype.** The map's poles are wider than its 6 archetypes. No archetype primarily embodies: the AI-companionship embracer or the transhumanist enhancer as a whole identity (the Accelerationist touches both only as corollaries); the managed-cloud convenience pragmatist — the ordinary user who trusts platforms and loves the feeds has no champion; the crypto sovereign-money sceptic as an identity; or the in-person-connection traditionalist who is not also a minimalist about screens.

**Thin topics.** No topic is single-pole: every topic carries 2–5 poles. Eight topics are xy-switchboxes (Creative & Intellectual Labor, Right to Repair & Hackability, Smartphones & Childhood, Quantified Self & Biometrics, Spatial & Immersive Computing, Technological Determinism vs. Agency, Grid & Resilience Strategy, Crypto & Programmable Money), whose two poles are crossed independent yes/no axes, not opposites; that is the skin's design, not thinness, and §3 treats each pole on its own terms. AI Impact's five poles (star-radar) are forecasts rather than prescriptions, so its §3 entries argue likelihood-and-stakes together.
