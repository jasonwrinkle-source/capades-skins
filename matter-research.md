## 1. Domain framing

Matter maps applied physics: what the field is for, which domains and platforms deserve a physicist's allegiance, how the future gets powered, how materials are discovered and fabricated, and what physics owes life and medicine. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's 72 questions — a pole a holder advocates for, against, or toward which they are averse (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence). Holding no stance is not a position; a 0 rating is. The meter word is **understanding**: the skin measures how strongly a profile stands for each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness × advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole, likewise derived. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance is builder's pluralism: mechanism-first purists, device-first pragmatists, and every platform partisan are placed on one map in one vocabulary, with manufacturability and replication treated as first-class virtues rather than bureaucratic afterthoughts.

## 2. Topic rationale

### The Applied Physics Philosophy & Strategy

**Applied goal** — The founding fork of the field: what applied physics is for. Fundamental Physical Mechanism First refuses to call a device understood until its microscopic mechanism is explained; Mechanism in service of the metric understands the mechanism far enough to move the figure of merit; Device Figure of Merit First optimizes the metric — Q-factor, efficiency, mobility, noise figure — and treats microscopic detail as secondary.

**Materials discovery** — How new materials get found. Rational Computational Design predicts structures, computes bandgaps and screens databases before touching a precursor; Design-build loop makes compute–synthesize–measure–repeat itself the method; Edisonian Empirical Synthesis mixes, fires and follows bench intuition, trusting nature to surprise the models.

**Autonomous Self-Driving Labs vs. Artisanal Synthesis Craft** — Who or what runs the bench. Closed-Loop Self-Driving Robotic Labs pair robotic synthesis with active-learning ML to explore phase diagrams faster than humans; Human-in-the-Loop Assisted Automation keeps expert experimentalists controlling verification and edge cases; Artisanal Hands-On Experimental Craft holds that pristine crystals and metastable phases still require seasoned bench intuition.

**TRL horizon** — Where on the readiness ladder a physicist lives. Low-TRL Deep Physics Discovery celebrates exotic phenomena in dilution fridges and 14-tesla fields regardless of manufacturability; Mid-TRL translation works the band carrying discoveries toward scale; High-TRL Industrial Scalability demands room-temperature operation, earth-abundant elements, CMOS compatibility and wafer-scale yield.

**Orientation** — The lean inside the applied. Fundamental treats the physics itself as the point, the device a way of learning; Use-inspired chooses fundamental questions inside an applied field; Applied treats the application as the point and the physics as a means.

**More is different** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “When new things appear at bigger scales, is that complicated physics, or a new law?” Reduction holds that emergence is complicated reduction and the lower law is the whole story; Real and irreducible holds that new scales have their own laws; Real but grounded holds that higher-level laws are real and rest on lower-level ones.

**Confronting the Synthesizability & Scale-Up Gap** — How strictly to police hero devices. Hero Proof-of-Concept Device Suffices lets early breakthroughs publish best-device performance first; Report Variability & Stability Early requires device-to-device spread, lifetime and synthesis sensitivity alongside peak results; Judge by Manufacturable Cost-Performance holds claims to scalable synthesis, yield and operational lifetime from the start.

**Anti-Hype & Extraordinary-Claim Discipline** — A single-pole affinity topic: how uncompromising a holder's skepticism is toward unreplicated breakthrough claims (negative ratings register aversion to that skepticism). Its one pole, Ruthless Skepticism Toward Unreplicated Breakthrough Claims, demands independent replication, raw data and public audit before crediting extraordinary claims, after the superconductor retractions and the LK-99 audit.

**Bridging the Hardware "Valley of Death"** — Which institution carries devices from proof-of-concept to factory. Academia Hands Off at TRL 3 has universities prove principles and exit; Public-Private Pilot Foundries (IMEC Model) uses shared pre-competitive pilot fabs and national hubs for the TRL 4–7 gap; Vertically Integrated Corporate R&D Labs unites physics and manufacturing under one roof.

**Lifecycle Sustainability of Materials Innovation** — When green constraints bind. Solve Performance First, Green Later proves figures of merit first and substitutes inputs later; Co-Optimize Performance & Lifecycle weighs energy payback, toxicity and recyclability during development; Reject Toxic / Non-Circular Tech Upfront filters out toxic or non-recyclable concepts before scale-up investment.

**Independent Device Certification & Standardized Reporting** — How tightly to standardize claims. Flexible Exploratory Metrics keeps protocols lightweight for fast-moving fields; Standardized Reporting Checklists requires uniform checklists and error bars without third-party testing; Mandatory Independent Certification Labs adopts NREL-style certification before headline records enter the literature.

**Critical Minerals & Material Circularity Constraint** — A single-pole affinity topic: how strongly critical-mineral scarcity should constrain device physics (negative ratings register aversion to the constraint). Its one pole, Earth-Abundant & Recyclable Design Mandate, rejects or deprioritizes concepts reliant on scarce minerals without a recycling or substitution path.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 The Applied Physics Philosophy & Strategy

#### Applied goal
- **Fundamental Physical Mechanism First** — Reasons: a device whose mechanism is unexplained cannot be systematically improved — every optimization is a lottery ticket; mechanism knowledge is the only asset that transfers when the device generation turns over. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a device lineage that kept breaking records for a generation on pure metric optimization, with mechanism work demonstrably contributing nothing.
- **Mechanism in service of the metric** — Reasons: understanding is instrumentally valuable exactly to the depth that moves the figure of merit, and deeper mechanism is a research luxury the project plan cannot always afford; this is how working device groups actually allocate effort. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: repeated cases where stopping at the metric stranded a programme that mechanism-first rivals then leapfrogged.
- **Device Figure of Merit First** — Reasons: customers, grids and patients buy metrics — efficiency, noise figure, Q — not explanations; history's transformative devices (the transistor radio, the LED) won on figures of merit while mechanism arguments continued in journals. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a record device that proved unmanufacturable or unreliable for want of mechanism understanding, in the holder's own field.

#### Materials discovery
- **Rational Computational Design** — Reasons: computation screens thousands of candidates for the cost of one synthesis, and DFT databases (Materials Project and kin) have already delivered battery and catalyst candidates to experiment; searching chemical space blind, when it can be searched in silico first, wastes the scarcest resource — skilled bench time. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sustained audit showing computationally predicted materials fail synthesis or stability checks at rates no better than chance.
- **Design-build loop** — Reasons: computation is wrong in instructive ways and synthesis is surprising in instructive ways, and the loop converts both errors into information fastest; most real discoveries of the database era were loop products — predicted, made, measured, re-predicted. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either pure computation or pure empiricism demonstrably out-discovering the loop per dollar across a field.
- **Edisonian Empirical Synthesis** — Reasons: the canonical materials — high-Tc cuprates, iron superconductors, most catalysts — were found by mixing and measuring, not by prediction, and models still cannot rank synthesizability; nature's phase diagrams contain phases no screening would have proposed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: computational design delivering a flagship material class that empirical search had missed for decades.

#### Autonomous Self-Driving Labs vs. Artisanal Synthesis Craft
- **Closed-Loop Self-Driving Robotic Labs** — Reasons: active-learning robots explore phase diagrams orders of magnitude faster than benches and never sleep, and early platforms have already synthesized dozens of predicted inorganic compounds autonomously; human attention is the bottleneck of materials discovery and this removes it. Statistic: an autonomous laboratory reported synthesizing 41 novel compounds from 58 targeted predictions in 17 days (Szymanski et al., Nature, 2023). What would move a holder: audits showing autonomous platforms' "novel compounds" are disproportionately mischaracterized or trivial.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Assisted Automation** — Reasons: robots excel at screening volume while experts excel at noticing the anomalous run, the misindexed pattern, the phase that should not exist; keeping experts on verification and edge cases captures the speed without automating away the judgement that catches machine error. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: fully closed loops matching human-in-the-loop quality on verification, or automation adding cost without discovery.
- **Artisanal Hands-On Experimental Craft** — Reasons: pristine single crystals and metastable phases still come from crucible intuition accumulated over decades — Canfield-style flux growth has no robotic equivalent; the field's sample quality ceiling is set by craftspeople, and every characterization advance rides on their crystals. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a robotic platform growing crystals of quality matching the best artisanal flux growth, in a hard system.

#### TRL horizon
- **Low-TRL Deep Physics Discovery** — Reasons: every technology in this skin began as an exotic phenomenon nobody could manufacture — superconductivity spent 75 years as cryogenic exotica before MRI; judging deep physics by manufacturability would have strangled it at birth. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstrated pattern of low-TRL exotica failing to seed any technology across a generation, while translation-band work delivered.
- **Mid-TRL translation** — Reasons: the translation band is where discoveries die for lack of owners — too applied for physics funding, too risky for industry — so staffing it is the highest-leverage career choice in the field; translators who speak both languages are the scarcest people in the pipeline. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence the valley is adequately staffed and funded, making the marginal translator redundant.
- **High-TRL Industrial Scalability** — Reasons: a phenomenon that only works at millikelvin in a 14-tesla magnet helps no patient, grid or customer; room-temperature, earth-abundant, CMOS-compatible, high-yield is the filter that separates physics from technology, and applying it early wastes the least money. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a low-TRL exotic phenomenon that scaled despite violating every scalability criterion at discovery.

#### Orientation
- **Fundamental** — Reasons: even inside applied physics, the durable contributions are the fundamental ones — the device is forgotten, the mechanism is textbook; choosing fundamental questions inside applied fields (Pasteur's quadrant) has produced both Nobels and industries. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a career-audit showing applied-first work in their field out-lasting and out-citing the fundamental work.
- **Use-inspired** — Reasons: Stokes' Pasteur quadrant is empirically where the field's greatest figures lived — Bell Labs being the standing proof; use-inspired choice gets fundamental depth with a relevance filter that keeps questions honest. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that use-inspiration systematically distorts question choice away from where the deep physics is.
- **Applied** — Reasons: the application is the deliverable — a solved problem, a shipped device — and fundamental questions have their own departments; applied physics earns its social licence by usefulness, and drifting fundamental betrays the brief. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: applied projects in their field repeatedly failing for want of the fundamental understanding the holder declined to pursue.

#### More is different
- **Reduction** — Reasons: every emergent law yet examined rests on lower-level physics with no contradiction and no remainder — superconductivity is BCS, chemistry is quantum mechanics; "new laws" at higher scales are bookkeeping conveniences over the one true lower story. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a higher-level law proven irreducible in principle — not merely underived in practice.
- **Real and irreducible** — Reasons: Anderson's argument stands — the constructionist hypothesis fails at each scale, and genuinely new principles (broken symmetry, topology) appear that lower-level vocabulary cannot state; the working sciences of each scale are autonomous in fact, whatever reduction promises in principle. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: derivations of the flagship emergent laws that preserve their explanatory content, showing irreducibility was temporary ignorance.
- **Real but grounded** — Reasons: higher-level laws do real explanatory work — no one designs a bridge from quarks — yet nothing at any scale violates the levels below; grounding without elimination is the position that matches both the practice and the metaphysics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either a true in-principle irreducibility proof (toward Real and irreducible) or a derivation programme dissolving the higher laws' autonomy (toward Reduction).

#### Confronting the Synthesizability & Scale-Up Gap
- **Hero Proof-of-Concept Device Suffices** — Reasons: breakthroughs need room to be breakthroughs — the first transistor and the first perovskite cell were hero devices with terrible statistics; demanding yield data at the proof-of-concept stage filters out exactly the fragile novelties the field exists to find. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field visibly damaged — funding, credibility — by hero-device claims that collapsed, beyond what early variability reporting would have cost.
- **Report Variability & Stability Early** — Reasons: a record without a spread is advertising, and perovskite photovoltaics' credibility crisis taught the field that lifetime and variability are part of the result; early reporting costs little and is the difference between a claim and a measurement. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that early-reporting requirements measurably suppress genuine breakthrough submissions.
- **Judge by Manufacturable Cost-Performance** — Reasons: the graveyard of applied physics is full of record devices that could never be made twice; cost-performance at scale is the only metric the eventual users will ever see, so it is the honest one from day one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hero device, initially unmanufacturable by the holder's criteria, that became a manufactured technology without a fundamental redesign.

#### Anti-Hype & Extraordinary-Claim Discipline
- **Ruthless Skepticism Toward Unreplicated Breakthrough Claims** — Reasons: the field's recent record — retracted room-temperature superconductivity papers, the LK-99 episode consuming global laboratory effort on an artefact — shows extraordinary claims imposing extraordinary costs; replication, raw data and public audit before credit is simply the claim's price of admission, proportional to its extraordinariness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a genuine breakthrough that ruthless skepticism delayed or suppressed, where credulity would have served science better.

#### Bridging the Hardware "Valley of Death"
- **Academia Hands Off at TRL 3** — Reasons: universities are structurally bad at manufacturing — no pilot lines, no yield engineers, tenure incentives against it — and good at exactly one thing, proving principles; early hand-off to startups and industry puts each stage with the institution built for it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: data showing university-carried technologies crossing the valley at higher rates than handed-off ones.
- **Public-Private Pilot Foundries (IMEC Model)** — Reasons: IMEC's decades of carrying process technology across the pre-competitive gap is the model's existence proof; shared pilot fabs spread the valley's enormous fixed costs across an industry no single startup could carry them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: pilot-foundry programmes failing to graduate technologies at rates beating the alternatives, cost-adjusted.
- **Vertically Integrated Corporate R&D Labs** — Reasons: Bell Labs and its modern descendants unite the physicist and the fab in one feedback loop, and the transistor-to-system pipeline is the historical gold standard; integration removes the hand-off interfaces where valley deaths actually occur. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the integrated labs' modern record thinning to nostalgia, with valley crossings happening elsewhere.

#### Lifecycle Sustainability of Materials Innovation
- **Solve Performance First, Green Later** — Reasons: a technology that does not work saves no planet, and input substitution is a solved genre of engineering once the platform exists — lead left solder and petrol after the platforms worked; sequencing performance first is how every incumbent green technology actually arrived. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a platform locked into a toxic input so deeply that later substitution proved impossible, in the holder's own field.
- **Co-Optimize Performance & Lifecycle** — Reasons: lifecycle costs are design variables, and retrofitting them is systematically more expensive than co-designing them; energy payback, toxicity and recyclability are knowable early, and ignoring knowable constraints is not focus but negligence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: co-optimization demonstrably killing viable technologies that performance-first rivals later greened successfully.
- **Reject Toxic / Non-Circular Tech Upfront** — Reasons: deploying a toxic or non-recyclable technology at scale creates a waste legacy that dwarfs its performance benefit — the lead and asbestos precedents are the field's own history; an upfront filter is the only point where the decision is cheap. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rejected-upfront technology proving both indispensable and safely manageable, with no substitute.

#### Independent Device Certification & Standardized Reporting
- **Flexible Exploratory Metrics** — Reasons: new device classes do not yet know what their right metrics are, and premature standardization freezes the wrong numbers into gatekeeping; NREL-style certification fits a mature field (solar) and would smother an infant one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a young field whose literature became unusable through metric chaos before any standard emerged.
- **Standardized Reporting Checklists** — Reasons: uniform checklists and error bars (the ISOS protocols for perovskite stability are the model) make literatures comparable without third-party cost; most irreproducibility in device physics is reporting variance, not fraud, and checklists fix reporting. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: checklist regimes decaying into box-ticking that certifies form while missing substance.
- **Mandatory Independent Certification Labs** — Reasons: the NREL efficiency chart's authority comes precisely from independence — self-reported records in photovoltaics diverged from certified ones often enough to require it; headline claims move markets and funding, so headline claims should carry laboratory verification. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: certification bottlenecks measurably delaying a field, or certified and self-reported records converging so completely that the mandate is redundant.

#### Critical Minerals & Material Circularity Constraint
- **Earth-Abundant & Recyclable Design Mandate** — Reasons: a device physics built on scarce, geopolitically concentrated minerals exports its costs to supply chains that have already shown their fragility (rare-earth and cobalt precedents); designing for abundance and recyclability at the concept stage is the only point where substitution is free. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a scarce-mineral technology demonstrating secure, circular supply at scale, relaxing the constraint in fact.

### 3.2 Domains & Scale

#### The Four Domains
- **Condensed Matter & Quantum Materials** — Reasons: condensed matter is the largest and most generative domain — superconductivity, topology, 2D materials and spintronics all live here, and it feeds every other domain its materials; it is also where "more is different" is a daily laboratory fact. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: another domain demonstrably out-producing condensed matter in fundamental surprises per researcher over a generation.
- **Optics, Photonics & Lasers** — Reasons: light is the field's fastest, cleanest carrier — photonics underlies communications, sensing, imaging and now computing's interconnect frontier; lasers created entire sciences (nonlinear optics, attosecond physics) by existing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: photonics' frontier problems migrating decisively into other domains' territory (electronics solving interconnects, say).
- **Plasmas & High-Energy-Density Physics** — Reasons: plasma is the universe's dominant state of matter and the medium of both fusion energy and semiconductor etching — one domain spanning stars and fabs; high-energy-density physics accesses regimes (stellar interiors, planetary cores) no other domain can reach. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: fusion's timeline collapsing into another domain's solution, or plasma processing being displaced wholesale.
- **Biological & Soft Condensed Matter Physics** — Reasons: soft and biological matter is where physics meets the systems that matter most to humans — cells, membranes, tissues — with its own laws (active matter) found nowhere else; the domain is young, under-occupied relative to its importance, and rich in unclaimed fundamental questions. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: soft matter's flagship questions resolving into ordinary condensed matter or ordinary biochemistry, leaving no distinct physics.

#### Miniaturization
- **Continue** — Reasons: reports of scaling's death have been annual for forty years, and each wall (lithography, tunneling, heat) has so far been engineered around — gate-all-around, 3D stacking, chiplets; the trend's engine is economic as much as physical, and it has not stalled. Statistic: leading-edge production reached the 3-nanometre node class in 2023, with Apple's M3 family built on it at 25 billion transistors in its base configuration (Apple, 2023). What would move a holder: a node generation that ships late, at worse cost per transistor, with no successor on any roadmap.
- **Slowing, not stopped** — Reasons: Dennard scaling ended around 2005 and cost per transistor has flattened at recent nodes, yet density and capability still improve — the data describe a deceleration, not a wall; this position simply refuses both the hype and the obituary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either a new scaling discontinuity (toward Continue) or a generation with no density gain at any cost (toward Plateau).
- **Plateau** — Reasons: transistors now number their atoms in the dozens across critical dimensions, tunneling leakage is physics rather than engineering, and the industry's own centre of gravity has moved to packaging and architecture — the behaviours of a field past its scaling era; the future is elsewhere: integration, specialization, new devices. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a post-3nm generation delivering historical-rate density and cost gains from dimensional scaling alone.

#### Tradeoff
- **Reliability** — Reasons: field failure costs dwarf laboratory performance gains — a grid inverter, implant or satellite component that fails early destroys the value its peak numbers created; reliability is also the metric users actually experience. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a market in their field where fragile record devices consistently beat reliable ones in total deployed value.
- **Performance within reliability bounds** — Reasons: engineering is constrained optimization, and the reliability envelope is a constraint like any other — inside it, maximize performance without apology; the position is simply the professional standard stated plainly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: domains where the envelope itself is the contested variable, forcing a purer stance.
- **Performance** — Reasons: peak performance is what opens new regimes — the fastest laser, the most efficient cell, the record magnet create the headroom everyone else's reliability engineering then occupies; records are the field's research frontier, and frontier work is judged by the peak. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: record devices in their field repeatedly proving unusable even as research platforms.

#### Metallic Plasmonics vs. Lossless All-Dielectric Metasurfaces
- **Sub-Diffraction Metallic Plasmonics** — Reasons: nothing else confines light to nanometre hot spots — single-molecule sensing, SERS and nonlinear enhancement at volumes dielectrics cannot approach; loss is the price of confinement, and for sensing the trade is worth it every time. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: dielectric structures matching plasmonic field confinement and enhancement in a working sensor.
- **Application-Split Metal + Dielectric** — Reasons: the two material classes win in different functions — metals localize, dielectrics propagate — and insisting on one for both is ideology, not engineering; hybrid metal–dielectric systems already dominate real metasurface designs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one class capturing the other's flagship function outright.
- **Low-Loss All-Dielectric Photonics** — Reasons: ohmic loss is not a detail — it caps efficiency, heats devices and kills propagation, while high-index dielectrics achieve Mie-resonant wavefront control at CMOS-compatible losses; for the photonic systems that must scale, losslessness is the requirement and dielectrics meet it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a plasmonic system-level application (not a hot-spot demo) beating its dielectric rival on efficiency.

#### Integrated Frequency Combs & Thin-Film Lithium Niobate Photonics
- **Investing in Chip-Scale Microcombs & Integrated Nonlinear Photonics** — Reasons: frequency combs won a Nobel as room-sized instruments; soliton microcombs and thin-film lithium niobate put the same physics on millimetre chips, opening optical clocks, spectroscopy, LiDAR and interconnects to mass deployment; the platform's components (modulators, combs, detectors) compound on one substrate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: chip-scale combs stalling on power or stability limits that benchtop systems do not share, across a product generation.

#### High-Power Laser Architecture: Fiber vs. Solid-State vs. Free-Electron
- **Industrial Yb/Er Fiber Lasers** — Reasons: fiber lasers already won the industrial market — efficiency, robustness and beam quality at kilowatt scale for cutting, welding and additive manufacturing; impact is measured in deployed systems, and fiber's deployment dwarfs the alternatives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: another architecture taking fiber's industrial share on efficiency or cost.
- **Ultrafast Chirped-Pulse Solid-State** — Reasons: chirped-pulse amplification (Nobel-recognized) opened peak powers that created attosecond science, precision micromachining and inertial-fusion drivers; peak power is the frontier variable, and solid-state CPA owns it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: fiber architectures reaching comparable peak powers with their efficiency advantage intact.
- **Coherent Tunable Free-Electron Lasers** — Reasons: X-ray FELs are uniquely transformative instruments — atomic-scale movies of chemistry and materials that no other source can make, as LCLS and European XFEL demonstrate; the payoff is a new observational science, not an incremental laser. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: tabletop sources (high-harmonic, plasma) matching FEL brightness and tunability for the flagship experiments.

#### Topological Photonics: Practical Disorder Immunity or Fragile Demo?
- **Overstated vs. Conventional Waveguides** — Reasons: production silicon and nitride waveguides already achieve losses and footprints topological lattices cannot match, and topological protection protects against a narrow class of disorder while adding bulk; a decade in, the flagship applications remain demonstrations. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a topological photonic product beating conventional waveguides on a deployed system's specifications.
- **Valuable Niche for Lasers & Isolators** — Reasons: topological lasers demonstrably enforce single-mode operation in arrays, and nonreciprocal isolators are a genuine pain point where edge-state physics offers something conventional design lacks; niches with real pain are how new physics enters technology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the niches being filled conventionally, or topological designs graduating to general routing (promoting the position).
- **Next-Gen Backscatter-Immune Photonic Circuits** — Reasons: as photonic circuits densify, fabrication disorder becomes the yield limiter, and protection that is topological — guaranteed by a global invariant, not by tolerances — scales in a way trimming cannot; today's demos are the transistor-in-1948 stage of the architecture. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: disorder-immune routing failing to materialize in foundry-fabricated topological circuits at scale.

#### On-Chip Quantum Light Sources: Spontaneous Parametric vs. Solid-State Emitters
- **CMOS-Ready Nonlinear Parametric (SPDC/SFWM)** — Reasons: parametric sources run at room temperature on CMOS-compatible platforms today, and multiplexing converts their probabilistic nature into an engineering problem with known solutions; manufacturability now beats determinism later for a field that needs systems, not heroes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: multiplexing overheads (switching loss, feedforward latency) proving fatal at system scale.
- **Heterogeneous Hybrid Integration** — Reasons: bonding the best emitters (III–V dots, diamond defects) onto the best circuits (silicon nitride) takes each material at its strength instead of demanding one material do everything; heterogeneous integration is how the semiconductor industry itself solved the same dilemma. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: bonding yield or emitter–circuit coupling losses defeating the hybrid at scale.
- **Deterministic Quantum Dots & Color Centers** — Reasons: quantum protocols ultimately need photons on demand with high purity and indistinguishability — quantum-dot sources have demonstrated among the highest single-photon purities and indistinguishabilities of any platform; probabilistic sources pay a multiplexing tax that grows with system size. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: parametric multiplexed systems reaching fault-tolerant photonic architectures first, at scale.

#### Tribology, Interfaces & Corrosion Stewardship
- **Elevating Surfaces, Friction, Wear & Corrosion as First-Class Applied Physics** — Reasons: friction and wear are not engineering residue — they are where a fifth to a quarter of the world's energy goes, and corrosion consumes a comparable share of GDP in replacement and protection; the prestige hierarchy that ranks these below exotic phases is measuring fashion, not impact. Statistic: friction and wear consume roughly 20–25% of global energy use (Holmberg & Erdemir, Friction, 2017). What would move a holder: a credible accounting showing friction, wear and corrosion losses far smaller than the cited estimates.

### 3.3 Quantum Technology & Hardware

#### Quantum Computing Hardware
- **Superconducting Circuits** — Reasons: transmon-style qubits combine nanosecond gates, lithographic fabrication and the deepest industrial investment (Google, IBM) of any platform; below-threshold surface-code scaling has now been demonstrated on this platform, which is the field's central milestone. Statistic: Google Quantum AI demonstrated quantum error correction below the surface-code threshold, with logical error falling by a factor of about 2.14 per step in code distance (Google Quantum AI and Collaborators, Nature, 2024). What would move a holder: another platform demonstrating below-threshold scaling with better overhead or fidelities.
- **Trapped Ions** — Reasons: atomic qubits are identical by nature — no fabrication variance — with the highest demonstrated gate fidelities and all-to-all connectivity that removes routing overhead; the platform's physics is the cleanest, and its engineering (shuttling, modular photonic links) is progressing on schedule. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: ion systems' gate speeds and scaling demonstrably capping below solid-state rivals' fault-tolerant thresholds.
- **Neutral Atom Arrays** — Reasons: tweezer arrays have scaled to thousands of atoms with reconfigurable geometry — an order of magnitude more qubits than any rival — and Rydberg gates plus transversal logical operations have moved the platform from analogue simulator to digital contender; scale itself is a capability. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: array fidelities plateauing below fault-tolerance requirements while qubit counts stop mattering without them.
- **Photonic Quantum Computing** — Reasons: photons run at room temperature, network natively and ride telecom fabrication — fusion-based architectures turn source and detector engineering into a scaling path with no cryogenics; modularity is built in rather than bolted on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: loss budgets in sources, switches and detectors proving insurmountable at the photon numbers fault tolerance requires.
- **Silicon Spin Qubits** — Reasons: spins in isotopically purified silicon are CMOS-compatible — the only platform the world's foundries already know how to manufacture at billion-unit scale; two-qubit fidelities above fault-tolerance thresholds have been demonstrated, removing the physics objection and leaving engineering. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: uniformity and yield across foundry-fabricated qubit arrays failing where laboratory devices succeeded.
- **Topological Qubits** — Reasons: hardware-level protection would collapse the error-correction overhead that dominates every other platform's resource estimates — the difference between millions and thousands of physical qubits; the prize is so large that a disciplined long-term programme is rational even after setbacks. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a definitive demonstration that the observed Majorana signatures are entirely trivial Andreev physics, closing the materials route.

#### Quantum strategy
- **Near-Term NISQ Optimism** — Reasons: error mitigation has extracted useful results from noisy machines, and variational methods target exactly the chemistry and materials problems classical machines find hardest; commercial value does not require fault tolerance, only advantage on a paid problem. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a systematic audit showing every claimed NISQ advantage matched or beaten classically within a year of publication.
- **NISQ now, fault-tolerance as the goal** — Reasons: this is the strategy the serious laboratories actually execute — publish NISQ science, build the error-correction stack, let each fund the other; purism about either end wastes the decade the hardware needs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either a decisive NISQ commercial win (promoting optimism) or NISQ results collapsing wholesale under classical challenge (promoting purism).
- **Fault-Tolerant Error-Correction Purism** — Reasons: no NISQ algorithm has a proven asymptotic advantage, and heuristic variational methods keep falling to classical simulation; the field's one rigorous promise is fault-tolerant algorithms (factoring, quantum simulation), and resources follow rigour. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a NISQ device delivering a paid, classically unmatchable result in production.

#### Quantum application
- **Quantum Sensing & Metrology Priority** — Reasons: sensors are shipping physics — atomic clocks, NV magnetometers, SQUIDs and quantum gravimeters deliver precision no classical device matches, today, without error correction; metrology is also where quantum advantage was first proven (the SI second is a quantum standard). Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fault-tolerant computer delivering its civilizational result, reordering the priority by fact.
- **Both races** — Reasons: sensing and computing share platforms, components and people, and their timelines are independent uncertainties; portfolio thinking is correct when neither race's outcome predicts the other's. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a resource crunch forcing a genuine either/or at national funding scale.
- **Quantum Computing Supremacy** — Reasons: a fault-tolerant quantum computer changes what is computable — factoring, quantum simulation of materials and chemistry — a category change, while sensing improves measurements along existing categories; civilization-scale stakes belong to the category change. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: quantum simulation's flagship problems (catalysts, superconductors) being solved classically or by sensors first.

#### Expectations
- **Hype** — Reasons (the skin's bullish pole, labelled "Hype" for the hype itself): the breakthroughs are compounding — below-threshold error correction, thousand-qubit devices, record investments — and every platform technology looked like a bubble from inside its development curve; short timelines are a coordination device that concentrates the talent and capital the revolution needs. Statistic: private fusion and quantum ventures alike have drawn record capital; cumulative private investment in fusion alone surpassed $7 billion by 2024 (Fusion Industry Association, 2024). What would move a holder: a five-year stretch of missed milestones across all major platforms simultaneously.
- **Cautious optimism** — Reasons: the progress is measurable (logical qubits, fidelities, array sizes) and the timelines in press releases are not — both facts at once; calibrated optimism funds the field without mortgaging its credibility. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: milestones arriving on press-release schedule (toward Hype) or a platform-wide winter (toward Reality).
- **Reality** — Reasons (the skin's bearish pole, labelled "Reality" for the sober assessment): quantum computing has consumed decades and billions without a single production application, error correction's overhead keeps receding, and the field's incentive structure rewards announcements over deliveries; the sober prior for platform technologies with this profile is disappointment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one production application — a paid result no classical system can match.

#### Topological Qubits & Majorana Zero Modes
- **Overpromised — Bet on Proven Qubits** — Reasons: the Majorana record includes retracted high-profile claims and conductance peaks reattributed to trivial Andreev states — the field's credibility budget is spent; proven modalities (superconducting, ions, atoms) are demonstrating error correction now, and funding follows demonstration. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an unambiguous non-Abelian braiding or fusion experiment, replicated independently.
- **Fascinating Basic Physics First** — Reasons: non-Abelian anyons are among the deepest objects in condensed matter whether or not they become qubits, and Majorana research has already enriched superconductivity and topology; freeing the science from hardware promises restores honest incentives to a damaged subfield. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a topological device milestone (a protected qubit operation) re-legitimizing the hardware framing.
- **Best Long-Term Hardware Protection** — Reasons: every other platform buys fault tolerance with enormous active overhead; topological protection is the only route that makes errors rare by physics rather than by correction, and long-term bets are priced by their endgame, not their decade. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: resource estimates showing active correction on a rival platform reaching useful scale cheaply enough that protection's advantage evaporates.

#### The True Quantum-Hardware Scaling Bottleneck
- **Materials Defects & Surface Coherence** — Reasons: two-level-system defects in oxides and interfaces remain the dominant, least-controlled error source across solid-state platforms, and coherence times track materials quality more than design cleverness; the wall is in the stack, not the schematic. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a materials breakthrough (a defect-free platform) shifting the binding constraint to control or packaging.
- **Cryo-Electronics & Control Packaging** — Reasons: a million qubits at a few control lines each is a wiring and heat-load impossibility with room-temperature electronics — the cryostat's I/O wall arrives before any coherence wall at scale; cryo-CMOS and 3D packaging are therefore the critical path. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstrated multiplexing or cryo-control solution with headroom to a million qubits.
- **Modular Photonic Interconnects** — Reasons: monolithic chips hit yield and size limits in every technology's history, and quantum is no exception — the machine will be a network, so the interconnect rate and fidelity between modules is the true bottleneck; photonics is the only interconnect that leaves the cryostat gracefully. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: monolithic scaling (arrays, foundry spins) reaching fault-tolerant module sizes with no networking required.

#### Electric-Field Control of Magnetism & Multiferroics
- **Prioritizing Voltage-Controlled Magnetoelectric & Ferroic Switching** — Reasons: switching magnetism with voltage rather than current attacks the dominant energy cost of electronics at its root — charge motion — with demonstrated magnetoelectric switching of BiFeO₃-based devices and ferroelectric HfO₂ already CMOS-adjacent; the dissipation ceiling of charge-based logic (see §3.3, Spintronics) makes this the post-CMOS main line, not a curiosity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: magnetoelectric devices failing endurance or speed requirements at product-relevant cycling, across materials systems.

#### Spintronics & Magnetic Memory (MRAM) vs. Charge-Based Electronics
- **Charge-Based CMOS + Flash/DRAM** — Reasons: density, cost and ecosystem are not details — they are why technologies win, and charge-based memory wins all three by orders of magnitude of installed base; spintronics has promised displacement for thirty years and delivered excellent niches. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: MRAM or spin logic taking a mainstream (not embedded) memory or logic market on cost-performance.
- **Embedded Non-Volatile MRAM Complement** — Reasons: STT-MRAM is already shipping in embedded products — fast, endurant, non-volatile where flash cannot scale — a real, growing business that needs no revolution narrative; complementing CMOS where it is weak is the honest success case. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: embedded MRAM stalling at its current nodes, or SOT-MRAM graduating to standalone memory (expanding the claim).
- **All-Spin & Skyrmion Logic/Memory** — Reasons: moving spin instead of charge removes Joule heating from the information carrier itself — the only route that changes the dissipation scaling rather than optimizing within it; racetrack and skyrmion concepts remain the field's most original ideas and deserve their long bet. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: spin-logic cascadability or skyrmion stability failing at room temperature across a sustained effort.

#### Ferromagnets vs. Antiferromagnetic & Altermagnetic Spintronics
- **Proven Ferromagnetic Tunnel Junctions** — Reasons: high-TMR ferromagnetic junctions are manufactured by the billion in MRAM and read heads — readout reliability is the product; zero-moment magnets' readout problem is not a detail but the whole device. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an altermagnetic or antiferromagnetic junction demonstrating product-grade TMR at scale.
- **Altermagnetic Best-of-Both Bridge** — Reasons: altermagnets combine ferromagnets' spin-split bands (hence electrical readout) with antiferromagnets' zero stray field and fast dynamics — the bridge dissolves the field's central trade-off; the class was identified theoretically and confirmed experimentally within a few years, a sign of fertility. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: altermagnetic readout signals proving too weak or irreproducible across material families.
- **Terahertz Antiferromagnetic Spintronics** — Reasons: antiferromagnetic resonance lives at terahertz frequencies — orders faster than ferromagnetic dynamics — with no stray fields and intrinsic radiation hardness; the fastest, densest spintronics is compensated, and the readout problem is an engineering challenge, not a law. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: THz antiferromagnetic switching failing to couple to any readable signal at device scale.

#### Superconducting Cryogenic Computing (RSFQ/AQFP) vs. Room-Temperature CMOS
- **Room-Temp CMOS Wins Total System Power** — Reasons: honesty requires counting the roughly 500× Carnot penalty for removing heat at 4 K, and when it is counted, Josephson logic's femtojoule switching advantage evaporates at system level for general computing; thermodynamics is not negotiable by enthusiasm. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a full-system demonstration (cooling included) beating CMOS on a real workload's total energy.
- **Cryo-Logic Strictly for Quantum & Exascale** — Reasons: inside a cryostat that already exists — a quantum processor's control stack — superconducting logic's overhead is already paid, and its speed and low dissipation are pure gain; specialized ultra-high-throughput nodes make the same argument. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: cryo-CMOS control proving sufficient for quantum processors, removing the beachhead.
- **Superconducting Data Centers Are the Future** — Reasons: adiabatic quantum-flux-parametron logic switches at energies orders below CMOS, and at data-center scale even a 500× cooling multiplier can net out ahead when the compute energy falls by more; the argument is arithmetic about the largest energy consumers on Earth, not a laboratory curiosity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: rigorous system studies closing the arithmetic against superconducting logic at realistic utilization.

#### Magnonics & Wave-Based Spin Computing
- **Niche RF Microwave Filters Only** — Reasons: YIG filters and isolators are real products, but magnon damping and the transduction tax at every electrical interface have confined spin waves to them for decades; a computing platform needs cascadability magnonics has not shown. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a magnonic circuit performing a cascaded logic operation with gain, at room temperature.
- **Hybrid Magnon–Photon / Qubit Transducers** — Reasons: magnons couple microwaves, optics, phonons and spins in one system — cavity magnonics has demonstrated strong coupling to superconducting qubits — making magnonics the natural translator between quantum modalities; transduction is a bottleneck every quantum platform shares (see §3.3, scaling bottleneck). Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a competing transduction route (electro-optic, optomechanical) reaching high efficiency first.
- **Charge-Free Magnonic Wave Computing** — Reasons: spin-wave interference computes without moving charge — no Joule heat in the medium — and majority-gate and holographic-memory demonstrations show the logic is real; wave computing's parallelism is native, not simulated. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: damping lengths and transduction losses proving fatal at circuit scale after sustained effort.

#### Neuromorphic & Memristive Hardware Physics
- **Digital CMOS Accelerators Beat Analog Noise** — Reasons: AI's production workloads run on digital accelerators whose precision and programmability analog crossbars cannot match, and device variability, drift and ADC overhead eat the analog efficiency advantage at system level; the market has voted with billions. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an analog in-memory system beating digital accelerators on a production workload, accuracy included.
- **Hybrid Digital + Analog In-Memory Inference** — Reasons: inference at the edge is low-precision, energy-starved and always-on — exactly where analog crossbars win — while digital control keeps the system programmable and accurate where it matters; the hybrid is already the architecture of shipping neuromorphic products. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either digital efficiency (toward CMOS purism) or analog reliability (toward physics-native) closing the hybrid's window.
- **Physics-Native Ion/Phase-Change Neuromorphics** — Reasons: the brain's efficiency is physics-native — ions, thresholds, spikes, plasticity in the device physics itself — and memristive/phase-change systems are the only hardware that computes the way cortex does rather than simulating it; cortical efficiency will not be reached by better simulation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: variability and drift proving irreducible at network scale, capping physics-native systems below useful accuracy.

### 3.4 Energy, Power & Clean Technology

#### Energy
- **Fusion** — Reasons: fusion fuel is effectively limitless (deuterium from seawater, lithium-bred tritium), produces no long-lived actinide waste and cannot melt down; ignition has now been demonstrated in the laboratory, converting the question from physics possibility to engineering economics. Statistic: the National Ignition Facility achieved target gain above one in December 2022, releasing 3.15 MJ of fusion energy from 2.05 MJ of laser energy (US Department of Energy / Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, 2022). What would move a holder: a decade of post-ignition engineering failing to approach net-electric, materials-surviving operation.
- **Fission** — Reasons: fission is the only dense, dispatchable, carbon-free source already proven at civilizational scale — France decarbonized most of its electricity with it in about fifteen years; its problems (waste, cost, fear) are governance problems with known solutions, unlike fusion's open physics-engineering. Statistic: nuclear power supplied about 9% of the world's electricity in 2023 (IAEA Power Reactor Information System, 2024). What would move a holder: new-build fission costs continuing to rise while firm alternatives (geothermal, storage-backed renewables) undercut it in market after market.
- **Renewables** — Reasons: solar and wind are now the cheapest new electricity in most of the world and still falling on learning curves no other source matches; abundance and modularity — deployment in months, at any scale — are strategic properties, not just prices. Statistic: the global average cost of utility-scale solar PV electricity fell by about 89% between 2010 and 2022 (IRENA, Renewable Power Generation Costs, 2023). What would move a holder: system-level integration costs (storage, transmission, curtailment) overwhelming the generation cost advantage at high penetration.
- **Grid-Scale Batteries & Storage** — Reasons: storage is the binding constraint on every clean generation technology — whoever solves duration solves the transition; electrochemical, thermal and mechanical storage are improving on battery learning curves that have already cut lithium-ion pack prices by an order of magnitude in a decade. Statistic: average lithium-ion battery pack prices fell to $139 per kWh in 2023, from over $780 per kWh in 2013 (BloombergNEF, 2023). What would move a holder: long-duration storage stalling on cost while transmission or firm generation solves the duration problem instead.
- **Grid Transmission & Efficiency** — Reasons: the cheapest, fastest decarbonization is the electron never generated and the line that connects cheap renewables to load — interconnection queues, not generation costs, are the current bottleneck in leading economies; efficiency and transmission have no fuel, no waste and no decade-long construction physics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: transmission build-out proving politically impossible at the needed pace while generation-side options deploy.

#### Fusion
- **Large Public Tokamaks (ITER / DEMO)** — Reasons: the tokamak is the most-studied confinement concept by decades, and ITER is the only device designed to demonstrate a burning plasma at power-plant scale — its Q=10 mission answers the field's central question in one experiment; public scale matches the problem's scale. Statistic: ITER is designed to produce 500 MW of fusion power from 50 MW of external heating (ITER Organization, 2024). What would move a holder: ITER's burning-plasma results disappointing, or a compact device reaching net-electric first.
- **Multi-Concept Portfolio Diversity** — Reasons: nobody knows which confinement concept wins on economics, and the concepts fail independently — a portfolio across tokamaks, stellarators, inertial and alternatives is the rational funder's position under genuine uncertainty; the private fusion boom has made diversity cheap to buy. Statistic: cumulative private investment in fusion companies surpassed $7 billion by 2024, spread across dozens of companies and multiple confinement concepts (Fusion Industry Association, 2024). What would move a holder: one concept demonstrating net-electric operation, collapsing the portfolio into the winner.
- **Inertial Confinement Fusion (ICF)** — Reasons: ICF is the only approach that has demonstrated ignition and target gain — the physics milestone magnetic confinement still awaits; pulsed operation sidesteps steady-state plasma control, and laser and target costs ride optics learning curves. Statistic: NIF's December 2022 shot released 3.15 MJ from 2.05 MJ of laser input, the first laboratory target gain above one (US Department of Energy, 2022). What would move a holder: driver efficiency and target cost proving incapable of reaching a power plant's repetition-rate economics.
- **Compact High-Field HTS Tokamaks** — Reasons: fusion power density scales with the fourth power of magnetic field, and REBCO high-temperature superconductors roughly double achievable field — shrinking the machine, the cost and the schedule together; the private compact-tokamak programme (SPARC and successors) is the field's fastest-moving front. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: HTS magnet performance or quench protection failing at reactor scale, or compact devices hitting plasma limits the big machines avoid.
- **3D Stellarators** — Reasons: stellarators confine in steady state by geometry — no driven current, no disruptions, the two failure modes that haunt tokamaks; modern optimized stellarators (Wendelstein 7-X) have demonstrated the predicted neoclassical optimization, and computation has removed the concept's historical design barrier. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: stellarator coil complexity proving unmanufacturable at reactor scale at acceptable cost.

#### Baseload
- **Nuclear & fusion source** — Reasons: a grid needs firm power that does not negotiate with weather, and nuclear (fission now, fusion later) is the only carbon-free source that is dense, dispatchable and land-sparing; every deep-decarbonization study that excludes nuclear pays more for the same grid. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstrated high-renewables grid maintaining reliability at lower total system cost than nuclear-inclusive grids.
- **Centralized grid** — Reasons: centralization buys economies of scale, professional operation and geographic smoothing across a continent-sized synchronous system; the grid's century of reliability improvement is a centralization story. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: distributed systems demonstrating equal reliability through major disturbances at lower cost.
- **Distributed solar + storage** — Reasons: generation at the point of use deletes transmission losses, land conflicts and single points of failure, and rooftop-plus-storage costs fall on consumer-electronics learning curves; resilience under storms and attacks is a property centralized grids structurally lack. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: distribution-level integration (hosting capacity, protection, markets) proving harder than the transmission build-out it was meant to avoid.

#### Energy storage
- **Short-Duration** — Reasons: the daily solar cycle is the storage problem that matters first and most, and lithium-ion (plus emerging sodium and solid-state) owns hours-scale storage on cost and deployment speed; most grid value stacks (arbitrage, frequency, capacity) live in the hours window. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: high-renewables grids hitting seasonal, not daily, shortfalls as their binding failure mode.
- **Layered durations** — Reasons: the grid's storage needs span seconds to seasons, and no single chemistry is cheapest across that range — layering is the engineering answer to a multi-scale problem; this is what system operators actually procure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one technology (a breakthrough battery, cheap hydrogen) collapsing the cost curve across all durations.
- **Long-Duration** — Reasons: dunkelflaute — weeks of low wind and sun — is the renewable grid's existential case, and only hydrogen, pumped hydro, compressed air and thermal storage address it at terawatt-hour scale; solving the hardest duration solves the transition's credibility. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: interconnection and overbuilding proving cheaper than seasonal storage at the required reliability.

#### Optimization
- **Efficiency** — Reasons: efficiency compounds — every percent at generation is a percent saved at transmission, storage and end use — and the physics of a device is its efficiency frontier; squeezing percent is what applied physics is, the rest is accounting. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: efficiency gains in their field demonstrably priced out of every market by cheaper, less efficient rivals.
- **Efficiency at a price point** — Reasons: real products live at price points, and efficiency beyond the point's willingness-to-pay is waste of a different kind; the professional skill is knowing exactly where the trade turns. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their market's price point moving (carbon pricing, say) until pure efficiency or pure cost becomes the whole answer.
- **Cost** — Reasons: deployment is what decarbonizes and heals, and deployment is bought with cost per watt — the solar revolution was a cost curve, not an efficiency record; the cheapest device wins because winning means existing at scale. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a premium-efficiency technology taking a mass market from a cheaper rival on performance alone.

#### Nuclear waste, safety, and proliferation
- **Manageable** — Reasons: deep geological disposal is demonstrated engineering — Finland's Onkalo repository is built and loading — and the total volume of high-level waste is small (a football field's worth for the US fleet's entire history, by the usual comparison); reprocessing and fast reactors can shrink even that. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a repository programme failing geologically (not politically) after licensing.
- **Unmanageable** — Reasons: the waste's hazard horizon (tens of thousands of years) exceeds every human institution's demonstrated lifetime, and "solutions" that require millennia of stewardship or perfect geology are not solutions but deferrals; several repository programmes have already collapsed under their host communities' refusal. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a repository operating safely through its loading era with durable community consent, plus actinide-burning reactors shrinking the hazard horizon to centuries.
- **Proliferation is the real risk** — Reasons: waste sits still and accidents are local, but separated plutonium and enrichment capability are forever — the binding constraint on nuclear expansion is the weapons pathway it normalizes; safety engineering has answers for the reactor, none for the geopolitics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: proliferation-resistant fuel cycles (integral fast reactors, safeguarded-by-design SMRs) demonstrating diversion resistance in practice.

#### Nuclear Fission Architecture: Gen-III+ vs. SMRs vs. Gen-IV Breeders
- **Standardized Gen-III+ Gigawatt Fleets** — Reasons: the historical cost evidence (France, South Korea) shows large reactors get cheap when built repeatedly by experienced crews to one design — standardization, not size, is the variable; gigawatt units match grid-scale demand in single projects. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a standardized fleet programme still overrunning, showing the disease is not bespoke design.
- **Factory-Built Small Modular Reactors (SMRs)** — Reasons: SMRs move cost from site construction (where nuclear overruns live) to factory fabrication (where learning curves live), and passive safety plus smaller absolute capital per unit fix the financing problem that actually kills nuclear projects. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: first SMR deployments reproducing large reactors' cost per kW, showing factory learning does not materialize.
- **Gen-IV Fast-Breeder & Molten-Salt Reactors** — Reasons: closed fuel cycles burn the actinides that make waste a millennial problem and stretch uranium resources by orders of magnitude, while high-temperature heat serves industry, not just electricity; Gen-IV is the only architecture that answers the waste objection (§3.4, Nuclear waste) in physics rather than in geology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: Gen-IV demonstrations failing on materials or licensing where Gen-III+ fleets deploy cleanly.

#### Power Electronics & Wide-Bandgap Priority
- **Prioritizing SiC, GaN & Ultrawide-Bandgap Power Efficiency** — Reasons: most electricity now passes through power electronics — EVs, solar inverters, data centers, grids — so conversion efficiency there multiplies across the whole energy system, unlike a logic node's gains; SiC and GaN devices are already displacing silicon in EVs and chargers on merit, and Ga₂O₃ and diamond extend the same direction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: wide-bandgap devices stalling on cost or reliability while silicon power devices close the efficiency gap.

#### Battery Chemistry Frontier
- **Evolutionary Li-Ion Optimization** — Reasons: lithium-ion's manufacturing base is measured in terawatt-hours of annual capacity under construction, and silicon-carbon anodes, LFP/LMFP cathodes and cell-to-pack engineering keep compounding — incumbency plus improvement is the hardest combination in technology to beat; every "post-lithium" claim must beat not today's Li-ion but its trajectory. Statistic: average lithium-ion pack prices reached $139 per kWh in 2023, continuing a decade-long decline (BloombergNEF, 2023). What would move a holder: Li-ion's improvement rate flattening while a rival chemistry's cost curve crosses it.
- **Solid-State Lithium Metal** — Reasons: replacing flammable liquid electrolyte with ceramic or sulfide solids plus a lithium-metal anode is the one route to a step change (roughly doubling) in energy density with a safety improvement, not a trade; the failure modes are manufacturing problems, which yield to capital and iteration. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: solid-state pilot lines failing on yield or cycle life across the industry, cycle after cycle.
- **Earth-Abundant Na-Ion & Flow Storage** — Reasons: the grid's storage need is so large that mineral supply (lithium, nickel, cobalt) becomes the constraint, and sodium, iron and vanadium-flow chemistries are built from abundant inputs; for stationary storage, cost and durability beat density — the metrics these chemistries win. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: lithium supply scaling cheaply enough that abundance stops mattering, or Na-ion costs failing to undercut LFP.

#### Photovoltaics: Silicon Scaling vs. Perovskite Tandems
- **Silicon Learning Curve Dominates** — Reasons: crystalline silicon pairs a ~30-year field-proven lifetime with the steepest sustained cost decline in energy history, and bankability — the willingness of finance to fund it — is itself a technology property perovskites do not yet have; beating the device is not beating the industry. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: perovskite modules passing the full IEC qualification suite with multi-decade field data.
- **Perovskite-on-Silicon Tandems** — Reasons: tandems stack a wide-bandgap perovskite on silicon to harvest more of the spectrum, breaking the single-junction Shockley–Queisser limit while standing on silicon's manufacturing base — the records have moved with unprecedented speed. Statistic: perovskite–silicon tandem cells reached a certified efficiency of 33.7% in 2023 (NREL Best Research-Cell Efficiency Chart, 2023). What would move a holder: tandem degradation under heat, humidity and illumination proving intrinsic rather than encapsulable.
- **Radical Thin-Film & Multijunction** — Reasons: lightweight, flexible and high-specific-power photovoltaics open markets silicon cannot enter — vehicles, buildings' facades, space, wearables — and all-perovskite or III–V multijunctions hold the absolute efficiency frontier; new form factors create new demand rather than fighting silicon's. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: thin-film and multijunction costs failing to fall outside their space/defence niches.

#### Hydrogen & Electrofuels vs. Direct Electrification
- **Direct Electrification Everywhere Possible** — Reasons: round-trip arithmetic is brutal — electrolysis, compression and reconversion waste most of the input electricity that a heat pump or battery would deliver nearly whole; electrifying first is not ideology but thermodynamics applied to scarce clean power. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: clean electricity becoming so abundant and cheap that conversion losses stop mattering against hydrogen's storage and transport advantages.
- **Hydrogen Strictly for Hard-to-Abate Sectors** — Reasons: steel reduction, ammonia, chemicals, shipping and long-haul aviation have no direct-electric route at scale — hydrogen is indispensable exactly there and wasteful everywhere else; rationing a scarce, expensive carrier to its irreplaceable uses is the efficient allocation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hard-to-abate sector finding a direct-electric route (moving the boundary) or electrolyzer costs collapsing (widening it).
- **Broad Hydrogen Energy Carrier** — Reasons: hydrogen is storable at seasonal scale, transportable by ship and pipeline, and usable across heat, transport and industry — a systems integrator no other carrier matches; infrastructure built broadly creates the scale that makes the hard-to-abate uses cheap. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: hydrogen infrastructure projects failing on leakage, embrittlement and cost at the first broad deployments.

#### Extreme-Environment & Fusion First-Wall Materials
- **Refractory Metals & Oxide-Dispersion Steels** — Reasons: tungsten armor and ODS ferritic-martensitic steels have the deepest irradiation databases and a licensable engineering story today; a first wall must be built from materials regulators can qualify, and maturity is a physical property of a programme, not a preference. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: ODS/tungsten systems failing ductility or joining requirements under 14 MeV spectra in IFMIF-class testing.
- **SiC/SiC Ceramic Composites & UHTCs** — Reasons: silicon-carbide composites are low-activation, retain strength at temperatures that end metals, and their radiation tolerance is intrinsic to the covalent lattice; ultra-high-temperature ceramics cover the thermal extremes no metal survives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: SiC joining, hermeticity or irradiation-induced conductivity failing at component scale.
- **Self-Healing Liquid-Metal First Walls** — Reasons: a flowing liquid wall has no lattice to damage — displacement damage, the solid wall's fundamental limit, does not exist for it — and liquid lithium/tin handles divertor heat fluxes beyond any solid's conduction; the concept converts the hardest materials problem into a fluid-engineering problem. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: MHD drag, tritium retention or corrosion proving unmanageable in a flowing prototype loop.

#### Solid-State Cooling & Waste-Heat Recovery (Thermoelectrics & Caloric Materials)
- **Vapor-Compression Physics Is Unbeatable** — Reasons: a century of optimization gives vapor compression coefficients of performance solid-state devices approach only in niches, and thermoelectric ZT has crept, not leapt, for decades; cooling is a huge market precisely because the incumbent is excellent. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a caloric or thermoelectric system matching vapor-compression COP at building scale in production.
- **Niche Thermoelectrics & Spot Thermal Control** — Reasons: solid-state cooling already owns its niches — laser and detector stabilization, spacecraft RTGs' inverse, silent spot cooling — where silence, precision and no moving parts beat efficiency; niches pay for the research that might widen them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the niches being invaded by miniature vapor-compression or passive solutions.
- **Breakthrough Electro/Elasto/Magnetocaloric Cooling** — Reasons: caloric effects in modern materials (barocaloric plastic crystals, elastocaloric alloys) show large entropy changes at modest fields, and the prize — heat pumping with zero refrigerant leakage, against HFC phase-down treaties — is regulatory as well as physical; vapor compression's refrigerant problem is solid-state's opening. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: caloric prototypes failing on fatigue life (millions of cycles) across material families.

#### Direct Air Capture & Separations Materials Realism
- **Thermodynamics Favors Emissions Prevention** — Reasons: the minimum work of separating CO₂ at 420 ppm is fixed by entropy of mixing, and real systems pay multiples of it — every DAC tonne costs energy that could have prevented several tonnes at a smokestack or by not emitting; prevention-first is arithmetic, not pessimism. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: DAC energy and cost falling to where the prevention comparison no longer dominates — very cheap clean energy plus cheap sorbents.
- **Point-Source Capture + Industrial Separations** — Reasons: separations consume a significant share of industrial energy, and designer MOFs, zeolites and membranes deployed on concentrated streams deliver the same materials science at a fraction of DAC's thermodynamic penalty; the field's materials should earn their credibility where physics is kind. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: point-source capture being outpaced by emissions elimination, shifting the materials problem to ambient air anyway.
- **Scalable Solid-Sorbent & Electrochemical DAC** — Reasons: residual and historical emissions must be removed by something, and solid sorbents plus electrochemical swing systems are modular, siting-flexible and improving on manufacturing learning curves; first commercial plants are operating, making this an engineering trajectory, not a speculation. Statistic: Climeworks' Mammoth plant, opened in Iceland in 2024, was designed to capture up to 36,000 tonnes of CO₂ per year (Climeworks, 2024). What would move a holder: operating plants' real capture costs and sorbent lifetimes refusing to improve across generations.

### 3.5 Materials & Fabrication

#### Materials
- **Natural** — Reasons: evolution has optimized silk, bone, nacre and wood over hundreds of millions of years — toughness and hierarchy no laboratory matches at ambient conditions; working with nature's materials is also the sustainable default, grown rather than manufactured. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: engineered materials matching natural materials' toughness-per-density and self-repair in a structural application.
- **Biomimicry** — Reasons: biomimicry extracts nature's design principles (hierarchy, gradients, self-assembly) without nature's constraints — nacre's architecture in ceramics, gecko adhesion in polymers; it is the productive middle: principles from biology, performance targets from engineering. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: biomimetic designs repeatedly failing to beat either natural originals or clean-sheet engineering.
- **Engineered** — Reasons: metamaterials with negative index, alloys with no natural analogue, composites tuned ply by ply — engineered materials do what no evolved material attempts; the design space of matter is vastly larger than biology's corner of it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship application where a natural material beats every engineered candidate on the full requirement set.

#### 2D materials
- **Revolution** — Reasons: graphene's properties remain extreme — among the strongest and most conductive materials measured, one atom thick — and the 2D family has grown into a platform (semiconducting TMDs, hBN, moiré systems) generating new physics (twistronics) and devices yearly; revolutions in materials take decades, and this one's clock started in 2004. Statistic: graphene's intrinsic tensile strength was measured at about 130 GPa, making it among the strongest materials known (Lee et al., Science, 2008). What would move a holder: another decade without a 2D material in a mass product or a unique scientific instrument.
- **Useful, not revolutionary** — Reasons: 2D materials have earned real niches — membranes, composites, sensors, research platforms — while the revolutionary claims (replacing silicon, transforming energy) keep receding; the calibrated position prices the niches and discounts the era. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a genuinely mass application (toward Revolution) or the niches themselves drying up (toward Hype).
- **Hype** — Reasons: two decades of graphene hype have produced no killer application, and each year's promised breakthrough becomes next year's review-article caveat; the Nobel honoured beautiful physics, and the product narrative was venture dressing on it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 2D material shipping in a product category it created, at scale.

#### Superconductors
- **Unconventional Cuprate / Nickelate Physics** — Reasons: the pairing mechanism of the cuprates remains the outstanding unsolved problem of condensed matter, and solving it is the likeliest route to designing higher-Tc materials rather than finding them by luck; nickelates have reopened the question with a second family to triangulate from. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a design rule for Tc emerging from another programme (hydrides, engineering) first.
- **Extreme-Pressure Hydrides** — Reasons: hydrides hold the Tc records — H₃S superconducting at about 203 K under megabar pressure — proving phonon pairing can approach room temperature; the pressure is a laboratory probe of what chemistry might later stabilize at ambient. Statistic: H₃S was reported superconducting at 203 K at about 155 GPa (Drozdov et al., Nature, 2015). What would move a holder: the hydride programme's claims failing replication at the level of the retracted carbonaceous-sulfur-hydride episode, generally.
- **Engineering Ambient-Pressure Wire & Magnet Tech** — Reasons: REBCO tape is transforming fusion (compact tokamaks), grids and MRI now, and its bottlenecks — cost per kiloamp-metre, critical current, joining — are engineering with known levers; deployed superconductivity does more for the field's future than another mechanism paper. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: REBCO costs plateauing while a new ambient-pressure superconductor family emerges from the physics programmes.

#### Metamaterials
- **Active Electronic Circuits** — Reasons: amplifiers, transistors and DSP offer gain, reconfigurability and programmability that passive structures cannot — a phased array reprogrammes in software, a metasurface is frozen at fabrication; active control is how wave systems actually get built at RF. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: passive metasurfaces matching active arrays' reconfigurability through tunable materials.
- **Hybrid active metasurfaces** — Reasons: geometry gives the wavefront shaping for free while sparse active elements give tunability — the hybrid takes both advantages and is where reconfigurable intelligent surfaces for communications are actually heading. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either pure approach absorbing the hybrid's use cases.
- **Passive Geometric Metasurfaces** — Reasons: a patterned surface that shapes phase, polarization and amplitude with no power, no latency and no electronics is a new optical element class — metalenses already outperform refractive stacks in thickness and aberration control; geometry is the cheapest actuator there is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: efficiency and bandwidth limits proving intrinsic to passive metasurfaces at system level.

#### Nanofabrication
- **Top-Down Extreme Lithography** — Reasons: EUV lithography is the most complex manufacturing technology ever deployed and it works — every advanced chip is its proof; top-down control gives deterministic placement and registration no self-assembly matches. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: bottom-up assembly achieving registered, defect-tolerant fabrication of a working complex device.
- **Combined processes** — Reasons: real process flows already combine — lithography defines the scaffold, self-assembly and epitaxy fill it (directed self-assembly is literally this); purity about one method is a seminar position, not a fab position. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one method reaching the full device stack alone.
- **Bottom-Up Supramolecular Self-Assembly** — Reasons: self-assembly fabricates in parallel — every molecule is a worker — reaching feature sizes and 3D complexity (DNA origami, block copolymers) that serial top-down tools cannot economically touch; biology, the existence proof, builds bottom-up exclusively. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: defect rates in self-assembled systems proving irreducible at device-relevant scales.

#### Semiconductors
- **Dimensional Silicon Scaling (Moore's Law)** — Reasons: silicon scaling has survived fifty years of obituaries through materials and geometry reinvention (high-k, FinFET, gate-all-around, CFET), and each "end" has so far been a transition, not a terminus; the roadmap to angstrom-class nodes is staffed and funded. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an angstrom-class node shipping without density or cost benefit over its predecessor.
- **Scale silicon, integrate beyond** — Reasons: squeeze silicon where it still pays and integrate beyond-silicon elements (optical I/O, 2D channels, novel memory) where it does not — the pragmatic composite every roadmap actually describes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: dimensional scaling either reviving fully or dying fully, simplifying the composite.
- **Beyond-Silicon Heterogeneous Integration** — Reasons: silicon's mobility, bandgap and electrostatics are near their material limits, while nanotubes, TMDs and wide-bandgap materials each beat it somewhere that matters; heterogeneous integration lets each device use its best material instead of the incumbent's. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: beyond-silicon channels failing uniformity and contact resistance at wafer scale across a sustained effort.
- **Domain-Specific & Neuromorphic Hardware** — Reasons: the von Neumann memory wall, not transistor physics, is the binding constraint on AI-era computing, and in-memory/domain-specific architectures attack it directly — the largest performance gains available are architectural; general-purpose scaling is over whichever substrate wins. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: general-purpose processors closing the efficiency gap with domain-specific accelerators on AI workloads.
- **Advanced 3D Packaging & Chiplets** — Reasons: chiplets, hybrid bonding and backside power delivery are delivering system-level scaling now — yield by partition, mix-and-match process nodes, memory stacked on logic; packaging is where Moore's law moved, and it is shipping. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: packaging interconnect density or thermal limits capping system gains before transistor scaling does.

#### Strong Correlations vs. Topological Band Theory
- **Strong Electron Correlations & Strange Metals** — Reasons: strong correlations hold condensed matter's deepest unsolved problems — the cuprate mechanism, strange-metal linear resistivity, Mott physics — where band theory itself fails; the frontier is defined by what the successful theory cannot do. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a controlled theoretical method (a "DFT for correlations") dissolving the frontier's hardness.
- **Topological Band & Symmetry Classification** — Reasons: topological band theory delivered a complete, predictive classification — symmetry indicators now screen databases for topological materials computationally, a design capability correlations cannot match; it is the part of condensed matter that became engineering. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: topological classification's predicted materials failing experimental confirmation at scale.

#### Defects & Disorder: Enemy or Functional Lever?
- **Chase Ultra-Clean Ballistic Purity** — Reasons: intrinsic quantum phenomena — fractional quantum Hall, fragile many-body states, long coherence — appear only in the cleanest samples, and materials purity has been the enabling variable of condensed matter's Nobel history (MBE GaAs above all); disorder hides the physics the field exists to find. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: disorder-engineered systems producing the flagship quantum phenomena purity was supposed to be required for.
- **Precision Engineered Doping** — Reasons: semiconductors are defect engineering — doping is controlled impurity — and NV centers, single dopants and dislocation engineering extend the same principle into quantum devices; the defect is a tool with an atomic address. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: defect control reaching its placement/stability limits while high-entropy approaches deliver the same functions statistically.
- **Embrace Disorder & High-Entropy Complexity** — Reasons: high-entropy alloys achieve strength–ductility combinations ordered alloys do not, metallic glasses and disordered systems own whole property niches, and configurational entropy is a design variable with a vast unexplored space; purity is one point in a space the disordered majority of which is unmapped. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: high-entropy systems' properties proving reproducible only as averages, unusable where specification matters.

#### Electrical Interconnects vs. Integrated Silicon Photonics
- **Optimize Copper & Advanced Packaging** — Reasons: copper at short reach keeps winning because optical conversion (modulation, detection) costs energy and latency at every interface, and packaging advances keep extending copper's range; the burden of proof sits on the converter, and it has not been met inside the package. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: co-packaged optics shipping in volume AI systems with better energy per bit than copper at package distances.
- **Optical Interconnects + Electronic Logic** — Reasons: bandwidth density and distance-independent loss make photonics inevitable between packages and racks, while electronics keeps switching — the division of labour every hyperscaler roadmap now assumes; co-packaged optics is the current, funded step. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: copper SerDes extending its energy-efficient reach another order of magnitude.
- **All-Optical & Photonic Computing** — Reasons: photonic linear algebra executes matrix operations at propagation speed with minimal energy, and the AI era's core operation is exactly that; pushing optics into the compute fabric is the logical end of the interconnect argument. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: photonic processors' precision and programmability limits proving intrinsic at useful problem sizes.

#### Custom University Cleanroom Craft vs. Foundry PDKs
- **Custom In-House Cleanroom Craft** — Reasons: novel physics lives in materials stacks no foundry will run — bespoke cleanroom craft is where new device concepts are born, and process intuition is itself a research instrument; foundry-first design would filter out the field's future at birth. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: foundries opening genuinely flexible multi-material lines that absorb the bespoke use cases.
- **Prototype Local, Scale at Foundry** — Reasons: explore where exploration is free, translate early enough that manufacturability shapes the design before it hardens — the valley of death (§3.1) is crossed by designs that meet the foundry halfway. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: translation repeatedly failing because foundry processes cannot reproduce university-stack physics, arguing for staying bespoke.
- **Foundry PDKs & MPW Tape-Outs First** — Reasons: designing inside a PDK makes results reproducible and manufacturable by construction — anyone can re-run the tape-out; university one-off processes produce unreproducible heroes (§3.1, scale-up gap) and the field's credibility pays. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: PDK constraints demonstrably excluding a device class that later proves important.

#### Materials Synthesis vs. Operando Characterization
- **Better Samples Drive Everything** — Reasons: the historical pattern is unambiguous — better crystals and films (cleaner GaAs, better cuprates, pristine graphene) precede the breakthroughs, and characterization of bad samples produces literature, not physics; synthesis is the field's rate-limiting art. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship discovery credited to operando observation of an unchanged, ordinary sample.
- **Co-Evolving Synthesis & Operando Probes** — Reasons: growth informed by live characterization iterates in days what blind iteration does in years — the loop (§3.1, design-build) applied to samples themselves; each side's advances raise the other's ceiling. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either side demonstrably advancing alone at the same rate.
- **In-Situ & Operando Diagnostics Lead** — Reasons: devices fail and function under bias, temperature and electrolyte — conditions ex-situ characterization never sees — and operando methods have repeatedly found the active species is not the as-made material (catalysis above all); reality is in the operating state. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: operando observations proving systematically unrepresentative (beam effects, cell artefacts) across techniques.

#### Additive Manufacturing (3D-Printed Alloys) vs. Traditional Forged Metallurgy
- **Proven Wrought, Cast & Forged Metallurgy** — Reasons: fatigue-critical structures fly on forged metallurgy because its grain structures, inspection regime and century of service data are what certification means; printed metals' pores, residual stress and anisotropy are not details but the failure modes themselves. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: printed alloys achieving forged fatigue performance in certified flight hardware at production scale.
- **Hybrid AM + Post-Process HIP & Qualification** — Reasons: hot isostatic pressing closes the pores, qualification regimes are maturing, and the hybrid keeps AM's geometric freedom while buying back metallurgical respectability — the route by which AM is actually entering service. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: HIP and post-processing erasing AM's cost advantage, or as-printed properties maturing enough to skip them.
- **Design-for-AM Topology & Microstructure Control** — Reasons: AM's real prize is not printing old shapes but impossible ones — topology-optimized, lattice-filled, site-specifically graded microstructures no forging can make; designing for the process, not despite it, is where the property frontier is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: design-for-AM parts failing qualification on fatigue scatter despite geometric advantage.

#### Strength vs. Toughness & Fracture-Mechanics Realism
- **Chase Record Ideal Strength & Modulus** — Reasons: approaching theoretical strength (nanowires, graphene, ultra-hard phases) defines the envelope inside which all engineering trades occur, and record materials seed the composites that later deliver toughness too; the limit is knowledge, not a product spec. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: record-strength materials proving unusable even as composite constituents.
- **Ashby Multi-Objective Property Trade-Offs** — Reasons: materials selection is multi-objective by nature — strength, toughness, density, cost, temperature — and Ashby's maps are the discipline's working method for not lying about trade-offs; single-metric chasing is how bad materials get chosen. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a design problem where one metric so dominates that the maps add nothing.
- **Damage Tolerance, Fracture & Fatigue First** — Reasons: real structures essentially never fail by reaching ideal strength — they fail by crack growth and fatigue, so fracture toughness and damage tolerance are the properties that kill or save; Ritchie's critique of strength-first nano-materials is the field's own conscience. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a structural failure traced to insufficient ideal strength in a damage-tolerant design.

#### Ultrafast Non-Equilibrium & Floquet Materials Engineering
- **Equilibrium Ground-State Materials Only** — Reasons: light-induced "hidden phases" have repeatedly shrunk under scrutiny into heating artefacts and surface effects, and a material that exists only under continuous pumping is a demonstration, not a material; ground-state phases are what engineering can hold. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a light-induced phase with a replicated, artefact-excluded signature and a stabilized lifetime.
- **Metastable Quenched & Strain-Engineered Phases** — Reasons: strain and quenching stabilize genuinely useful non-equilibrium phases permanently — strained silicon built an industry, metastable polymorphs fill pharmaceutics — capturing Floquet-style novelty without the pump; this is non-equilibrium engineering with a shelf life. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: strain/quench windows proving too narrow for the phases that matter.
- **Light-Driven Floquet & Transient Quantum Matter** — Reasons: Floquet engineering has demonstrated light-induced topology and anomalous Hall effects in driven systems, and transient superconducting-like states above Tc are reported by multiple groups — the driven phase space is real physics even where individual claims are contested; equilibrium is a subset of what matter can do. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the flagship light-induced claims (superconductivity especially) failing artefact-controlled replication wholesale.

#### 2D Twistronics vs. Bulk 3D Crystals
- **Pristine Bulk 3D Single Crystals** — Reasons: bulk crystals give thermodynamic measurements (specific heat, magnetization, neutron scattering) on macroscopic samples — the definitive probes of phase transitions — without substrate, twist-disorder and device artefacts; the field's canonical phase diagrams are bulk work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: moiré systems producing thermodynamic-grade phase diagrams that bulk cannot.
- **Complementary Dimensionality** — Reasons: 2D stacks offer gate tunability and designed band structures, bulk offers thermodynamic truth — the same material questions are increasingly answered by both in sequence; dimensionality is a dial, not a faction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one dimensionality's methods fully absorbing the other's evidence.
- **2D Twistronics & Moiré Engineering** — Reasons: magic-angle graphene converted twist angle into a tuning knob for correlations, superconductivity and topology in a single device — a quantum simulator you dial; no bulk technique offers in-situ control of bandwidth versus interaction. Statistic: correlated insulating and superconducting states in magic-angle graphene were reported at a twist angle of about 1.1° (Cao et al., Nature, 2018). What would move a holder: twist disorder proving irreducible, capping moiré reproducibility below bulk standards.

### 3.6 Life, Medicine & the Applied Life

#### Biophysics
- **Universal Physical & Active-Matter Laws** — Reasons: active matter, flocking, cytoskeletal mechanics and phase behaviour in cells obey statistical-mechanical principles that transcend molecular identity — the same equations describe bacteria and synthetic swimmers; universality is what makes it physics rather than cataloguing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: biological systems' behaviour proving irreducibly molecule-specific across the flagship active-matter cases.
- **Mesoscale Bridge Models** — Reasons: the cell lives at the mesoscale, where coarse-grained laws must meet molecular parameters — models that bridge (polymer physics of chromatin, membrane elasticity with real lipid numbers) are the ones that predict experiments; pure universality is too loose, pure atomism too slow. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either extreme — a universal law predicting cellular behaviour quantitatively, or atomistic simulation reaching cellular scales.
- **Atomistic Biochemical Specificity** — Reasons: a single point mutation can cause a disease or rewire a pathway — evolution's information is stored at atomic specificity, and coarse-graining averages away exactly what the organism selected; drug discovery, the field's applied test, is atomistic or it is nothing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: mesoscale models predicting a drug-relevant or mutation-relevant outcome atomism missed.

#### Medical physics
- **Therapy** — Reasons: physics that heals — proton and photon therapy, FLASH, focused ultrasound — delivers dose with spatial precision no drug matches, and radiotherapy is already part of roughly half of cancer care pathways in developed systems; therapy is medical physics' proven clinical identity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: therapeutic outcomes plateauing while diagnostic advances demonstrably save more lives per physicist.
- **Diagnostics** — Reasons: seeing precedes treating — MRI, PET and CT define modern diagnosis, and imaging physics keeps compounding (photon-counting CT, low-field portable MRI); every therapy in this list is aimed by a diagnostic. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: diagnostics reaching diminishing returns while therapy physics (FLASH, protons) opens new outcome gains.
- **Bioelectronics & Neural Interfaces** — Reasons: recording and modulating neural circuits directly — the interface is where physics meets the nervous system, and soft, safe, chronic bioelectronics is the enabling problem of neurotechnology's whole future. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: chronic interface failure modes (scarring, drift) proving intrinsic across materials strategies.
- **Frugal Point-of-Care Diagnostics** — Reasons: most of the world has no imaging centre — low-cost, rugged, bedside diagnostics (paper microfluidics, portable ultrasound, low-field MRI) deliver medical physics to the majority who currently get none; the impact per dollar dwarfs flagship-instrument work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: frugal devices failing clinical accuracy requirements in field deployment, repeatedly.

#### Imaging
- **Resolution** — Reasons: diagnosis is limited by what can be seen — earlier, smaller lesions are the whole game in cancer and neurology — and resolution physics (detectors, reconstruction, AI) keeps converting directly into earlier detection. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: outcome studies showing resolution gains no longer change diagnoses or survival.
- **Resolution at the dose limit** — Reasons: the professional standard is ALARA with maximum information — sharpness purchased inside a dose budget is the actual clinical optimization, practised daily; purity on either side is a seminar position. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: dose limits relaxing (photon-counting detectors) or tightening (new radiobiology) enough to change the trade's terms.
- **Dose** — Reasons: radiation dose is harm done by the diagnostic itself, cumulative across a population's lifetime of scans, and the patient-first ordering (do less harm) is medicine's own first principle applied to physics; modern iterative and AI reconstruction exists to serve it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that dose reduction at current margins costs more missed diagnoses than the radiation risk it removes.

#### Sector
- **Industry** — Reasons: products are where applied physics touches the world — industry offers the fab, the supply chain, the customers and the salary scale that academia cannot; shipping is the discipline that separates applied physics from commentary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: industry's time horizons shortening until deep applied work becomes impossible there.
- **Straddling both** — Reasons: the straddle — joint appointments, spin-outs kept close to the lab, sabbaticals in industry — imports each sector's discipline into the other and is how Pasteur's-quadrant careers (§3.1, Orientation) are actually built. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: straddling proving to mean second-class citizenship in both sectors.
- **Academia** — Reasons: academia owns the long questions, the students and the freedom to publish negative results — the field's knowledge base is a university product, and industry free-rides on it; teaching multiplies a physicist's impact beyond any single device. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: academic incentives (publication counts over devices) corrupting applied work beyond industry's short-termism.

#### Identity
- **Proud Applied Physicist** — Reasons: first-principles command of electromagnetism, quantum mechanics and thermodynamics is a genuinely different instrument from engineering design rules — it is what lets its holder leave the rulebook when the rules run out; the identity names a real capability, not a vanity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: applied problems in their field being solved entirely within engineering method, with physics insight contributing nothing identifiable.
- **Physicist-engineer** — Reasons: the hybrid identity matches the hybrid work — physics for the mechanism, engineering for the delivery — and refusing either half is a pose; most successful applied careers are this, whatever the business card says. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the two halves' professional standards diverging until one identity must be chosen.
- **Pragmatic Specialized Engineer** — Reasons: design rules, standards, yield economics and project management are not baggage-free zones but the accumulated competence that ships safe products; physics romanticism has its own failure record (beautiful prototypes, no product). Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship failure caused precisely by rule-following where first-principles physics would have caught the problem.

#### Intellectual property
- **Commercial Patents & Spin-Out Startups** — Reasons: deep-tech hardware needs capital that only defensible IP attracts, and the spin-out is the vehicle that carries a breakthrough across the valley of death (§3.1); publication without protection is, for hardware, often abandonment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: patent-protected spin-outs in their field systematically underperforming open or public routes to deployment.
- **Patent, then publish** — Reasons: file first, then disclose — the sequence captures commercial protection and scientific credit together, and is standard practice at every serious research university; purity on either side leaves value on the table. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: patent-first delays measurably costing priority or citations in fast fields.
- **Open Academic Publishing** — Reasons: physical knowledge compounds only when shared — the arXiv-and-journal commons is why the field moves at its current speed, and patent thickets on fundamental methods tax everyone downstream; publicly funded knowledge belongs to the public. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: open publication of a hardware breakthrough resulting in its capture without deployment, where a spin-out would have shipped it.

What would move a holder: venture-backed deep-tech systematically dying at the valley's far side for want of patient follow-on capital.

#### Triumph

Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their field's products proving ephemeral while its papers or prototypes define the era.

Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: prototypes in their field routinely failing to predict product success, demoting the prototype's evidential value.

Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their field's landmark papers proving less durable than its devices.

#### Biomolecular Condensates & Liquid–Liquid Phase Separation (LLPS)
- **Classical Specific Binding Explains Most** — Reasons: cellular regulation's demonstrated mechanisms are stoichiometric and specific — transcription factors, kinases, scaffolds with defined interfaces — and condensate claims have repeatedly outrun their evidence (overexpression artefacts, non-physiological conditions); Musacchio's critique is the field's own audit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a condensate whose phase behaviour, at endogenous concentrations, is shown necessary for a cellular function.

Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the evidence consolidating decisively behind one mechanism across systems.
- **Soft-Matter Phase Separation Is Central** — Reasons: since 2009, membraneless organelles across cell biology have been reframed as condensates with liquid properties — fusion, wetting, rapid exchange — that binding language never predicted; LLPS gives the cell a physical organizing principle, not just a parts list. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: flagship condensates (stress granules, nucleoli) being fully explained by specific binding with phase language adding nothing.

#### Architected Metamaterials vs. Bio-Inspired Soft Matter
- **Architected Optical & Mechanical Metamaterials** — Reasons: geometry is a design variable with no biological equivalent — negative index, cloaking, auxetic and programmable mechanical response from lattice architecture alone; architected materials are designable in software and printable, a compiler for matter. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: architected materials failing mechanical reliability at scale while soft materials deliver the adaptive functions.
- **Bio-Inspired Soft & Active Materials** — Reasons: living materials heal, adapt, actuate and compute chemically — properties no passive lattice has — and hydrogels, elastomers and active matter bring those functions toward engineering; the future of materials is responsive, not static. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: soft/active materials' reproducibility and lifetime failing to reach engineering thresholds across a generation.

#### Applied Physics Aesthetic Ideal
- **Exotic Emergent Quantum Phase** — Reasons: a new phase of matter is the deepest thing applied physics can find — it rewrites what matter can do, and every device era began as someone's exotic phase (superconductivity, the quantum Hall effects). Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a generation of new phases producing no new capability, while device metrics compound.
- **Clean Room-Temperature Device Metric** — Reasons: a crisp, reproducible room-temperature benchmark — an efficiency, a fidelity, a mobility — is beauty with a number attached, checkable by any laboratory on Earth; room temperature is where the world lives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: benchmark-chasing demonstrably distorting their field's science (toward §3.1's certification concerns).
- **Massive Industrial & Energy Scale** — Reasons: physics deployed at gigawatt or billion-unit scale changes civilization's material basis — the transistor, the laser and the solar panel are supreme because they are everywhere; scale is impact made visible. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a supreme-at-scale technology proving a civilizational error (an asbestos of the energy age).
- **Seamless Bio-Physical Integration** — Reasons: hardware that interfaces cleanly with living tissue — recording neurons, dosing insulin, imaging without harm — joins physics to the system humans care about most; the aesthetic is of fit between made and grown things. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: bio-integration's clinical translation failing on safety across a technology generation.
