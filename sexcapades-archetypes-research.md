## How to read the flags

- **Well-established** — a named role in a handbook, a monograph, or a stable community instrument, with a citable source.
- **Community-established, thin in print** — stable on BDSMTest.org result sheets and in current community speech, scarce in the 1972–2001 handbooks and in academic role clusters.
- **Practice, not a role** — the literature has the activity or the risk class, and does not have a persona under that name.
- **No established archetype** — do not invent one to fill the slot.

Kink roles are not one axis. The handbooks keep three apart, and a list that collapses them will mis-file people:

| Axis | Question it answers | Canonical pair |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Scene | Who does the action in this scene? | Top / Bottom, plus Switch |
| Authority | Who holds the power? | Dominant / submissive, and the leather pair Master/Mistress / slave |
| Sensation | Who gives or receives pain? | Sadist / Masochist |

A rigger can be the submissive. A sadist can be the bottom. A service submissive may want no pain at all. Those splits are the finding, not a footnote. Source for the split: Jay Wiseman, *SM 101: A Realistic Introduction* (Greenery Press, 1996); Dossie Easton and Janet W. Hardy, *The New Topping Book* (Greenery Press, 2003) and *The New Bottoming Book* (Greenery Press, 2001).

## 1. What the literature actually treats as the role canon

Two instruments, used below as the checklist. Neither was taken from the Sexcapades skin.

### 1.1 BDSMTest.org result categories

BDSMTest.org (`https://bdsmtest.org/`) is the test the queue named. Its homepage is client-rendered, so this pass did not scrape an official legend page. The category names are attested by full result sheets the site itself renders, posted independently across years. They converge.

- A result page on bdsmtest.org dated 29 May 2026 (result id `8PQ27RrM`) that names Little in the same set (Brat, Degradee, Little, Submissive, Pet, Rope bunny, Masochist, Experimentalist, Primal (Prey), Ageplayer, Switch, Slave, and the dominant-side counterparts further down the sheet).

**Stable names across those sheets:** Dominant, Submissive, Switch, Vanilla, Sadist, Masochist, Master/Mistress, Slave, Rigger, Rope bunny, Brat, Brat tamer, Daddy/Mommy, Primal (Hunter), Primal (Prey), Pet, Owner, Degrader, Degradee, Experimentalist, Voyeur, Exhibitionist, Non-monogamist, Ageplayer.

### 1.2 Academic role clusters

Katherine Martinez, "BDSM Role Fluidity: A Mixed-Methods Approach to Investigating Switches Within Dominant/Submissive Binaries," *Journal of Homosexuality* 65, no. 10 (2018): 1299–1324. From 202 surveys and 25 interviews she groups self-labels into **DMTS** (Dominant, Master, Top, Sadist) and **SSBM** (Submissive, Slave, Bottom, Masochist), with **Switch** as the identity that moves between them.

Andreas A. J. Wismeijer and Marcel A. L. M. van Assen, "Psychological Characteristics of BDSM Practitioners," *Journal of Sexual Medicine* 10, no. 8 (2013): 1943–1952, use the same three-way cut: dominant, submissive, switch.

Theodore Bennett, "'Switch it up': A qualitative analysis of BDSM switches," *Sexualities* (2024), doi:10.1177/13634607241305967. Switches are a large part of the population and were under-studied. The paper treats switching as situational role choice, not as a blended average of the two sides.

A 2026 qualitative study records the labels people actually give themselves, including dominant, submissive, sadist, masochist, rigger, rope bunny, Owner, Master, Mistress, slave, and sensualist. It does not surface sensation-seeker or edge-player as role names. (Archives of Sexual Behavior qualitative paper on BDSM/kink self-identity, published online 2026; the public abstract and quoted self-labels are the evidence used here.)

Older sociology of the subculture, before the test existed: Martin S. Weinberg, Colin J. Williams, and Charles Moser, "The Social Constituents of Sadomasochism," *Social Problems* 31, no. 4 (1984): 379–389. Laurence Alison, Pekka Santtila, N. Kenneth Sandnabba, and Niklas Nordling, "Sadomasochistically Oriented Behavior: Diversity in Practice and Meaning," *Archives of Sexual Behavior* 30 (2001): 1–12, doi:10.1023/A:1026438422383. Alison's four scripts are **hypermasculinity, administration and receipt of pain, physical restriction, and psychological humiliation**. Those are clusters of acts, not job titles. Humiliation is the script behind degrader/degradee. Pain is the script behind sadist/masochist. Restriction is the script behind rope. None of the four is "edge-player" or "sensation-seeker."

## 2. The eighteen names in the brief

### Dominant — Well-established

- **Source:** Wiseman, *SM 101* (1996); Larry Townsend, *The Leatherman's Handbook* (1972); Martinez 2018 (the D in DMTS); BDSMTest result sheets.
- **Stance:** Holds authority in the dynamic: sets the frame, takes responsibility for the structure, and is answered by a submissive. Distinct from Top, who is only the person doing the action.
- **Flag:** Well-established. The brief's list has Dominant and does not have submissive. That hole is the largest gap in the eighteen. See §3.

### Sadist — Well-established

- **Source:** Richard von Krafft-Ebing coined *Sadismus* (after Sade) and *Masochismus* (after Sacher-Masoch) in *Psychopathia Sexualis* (first edition 1886; both terms are in place by the 1890 edition). Modern kink use, stripped of his pathology frame: Wiseman, *SM 101*; BDSMTest; Martinez's DMTS.
- **Stance:** Aroused by giving pain, intensity, or suffering inside a negotiated scene. A sadist is not automatically the dominant, and not automatically the top.
- **Flag:** Well-established. The clinical coinage and the consensual role are different objects. Cite Krafft-Ebing for the word, Wiseman and the test for the role.

### Masochist — Well-established

- **Source:** Same as sadist. Krafft-Ebing named masochism for Leopold von Sacher-Masoch. Community role: Wiseman; Easton and Hardy, *The New Bottoming Book*; BDSMTest; Martinez's SSBM.
- **Stance:** Aroused by receiving pain, intensity, or suffering inside a negotiated scene. A masochist need not be submissive.
- **Flag:** Well-established.

### Switch — Well-established

- **Source:** Easton and Hardy, both *New* books; Wiseman, *SM 101*; Wismeijer and van Assen 2013; Martinez 2018; Bennett 2024; BDSMTest.
- **Stance:** Takes both a dominant-type role and a submissive-type role, across partners, across scenes, or inside one scene. Not a midpoint and not an average of the two sides.
- **Flag:** Well-established.

### Rigger — Well-established as a craft role, not as a power role

- **Source:** FetLife Kinktionary, "Rigger," `https://fetlife.com/kinktionary/roles/rigger-cl13s` (page updated 21 Oct 2026). Midori, *The Seductive Art of Japanese Bondage* (Greenery Press, 2001). BDSMTest. Alison et al. 2001, the "physical restriction" script, is the empirical cluster, not the job title.
- **Stance:** The person who ties. Responsible for anatomy, circulation, communication, and a way out. FetLife's own entry says a rigger is not necessarily the dominant, that rope top is the broader word, and that the Japanese craft titles are *nawashi* or *bakushi*.
- **Flag:** Well-established. Do not file it under Dominant.

### Rope bunny — Community-established; the print term is rope bottom

- **Source:** BDSMTest ("Rope bunny" on the 2020, 2022, and 2026 sheets). FetLife Kinktionary treats rope bottom as the role name and rope bunny as the common synonym (see the rigger entry above, which defines the partner as a rope bottom). Midori 2001 writes about the person being tied as a model and a bottom, not as a bunny.
- **Stance:** The person who is tied. In it for the rope — sensation, aesthetics, restraint, headspace — and not, by that fact, submissive.
- **Flag:** Community-established, thin in the handbooks under the word "bunny." The canonical pair to write, if one pair is wanted, is **rigger / rope bottom**, with rope bunny as the community alias. There is no established archetype in which "rope bunny" means "submissive who likes rope." That collapse is the error.

### Brat — Community-established, with a book ancestor under another name

- **Source:** BDSMTest. Easton and Hardy, *The New Bottoming Book* (2001), name the **smart-assed masochist (SAM)**: the bottom who complies by poking, joking, and making the top work for it.
- **Stance:** A submissive-side player whose submission is delivered as resistance, teasing, and negotiated defiance, in order to draw a response. Not a refusal of consent.
- **Flag:** The behavior is well-established. The word "brat" is community-established and thinner in monographs than SAM. Prefer citing both.

### Brat tamer — Community-established, thin in print

- **Source:** BDSMTest result sheets (the paired top-side category). No handbook in the Townsend–Wiseman–Easton line uses "brat tamer" as a chapter title found for this pass.
- **Stance:** The top or dominant who meets a brat's poking with structure, consequence, and play, without breaking the negotiation.
- **Flag:** Community-established, thin in print. Real as the pair of brat. Not a separate power axis.

### Service-submissive — Well-established as a style of submission, not as a fourth axis

- **Source:** Raven Kaldera and Joshua Tenpenny, *Real Service* (2011), a handbook for service-oriented submissives and the people they serve. They separate positional service (the person serves because that is who they are) from devotional and transactional service. Guy Baldwin and slave laura, *SlaveCraft* (Daedalus Publishing), treat erotic service as a craft rather than as pain. Jack Rinella, *The Master's Manual: A Handbook of Erotic Dominance* (Daedalus Publishing, 1994). FetLife Kinktionary, "In Service To," names service submissive as one serving role beside slave. `https://fetlife.com/kinktionary/relationships/in-service-to-a7ats`
- **Stance:** Fulfilled by competent, often proactive work for a dominant — domestic, personal, ritual, or administrative — whether or not the scene contains pain or sex.
- **Flag:** Well-established as a style. The missing primary role is still **submissive**. Service-submissive does not replace it.

### Exhibitionist — Well-established, and older than the BDSM role set

- **Source:** Charles Lasègue, "Les exhibitionnistes," *L'Union Médicale* (1877), coined the clinical term. Krafft-Ebing carried it into *Psychopathia Sexualis*. Consensual kink identity: BDSMTest; Gloria G. Brame, William D. Brame, and Jon Jacobs, *Different Loving: The World of Sexual Dominance and Submission* (Villard, 1993).
- **Stance:** Aroused by being seen, showing the body, or showing the scene, by agreement with the people who watch.
- **Flag:** Well-established. The DSM exhibitionistic disorder is the nonconsensual clinical object. It is not this role. Do not cite the disorder as if it were the kink archetype.

### Voyeur — Well-established, same caveat

- **Source:** Same clinical lineage (Krafft-Ebing and the later psychiatric manuals) for the nonconsensual disorder. Consensual kink identity: BDSMTest; *Different Loving* (1993).
- **Stance:** Aroused by watching a partner or a scene that is being shown on purpose.
- **Flag:** Well-established, with the same clinical/consensual split as exhibitionist. The pair is real. Neither one is a power-exchange role.

### Degrader — Practice well-established; the job title is test vocabulary

- **Source for the practice:** Alison et al. 2001, the psychological-humiliation script (their empirical cluster, separate from pain and from restraint). Pat Califia, *Sensuous Magic: A Guide to S/M for Adventurous Couples* (Masquerade, 1993), on humiliation as a scene type. *Different Loving* (1993).
- **Source for the word:** BDSMTest result sheets. This pass did not find "degrader" as a chapter-level role in Townsend, Wiseman, or Easton and Hardy.
- **Stance:** Gives humiliation, objectification, or degrading speech and treatment inside a negotiation that names humiliation as the point.
- **Flag:** Community-established via the test. Thin in print under this noun. Do not invent a more "literary" synonym and pretend it is older.

### Degradee — Same pair, same flag

- **Source:** BDSMTest, paired with Degrader. The receiving side of Alison et al.'s humiliation script.
- **Stance:** Receives humiliation as the erotic point, which can be separate from receiving pain and separate from service.
- **Flag:** Community-established, thin in print under this noun. The pair stands or falls together.

### Primal — Community-established only as Primal (Hunter)

- **Source:** BDSMTest names **Primal (Hunter)**, not bare "primal." Absent from Townsend (1972), Wiseman (1996), and Easton and Hardy (2001) as a role. The identity spreads with the FetLife-era community (FetLife launched 2008) and is now a stable test category.
- **Stance:** Plays the giving, chasing, pinning, or "hunter" side of animalistic, instinct-forward scenes, often with less protocol than leather D/s.
- **Flag:** Community-established, academically thin, and **mis-named if the label is only "primal."** The canonical giving-side name on the instrument is Primal (Hunter).

### Prey — Established only as Primal (Prey)

- **Source:** BDSMTest, **Primal (Prey)**. Same historical limit as Hunter: not a handbook role in the 1972–2001 line.
- **Stance:** The receiving side of a primal scene: pursued, caught, overpowered in a negotiated animalistic frame.
- **Flag:** Not an established standalone archetype. Bare "prey" without the primal pair is the thin form. Write **Primal (Prey)** or do not write it.

### Cuckold — Well-established in a different literature, and incomplete alone

- **Source:** David J. Ley, *Insatiable Wives: Women Who Stray and the Men Who Love Them* (Rowman & Littlefield, 2009), the monograph on consensual hotwife/cuckold arrangements. Justin J. Lehmiller, *Tell Me What You Want* (Da Capo, 2018), documents cuckolding and related consensual-non-monogamy fantasies in a large survey. Not a BDSMTest category on the sheets cited in §1.1. Not a DMTS/SSBM label.
- **Stance:** A man aroused by his partner having sex with someone else, often with a humiliation script (reclaiming, denial, comparison). That is a relationship role, not a D/s role.
- **Flag:** Well-established in consensual-non-monogamy and erotic-humiliation writing. Contested if filed as a BDSM power archetype next to Dominant. **Incomplete as a single name.** The literature's set is a triad, plus one related variant that is not the same archetype:
  - **Cuckold** — the man in the watching / excluded / humiliated seat.
  - **Hotwife or cuckoldress** — the partner who has the outside sex (cuckoldress names the dominant-woman framing).
  - **Cuckquean** — the woman in the cuckold seat. Different person, same structure.
  - **Bull** — the outside partner.
  - **Stag and vixen** — the compersion variant: the couple shares the outside sex without the humiliation script. Related, not the same archetype. Do not merge stag into cuckold.

### Sensation-seeker — No established kink archetype under this name

- **What exists instead:**
  - **Sensation play**, an activity class in Wiseman and in Easton and Hardy: wax, ice, texture, clamps, pins, electricity, on a spectrum that is not "pain is the point." That is a kind of scene, not a person.
  - **Sensualist**, the identity word people use. It shows up in the 2026 self-label study quoted in §1.2, and in FetLife's long role index. It is not on the BDSMTest sheets cited here.
  - **Sensation seeking**, Marvin Zuckerman, *Sensation Seeking: Beyond the Optimal Level of Arousal* (Erlbaum, 1979). A personality scale. A higher score among some kink participants would be a trait correlate. It would not be a role.
- **Stance:** None. There is no canonical persona here to summarize.
- **Flag:** **No established archetype.** Do not invent "sensation-seeker" as the counterpart of sadist. If a slot is wanted, the honest community word is sensualist, and even that is a style, not a side of the slash.

### Edge-player — Practice class, not a role

- **What exists:** **Edge play**, the community name for scenes where serious harm is harder to keep remote (breath, blood, fire, cutting, guns, play while intoxicated). The ethics frame is Gary Switch's proposal of **RACK** (Risk-Aware Consensual Kink) on the TES-Friends list, 25 November 1999, offered against Safe, Sane, and Consensual on the ground that nothing is completely safe. He is credited as a contributing editor of *Prometheus*. The scholarly ethnography of playing near that line is Staci Newmahr, *Playing on the Edge: Sadomasochism, Risk, and Intimacy* (Indiana University Press, 2011). Her "edge" is the community's risk-and-intimacy line. She is not naming a membership class called edge-player the way the test names Rigger.
- **What does not exist:** an "edge-player" row on the BDSMTest sheets, a DMTS/SSBM label, or a Townsend/Wiseman persona.
- **Do not confuse with edging.** Edging, as orgasm control, is a different practice. It is not this risk class and it is not a role.
- **Flag:** **No established archetype** under the name edge-player. The established object is the practice class edge play.

**Bootblack** is a real leather service office, with title contests (International Mr. Bootblack and the later International Ms. and Mx. titles). It is narrower than the roles above. Worth knowing. Not required for a first canon.

**Service top** (and FetLife's "service rigger") is a top who ties or plays for the bottom's scene rather than from dominance. It is the mirror fact to "a rigger need not be dominant." A style of Top, not a new axis.

### Contested, and not recommended as canon here

- **Ageplayer.** Present on the BDSMTest sheets. Highly contested inside the community. This research does not describe any ageplay practice. Sexual interest in minors is not a role, is not an archetype, and is out of scope.
- **Gorean.** John Norman's Gor novels, beginning with *Tarnsman of Gor* (1966), grew a literary Master/kajira subculture. Leather communities have long treated it as adjacent and contested. It is not on the BDSMTest sheets and it is not in the Wiseman role split. Flag it. Do not add it to fill a slot.

| Brief name | Verdict | Write it as |

| Sadist | Well-established | Sadist |

| Brat tamer | Community-established, thin in print | Brat tamer, only as brat's pair |
| Service-submissive | Well-established style | A style of submissive, not a substitute for one |
| Exhibitionist | Well-established consensual role | Exhibitionist. Not the DSM disorder. |

| Primal | Incomplete | Primal (Hunter) |
| Prey | Not standalone | Primal (Prey) |
| Cuckold | Real, wrong corpus, incomplete | The triad, or leave it off a D/s canon |
| Sensation-seeker | **No established archetype** | Do not invent. Sensation play is an activity. Sensualist is the nearest identity word. |
| Edge-player | **No established archetype** | Do not invent. Edge play is a risk class. Edging is a third, unrelated practice. |

Counted plainly: **eleven** of the eighteen are established roles or established styles (dominant, sadist, masochist, switch, rigger, rope bunny, brat, brat tamer, service-submissive, exhibitionist, voyeur). **Two** more (degrader, degradee) are real test titles sitting on an older humiliation script. **Two** (primal, prey) are real only as the Hunter/Prey pair. **One** (cuckold) is real in another literature and incomplete. **Two** (sensation-seeker, edge-player) are not archetypes.

The roles a canon loses by stopping at those eighteen are **submissive, top, bottom, Master/Mistress, and slave**. Those five are older and better sourced than brat tamer, rope bunny, degrader, primal, or cuckold.
