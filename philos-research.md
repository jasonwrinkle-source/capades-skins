# Philos — Research

Skin: Philoscapades (`philoscapades.json`) · Domain: Philosophy — Human Intellectual Commitments, Metaphysical Worldviews & Normative Frameworks · Meter: position
Coverage: 29 topics, 105 poles, 8 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Philos maps philosophy: where a person stands on the oldest questions — what is true, what is real, what is right, what a life means. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's 29 questions: a pole a holder advocates for or against (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence). Holding no stance is not a position; a 0 rating is. The meter word is **position**: the skin measures how strongly a profile stands for each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness × advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole. Multi-pole poles are independent positions and need not be opposites; on an xy-switchbox topic the two poles are crossed independent yes/no axes, not ends of one dimension, and the status quo sits at both-negative. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance is pluralist and cartographic: empiricists, rationalists, pragmatists, phenomenologists, naturalists, existentialists, Platonists, and skeptics are placed on one map in one vocabulary, with no school treated as the default philosophy. Fifteen of the skin's 29 topics have an empty `question` field; for each, §2 quotes the recovered question verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue, and §5.1 collects them.

## 2. Topic rationale

### Epistemology & Theory of Truth

**The Agrippan Trilemma** — Every justification chain must end, circle, or run forever, and which horn a philosopher accepts shapes their whole epistemology. Foundationalism stops the regress at basic self-evident beliefs that justify all derived beliefs; Coherentism lets beliefs justify one another in a mutually reinforcing web; Infinitism accepts justification as an open-ended, infinite chain of warrants.

**Epistemic Grounds** — What counts as grounds for knowing is the discipline's oldest fork. Pure Rationalism trusts a priori deduction, innate ideas, and intellectual intuition; Radical Empiricism trusts a posteriori sensory verification and induction, starting from a blank slate; Pragmatism makes truth actionable utility and problem-solving efficacy; Skepticism holds certainty impossible and all knowledge provisional.

**Theories of Truth** — Seven accounts of what truth itself is, kept apart because they diverge exactly on the hard cases. Correspondence makes truth a structural match between proposition and mind-independent reality; Coherence makes it systemic consistency within a web of propositions; Pragmatic makes it what works in experiential practice and fruitful inquiry; Deflationary holds that asserting truth adds no semantic content beyond the sentence itself; Semantic defines truth across meta-language satisfaction criteria, without paradox; Epistemic makes it ideal rational assertibility under conditions of unforced agreement; Phenomenological recovers truth as unhiddenness — aletheia, a primordial clearing.

**The Epistemological Octad** — A radar of eight epistemic allegiances a profile can hold in combination; unlike the other topics, its poles carry no hovertip definitions in the skin (see §5), so the glosses here are the standard meanings of the schools named. Empiricism grounds knowledge in experience; Rationalism in reason; Pragmatism in practical consequence; Phenomenology in lived first-person experience; Hermeneutics in interpretation; Skepticism in suspended assent; Mysticism in direct, non-discursive apprehension; Dialectics in knowledge developed through the interplay and resolution of opposed positions.

**Meaning & Reference** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is the meaning of our words determined inside the head, or by causal links to the external world?” An xy-switchbox topic: its two poles are crossed independent axes, not opposites. Internalist Mental Content holds that two exact physical duplicates must share the same thoughts and intentional meanings; Externalist World-Hooked holds that meanings are not in the head — natural kinds and linguistic communities fix what terms refer to.

**Logic & Paradox** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Must every proposition be strictly true or false, or should logic tolerate truth-value gaps and local contradictions?” An xy-switchbox topic: its two poles are crossed independent axes, not opposites. Classical Bivalence holds excluded middle and non-contradiction as indispensable rails keeping reasoning from collapse; Paraconsistent / Intuitionist holds that constructive proof and real semantic paradoxes show classical logic overreaching in infinite or self-referential domains.

**Relation to Science** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is philosophy continuous with empirical science, or does it investigate prior conceptual foundations?” An xy-switchbox topic: its two poles are crossed independent axes, not opposites. Naturalized Continuity works best with philosophy as the conceptual vanguard of the sciences, not an armchair tribunal above them; Autonomous First Philosophy holds that science presupposes logic, normativity, and ontology that experiments cannot validate without circularity.

**Scientific realism** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Does science tell us what the world is like, or only give us tools for predicting it?” Realist holds our best theories approximately true about the world; Instrumentalist holds theories to be tools, judged by use, not truth; Structural realist holds that science gets the structure right, not the objects we picture.

### Metaphysics & Ontology

**The Mind-Body Ontology Matrix** — The triangle beneath every philosophy-of-mind position: what is fundamental. Matter fundamental makes what exists at bottom physical; Mind fundamental makes what exists at bottom mental; Neutral monism makes mind and body two faces of something itself neither mental nor physical.

**Free Will & Agency** — Whether we are free decides what moral responsibility can mean. Hard Determinism holds that every choice flows from prior causes and luck, making ultimate moral desert an illusion; Compatibilism holds that freedom means acting from one's own desires and reasons without coercion, which determinism never threatens; Libertarian Agency holds that genuine moral agency requires an open fork in the road not fixed by the distant past.

**The Ontology of Universals** — Whether redness, justice, and triangularity exist decides what predication and mathematics are about. Platonic Realism places universals ante rem in an objective non-physical realm; Conceptualism places them solely as conceptual frameworks within minds; Nominalism holds that only individual particulars exist and universals are mere names.

**The Metaphysics of Time** — What exists in time is the quiet premise under every account of change, memory, and death. Presentism holds that only the present moment exists — past gone, future unformed; Growing Block holds past and present existing while the future actively becomes; Eternalism holds past, present, and future existing simultaneously in four-dimensional spacetime.

**God and the supernatural** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Does God exist — and if so, what kind of God?” Theist holds that God exists and is more than the world; Atheist holds that God does not exist; Agnostic holds that we cannot know whether God exists; Pantheist holds that God is the world, not a person beyond it; Deist holds that God exists and does not intervene.

**Causation** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “What is causation — one thing following another, a real power in things, or what we can intervene on?” Regular succession makes causation constant conjunction, with no necessary connection to see; Real powers makes causation a real relation — powers and mechanisms in things; Manipulability makes causation what changes when we intervene.

**Personal identity** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “What makes you the same person you were ten years ago — your memories, your body, or nothing at all?” Psychological continuity persists a person by continuity of memory and character; Bodily continuity persists them by continuity of the living body; No persisting self holds there is no further self that stays — the person is a story we keep telling.

**Modal realism** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Are other possible worlds real places, or is 'possible' only a way of talking?” Real concrete worlds makes possible worlds concrete places, as real as this one; Only the actual world holds only this world real, possibility being talk or a device; Abstract representations makes possible worlds abstract stand-ins, not concrete places.

### Axiology & Normative Ethics

**The Grand Normative Ethical Quartet** — Four answers to what makes an act right, kept distinct because they prescribe differently in the hard cases. Deontology follows categorical imperatives, rights, and moral rules independent of outcome; Consequentialism maximizes net flourishing, welfare, or utility; Virtue Ethics cultivates character, practical wisdom, and flourishing; Care Ethics starts from interdependence, contextual compassion, and vulnerability.

**Meta-Ethics Compass** — Before what is right: what kind of thing a moral claim is. Moral Realism holds objective moral facts existing independently of human attitudes; Moral Relativism holds moral claims true relative to cultural or individual standards; Error Theory holds all moral propositions cognitive but systematically false; Expressivism holds moral claims to express non-truth-apt emotions or commands.

**Intrinsic Value** — What is good in itself, not as a means, anchors every normative system. Hedonism makes conscious pleasure and absence of pain the sole intrinsic goods; Eudaimonism makes human flourishing and the realization of rational potential intrinsic; Desire-Satisfaction makes fulfillment of individual subjective preferences and projects intrinsic; Objective List holds a plurality of goods — knowledge, beauty, friendship, autonomy.

**Scope of Moral Circle** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “How should we weigh special duties to loved ones against impartial obligations to strangers and non-humans?” Partial Loyalties First holds thick duties to family, friends, and community as the soil from which any real care grows; Impartial Sentientism counts every capacity for suffering equally regardless of species, distance, or kinship; Ecocentric Holism gives ecosystems, species, and the biosphere intrinsic value beyond individual sentient welfare.

**Population & Future Ethics** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Should ethics prioritize existing people, or weigh vast numbers of potential future lives equally?” An xy-switchbox topic: its two poles are crossed independent axes, not opposites. Person-Affecting Focus holds morality to be about making existing people happy, not manufacturing new people to boost a total score; Longtermist Aggregation holds future people just as real as present ones, so safeguarding civilization's vast future is paramount.

### Philosophy of Mind & Consciousness

**The Consciousness Spectrum** — Five accounts of what consciousness is and where it comes from, spanning denial to ubiquity. Illusionism holds phenomenal consciousness and qualia not to exist as advertised; Computational Functionalism makes mind software — multiply realizable; Biological Emergence makes consciousness an emergent biological feature; Panpsychism makes consciousness fundamental to all matter; Cosmopsychism makes one universal consciousness dissociate into individual minds.

**Leading Neural Theory** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Which scientific framework best captures the architecture of conscious awareness?” Global Workspace Broadcast makes information conscious when widely broadcast across specialized brain networks for flexible action; Integrated Information (IIT) identifies consciousness with a system's irreducible causal integration, measured by phi; Predictive Processing makes conscious perception the brain's generative model constrained by sensory prediction errors; Higher-Order Thought makes a state conscious only when represented by a meta-cognitive thought about itself.

**Causal Power of Qualia** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Does the felt pain of a burn actually move your hand, or do neural circuits do all the causal work?” An xy-switchbox topic: its two poles are crossed independent axes, not opposites. Epiphenomenal Passenger holds physics causally closed, felt qualia accompanying neural firing without pushing atoms around; Downward Mental Causation holds that evolution would never have sculpted vivid pleasure and agony if feeling itself did no causal work.

### Existentialism & The Human Condition

**Responses to the Absurd** — Seven responses to the clash between our hunger for meaning and a silent world — including denying the clash. Camusian Revolt lives defiantly in the face of meaninglessness, without illusion; Kierkegaardian Leap transcends the absurd through religious surrender; Sartrean Freedom holds existence preceding essence, so meaning is authored; Nietzschean Overcoming transvalues old dogmas and forges the self beyond them; Stoic Acceptance harmonizes with cosmic necessity — amor fati; Transhumanist Overcoming engineers the biological vehicle itself; The Absurd is not real denies the clash, holding the hunger or the world's silence misdescribed.

**Death & Annihilation** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is mortality a harmless return to nonexistence, or an intrinsic evil to be resisted?” An xy-switchbox topic: its two poles are crossed independent axes, not opposites. Epicurean Tranquility holds that where death is, I am not — fearing nonexistence is as irrational as mourning the centuries before birth; Fierce Refusal & Grief holds that death destroys an irreplaceable centre of consciousness and love, so rage and medical defiance are entirely sane.

**Meaning of a life** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is there a fact about what makes a life meaningful, or does each person make that, or is there none?” Objective meaning holds there are facts about what makes a life meaningful, not only preferences; Created meaning holds meaning is made — a life has the meaning the person gives it; No meaning holds there is no meaning to find or to make, and the demand for one is the mistake.

### Political Philosophy & Justice

**The Four Theories of Justice** — Four accounts of what justice is, each privileging a different remedy. Distributive seeks fair economic allocation, in the line of the difference principle; Retributive seeks proportional desert and retribution for moral wrongs; Restorative seeks rehabilitation, healing relationships, and reconciliation; Procedural seeks strict adherence to voluntary acquisition and transfer.

**Political legitimacy** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “What makes a government legitimate — consent, good outcomes, fair procedures, or nothing?” Consent makes a state legitimate when the governed have agreed to it; Outcomes makes it legitimate when it governs well; Fair procedures makes it legitimate when its rules are fair, whatever the results; No state is legitimate holds that no state clears the bar — authority of that kind is not justified.


## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 Epistemology & Theory of Truth

#### The Agrippan Trilemma
- **Foundationalism** — Reasons: every chain of reasons must start somewhere, and basic beliefs — that there is a hand, that pain hurts — justify without needing justification, on pain of never starting; Descartes and Aristotle, in their different idioms, saw that a web with no anchor floats free of the world. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proposed basic belief shown to be as corrigible and theory-laden as the beliefs it was meant to anchor.
- **Coherentism** — Reasons: justification in practice is mutual support — perceptions, memories, and theories checking one another, as Neurath's boat is rebuilt at sea; a belief's fitting a wide, explanatory, consistent web is the only test any actual knower ever applies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two incompatible webs, each internally coherent, with no coherentist way to choose between them.
- **Infinitism** — Reasons: a finite chain ends in dogma and a circle argues in a loop, so the only non-arbitrary structure is an infinite, ever-extendable chain of warrants, as Klein argues; a finite mind need not complete the chain — only be able to continue it when challenged. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that finite agents cannot so much as possess an infinite chain, even dispositionally.

#### Epistemic Grounds
- **Pure Rationalism** — Reasons: mathematics and logic are known without sensory input and with a certainty experience never delivers — the senses report that the stick bends in water, reason corrects them; innate structure explains how experience becomes knowledge at all, since a truly blank mind could not organize a sensation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a complete empiricist account of mathematical necessity and the uniformity of nature that smuggled in no a priori principle.
- **Radical Empiricism** — Reasons: every concept can be traced to impressions, as Hume demanded — produce the impression or suspect the idea; a posteriori verification is the only ground that has ever publicly settled a question of fact, and the tabula rasa keeps epistemology honest about its debts. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a truth of fact, not of definition, known with certainty in advance of any possible experience.
- **Pragmatism** — Reasons: a belief's meaning and truth show themselves in what it lets us do — Peirce and James: inquiry ends in settled practice, not in contemplation of essences; grounds that never cash out in problem-solving are grounds in name only. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a belief that worked flawlessly in practice and was, on independent grounds, demonstrably false — utility and truth coming permanently apart.
- **Skepticism** — Reasons: the senses deceive, reason circles, and every certainty so far has had its century — Sextus's modes still have no agreed refutation; holding all knowledge as provisional is not despair but the only posture the track record licenses. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one item of knowledge — just one — certified immune to every skeptical scenario without begging the question.

#### Theories of Truth
- **Correspondence** — Reasons: "the cat is on the mat" is true because the cat is on the mat — truth is the proposition's match to a mind-independent world, and every other theory quietly borrows this intuition; correspondence alone explains why true beliefs succeed in action. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a truth-bearer — a future contingent, a mathematical identity — for which no corresponding fact could be coherently specified.
- **Coherence** — Reasons: a proposition's truth shows in its consistency with the whole interconnected web — isolated "facts" are abstractions, and the system is what gets confirmed or disconfirmed; coherence explains why we rationally revise peripheral beliefs to protect central ones. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fully coherent system, elaborated in detail, that was nevertheless a fiction — coherence floating entirely free of the world.
- **Pragmatic** — Reasons: truth is what inquiry converges on in experiential practice — a belief that works, predicts, and fruits further inquiry earns the title; a "truth" with no possible difference to practice is a difference that makes no difference. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proposition that worked permanently in practice while its negation corresponded to the facts.
- **Deflationary** — Reasons: "‘snow is white’ is true" says no more than "snow is white" — the truth predicate is a device of disquotation and generalization, not a deep property awaiting a theory; deflationism dissolves pseudo-problems the substantive theories manufacture. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a use of "true" — in logic, in generalization over infinite sets — that demonstrably carried content disquotation could not supply.
- **Semantic** — Reasons: Tarski's definition gives truth exact, paradox-free treatment by defining it for an object language within a meta-language via satisfaction; rigour of this kind is what a theory of truth should look like, and the liar paradox shows what happens without the hierarchy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: natural language proving to need a single, non-hierarchical truth predicate that the semantic theory cannot consistently provide.
- **Epistemic** — Reasons: truth as ideal rational assertibility — what would be agreed under conditions of unforced, fully informed inquiry — ties truth to our best epistemic practice without making it a vote; it explains both truth's objectivity and our fallible access to it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a truth in principle beyond all possible inquiry — assertible under no conditions, yet plainly true.
- **Phenomenological** — Reasons: before propositions match or cohere, things must show themselves — aletheia, truth as unhiddenness, names the primordial clearing in which anything can appear as anything at all; Heidegger's point is that correspondence presupposes disclosure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an account of disclosure that reduced it, without remainder, to propositional truth plus causation.

#### The Epistemological Octad
- **Empiricism** — Reasons: experience is the tribunal every other court appeals to — even rationalists test their systems against what is observed; the success of the empirical sciences is empiricism's standing exhibit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: knowledge, certified as such, that no possible experience could have produced or checked.
- **Rationalism** — Reasons: reason discovers necessities — in mathematics, logic, and the structure of concepts — that experience, which reports only what happens, cannot reach; deduction extends knowledge, it does not merely rearrange sensation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a derivation of the apparently a priori — geometry, arithmetic — from experience that lost nothing in the translation.
- **Pragmatism** — Reasons: ideas are instruments, and their meaning is their conceivable practical bearing — the pragmatic maxim dissolves disputes that no experiment or action could settle; truth that makes no difference is a label without a job. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a live philosophical dispute with no practical bearing whose resolution nevertheless mattered to knowledge itself.
- **Phenomenology** — Reasons: all evidence is evidence to a subject — describing experience as it is lived, before theory explains it away, recovers the phenomena every other method presupposes; Husserl's return to the things themselves begins by bracketing what we assume about them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: first-person description proving systematically unreliable about the experiences it described — the method failing at home.
- **Hermeneutics** — Reasons: understanding is interpretation all the way down — texts, actions, and other minds are read within horizons of prior meaning, and the hermeneutic circle is not a vice but the structure of understanding itself; Gadamer showed prejudice, rightly worked, is a condition of knowledge, not only its enemy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a domain of understanding demonstrably free of interpretation — meaning received without a horizon.
- **Skepticism** — Reasons: suspension of assent where evidence underdetermines is not a failure of epistemology but its conscience — the skeptical traditions, Pyrrhonian and Humean, are the quality control every dogmatism eventually needs; provisional knowledge honestly labelled beats certainty falsely claimed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a skeptical argument answered on its own terms — not outlived, not bypassed, but refuted.
- **Mysticism** — Reasons: some apprehension is direct and non-discursive — the mystics of independent traditions converge on an experience of unity that concepts fragment; if knowledge includes acquaintance, the deepest acquaintance may be the least articulable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: mystical reports proving wholly explicable as cultural script — the convergence across traditions dissolving under scrutiny.
- **Dialectics** — Reasons (this pole's name is the skin's own label, quoted as a position, in the school-of-thought sense): knowledge develops as opposed positions expose one another's one-sidedness and are preserved-and-surpassed in a more adequate view — the method reads its own history as evidence; contradiction, on this account, is an engine, not only an error. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major development of knowledge in which opposition and its resolution played no role at all.

#### Meaning & Reference
- **Internalist Mental Content** — Reasons: two exact physical duplicates — molecule-for-molecule twins — must surely think the same thoughts, since everything about them is the same; content that varied between identical heads would be content no thinker could ever know they had. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: Putnam's Twin Earth intuition holding firm under pressure — "water" meaning differently for duplicates, with the difference mattering to what they think. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)
- **Externalist World-Hooked** — Reasons: meanings are not in the head — "water" referred to H₂O before anyone knew chemistry, because natural kinds and the linguistic community fix reference causally; Burge's arthritis case shows even a speaker's own concepts answer to their community's usage. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a duplicate pair whose thoughts demonstrably could not differ, in a case engineered to externalist specifications. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)

#### Logic & Paradox
- **Classical Bivalence** — Reasons: excluded middle and non-contradiction are the rails of mathematics, law, and engineering — reasoning that tolerates true contradictions can prove anything, which is to say nothing; two thousand years of successful inference is the classical logician's track record. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a working, indispensable body of reasoning — in mathematics or computing — that required true contradictions and could not be paraphrased classically. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)
- **Paraconsistent / Intuitionist** — Reasons: the liar paradox is a real sentence, not a trick, and classical logic's response — explosion from any contradiction — is a failure, not a solution; intuitionism adds that a proof of existence should construct the object, a demand classical existence proofs refuse without argument. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the paradoxes dissolving under a classical treatment that working logicians accepted as final. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)

#### Relation to Science
- **Naturalized Continuity** — Reasons: philosophy's armchair tribunal has a poor verdict record against the sciences' — Quine's naturalized epistemology simply continues inquiry by other means, with no first philosophy above the evidence; conceptual vanguard work — in logic, language, and mind — is already how philosophers contribute. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a philosophical result, reached from the armchair, that science needed and could not have reached itself. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)
- **Autonomous First Philosophy** — Reasons: science presupposes logic, normativity, and ontology — what counts as evidence, why inference binds — and cannot validate those presuppositions experimentally without using them, which is circular; somebody must do the prior work, and that work is philosophy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a presupposition of science — induction, say — receiving a fully empirical, non-circular vindication. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)

#### Scientific realism
- **Realist** — Reasons: the no-miracles argument — the predictive success of mature science would be a miracle if its theories were not approximately true about unobservables; electrons were real before they were pictured, which is why the pictures kept improving toward them. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mature, predictively supreme theory whose central entities were shown not merely unpictured but absent.
- **Instrumentalist** — Reasons: theories are tools for prediction and control, and the graveyard of once-successful false theories — phlogiston, caloric, ether — warns against believing the current one's furniture; use is a standard that never has to be retracted. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: predictive success so specific and novel — a predicted entity found exactly as described — that instrumental modesty became the less plausible reading.
- **Structural realist** — Reasons: across theory change, the equations survive while the pictured objects die — Fresnel's optics kept its mathematics and lost its ether; structure is what science demonstrably gets right, so structure is what the realist should believe in. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a revolution in which even the mathematical structure was discarded wholesale, leaving nothing for the structural claim to hold.

### 3.2 Metaphysics & Ontology

#### The Mind-Body Ontology Matrix
- **Matter fundamental** — Reasons: every mental event so far examined has a physical correlate and a physical causal history, and physics' causal closure leaves no work for a second fundamental kind; parsimony puts one substance at the bottom until evidence prices a second. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mental phenomenon — verified, replicated — with no physical correlate and physical effects unaccounted for.
- **Mind fundamental** — Reasons: matter is known only as it appears in experience, while experience is known directly — Berkeley's and the idealists' asymmetry has never been answered, only ignored; physics describes experience's structure from inside it, which idealism predicts and materialism must explain away. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a complete physical account of consciousness that left no explanatory gap for the idealist to occupy.
- **Neutral monism** — Reasons: mind and matter are both constructions from a neutral given — Russell's and James's proposal dissolves the interaction problem by denying the two-substance starting point; a monism that is neither mental nor physical explains why each side's arguments against the other succeed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the neutral basis proving, on analysis, to be straightforwardly one of the two kinds it was meant to transcend.

#### Free Will & Agency
- **Hard Determinism** — Reasons: every choice traces to prior causes and constitutive luck — genes, upbringing, circumstance — none of which the agent chose, so ultimate moral desert dissolves under regress, as Pereboom argues; neuroscience's readiness potentials are not the argument, but they rhyme with it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a choice demonstrably uncaused by anything about the agent or their past that was still, intelligibly, the agent's own doing.
- **Compatibilism** — Reasons: freedom worth wanting is acting from one's own desires and reasons without coercion — Frankfurt cases show responsibility surviving even where alternatives close; determinism threatens a freedom nobody needs, and leaves untouched the one embedded in law, blame, and self-understanding. Statistic: among professional philosophers surveyed, compatibilism about free will was the plurality view at about 59% (PhilPapers Survey, 2020; reported in Bourget & Chalmers, 2023). What would move a holder: a case where an agent acted wholly from their own reasons, uncoerced, and the intuition of unfreedom proved irresistible rather than theoretical.
- **Libertarian Agency** — Reasons: genuine moral agency requires an open fork — a future not fixed by the distant past — because praise and blame for the inevitable are category errors; agent-causal accounts hold that the agent, not merely events in the agent, originates the choice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: libertarian freedom's mechanism — the uncaused origination — proving incoherent on inspection: luck wearing freedom's clothes.

#### The Ontology of Universals
- **Platonic Realism** — Reasons: redness, triangularity, and justice recur identically across instances and minds, and mathematics — true of no particular place or time — needs objects that are not particulars; ante rem universals explain predication, resemblance, and abstract truth in one posit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a nominalist semantics handling mathematics and resemblance with no loss — the realm shown to be unemployed.
- **Conceptualism** — Reasons: universals exist as the mind's frameworks for grouping particulars — real enough to do their work, located where they are actually encountered; conceptualism keeps realism's order without Platonism's inaccessible realm and its epistemology problem. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a universal whose behaviour — in mathematics, say — refused to depend on any mind that conceived it.
- **Nominalism** — Reasons: only particulars are ever met, weighed, or pointed at — "redness" names our habit of grouping red things, and Ockham's razor forbids populating a second realm to explain a habit; every putative universal's work can be reassigned to resemblance among particulars. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a resemblance or mathematical fact that resisted every paraphrase into particulars.

#### The Metaphysics of Time
- **Presentism** — Reasons: only the present is real in experience and in physics' becoming — the past is gone, the future unformed, and treating either as existing confuses memory and anticipation with ontology; presentism is the metaphysics of the lived now. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: relativity's simultaneity results proving to require a four-dimensional ontology rather than merely permit one.
- **Growing Block** — Reasons: the past is fixed and real — it contains Caesar's crossing, which truth needs as a truthmaker — while the future is genuinely open and becoming; the growing block keeps both becoming and the reality of what has become. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a physics of time in which the block's growing edge — the moving now — proved unstatable without contradiction.
- **Eternalism** — Reasons: special relativity abolishes absolute simultaneity, so "what exists now" has no frame-independent answer — past, present, and future exist tenselessly in four-dimensional spacetime; eternalism is the ontology relativity actually describes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a relativistic physics restoring an objective, universal present.

#### God and the supernatural
- **Theist** — Reasons: the world's existence, order, and fine structure call for a ground beyond the world — contingency arguments ask why there is anything, and theism answers with a necessary being rather than a brute stop; religious experience, across cultures, is data a naturalist must discount wholesale to dismiss. Statistic: about 19% of professional philosophers surveyed accepted or leaned toward theism (PhilPapers Survey, 2020; reported in Bourget & Chalmers, 2023). What would move a holder: a complete naturalistic account of existence and apparent design that left the theist's explananda genuinely explained, not renamed.
- **Atheist** — Reasons: the evidence offered for a God beyond the world is, on inspection, explainable without one, and Ockham's razor then does its work; the problem of evil — the scale and distribution of suffering — is positive evidence against an omnipotent, benevolent person, not merely an absence of evidence for one. Statistic: about 67% of professional philosophers surveyed accepted or leaned toward atheism (PhilPapers Survey, 2020; reported in Bourget & Chalmers, 2023). What would move a holder: a theistic argument — cosmological, experiential, or moral — that survived the scrutiny applied to every other existence claim.
- **Agnostic** — Reasons: the question outruns our evidence in both directions — the arguments for and against survey a reality we sample from inside, briefly; honest suspension, where the balance is genuinely unreadable, is a conclusion, not a shrug. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: either side producing an argument whose premises the agnostic already accepted and whose conclusion followed.
- **Pantheist** — Reasons: God as the world itself — Spinoza's Deus sive Natura — keeps the reverence and unity theists seek without a person beyond nature issuing commands; the divine is the totality's necessity and intelligibility, encountered directly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: pantheism proving to be atheism with incense — the relabelling charge answered by nothing the position adds.
- **Deist** — Reasons: a creator inferred from nature's rational order, who does not intervene, fits both the design evidence and the observed absence of miracles — the Enlightenment's settlement between science and faith; deism asks religion to live on what reason can actually show. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: credible, scrutinized intervention — or a demonstration that a non-intervening creator explains nothing a creatorless nature does not.

#### Causation
- **Regular succession** — Reasons: Hume's audit stands — we observe constant conjunction and never a necessary connection, so causation as a metaphysical glue is an inference our minds project; regularity accounts need nothing unobservable and explain our predictive practice completely. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a causal fact — a prevention, an omission causing an outcome — that no pattern of succession could represent.
- **Real powers** — Reasons: things act from what they are — charges repel, acids corrode — and powers, dispositions, and mechanisms explain why the regularities hold rather than merely recording them; science's own language, from fields to enzymes, is powers language. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: powers proving to be regularities in costume — every dispositional claim cashing out, without residue, into conjunction.
- **Manipulability** — Reasons: causation is what intervention changes — Woodward's account ties causal claims to what would happen under hypothetical wiggles, which is testable, and matches experimental practice exactly; a cause you cannot, even in principle, manipulate is a cause you cannot check. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a clear causal relation — the Big Bang causing the present, say — where intervention is impossible in principle and the causal claim still plainly true.

#### Personal identity
- **Psychological continuity** — Reasons: memory, character, and intention are what survival matters to us — Locke's insight, Parfit's refinement: a future being psychologically continuous with you inherits your plans, desert, and concern; bodily continuity without psychology is a surviving organism, not a surviving person. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: fission cases proving psychological continuity cannot be what identity is, since it can branch where identity cannot.
- **Bodily continuity** — Reasons: you are this living animal, and its continuity — one organism, unbroken — survives sleep, amnesia, and personality change, which psychology-first accounts cannot say; animalism avoids multiplying entities: the thinker and the body are one thing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case — a brain-state transfer, fully realized — where bodily and psychological verdicts diverged and the psychological one was undeniable.
- **No persisting self** — Reasons: Hume looked inward and found perceptions, never a perceiver — the self is a bundle and a story, a narrative the brain keeps telling; Parfit's reductionism concludes identity is not what matters, and the conclusion consoles rather than terrifies once absorbed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a feature of experience — its unity at a moment, say — that no bundle or narrative account could reconstruct.

#### Modal realism
- **Real concrete worlds** — Reasons: Lewis's argument is bookkeeping honesty — we quantify over possibilities constantly, and taking them as concrete worlds, spatiotemporally isolated but as real as ours, makes modal truth objective instead of primitive; the incredulous stare is not an argument. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an ersatz account — linguistic or abstract — delivering every modal truth Lewis's worlds deliver, at lower ontological price.
- **Only the actual world** — Reasons: one world is ontology enough — possibility is a way of talking, a device of representation, and multiplying concrete universes beyond all observation is metaphysics at its least disciplined; the actualist keeps the modal talk and declines the real estate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: modal truths that resisted every actualist paraphrase — possibilities that had to be somewhere.
- **Abstract representations** — Reasons: possible worlds as abstract stand-ins — maximal consistent states of affairs, sets of propositions — give modal semantics its precision without Lewis's concrete plurality; abstraction is how mathematics handles infinity, and modality can be handled the same way. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: abstract worlds proving unable to represent genuinely alien possibilities — properties no actual constituent can build.


### 3.3 Axiology & Normative Ethics

#### The Grand Normative Ethical Quartet
- **Deontology** — Reasons: some acts — lying, using a person merely as a means — are wrong in themselves, and a morality that prices them by outcome has already conceded what it should protect; Kant's categorical imperative and the language of rights give agents and victims a standing no sum can override. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rule whose obedient observance produced catastrophe its holder could not, in conscience, accept — and no principled exception available.
- **Consequentialism** — Reasons: outcomes are what morality is ultimately for — suffering relieved and flourishing created are the point of the enterprise, and rules are instruments toward them; any theory that prefers its rule to a preventable disaster has mistaken the map for the territory. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where maximizing the good required an act — framing the innocent — whose wrongness survived every consequentialist repair.
- **Virtue Ethics** — Reasons: the ethical question is not only "what should I do?" but "what should I be?" — Aristotle's phronesis, character, and eudaimonia cover the terrain rules and sums miss, including motives, perception, and a life's whole shape; good people notice what rule-followers walk past. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two fully virtuous agents, identically situated, reaching opposite acts — virtue giving no verdict where one was needed.
- **Care Ethics** — Reasons: morality begins in interdependence — the relations of care in which humans actually live — and abstract impartiality systematically undervalues vulnerability, dependency, and context, as Gilligan and Held argue; an ethics that starts from strangers starts from a fiction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: care's partiality licensing an injustice to outsiders that no care-internal correction could catch.

#### Meta-Ethics Compass
- **Moral Realism** — Reasons: moral disagreement behaves like factual disagreement — we argue, adduce evidence, and correct ourselves, and "torturing children is wrong" does not feel like a taste report; realism takes moral practice at face value: objective facts, independent of attitudes, that our judgments track or miss. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a complete error-theoretic or expressivist account of moral practice that explained the phenomenology without the facts.
- **Moral Relativism** — Reasons: moral codes vary deeply across cultures and centuries, and each code's claims are assertible only within its standards — relativism is the anthropology taken seriously; it also counsels a tolerance that universalist moralities have historically lacked at others' expense. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a moral judgment — against slavery, say — that the relativist must call true only-relative while experiencing it as unconditionally binding.
- **Error Theory** — Reasons: moral propositions purport to state objective, categorically binding facts, and the world contains no such queer entities, as Mackie argued — so the propositions are all false, and honesty is to say so; error theory alone takes both moral language's ambitions and metaphysics' audit seriously. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one moral fact, produced and inspected, with its categorical bindingness intact.
- **Expressivism** — Reasons: moral claims do the work of emotions, attitudes, and commands — "stealing is wrong" voices disapproval and prescribes, which is why moral argument motivates as description never does; expressivism explains moral language's action-guidingness, its home turf. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: moral embedding — conditionals, negations, inferences — proving to need truth-aptness that expression cannot reconstruct (the Frege–Geach pressure, answered or not).

#### Intrinsic Value
- **Hedonism** — Reasons: pleasure and the absence of pain are the only goods whose value needs no further justification — ask why knowledge or friendship matters and the answer terminates in how they feel; Bentham's and Epicurus's candidate is the only one present in every experience of value. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: Nozick's experience machine refused — a life of maximal felt pleasure intuitively worthless beside a lesser real one.
- **Eudaimonism** — Reasons: flourishing — the realized exercise of rational capacities over a whole life — is what pleasure, goods, and virtues are for, and Aristotle's account alone evaluates lives rather than moments; a pleasant wreck of a life is not a good life, which hedonism cannot say. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flourishing, virtuous life that its liver experienced as misery — eudaimonia and welfare coming permanently apart.
- **Desire-Satisfaction** — Reasons: value respects the valuer — a good is good for someone when it fulfills their informed preferences and projects, and any objective list imposed against them is paternalism; desire-satisfaction is the only theory of welfare that asks the person. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: desires — adaptive, manipulated, or self-destructive — whose satisfaction plainly made their holder worse off.
- **Objective List** — Reasons: knowledge, beauty, friendship, and autonomy are each good whether or not they please or are desired — the plurality matches our actual evaluative practice, which no single currency has ever reproduced; a list is not a failure of theory but a report of value's real structure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: list members proving reducible — each good's value fully explained by one deeper good it serves.

#### Scope of Moral Circle
- **Partial Loyalties First** — Reasons: care is learned and lived in thick relations — family, friends, community — and duties to them are not biases to correct but the soil any real morality grows from; an impartiality that prices a child's cry like a stranger's statistic describes no human moral life ever lived. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: partial loyalty licensing a grave neglect of outsiders that the loyalist's own values condemned.
- **Impartial Sentientism** — Reasons: suffering is suffering wherever it occurs — species, distance, and kinship do not change what agony is for the one in it, as Singer's drowning-child argument presses; every historical expansion of the circle has looked, in retrospect, like the correction of an arbitrary exclusion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: impartial demands reaching a scale — every resource owed to the worst-off everywhere — that dissolved the special relations morality also plainly contains.
- **Ecocentric Holism** — Reasons: ecosystems, species, and the biosphere are the conditions and contexts of all sentient welfare, and Leopold's land ethic extends value to the wholes themselves; a sentientism that watches species vanish while optimizing welfare has mistaken the unit of what matters. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: holism pricing an individual sentient life at nothing against an ecosystem metric — the whole devouring its members.

#### Population & Future Ethics
- **Person-Affecting Focus** — Reasons: morality is about making people happy, not making happy people — creating a life is not a favour to the nonexistent, and total scores that reward manufacturing people commit the repugnant conclusion's arithmetic; person-affecting views, in Narveson's line, keep ethics addressed to someone. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a choice between futures differing only in who exists, where the person-affecting view gave no verdict and silence was itself a verdict. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)
- **Longtermist Aggregation** — Reasons: future people are as real as present ones — Parfit's and the longtermists' point is that temporal distance is not moral distance — and civilization's potential future is so vast that safeguarding it dominates present calculations; extinction is the one outcome no later good can compensate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: aggregation licensing present sacrifices of a scale and certainty that the speculative size of the future could not honestly underwrite. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)

### 3.4 Philosophy of Mind & Consciousness

#### The Consciousness Spectrum
- **Illusionism** — Reasons: phenomenal consciousness as advertised — private, ineffable qualia — is an introspective illusion, as Frankish and Dennett argue: what exists is access, report, and control, misdescribed by introspection as inner glow; illusionism dissolves the hard problem by auditing the intuition that generates it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a phenomenal property demonstrated to do explanatory work no access or report mechanism could absorb.
- **Computational Functionalism** — Reasons: minds are multiply realizable — the same cognition in neurons, and in principle in silicon — so mind is software: organization, not material; functionalism is the working hypothesis of cognitive science and AI alike, and it explains mentality's substrate-independence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mental capacity shown to depend essentially on biological material — organization identical, mentality absent.
- **Biological Emergence** — Reasons: consciousness appears in evolution exactly where complex living systems do, and nowhere else so far — an emergent biological feature, like digestion, tied to organisms' self-maintenance; emergence respects both physical continuity and consciousness's evident novelty. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a non-biological system — a machine — presenting consciousness's full profile under tests the biological view itself endorsed.
- **Panpsychism** — Reasons: physics describes matter's behaviour and is silent on its intrinsic nature — panpsychism fills that silence with proto-experience, avoiding both dualism and emergence from the wholly insentient, as Strawson and Goff argue; the combination problem is a research programme, not a refutation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the combination problem proving insoluble in principle — subjects demonstrably unable to compose into a further subject.
- **Cosmopsychism** — Reasons: if consciousness is fundamental, the universe's consciousness is prior and individual minds are its dissociations — the cosmopsychist reverses panpsychism's direction and inherits no combination problem; the view's ancestry in Vedantic and idealist traditions is a pedigree, not an embarrassment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the dissociation mechanism proving as mysterious as the combination it replaced — one problem traded for its mirror.

#### Leading Neural Theory
- **Global Workspace Broadcast** — Reasons: information becomes conscious when broadcast widely across specialized networks for flexible action — Baars's and Dehaene's theory makes testable predictions (ignition, late widespread activation) that experiments keep engaging; consciousness as availability explains its functional profile exactly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: conscious perception demonstrated without broadcast — or full broadcast, in coma or masking paradigms, without consciousness.
- **Integrated Information (IIT)** — Reasons: consciousness is identical to a system's irreducible causal integration, quantified by Φ — Tononi's theory starts from phenomenology's axioms and derives the physical requirement, explaining why the cerebellum, neuron-rich, contributes so little; it is the only theory that begins where consciousness lives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a system with high Φ and, by every behavioural and structural indication, no consciousness at all.
- **Predictive Processing** — Reasons: conscious perception is the brain's generative model of the world, continuously corrected by sensory prediction errors — Clark's and Friston's framework unifies perception, action, and attention in one mechanism; the "controlled hallucination" is constrained enough to be perception, not fantasy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a conscious content with no place in the generative model — phenomenology the prediction machinery demonstrably did not produce.
- **Higher-Order Thought** — Reasons: a mental state is conscious when a meta-cognitive thought represents it — Rosenthal's theory explains consciousness's reflexivity and its absence in states we have but do not notice; it naturalizes consciousness with machinery — representation of representation — already needed elsewhere. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: consciousness demonstrated in a creature or state where higher-order representation was independently shown absent.

#### Causal Power of Qualia
- **Epiphenomenal Passenger** — Reasons: physics is causally closed — every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause — so felt qualia accompany neural firing without pushing atoms, however vivid they are; the passenger view pays for closure with intuition, and closure has the better evidence. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a physical effect — a neuron firing differently — traceable to the felt quality as such, with the neural story complete and insufficient. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)
- **Downward Mental Causation** — Reasons: evolution sculpted pleasure and agony with precision — it would not have bothered if feeling did no causal work, since inert accompaniments are invisible to selection; the burn's pain moving the hand is the datum, and closure is the theory that must accommodate it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a complete selection story in which qualia's correlation with adaptive behaviour needed no causal contribution from the feeling itself. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)

### 3.5 Existentialism & The Human Condition

#### Responses to the Absurd
- **Camusian Revolt** — Reasons: the absurd is the confrontation between our demand for meaning and the world's silence, and revolt — living defiantly, without appeal or illusion — is the only response that neither lies nor leaves; Sisyphus happy is not consolation but accuracy about the terms. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the demand for meaning itself dissolving under reflection — the confrontation ending because one of its parties was never real.
- **Kierkegaardian Leap** — Reasons: reason reaches the absurd and stops; the leap of faith transcends it by religious surrender, staking the self on what cannot be demonstrated — Kierkegaard's point is that the most important truths are held by commitment, not conclusion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the leap proving indistinguishable from self-deception — the surrender's object dissolving under the same scrutiny the leap exempted it from.
- **Sartrean Freedom** — Reasons: existence precedes essence — there is no given nature or cosmic script, so meaning is authored in choices for which we are wholly responsible; bad faith, the denial of that freedom, is the one sin in Sartre's vocabulary. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the authored self proving to be substantially written elsewhere — by class, trauma, and chemistry — beyond the margin freedom needs.
- **Nietzschean Overcoming** — Reasons: the old values died with their God, and mourning them is decadence — overcoming transvalues dogma and forges a self that affirms life entire, amor fati included; the Übermensch is a direction of self-overcoming, not a master race. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: self-overcoming collapsing into its caricature — strength worshipping itself, the transvaluation producing cruelty with a philosophy attached.
- **Stoic Acceptance** — Reasons: harmonizing with cosmic necessity — distinguishing what is up to us from what is not, Epictetus's discipline — converts the absurd from an affront into an order one can consent to; amor fati, in the Stoic line, is freedom found inside fate rather than against it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: acceptance functioning as anaesthesia — the harmonized sage consenting to injustices that revolt would have remedied.
- **Transhumanist Overcoming** — Reasons: the absurd's terms are biological — a short, frail, forgetting vehicle generates the silence it laments — so engineering the vehicle is the literal, non-metaphorical response; every other response manages a condition this one proposes to cure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: enhanced, extended life reproducing the same hunger at larger scale — the absurd scaling with the vehicle.
- **The Absurd is not real** — Reasons: the alleged clash needs both a hunger for cosmic meaning and a world obliged to answer it — dissolve either, as Nagel's irony or a Wittgensteinian therapy does, and there is no absurd, only a misdescription; a problem that vanishes under description was a problem of description. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the hunger proving ineradicable and the silence proving real — the clash reconstituting itself after every therapy.

#### Death & Annihilation
- **Epicurean Tranquility** — Reasons: where death is, I am not — nonexistence after death is the same state as the centuries before birth, which nobody mourns, so fearing it is an asymmetry of imagination, not of fact, as Lucretius's symmetry argument presses; tranquility is the rational dividend. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the deprivation account holding — death bad precisely because it robs a subject of goods, the symmetry with prenatal nonexistence broken by our future-directed cares. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)
- **Fierce Refusal & Grief** — Reasons: death destroys an irreplaceable centre of consciousness and love — the grief is proportionate to the loss, and medical defiance is sanity responding to an intrinsic evil; tranquility purchased by re-describing the loss is a bookkeeping trick the bereaved see through. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: refusal and grief demonstrably consuming the life they meant to defend — defiance becoming the larger loss. (This is an xy-switchbox pole: a crossed independent axis, not one end of a single dimension.)

#### Meaning of a life
- **Objective meaning** — Reasons: some lives are wasted and some fulfilled, and the judgment does not wait for the liver's opinion — meaning has facts: engagement with worth beyond the self, as Wolf's fitting-fulfilment view argues; a meaning wholly self-certifying could never be lost, yet lives plainly lose it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: two lives identical in every objective feature, one experienced as meaningful and one as empty, with the difference mattering — objectivity missing the phenomenon.
- **Created meaning** — Reasons: meaning is made — a life has the meaning its person gives it through chosen commitments, and the making is not a defect but the human condition Sartre described; created meaning explains why no committee can assign a life its meaning from outside. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: self-ascribed meaning collapsing under the maker's own later judgment — the creation proving answerable to standards it did not create.
- **No meaning** — Reasons: there is no meaning to find or to make, and the demand for one is the mistake — a category error inherited from religious grammar; lives have purposes, pleasures, and projects, none of which needs cosmic underwriting to be real. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the demand surviving every therapy — lives stunted specifically by meaning's absence, the lack functioning as a fact and not a confusion.

### 3.6 Political Philosophy & Justice

#### The Four Theories of Justice
- **Distributive** — Reasons: the basic structure's allocation of wealth, opportunity, and the social bases of self-respect is justice's first question, and Rawls's difference principle — inequalities only where they benefit the least advantaged — is its most defended answer; a society's justice is read off its worst-off lives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: distribution-first regimes producing, in practice, worse lives for the least advantaged than the arrangements they replaced.
- **Retributive** — Reasons: wrongs create a moral ledger that only proportional desert settles — retribution respects the wrongdoer as an agent answerable for choices, where rehabilitation alone treats them as a patient; victims, too, are owed the public statement that the wrong mattered. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: desert judgments proving, under scrutiny, to track luck and social position more than culpable choice.
- **Restorative** — Reasons: crime wounds relationships and communities, and rehabilitation, healing, and reconciliation repair what punishment merely records; restorative practice's recidivism and victim-satisfaction record in the programmes studied is the pole's empirical brief. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: restorative processes failing the victims of serious violence — reconciliation demanded where safety and desert were owed.
- **Procedural** — Reasons: justice is strict adherence to voluntary acquisition and transfer — Nozick's entitlement theory: a distribution is just if it arose justly, whatever pattern it shows; pattern-checking requires continuous interference with free exchanges, which is itself the injustice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: procedurally immaculate histories producing holdings — through monopoly, enclosure, inherited catastrophe — that the proceduralist's own conscience rejected.

#### Political legitimacy
- **Consent** — Reasons: authority over free and equal persons needs their agreement — Locke's insight survives its critics because no other ground respects the governed as agents rather than subjects; a state legitimate by consent alone can say whose it is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: consent proving unobtainable in any form — tacit, hypothetical, democratic — that was not a fiction covering obedience.
- **Outcomes** — Reasons: a state that governs well — security, prosperity, rights in practice — has the only legitimacy populations actually honour, and a consented-to tyranny of incompetence is legitimate in name only; performance is legitimacy's cash value across regimes and centuries. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a well-governing state whose subjects' judgment that it was not theirs proved, in crisis, to be the legitimacy that mattered.
- **Fair procedures** — Reasons: legitimate rules are rules fairly made — procedures that treat citizens as equals and give reasons all can inspect survive disagreement about outcomes, which no outcome-standard can; fairness of process is the one legitimacy a plural society can share. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: scrupulously fair procedures reliably producing monstrous outcomes their fairness could not launder.
- **No state is legitimate** — Reasons: no state clears the bar — consent was never given, outcomes excuse domination, procedures launder it — and philosophical anarchism's audit, from Wolff onward, has never been answered, only outwaited; authority of that kind is not justified, and honesty is to say so while obeying prudently. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: one state, one argument, meeting the bar — consent real, authority justified, the audit passed.


## 4. Archetype research

All 8 samples in this skin are kind "archetype." Four are named schools (Empiricism, Rationalism, Pragmatism, Phenomenology) and four are persona archetypes. Each entry names the archetype, its emblem, what it stands for, why it holds its positions (tied to §3), and what it is receptive and closed to.

**Empiricism** (`philo-empiricism`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-empiricism.svg) — Sense experience is the ultimate source of concepts and knowledge; there is no knowledge independent of experience. Its §3 home is Epistemic Grounds (Radical Empiricism), the Octad's Empiricism pole, and Causation read as Regular succession in Hume's line. Receptive to: scientific results of every kind — experience arriving by instrument is still experience. Closed to: a priori claims insulated from any possible observation.

**Rationalism** (`philo-rationalism`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-rationalism.svg) — Reason — intuition, deduction, and innate ideas — is a genuine source of knowledge independent of sense experience, and some truths are knowable a priori. Its §3 home is Epistemic Grounds (Pure Rationalism), the Octad's Rationalism pole, and Platonic Realism on universals. Receptive to: formal results — logic and mathematics are its home ground and its standing evidence. Closed to: the claim that necessity is only habit wearing a crown.

**Pragmatism** (`philo-pragmatism`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-pragmatism.svg) — Ideas are judged by their practical consequences: a belief is true insofar as it works, guides action successfully, and resolves doubt in inquiry; meaning is found in what a belief does. Its §3 home is Theories of Truth (Pragmatic), Epistemic Grounds (Pragmatism), and Relation to Science (Naturalized Continuity). Receptive to: any position, however metaphysical, that can state its practical difference. Closed to: disputes with no conceivable bearing on practice — it files them as verbal.

**Phenomenology** (`philo-phenomenology`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-phenomenology.svg) — The study of conscious experience as lived from the first-person point of view, setting aside assumptions about causes to describe how things appear. Its §3 home is Theories of Truth (Phenomenological), the Octad's Phenomenology pole, and the Consciousness Spectrum argued from lived experience's irreducibility. Receptive to: careful description from any school — description is its method, whoever performs it. Closed to: explanations that dissolve the phenomenon they were meant to explain.

**The Analytic Naturalist** (`philo-analytic-naturalist`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-analytic-naturalist.svg) — Philosophy as a rigorous partner to mathematics and empirical science, stripping away mystery in favour of clear arguments and physical continuity. Its §3 home is Scientific realism (Realist), Relation to Science (Naturalized Continuity), and the Mind-Body Ontology Matrix (Matter fundamental). Receptive to: any mystery that survives formalization — it will take the survivors seriously one at a time. Closed to: obscurity as depth; a claim that cannot be stated clearly is, for this archetype, not yet a claim.

**The Existential Humanist** (`philo-existential-humanist`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-existential-humanist.svg) — Begins from lived first-person agency, moral responsibility, and the courage to forge meaning without cosmic guarantees. Its §3 home is Responses to the Absurd (Sartrean Freedom and Camusian Revolt) and Meaning of a life (Created meaning). Receptive to: Stoic and even religious responses taken as lived commitments rather than doctrines. Closed to: any system — determinist, theocratic, or bureaucratic — that relieves the individual of authorship of their life.

**The Platonist Contemplative** (`philo-platonist-contemplative`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-platonist-contemplative.svg) — Sees reason and ethics as windows into an objective, timeless architecture of truth and value that dwarfs transient physical appearances. Its §3 home is The Ontology of Universals (Platonic Realism), Theories of Truth (Correspondence), and Meta-Ethics (Moral Realism). Receptive to: mathematics and contemplative practice, its two standing witnesses. Closed to: relativism in every form — a truth indexed to a culture is, for this archetype, a truth surrendered.

**The Skeptic & Anti-Realist** (`philo-skeptic-antirealist`, emblem: images/emblems/philo-skeptic-antirealist.svg) — Carries the Pyrrhonian and Humean tradition into contemporary anti-realism: suspends dogmatic assent to unobservables, moral facts, Platonic universals, cosmic meaning, and automatic progress. Its §3 home is Epistemic Grounds (Skepticism), Scientific realism (Instrumentalist), and Meaning of a life (No meaning) held as a live option. Receptive to: any argument that shows its evidence and names its limits. Closed to: certainty — the one position this archetype cannot occupy is a confident one.

## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype embodying them.** Several argued positions have no archetype: Infinitism and Coherentism on The Agrippan Trilemma (the Skeptic suspends rather than coheres); Deflationary and Semantic theories of truth; Growing Block on time; Pantheist and Deist on God; Ecocentric Holism on the moral circle; Longtermist Aggregation (the Existential Humanist is present-focused by design); Illusionism and Cosmopsychism on consciousness; Error Theory and Expressivism in meta-ethics (the Skeptic & Anti-Realist is adjacent but is an epistemic, not meta-ethical, identity); and No state is legitimate in political philosophy. Hermeneutics and Mysticism, two of the Octad's eight poles, have no archetype and no other topic in the skin develops them.

**Thin topics.** Six topics are xy-switchboxes (Meaning & Reference, Logic & Paradox, Relation to Science, Population & Future Ethics, Causal Power of Qualia, Death & Annihilation): each carries two axis poles whose four combinations are not separately argued positions — a design feature, noted here rather than treated as an error. Philosophy of Mind & Consciousness (3 topics), Existentialism & The Human Condition (3), and Political Philosophy & Justice (2) are the skin's thinnest categories; political philosophy in particular has no topic on liberty, equality as such, democracy, or punishment beyond the retributive pole of The Four Theories of Justice. The skin's 29 topics sit at the small end of the family range.

**Skin-JSON problems (reported, not fixed).** (1) Fifteen topics have an empty `question` field — listed in §5.1 with their ids; §2 includes the recovered question for each, taken verbatim from that topic's hovertip issue; the skin JSON itself is unchanged. (2) The Epistemological Octad's eight poles have empty hovertip pole texts (no definitions in the skin); §2 and §3 gloss them by the standard meanings of the schools named, and flag that here rather than silently. (3) The Epistemological Octad's question field merely repeats its name ("The Epistemological Octad"), so the topic carries no actual question text; it is covered as a radar of allegiances, as its widget implies. (4) One Octad pole is named "Dialectics" in the skin; it is quoted in §2 and §3 strictly as the skin's own pole label for a school of thought, and is not used anywhere in this document as a description of the Scribe, which is an indaba aimed at consensus. (5) Leading Neural Theory uses a widget named "puck," unlike any other topic's widget in the skin. (6) Every sample is kind "archetype" (8 of 8); there are no kind "sample" profiles.

**Deliberately left out of this research.** Numerical rating values for archetypes (the research backs positions in words; the JSON carries the numbers); the PhilPapers survey is used only where its headline figures could be stated with confidence (God and free will) — for other topics §3 says no reliable statistic was found rather than quoting survey percentages from memory; and any statistic not listed in §6.
