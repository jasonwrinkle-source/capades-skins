# Voca — Research

Skin: Vocapades (`vocapades.json`) · Domain: Work & Vocation — Career, Calling & the Meaning of Work · Meter: inclination
Coverage: 34 topics, 101 poles, 7 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Voca maps work and vocation: what work means to a person — purpose, ambition, security, and where they draw the line — across the whole shape of a working life, from calling and craft through structure, reward and boundaries to retirement. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's 34 questions: a pole a holder advocates for, against, or toward which they are averse (negative advocacy is aversion, not absence). Holding no stance is not a position; a 0 rating is. The meter word is **inclination**: the skin measures how strongly a profile inclines toward each pole (advocacy, per pole) and how open it is to each topic at all (openness, per topic). Receptivity and intensity are not rated directly — receptivity is derived as round-half-even(openness × advocacy) computed with exact fractions, and intensity is the signed largest-|value| pole, derived from the poles. The Scribe is an indaba: it uses this research to show each position with its backing, name where holders meet and part, and ask what would let the group reach consensus. The skin's stance, taken from its own `about`, is comparative and value-by-value: it places callings, climbers, security seekers, balance keepers, craftsmen, founders and free agents on one map "in the same shared vocabulary," with no working life treated as the default — a job, a career and a calling are three poles of its first topic, none of them the map's hero.

## 2. Topic rationale

### Purpose & Meaning

**Vocation** — The founding triad, from Wrzesniewski's typology: what category of thing work is. Calling makes work an expression of moral purpose; Career makes it a ladder of advancement, status and mastery; Job makes it a pragmatic transaction, with real life off the clock.

**Service** — Who the work is for, which quietly decides every later trade-off. Serve Others locates the point in helping people; Serve Yourself in self-fulfilment; Serve The Work in the work itself — the craft is the point.

**Passion vs. Market Demand** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Should career choices start from what fascinates you, or from what society demonstrably pays well to solve?” Where career choice should start. An xy-switchbox: Follow Intrinsic Curiosity and Solve Scarce Market Needs are crossed independent yes/no axes, not opposites — a holder can start from fascination and check the market, or neither; the status quo of drifting into whatever is hiring is both negative.

**Mentoring the next generation** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is mentoring a duty, a cost, or part of the mission itself?” What passing the work on is. Duty makes it owed and not optional; Cost counts the time the career needed; Part of the mission makes passing the work on the work itself.

### Effort & Ambition

**Effort** — The tempo of striving. Hustle / Grind pushes continuously; Bursts & Recovery sprints hard, then rests fully — effort in seasons; Balance / Rest paces for the long run.

**Drive** — Whether enough is enough. Contentment stops at enough; Balanced is content today, still reaching tomorrow; Ambition always wants more.

**Career Risk** — The appetite for variance in a livelihood. Safe / Stable chooses the steady job; Calculated takes risks with the odds counted first; Risky / Entrepreneurial bets on the venture.

**Breadth** — The shape of competence. Polymath / Generalist connects dots across fields; Unformed is the honest zero — no breadth, no depth yet, the map not drawn; Deep Specialist goes vertical in a single domain. Unformed is a real pole, not a missing rating: it names the beginner's position.

**Sacrifice for the mission** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “How much should you give up for the mission — everything, nothing, or a bounded share?” How much a mission may claim. Required holds that holding back is how missions fail; Wasteful counts sacrifice as spending the career, not a virtue; Bounded gives, and does not give everything.

### Place & Mode

**Workplace** — Where the work physically happens. Remote is anywhere, async by default; Hybrid splits the week; Office holds that showing up and the room itself matter.

**Working style** — The social grain of the work. Solo / Independent works alone; Mixed designs solo for depth and team for reach; Team / Collaborative works with others as the default.

**Hours** — When work happens. Fixed schedule is on the clock; Core Hours fixes a core with flexible edges; Flexible / Async runs on one's own rhythm.

**Hands vs. Screens** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “What balance of tangible physical making and symbolic screen work keeps you grounded?” The material of the work: tangible making against symbolic screen work. Physical & Trades Craft trusts the reality checks of matter; Abstract Knowledge Work trusts scale without physical friction.

**AI & Automation Posture** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “As AI absorbs cognitive and creative tasks, how aggressively should workers and firms hand work over?” How aggressively to hand work to AI as it absorbs cognitive and creative tasks. Human-Craft Protection preserves judgement and apprenticeship; Centaur Augmentation pairs human taste and accountability with AI speed; Full Workflow Automation hands over drudgery as fast as possible.

**Peers: rivals or allies** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Are your peers rivals, allies, or fellow travellers on the same mission?” What the people beside you are. Rivals compete for scarce places; Allies are collaborators to support; Fellow travellers share the mission — the relation is the work, not the contest or the club.

### Structure & Authority

**Structure** — How work should be organised. Hierarchical runs a clear chain of command; Hybrid keeps teams with a spine — hierarchy only where it earns its keep; Flat is egalitarian, no bosses.

**Freedom** — How much freedom in how one works. Autonomy decides for yourself; Guardrails is free inside agreed rails; Direction is being told what to do.

**Management Philosophy** — How a manager should manage. Command is top-down, the boss decides; Coach is supportive and developmental; Laissez-faire is hands-off, out of the way.

**Workplace Ownership** — Who should own the workplace, the diamond of four ownership forms. Corporate / Capital Hierarchy is investor-owned, executive command; Shared Ownership (ESOP) gives employees a real stake short of control; Worker Cooperative / Collective is employee ownership with democratic decisions; Founder / private ownership keeps the founded place private and founder-run. The four poles are independent ownership forms, not opposites.

**Work Models** — The shape a working life takes, as a radar of five independent forms. Full-time Employee is one employer, steady role; Freelancer / Independent has clients, not bosses; Founder / Entrepreneur builds it yourself; Part-time / Portfolio is a patchwork of roles; Public Service / Nonprofit puts mission over market. Each pole is a form a life can take — they are alternatives to weight, not ends of one scale.

**Bargaining Power** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Are workers better served by negotiating individually on performance, or standing together through collective bargaining?” Where a worker's leverage comes from. An xy-switchbox: Individual Merit Negotiation and Collective Union Solidarity are crossed independent yes/no axes, not opposites — a holder can negotiate individually and support a union floor; the status quo of accepting the offered terms is both negative.

**Credentials vs. Proof of Work** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “How should organizations filter and trust competence?” How organisations should filter and trust competence. An xy-switchbox: Formal Degrees & Licenses and Portfolios & Practical Tests are crossed independent yes/no axes, not opposites — a field can license and test.

### Reward & Security

**Compensation** — What matters in the pay itself. High pay is the number; Good perks / benefits is the package.

**Compensation Risk** — The shape of the reward's certainty. Security is stable and predictable; Balanced mixes certainty with upside; Upside is equity, bonus and growth.

**Recognition** — How much being seen matters. Quiet contribution lets the work speak; Selective wants to be seen by the people whose seeing matters; Public recognition needs the credit.

**Career Anchors** — Schein's anchors as a hexad: what a career is organised around, each an independent centre of gravity. Technical / Craft Mastery anchors in deep competence; Executive / Managerial Authority in steering organisations; Autonomy / Creative Freedom in self-direction; Security / Institutional Stability in a reliable foundation; Service / Dedication in a cause beyond the self; Pure Challenge in ever-harder problems. The poles are not opposites — a career can weight several.

**Frugality vs. Lifestyle Enjoyment** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Should high earners live lean to buy early freedom, or enjoy the fruits of their labor right now?” What a high income is for. An xy-switchbox: High-Savings Minimalism and Present-Tense Generosity are crossed independent yes/no axes, not opposites — a holder can save lean and give generously now.

**Pay Transparency & Spread** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Should organizations keep pay ratios tight and transparent, or reward outsized impact with outsized compensation?” How pay inside an organisation should be distributed and known. An xy-switchbox: Compressed Egalitarian Pay and Steep Performance Differentiation are crossed independent yes/no axes in the widget's form, though holders usually argue them as rivals — §3 argues each on its own terms.

**Tenure and mobility** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is tenure a trap, a security, or a responsibility to the institution?” What long tenure is. Trap holds that tenure locks you in and mobility is the power; Security holds that tenure is the stability that lets the work happen; Responsibility holds that tenure is a duty to the institution that granted it.

### Identity & Boundaries

**Work as Identity** — How much of the self the job is. Job is who I am makes it identity; Part of Me makes it part, not all; Job is what I do keeps it an activity, not a self.

**Boundaries** — How separate work and life are, and should be. Permeable lets work bleed into life; Flexible keeps a real wall with a door in it; Firm keeps clear separation.

**Retirement** — What the end of work is for. Retire stops when you can; Phased / Encore winds down gradually or starts a second act; Work for life never stops.

**Prestige of the field** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Does the prestige of your field matter, or is it a distraction from the work?” Whether the field's standing matters. Prestige matters holds that prestige opens doors; Distraction holds that prestige corrupts the work; Irrelevant holds that the field's standing is not the work.

**Family claims on the career** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Do family claims limit the career, come first, or belong inside the mission?” Where family sits relative to the career. Constraint counts family claims as limiting how far the career can go; Responsibility puts family first and fits the career around them; Part of the mission includes the family inside the mission itself.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 Purpose & Meaning

#### Vocation
- **Calling** — Reasons: work as an expression of moral purpose gives effort a meaning no wage purchases, and holders judge their work by mission impact over salary or title; the calling orientation is the one Wrzesniewski's research links to finding the work itself rewarding apart from advancement or pay. Statistic: in the original Jobs/Careers/Callings study, the three orientations appeared in roughly equal thirds across a mixed workforce sample (Wrzesniewski et al., 1997). What would move a holder: their mission proving, on inspection, to be the employer's slogan rather than their own purpose.
- **Career** — Reasons: a ladder of advancement, status and mastery gives a working life a legible plot — progress can be seen, planned and recognised; mastery compounds, and the career is the form that banks it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: reaching the ladder's top rungs and finding the view did not repay the climb.
- **Job** — Reasons: a pragmatic transaction — work funds the real life that happens off the clock, and honesty about the exchange protects both: the employer gets the hours, not the soul; the job pole is the only one that never asks work to be what it isn't. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: work that began asking — genuinely, not rhetorically — for the part of them the transaction had reserved.

#### Service
- **Serve Others** — Reasons: the work matters because it helps people — beneficiaries are the court that validates effort; service gives ambition a direction that self-interest cannot: outward. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their "service" proving to serve the organisation's image while the helped saw little change.
- **Serve Yourself** — Reasons: the work matters because it fulfils you — a life is the one non-renewable budget, and spending its best hours on self-fulfilment is not selfishness but stewardship; fulfilled workers, holders add, serve others better anyway. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: fulfilment proving hollow without a beneficiary — the work turning to ashes when only they applauded it.
- **Serve The Work** — Reasons: the work matters in itself; the craft is the point — a bridge, a proof, a loaf has standards internal to it that neither customers nor careers set; serving the work is the oldest of the three loyalties and the least corruptible. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the work's internal standards proving to be a guild's self-protection dressed as quality.

#### Passion vs. Market Demand
- **Follow Intrinsic Curiosity** — Reasons: sustained excellence requires genuine fascination that survives inevitable setbacks — passion is not the reward of a career but its fuel through the years when rewards are absent; careers chosen on demand alone die at the first downturn of either. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their fascination failing to feed them for a decade while a marketable skill sat unlearned beside it.
- **Solve Scarce Market Needs** — Reasons: mastering rare, valuable skills creates autonomy and financial freedom that eventually fund any passion — demand is information about what the world actually needs, and the market pays for scarcity, not for sincerity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the scarce skill's premium collapsing — automation or fashion — leaving neither passion nor premium.

#### Mentoring the next generation
- **Duty** — Reasons: you owe the next generation — every holder was mentored, formally or by example, and the debt is paid forward or not at all; mentoring is not optional because the chain has no other links. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their mentoring proving to transmit the field's pathologies as faithfully as its skills.
- **Cost** — Reasons: mentoring spends time the career needed — hours are the career's capital, and seniors who give them away wholesale finish with proteges and no work of their own; the cost is real even when worth paying. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: accounting that showed their mentoring returned more — in loyalty, learning, legacy — than the hours cost.
- **Part of the mission** — Reasons: passing the work on is the work, not a side task — a craft that dies with its holder failed at its final test; the mission was never only the artefacts but the continuation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where transmission is genuinely automated — documentation and tools carrying what only persons once could.

### 3.2 Effort & Ambition

#### Effort
- **Hustle / Grind** — Reasons: push, grind — the compounding of effort is the one advantage fully in the worker's control, and most fields' rewards are tournaments where the extra hour is the entry fee; holders distrust the balance talk of people whose position is already secure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their own output data showing the grind hours producing nothing but errors and rework.
- **Bursts & Recovery** — Reasons: sprint hard, then rest fully — effort in seasons matches how demanding work is actually structured (launches, harvests, deadlines) and how bodies and minds actually recover; the season, not the day, is the unit of sustainable intensity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the recovery phases quietly disappearing under deadline pressure, leaving bursts all the way down.
- **Balance / Rest** — Reasons: pace yourself, rest — a working life is forty years long and the sprinter's strategy spends the asset the marathon needs; holders note that only about a fifth of the world's employees are engaged at work, and read chronic overdrive as one of the causes. Statistic: about 23% of employees worldwide were engaged at work (Gallup, 2024). What would move a holder: a season of genuine ambition arriving — a chance that pacing would plainly forfeit.

#### Drive
- **Contentment** — Reasons: enough is enough — the person who cannot say "enough" has outsourced their satisfaction to an endless variable; contentment is not the absence of drive but its completion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their contentment proving to be fear in disguise — a test they declined, not a sufficiency they chose.
- **Balanced** — Reasons: content today, still reaching tomorrow — gratitude and ambition are not opposites, and each corrects the other's pathology: ambition without contentment is misery, contentment without ambition is drift. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the balance proving unstable — one side always eating the other in practice.
- **Ambition** — Reasons: always more — the reaching is itself the good, and every plateau is a local maximum mistaken for the summit; holders point to how much of what the content enjoy was built by the ambitious. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: achieving the long-sought goal and feeling the reaching immediately re-target, unchanged — evidence the appetite was the point and the cost.

#### Career Risk
- **Safe / Stable** — Reasons: a steady job is the foundation every other good in a life is financed on — rent, family, health; variance in income is not excitement to the person whose dependents eat the downside. Statistic: median employee tenure in the US was 4.1 years in January 2024 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2024). What would move a holder: the "safe" employer collapsing or automating the role — safety proving to have been concentration risk all along.
- **Calculated** — Reasons: risks taken with the odds counted first — the pole is not timidity but method: size the bet, keep a reserve, and let expected value, not temperament, decide. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their calculations proving to be rationalisations — the odds never actually counted, only narrated.
- **Risky / Entrepreneurial** — Reasons: the bet is the only route to an outcome a salary cannot reach — equity, authorship, scale; salaried safety has a ceiling the bet does not, and youth's risk capacity is a wasting asset. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a failed bet whose costs fell on people who never consented to it — family, employees — changing the moral arithmetic.

#### Breadth
- **Polymath / Generalist** — Reasons: wide breadth connects dots across fields — the valuable problems live at boundaries, and specialists must hire translators the generalist doesn't need; breadth is also insurance: no single field's collapse ends the generalist. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: repeatedly losing the room to specialists whose depth their breadth could not audit.
- **Unformed** — Reasons: no breadth, no depth yet — the map is not drawn; the pole's honesty is its content: naming the beginner's state without pretending a direction, which is the precondition for choosing one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: time passing — the state is defined by its impermanence, and staying in it becomes a choice needing its own defence.
- **Deep Specialist** — Reasons: vertical mastery, single-domain depth — the world's hardest problems yield only to decades in one seam, and depth is the scarcest, least automatable form of expertise; the specialist's knowledge cannot be summarised, which is its protection. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their domain being absorbed by a neighbouring field or a model, leaving depth in a seam that no longer exists.

#### Sacrifice for the mission
- **Required** — Reasons: the mission demands the sacrifice — holding back is how it fails; every cause that ever succeeded was carried by people who gave what comfort would have kept, and the mission does not grade on a curve. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the mission consuming the sacrifice and failing anyway — the gift spent by incompetence rather than necessity.
- **Wasteful** — Reasons: sacrifice spends the career — it is not a virtue; organisations that run on sacrifice are running on an unpriced input, and the worker who burns out serves the mission for two years instead of twenty. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a moment where only sacrifice — theirs, specifically — stood between the mission and its end.
- **Bounded** — Reasons: give, and do not give everything — a limit is part of doing it well; bounded sacrifice is sustainable across a whole career, which is the timescale missions actually run on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the bound proving to be set by comfort rather than by sustainability — a limit that always coincides with convenience.

### 3.3 Place & Mode

#### Workplace
- **Remote** — Reasons: work from anywhere, async by default — the office was a technology for an era of paper and supervision, and knowledge work kept the habit after the reason left; remote returns commute hours to life and widens the hiring map to the world. Statistic: about 28% of paid full days worked by US employees were worked from home in early 2024 (WFH Research, 2024). What would move a holder: their remote seasons showing measurably weaker work and lonelier weeks than their office seasons.
- **Hybrid** — Reasons: some days in, some days out — the office for what it's good at (collision, apprenticeship, trust) and home for what it's good at (depth, quiet, life); the split is a design, not a compromise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the hybrid schedule inheriting both settings' costs — commute and isolation — and neither's benefit.
- **Office** — Reasons: show up, be present, the room matters — apprenticeship, trust and serendipity are physical phenomena, and junior workers pay the remote era's hidden tuition; presence is also a signal: the work is worth travelling to. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their team's remote period producing equal work and visibly better lives, sustained over years.

#### Working style
- **Solo / Independent** — Reasons: deep work is solitary — interruption is the tax collaboration levies on thought, and the solo worker's output is theirs to stand behind entire; independence is also a temperament, and fighting it wastes the worker. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a project whose scale plainly exceeded any solo capacity, where teaming multiplied rather than taxed them.
- **Mixed** — Reasons: solo for depth, team for reach — both, by design; the pole is the working week's honest architecture: different tasks genuinely want different social grains. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the mixing proving to be indecision — contexts switching so often that neither depth nor reach materialised.
- **Team / Collaborative** — Reasons: the work that matters most is too large and too various for one head — collaboration is not a tax on output but the form of the hardest output; holders add that teams catch the errors solo confidence misses. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: team process repeatedly dissolving their best work into committee average.

#### Hours
- **Fixed schedule** — Reasons: on the clock — fixed hours make work predictable for colleagues, customers and family, and the boundary they draw protects the evening better than any personal resolve; the schedule is a coordination technology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their best work demonstrably arriving off-clock, with the fixed hours merely warehousing them.
- **Core Hours** — Reasons: a fixed core with flexible edges — the core hours carry the coordination, the edges carry life's errands and the individual's rhythm; it is the schedule form of the hybrid principle. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: core hours creeping until the flexible edges vanish in practice.
- **Flexible / Async** — Reasons: async, your own rhythm — knowledge work's output is not hours-shaped, and people peak at different times; judging by deliverables instead of presence is the adult arrangement. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: async freedom decaying into always-on — flexibility becoming the absence of any off-switch.

#### Hands vs. Screens
- **Physical & Trades Craft** — Reasons: repairing, building and working with physical matter yields immediate, undeniable reality checks — the pipe is fixed or it leaks, and no narrative changes it; holders add that the trades' work cannot be offshored to a server or summarised by a model as readily. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the body's bill arriving — injury and wear pricing them out of the craft they love.
- **Abstract Knowledge Work** — Reasons: software, analysis and writing scale ideas across millions of minds without physical friction — one artefact, infinite copies; the leverage is historically unprecedented and the location freedom follows from it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a career of artefacts that vanished with their platforms, leaving nothing they could point to and say "I made that."

#### AI & Automation Posture
- **Human-Craft Protection** — Reasons: preserving human judgement and apprenticeship keeps skills alive and protects dignity in work — a pipeline that automates the junior tasks automates away the making of seniors; some goods (judgement, care, taste) are the human performance, not its inefficiency. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: protected crafts becoming protected rents — guilds sheltering mediocrity while clients pay.
- **Centaur Augmentation** — Reasons: pairing human taste and accountability with AI speed lets small teams do astonishing work — the centaur keeps a named human answerable for the output, which pure automation abandons and pure handwork cannot scale. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: centaur workflows proving to be a transition state — the human's role shrinking to rubber-stamping on its way out.
- **Full Workflow Automation** — Reasons: automating away drudgery as fast as possible frees human beings for higher-order problems and leisure — every prior automation wave's "irreplaceable" task became a chore we pity the past for doing by hand. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: automation consuming the interesting work too, leaving humans the residue of monitoring and exception-handling.

#### Peers: rivals or allies
- **Rivals** — Reasons: peers compete for scarce places — promotions, grants, clients are finite, and treating rivals as allies is how the naive subsidise the ambitious; clear-eyed rivalry is also respect: it takes peers' ambitions seriously. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rivalrous culture visibly destroying the information-sharing their own work depended on.
- **Allies** — Reasons: peers are the people you collaborate with and support — the network is the career's real asset, and allies compound: they teach, refer, rescue and co-found; most individual wins are downstream of an alliance. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an ally exploiting shared knowledge in a zero-sum contest they had pretended was collaborative.
- **Fellow travellers** — Reasons: peers are on the same mission — the relation is the work, not the contest or the club; fellow-travelling survives changing employers because it attaches to the cause, not the org chart. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the "mission" proving to be a management slogan, with the travellers dispersed the moment budgets tightened.

### 3.4 Structure & Authority

#### Structure
- **Hierarchical** — Reasons: a clear chain of command makes decisions fast, accountability findable and coordination cheap at scale; when the building is on fire nobody votes — hierarchy is the structure of responsibility under time pressure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their hierarchy's information problem — bad news never reaching the top — costing more than flatness ever did.
- **Hybrid** — Reasons: teams with a spine — hierarchy only where it earns its keep; most decisions are local and reversible, a few are global and final, and one structure for both is the error this pole refuses. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the hybrid's ambiguity — nobody sure which decisions are whose — generating the conflicts both pure forms avoid.
- **Flat** — Reasons: egalitarian, no bosses — hierarchy's information and dignity costs are structural, not incidental, and adults who can do the work can share the deciding; flat structures keep the mission's ownership where the work is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flat organisation developing an informal, unaccountable hierarchy worse than the formal kind — the tyranny of structurelessness arriving on schedule.

#### Freedom
- **Autonomy** — Reasons: decide for yourself — professionals do their best work under self-direction, and autonomy is both a motivator and a filter: it attracts exactly the people who can be trusted with it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their autonomous choices repeatedly colliding with colleagues' — freedom's coordination bill coming due.
- **Guardrails** — Reasons: free inside agreed rails — the rails carry the non-negotiables (safety, law, the mission's edges) and everything inside them is genuinely free; it is autonomy with the cliffs fenced, not the field. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the rails multiplying until the field inside them is a corridor.
- **Direction** — Reasons: be told what to do — clear direction frees the worker from decision fatigue and political exposure, and in coordinated work (kitchens, wards, sites) it is how the whole functions; wanting direction is a preference, not a failure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: direction proving stupid in a case where their own judgement was right and suppressed.

#### Management Philosophy
- **Command** — Reasons: top-down, clear orders, the boss decides — command is honest about where accountability sits, and in crises, safety-critical work and turnarounds it is the philosophy that functions; ambiguity about who decides is crueller than orders. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a commanded team that stopped reporting problems upward, making the clarity fictional.
- **Coach** — Reasons: supportive, developmental, grows the team — the manager's product is not decisions but people who decide better each year; coaching compounds, command merely spends. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a situation — fraud, danger, collapse — where coaching's patience became complicity.
- **Laissez-faire** — Reasons: hands-off, get out of the way — expert adults are slowed, not helped, by management attention; the manager's best work is hiring well and then absenting. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hands-off team drifting into duplicated work and quiet despair that one intervention would have fixed.

#### Workplace Ownership
- **Corporate / Capital Hierarchy** — Reasons: investor-owned, executive command — outside capital at scale is what lets workplaces grow past what workers or founders can fund, and the hierarchy it brings is the price of the capital's risk; the form built most of the modern economy's capacity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their own workplace's owners extracting until the work itself — the thing the capital was for — was hollowed out.
- **Shared Ownership (ESOP)** — Reasons: employees hold a real stake, short of full cooperative control — ownership aligns the daily worker with the firm's fate without handing operations to a parliament; the stake is retirement wealth built from one's own labour. Statistic: about 14 million US employees participate in employee stock ownership plans (National Center for Employee Ownership, 2024). What would move a holder: ESOP stakes proving illusory — valuations manipulated, the "stake" untradeable and voiceless.
- **Worker Cooperative / Collective** — Reasons: employee ownership, democratic decisions — the people who do the work should govern it, and surplus should follow labour, not capital; cooperatives answer the workplace's legitimacy question the way democracy answers the state's. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a cooperative's decision speed and capital starvation costing its members the livelihoods the form was meant to protect.
- **Founder / private ownership** — Reasons: you own the place: you founded it, you run it, and it stays private — founder ownership keeps the mission undiluted by quarterly markets and committee governance; the long view is a private owner's privilege. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a founder (or being the founder) whose unchecked control became the workplace's ceiling and its risk.

#### Work Models
- **Full-time Employee** — Reasons: one employer, steady role — the form bundles income, benefits, colleagues and identity into one reliable package, and the bundling is the point: cognitive and financial overhead near zero, all attention free for the work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the single employer becoming a single point of failure — one reorganisation erasing the whole bundle at once.
- **Freelancer / Independent** — Reasons: clients, not bosses — the freelancer sells outcomes to many buyers, so no single buyer's politics is their fate; the form prices skill at market, continuously, which employees never learn about themselves. Statistic: about 64 million Americans performed freelance work in 2023 (Upwork, 2023). What would move a holder: the feast-famine cycle and self-funded benefits costing more, over a decade, than the freedom paid.
- **Founder / Entrepreneur** — Reasons: build it yourself — the founder trades salary for authorship and equity: the venture is the work and the asset at once; no other form lets a person set the mission rather than rent one. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a venture consuming a decade and returning less — money, craft, life — than the employment it replaced.
- **Part-time / Portfolio** — Reasons: multiple roles, a patchwork — the portfolio diversifies identity and income the way an investor diversifies assets: no single role's loss is total, and the variety is itself a good. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the patchwork's coordination costs — every role's overhead, no role's depth — exceeding the diversification's value.
- **Public Service / Nonprofit** — Reasons: mission over market — the public servant's and nonprofit worker's wage discount buys work whose beneficiary is the public itself; some goods only exist because people accept that trade knowingly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the mission organisation wasting its people's sacrifice on bureaucracy — the discount funding process, not purpose.

#### Bargaining Power
- **Individual Merit Negotiation** — Reasons: top performers deserve direct rewards without being held back by rigid seniority grids — merit negotiation prices the individual's actual contribution, and the best workers subsidise the average under collective scales. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: discovering their "merit" pay tracked their manager's favour and their own demographic luck more than their output.
- **Collective Union Solidarity** — Reasons: alone, an individual worker rarely has equal leverage against a large employer; solidarity secures fair floors — the floor is the achievement: minimums beneath which no one's desperation can be priced. Statistic: union membership was 9.9% of US wage and salary workers in 2024 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2025). What would move a holder: their union protecting the indefensible — a member's misconduct or a rule that plainly harms the work — at the cost of its legitimacy.

#### Credentials vs. Proof of Work
- **Formal Degrees & Licenses** — Reasons: structured training and licensing protect the public and prove sustained discipline — where incompetence kills (medicine, structures, aircraft), the credential is a floor the public can verify without expertise of its own. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: licensed fields demonstrably failing at competence while unlicensed portfolios outperformed them.
- **Portfolios & Practical Tests** — Reasons: gatekeeping by pedigree wastes brilliant self-taught talent; show what you can actually build — the portfolio is evidence, the degree is a proxy, and hiring on evidence widens the gate to everyone the proxy excluded. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: portfolio hiring proving gameable — polished artefacts masking hollow skill — at rates pedigree never reached.

### 3.5 Reward & Security

#### Compensation
- **High pay** — Reasons: the number — cash is fungible, honest and portable: it becomes savings, freedom or generosity at the holder's choice, while perks are the employer's choices spent on the employee; the number is also the market's clearest verdict on the work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a package whose benefits (health, pension, time) proved worth more than a higher number they had chased instead.
- **Good perks / benefits** — Reasons: the package, benefits — health cover, pension, leave and security are worth more bought collectively than retail, and they protect exactly the life-events cash salaries arrive too late for; the package is compensation shaped like a life. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: perks proving to be golden handcuffs — value that exists only while employed, vanishing at the exit when it is needed most.

#### Compensation Risk
- **Security** — Reasons: stable, predictable — security is what lets a life be planned: mortgages, children, care; the predictable wage is not a failure of ambition but the foundation risk elsewhere is taken on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: inflation or a stagnant scale quietly converting their "security" into a slow pay cut.
- **Balanced** — Reasons: some certainty, some upside — the balanced package hedges the two failure modes: pure security's stagnation and pure upside's destitution; it is the portfolio principle applied to a wage. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the balanced package proving to deliver neither — security too thin to plan on, upside too thin to matter.
- **Upside** — Reasons: equity, bonus, growth — upside is the only compensation that can change a life's category rather than its comfort; holders accept variance as the price of a non-linear outcome. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the upside repeatedly expiring worthless while the forgone salary compounded in a peer's pension.

#### Recognition
- **Quiet contribution** — Reasons: the work speaks — recognition-seeking corrupts judgement toward the visible over the valuable; the quiet contributor's reputation among the few who actually know is worth more than applause. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: watching credit for their quiet work repeatedly harvested by louder colleagues, with consequences for pay and promotion.
- **Selective** — Reasons: seen by the people whose seeing matters — recognition is information, and like information it should be routed, not broadcast; the master recognised by masters has the real currency. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the people whose seeing matters proving to be a closed circle that recognition from outside would have broken open.
- **Public recognition** — Reasons: need the credit — public recognition is portable capital: it becomes the next job, the book, the invitation; in a world that cannot inspect everyone's work, visible credit is how merit travels. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: public credit arriving detached from the work's quality — fame rewarding performance of work over work.

#### Career Anchors
- **Technical / Craft Mastery** — Reasons: deep competence in the work itself is the anchor that cannot be taken by reorganisation — organisations rename, crafts endure; mastery also carries its own reward, the felt rightness of difficult things done well. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their craft being automated at the level of mastery itself, not merely at its routine edge.
- **Executive / Managerial Authority** — Reasons: steering organisations, accountability, power — the executive anchor values leverage: one good decision at scale outweighs a thousand at the bench; authority is how a person's judgement becomes many people's work. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: discovering their authority was ceremonial — real decisions made elsewhere, accountability alone remaining.
- **Autonomy / Creative Freedom** — Reasons: self-direction, working on your own terms — the autonomy anchor treats freedom as the compensation that outranks compensation; every other good can be bought, but a self-directed day cannot. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: autonomy proving to be isolation — the absence of colleagues, structure and shared purpose costing more than direction ever did.
- **Security / Institutional Stability** — Reasons: predictability, safety, a reliable foundation — the security anchor is the institutional form of the safe-job pole: a great institution outlasts markets and moods, and a life can be built against it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the institution's stability proving to be stagnation — the foundation becoming the ceiling.
- **Service / Dedication** — Reasons: the work serves others; dedication to a cause beyond the self — Schein's service anchor names the pull the calling archetype lives by: the cause is the compensation, and titles are weather. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the cause organisation exploiting dedication as a wage discount, converting devotion into a business model.
- **Pure Challenge** — Reasons: the work is hard, and that is the point — ever-harder problems to overcome; the challenge anchor is bored by mastery achieved and security alike: difficulty is the nutrient. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: challenge-seeking proving to be novelty-seeking — abandoning problems at the exact point they became merely difficult rather than new.

#### Frugality vs. Lifestyle Enjoyment
- **High-Savings Minimalism** — Reasons: every dollar saved early buys future autonomy and shrinks the hours you owe any boss — savings are stored freedom, and compounding makes early leanness the highest-paid work a high earner does. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: reaching the freedom number and finding the lean years had cost experiences that no later money could repurchase.
- **Present-Tense Generosity** — Reasons: tomorrow is never guaranteed; sharing good meals, travel and comfort now honours the work you did today — generosity is also a use of money that compounds socially: the table gathers the people the lean years would have postponed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a financial shock revealing that present-tense spending had left no reserve — generosity to the present billed to a desperate future.

#### Pay Transparency & Spread
- **Compressed Egalitarian Pay** — Reasons: narrow pay spreads build genuine camaraderie and recognise that every role keeps the ship afloat — compressed pay is also information hygiene: it removes the suspicion economy that wide, secret spreads create. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: compression driving away the scarce specialists the organisation genuinely could not replace, to the whole crew's cost.
- **Steep Performance Differentiation** — Reasons: when one breakthrough contributor creates ten times the value, compensation should reflect that reality — flat pay for unequal contribution is its own injustice, and it taxes exactly the people the organisation can least afford to lose. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the "ten times" measurements proving to be politics in numerical dress — differentiation tracking visibility, not value.

#### Tenure and mobility
- **Trap** — Reasons: tenure locks you in; mobility is the power — the mobile worker's outside option disciplines every employer, and skills priced by the market stay honest; staying is how wages and curiosity both stagnate. Statistic: median employee tenure in the US was 4.1 years in January 2024 (US Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2024). What would move a holder: mobility's costs — lost depth, lost relationships, perpetual probation — exceeding the raises it bought.
- **Security** — Reasons: tenure is the stability that lets the work happen — long projects and deep institutional knowledge require people who will be there; the secure worker can take the long view the perpetual candidate cannot. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their institution using tenure's comfort to freeze them out of every interesting problem.
- **Responsibility** — Reasons: tenure is a duty to the institution that granted it — the tenured owe the place their protection of its mission, including against its own management; security received becomes stewardship owed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the institution abandoning its mission while demanding the tenured defend the husk.

### 3.6 Identity & Boundaries

#### Work as Identity
- **Job is who I am** — Reasons: identity — the work is the answer to "who are you?" because it is where the holder's best hours and deepest competencies live; a self that excludes its main activity is the fiction. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: losing the job — by layoff or retirement — and discovering the identity had no second leg.
- **Part of Me** — Reasons: work is part of who I am — not all of it; the part-whole honesty of this pole protects both: the work gets a real share of the self, and the self survives the work's changes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the "part" proving unstable — work expanding to fill every vacancy the other parts left.
- **Job is what I do** — Reasons: just something you do — identity lives in family, place, faith, craft of living; the job is an instrument, and mistaking the instrument for the self is the error the other poles risk. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: realising the "instrument" had quietly shaped their days, friends and thoughts for thirty years — identity by accumulation, unchosen.

#### Boundaries
- **Permeable** — Reasons: work bleeds into life — and for work that is a calling or a craft, the bleeding is not a leak but the medium: ideas arrive in the shower, and the holder would not seal them out if they could. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: permeability proving one-directional — work entering life freely, life never entering work.
- **Flexible** — Reasons: a real wall, with a door in it — the boundary exists and is theirs to open; flexible boundaries fit a life whose seasons differ, unlike both the seal and the flood. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the door proving to open only under others' pressure — flexibility as availability in disguise.
- **Firm** — Reasons: clear separation — the firm boundary is what makes rest rest and family family; it is also a worker's right that no employer's enthusiasm should be able to purchase informally. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: firmness costing them the one opportunity that required a season of bleed — and judging the price wrongly paid.

#### Retirement
- **Retire** — Reasons: stop when you can — work was the means, and a good means knows its end; the retiree's decades are the dividend the whole structure existed to pay. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: retirement arriving and revealing that work had been carrying their structure, friendships and purpose unnoticed.
- **Phased / Encore** — Reasons: wind down gradually — or start a second act; the phased form keeps income, identity and usefulness while shedding the grind, and the encore lets a second vocation grow in the first's shade. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: phasing proving to be the worst of both — full availability, partial pay, no clean break.
- **Work for life** — Reasons: never stop — for the called and the craftsmen, stopping is not a reward but an amputation; the work is the life, continued at a chosen pace, and "retirement" names a transaction person's milestone, not theirs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the body or the market ending the work unilaterally — and the absence of any prepared second self.

#### Prestige of the field
- **Prestige matters** — Reasons: prestige opens doors — funding, students, collaborators and the benefit of every doubt follow standing; ignoring it is how you stay stuck, and the stuck call their stuckness purity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: prestige proving to open doors into rooms where the real work was not happening.
- **Distraction** — Reasons: prestige corrupts the work — chasing it is how the mission dies: questions chosen for applause, results trimmed for headlines; the distraction is not innocent because it spends the scarcest resource, attention. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: their unglamorous field losing funding and people for want of exactly the standing they disdained to cultivate.
- **Irrelevant** — Reasons: the standing of the field is not the work — it does not matter; the proof is true, the bridge stands, the patient heals, at every level of the field's fashion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: discovering their indifference was affordable only because their field's prestige was high — irrelevance as a luxury of the already-honoured.

#### Family claims on the career
- **Constraint** — Reasons: family claims limit how far the career can go — stated plainly, without resentment: hours and geography are finite, and the career's ceiling is set partly at home; naming the constraint honestly beats pretending the trade-off away. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the "constraint" proving to have been the career's actual foundation — the stability that made its risks takeable.
- **Responsibility** — Reasons: family claims come first; the career fits around them — the ordering is the position: dependents did not choose the career, and its ambitions are the adjustable variable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: family-first ordering being used — by an employer or by habit — to cap a career the family itself wanted uncapped.
- **Part of the mission** — Reasons: the mission includes the family; they are not an outside limit — for the called especially, the household is the first congregation of the work, and a mission that exiles the family contradicts itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: the mission's inclusion of family becoming the mission's claim on family — the household conscripted rather than embraced.

## 4. Archetype research

All 7 samples in the skin have kind "archetype". Archetype ids covered in this section, in skin order: voc-calling, voc-climber, voc-security, voc-balance, voc-craftsman, voc-founder, voc-free-agent. Ratings in the skin rate advocacy per pole and openness per topic; the receptivity and intensity discussed below are the derived quantities (see §1), described in words, not re-rated here.

### Calling / Service-to-a-Cause Orientation — `voc-calling.svg`
Work as vocation: an intrinsically meaningful calling inseparable from personal purpose and contribution to the common good, judged by mission impact over salary or title — anchored in Wrzesniewski's Jobs/Careers/Callings typology, Bellah's *Habits of the Heart*, and Schein's Service/Dedication anchor. Why: it holds the §3 Calling, Serve Others, Part of the mission and Service / Dedication reasons as one orientation (§3, Vocation; Service; Mentoring the next generation; Career Anchors). Receptive to: craftsman mastery in service of the cause, and bounded-sacrifice arguments that protect the mission's longevity (§3, Sacrifice for the mission). Closed to: Job-as-transaction framing and steep pay differentiation, because both deny the premise that the work's worth is its beneficiary (§3, Vocation; Pay Transparency & Spread). Emblem: images/emblems/voc-calling.svg

### The Ambitious Climber — `voc-climber.svg`
The riser: driven by promotions, status, recognition and ever-greater responsibility, measuring success by how far and how fast one advances. Why: it holds the §3 Career, Ambition, Public recognition and Executive / Managerial Authority reasons (§3, Vocation; Drive; Recognition; Career Anchors). Receptive to: calculated risk and individual merit negotiation, the ladder's own instruments (§3, Career Risk; Bargaining Power). Closed to: contentment and quiet contribution, which read to the Climber as surrender dressed as wisdom (§3, Drive; Recognition). Emblem: images/emblems/voc-climber.svg

### The Security Seeker — `voc-security.svg`
Stability above all: steady income, job security, benefits and predictability; career choices favour safe, reliable paths over risky upside. Why: it holds the §3 Safe / Stable, Security (compensation risk and tenure) and Good perks / benefits reasons (§3, Career Risk; Compensation Risk; Tenure and mobility; Compensation). Receptive to: hierarchy and direction, which make the world legible and the future plannable (§3, Structure; Freedom). Closed to: entrepreneurial risk and upside-only compensation, because variance is the enemy the whole orientation exists to defeat (§3, Career Risk; Compensation Risk). Emblem: images/emblems/voc-security.svg

### The Balance Keeper — `voc-balance.svg`
Work as one part of a good life, not the whole of it: career choices protect time and energy for family, health, rest and outside interests. Why: it holds the §3 Balance / Rest, Firm boundaries, Job is what I do and Retire reasons (§3, Effort; Boundaries; Work as Identity; Retirement). Receptive to: flexible hours and hybrid workplaces, the instruments of protection (§3, Hours; Workplace). Closed to: hustle and required sacrifice, because both spend the life the balance exists to keep (§3, Effort; Sacrifice for the mission). Emblem: images/emblems/voc-balance.svg

### Master Craftsman / Technical Competence Anchor — `voc-craftsman.svg`
Dignity and satisfaction in deep technical mastery — doing the work well for its own sake — resisting promotions into pure management that pull away from the craft: Sennett's *The Craftsman*, Schein's Technical/Functional Competence anchor, Newport's craft-before-passion rule. Why: it holds the §3 Serve The Work, Deep Specialist, Technical / Craft Mastery and Human-Craft Protection reasons (§3, Service; Breadth; Career Anchors; AI & Automation Posture). Receptive to: portfolios and practical tests, which honour demonstrated craft, and to mentoring as the craft's continuation (§3, Credentials vs. Proof of Work; Mentoring the next generation). Closed to: managerial-authority anchors and passion-first career advice alike — the first leaves the bench, the second skips the mastery (§3, Career Anchors; Passion vs. Market Demand). Emblem: images/emblems/voc-craftsman.svg

### Entrepreneurial Founder & Autonomous Builder — `voc-founder.svg`
Rejects institutional permission and salaried ceilings for equity ownership, creative autonomy and building new ventures that scale with judgement and leverage rather than hours billed: Schumpeter's entrepreneur, Schein's Entrepreneurial Creativity and Autonomy anchors, Ravikant's *Almanack* as modern handbook. Why: it holds the §3 Risky / Entrepreneurial, Founder / private ownership, Upside and Autonomy reasons (§3, Career Risk; Workplace Ownership; Compensation Risk; Freedom). Receptive to: full workflow automation as leverage, and to market-demand-first career logic (§3, AI & Automation Posture; Passion vs. Market Demand). Closed to: tenure, security anchors and collective bargaining grids, because each trades away the ownership and variance the Founder exists to hold (§3, Tenure and mobility; Bargaining Power). Emblem: images/emblems/voc-founder.svg

### The Free-Agent Portfolio & Turnaround Operator — `voc-free-agent.svg`
Works a fractional, contract or interim-executive portfolio — stepping into organisations to solve high-stakes bottlenecks with decisive command authority and hybrid structure, then moving cleanly to the next mission; treats long single-employer tenure as a trap, refuses rigid career-track boxes, negotiates individually on proof of work, sprints intensely during engagements, and protects open recovery time between contracts. Why: it holds the §3 Trap (tenure), Freelancer / Independent, Part-time / Portfolio, Bursts & Recovery and Individual Merit Negotiation reasons as one operating system (§3, Tenure and mobility; Work Models; Effort; Bargaining Power). Receptive to: hybrid structures and command-in-crisis management philosophies, its working tools (§3, Structure; Management Philosophy). Closed to: security and institutional-stability anchors, because the portfolio's whole edge is refusing the single institution (§3, Career Anchors). Emblem: images/emblems/voc-free-agent.svg

## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype.** The map's poles are wider than its 7 archetypes. No archetype primarily embodies: the union solidarist (Collective Union Solidarity has no holder whose identity it is — the Free Agent is its opposite number); the worker-cooperative collectivist; the FIRE minimalist living lean for early freedom; the prestige-seeker as such (the Climber wants advancement, not field-standing); or the public servant whose identity is institutional service (the Calling archetype approaches it only through cause, not institution).

**Thin topics.** No topic is single-pole: every topic carries 2–6 poles. Breadth's middle pole, Unformed, is a single-position pole in spirit — it names the absence of a position, so §3 gives it a thin but honest entry (its receptivity story is impermanence). Five topics are xy-switchboxes (Passion vs. Market Demand, Bargaining Power, Credentials vs. Proof of Work, Frugality vs. Lifestyle Enjoyment, Pay Transparency & Spread), whose poles are crossed independent yes/no axes, not opposites; §3 argues each on its own terms. The radar topics (Work Models, five poles; Career Anchors, six poles; Workplace Ownership, four poles) carry independent forms rather than opposites, and §3 treats each form separately.

**Skin-JSON problems (reported, not fixed).** (1) The skin file itself is named `vocapades.json`, not `vocacapades.json` — the filename drops the second "a" that the `<prefix>capades.json` pattern (and the skin's own subdomain logic) would predict; the skin's internal `skin` field is likewise "vocapades". This research document is named `voca-research.md` for the skin's working prefix, and the discrepancy is reported here, not repaired. (2) Thirteen topics have an empty `question` field: Passion vs. Market Demand (xvo0022), Frugality vs. Lifestyle Enjoyment (xvo0023), Bargaining Power (xvo0024), Pay Transparency & Spread (xvo0025), Credentials vs. Proof of Work (xvo0026), Hands vs. Screens (xvo0027), AI & Automation Posture (xvo0028), Tenure and mobility (xvo0029), Sacrifice for the mission (xvo0030), Mentoring the next generation (xvo0031), Prestige of the field (xvo0032), Peers: rivals or allies (xvo0033), Family claims on the career (xvo0034). In each case the hovertip's `issue` states the question in full: §2 quotes it at the topic as the recovered question, and §5.1 collects all thirteen verbatim. (3) Three archetypes rate fewer topics than the skin's 34: voc-climber (count 31), voc-security (count 32) and voc-balance (count 32) — their unrated topics are absences in the JSON, reported here, not repaired.

**Deliberately left out of this research.** Occupation-specific guidance (the skin maps orientations, not professions); financial-planning detail behind the frugality and retirement poles beyond the poles' own terms; and any statistic not listed in §6 — where §3 could not ground a number in a source the author could name with confidence, it says so rather than estimating.
