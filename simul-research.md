# Simul — Research

Skin: Simulcapades (`simulcapades.json`) · Domain: Computational Physics — the Computer as a Virtual Laboratory · Meter: stance
Coverage: 80 topics, 237 poles, 16 archetypes (all counts taken from the skin JSON).

## 1. Domain framing

Simulcapades maps computational physics: what a simulation is, what it can show, where it misleads, and when its output is real enough to act on. The domain is the practice of building copies of the world — electronic-structure and molecular dynamics, continuum solvers, Monte Carlo, plasma kinetics, Earth-system forecasting — together with the philosophy that practice forces: the epistemic status of a run, the honesty of its error bars, and the metaphysical edge where practitioners ask whether the world itself is a computation.

The meter word is **stance**. What is measured is the position a practitioner takes on each topic — not skill, not output, but where they stand on what a simulation is and what it may be trusted to do. A position here is a rated stance on one of the skin's questions: advocacy for a pole of that question. What does not count as a position is a preference with no epistemic content (a favourite editor, a favourite colour scheme for plots) or a bare fact (a machine's peak FLOPS); the skin rates commitments about method, trust and ontology, not tastes in tooling decoration.

**Rating contract.** The inputs a profile supplies are **advocacy per pole** (a signed number, −100 to +100, for each pole of each topic; positive is for the pole, negative is against it) and **openness per topic** (0–100, how willing the profile is to hear other positions on that topic). Everything else is derived, never rated directly: **receptivity** is `round-half-even(openness × advocacy)`, computed with exact fractions rather than floating-point arithmetic, and **intensity** is the signed largest-|value| pole — the strongest pole, carrying its sign. Aversion therefore lives only in a rating's sign; it is never encoded anywhere else, and no sign is ever flipped or folded. On single-pole (monopole) topics the one number is an affinity, and a negative value reads as aversion to that pole. On xy-switchbox topics the two poles are crossed, independent yes/no axes — a holder may affirm either, both, or neither — not opposite ends of one scale.

The skin's stance is that simulation is a genuine way of knowing with its own characteristic failure modes, so neither blanket trust nor blanket suspicion is a position the map rewards: every pole, including the sceptical and the dismissive ones, is stated in its holders' strongest terms. The Scribe for this skin is an **indaba** — a meeting of the recorded stances that works toward consensus by naming where positions meet, where they part, and what would move each holder — and the research below is written to be lifted into that meeting: reasons and numbers per position, and, for each archetype, what it can hear and what it cannot.

## 2. Topic rationale

### The Epistemology of Simulation

**The Three Pillars: What Is a Simulation?** — The founding question of the field — what kind of knowledge a run produces — on which every later trust judgement depends. Virtual Experiment stands for the position that a run has a setup, an intervention and measured output, like a bench experiment. Extended Theory / Numerical Calculation stands for the position that a simulation only evaluates consequences already contained in its equations and inputs. Distinct Third Pillar of Science stands for the position that simulation has its own methods, errors and institutions, irreducible to either theory or experiment.

**Purpose** — Whether a run is a searchlight or a check — the purpose decides what counts as a good result. Discovery: Simulation as a tool for finding new physics — exploring parameter space, finding emergent phenomena, predicting what no one has seen. Verification: Simulation as a tool for checking what you already believe — confirming analytic predictions, testing a theory against its consequences.

**Computational experiment** — How literally to take the word 'experiment', which governs what evidential weight a run may claim. Real: A simulation IS an experiment — it has a setup, a run, data, and error bars, and the results count as evidence in the same way lab data does. Quasi-experiment: Not a real experiment, but more than a metaphor: it interrogates a model of the world. Metaphor: Calling it an "experiment" is just a metaphor — it is a calculation, and its results are only as good as the equations you fed in.

**Tradeoff** — The field's permanent budget fight — accuracy against throughput — stated as a value, not a compromise. Maximum Physical Fidelity: Double/quad precision, all electron shells, full quantum electrodynamics, zero empirical fudge factors; willing to wait 6 months on a supercomputer. Fidelity where it matters: Full fidelity at the sensitive points; speed everywhere else. High-Throughput Speed & Scalability: Coarse-grained approximations, GPU parallelism, real-time interactive parameter exploration; "good enough" physics at 100x the speed.

**Validation** — What 'validated' cashes out to first — contact with the laboratory or contact with the mathematics. Empirical Validation First: A simulation is meaningless unless its output curves match physical laboratory measurements within experimental error bars. Internal Code Verification First: The supreme test is mathematical: proving grid convergence, asymptotic order of accuracy, and exact conservation of energy, momentum, and symplectic structure.

**Simulation Trust** — The default prior a practitioner carries into every new result, before any specific check is run. Bold Simulation Confidence: Trust the computational output as ground truth; modern simulations are often cleaner and less prone to systematic errors than real-world noise. Calibrated trust: Trust proportioned to the validation behind the result. Permanent Self-Skepticism: Never trust a simulation fully; discretization errors, floating-point roundoff, and unmodeled boundary artifacts are always lurking.

**Are we living in a computer simulation?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Are we inside someone else's simulation, is the universe itself a computation with no programmer, is this base reality, or is the hypothesis operationally unfalsifiable and irrelevant to scientific computing?” The metaphysical edge of the map — whether this world is itself a run — included because it shapes what some practitioners think a simulation is. Ancestor simulation (made by others): Posthuman civilizations run ancestor simulations, and we are likely in one. It from bit (computation, no simulator): The universe is a computation. There is no external programmer. Base reality: This is the physical world. The simulation hypothesis is not the likely truth. Agnostic / Metaphysically Irrelevant: Whether reality is a simulation is operationally unfalsifiable; scientific computing cares about predictive models, not metaphysics.

**Is the universe a computation?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is the universe at bottom a computation made of information, or is physical stuff the real thing?” The ontological version of the same question, separated from the simulation hypothesis because one can hold it with no simulator at all. The universe is a computation: Information is fundamental. The computation is the universe. A useful description, not the stuff: Computation describes the world. It is not what the world is made of. Matter and fields are fundamental: Physical stuff is fundamental. Computation is a tool we bring to it.

**Can a simulated mind be conscious?** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “If a mind runs on a computer, is it really conscious, or only a clever imitation?” Whether a simulated mind would be someone — the premise on which the ethics topic and the simulation argument both turn. Yes, computation suffices: A mind that runs as a computation is a real mind. Maybe, unknown: Whether computation is enough for consciousness is not settled. No, minds need more than computation: Minds need biology, or physics that computation does not capture.

**Looking for glitches** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Should physicists look for pixels, glitches, or rounding errors in nature?” Whether the simulation hypothesis can be made empirical by hunting for the seams of the supposed computation. Hunt for artifacts: Look on purpose for the signatures a computation would leave. Only as a byproduct of normal physics: Ordinary experiments may bound the idea. Do not build a hunt around it. Untestable metaphysics: There is nothing here a telescope can decide.

**Ethics of simulated minds** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “If we build a mind inside a simulation, do we owe it anything?” What we would owe minds we ourselves create in silico — the applied edge of the consciousness question. The regard owed to a mind: A simulated mind is a moral patient. We owe it what we owe any mind. Caution, because we might: We may owe it something. Do not create one casually. No duty owed: A simulation is a model. It is not someone we owe.

**Determinism underneath** — Recovered question (skin's `question` field is empty; taken verbatim from the topic's hovertip issue): “Is nature at bottom a fixed rule, with quantum randomness only how it looks, or is randomness real?” What kind of rule, if any, runs underneath quantum randomness — a premise silently imported into many simulation designs. Fully deterministic underneath: A fixed rule runs nature. Randomness is only how it looks from inside. Deterministic underneath, random on top: The evolution is fixed, and the outcomes we meet are random. Random at bottom: Randomness is real. It is not a surface on a hidden rule.

**Prediction Fidelity vs. Conceptual Understanding** — Hamming's dictum as a design choice — insight against prediction — which decides what a model is for. Minimal Models for Insight stands for the position that a model you cannot understand cannot teach you anything, however accurate its numbers. Hierarchical Model Ladder stands for the position that insight and prediction are rungs, not rivals: simple models explain, complex ones predict, and each checks the other. High-Fidelity Quantitative Prediction stands for the position that decisions — engineering, climate, stewardship — need numbers with error bars, not parables.

**Subgrid Tuning & Parameterization Honesty** — The honesty question of the field: whether tuned subgrid parameters are calibration or concealment. Reject Ad-Hoc Tuning—Resolve Physics stands for the position that a tuned parameter that absorbs missing physics hides the model's real error and breaks outside its calibration range. Transparent Documented Calibration stands for the position that some processes cannot yet be resolved at scale, and calibration against data is normal science — provided every tuned value, target and sensitivity is published. Pragmatic Tuning for System Skill stands for the position that a model is judged by the skill of the whole system, and tuning is how complex systems are made skilful.

**Trusting Simulations in Unobserved Regimes** — How far past the data a simulation may be trusted — the question stockpile stewardship and century-scale climate force. Never Trust Beyond Validated Data stands for the position that models are interpolators with good public relations. Physics-Bounded Extrapolation with UQ stands for the position that conservation laws and dimensional constraints do not stop at the edge of the dataset. First-Principles Laws Guarantee Reach stands for the position that the same equations govern the unobserved regime — that is what makes them laws.

**Physics-Based "Digital Twins": Genuine Paradigm or Buzzword Hype?** — Whether the digital twin is a new cyber-physical thing or a new name for an old practice. Rebranded Old Simulation Buzzword stands for the position that engineering simulation with a dashboard is still engineering simulation. Valuable When Closed-Loop & UQ-Calibrated stands for the position that a twin earns its name only when live data continuously update it and its uncertainty is quantified. Transformative Live Bidirectional Cyber-Physical Paradigm stands for the position that when the model steers the asset and the asset retrains the model in real time, the pair is a new kind of object — neither experiment nor simulation — with its own epistemology.

### Methods

**Computational Methods** — The practitioner's primary representation of matter — five worldviews that between them cover the field's working methods. Electronic Structure & Ab Initio Quantum stands for the position that electrons decide chemistry, so start where the deciding happens. Classical & Molecular Dynamics (MD) stands for the position that atoms in motion explain most of matter's behaviour at usable scales. Mesh & Continuum Solvers stands for the position that most engineering and geophysics lives at continuum scale, where PDEs on meshes are the mature, verified instrument. Statistical & Stochastic Monte Carlo stands for the position that when the state space defeats enumeration, sampling is not a fallback but the correct mathematics. Discrete-Event & Agent-Based stands for the position that many systems are made of agents and events, not fields.

**Forces** — Where forces come from — computed from quantum mechanics or fitted to the world — the accuracy/scale trade in its purest form. Ab Initio Quantum Purity (DFT): Calculate electronic forces directly from Schrödinger/Kohn-Sham equations; zero fitted parameters; limited to thousands of atoms. Bottom-Up Systematic Coarse-Graining stands for the position that coarse-graining done systematically — force-matching, relative entropy — keeps a chain of custody from quantum mechanics to large scale. Empirical Classical Potentials: Embedded Atom Method, Tersoff, ReaxFF; fitted to bulk properties; capable of simulating billions of atoms.

**Simulators** — Who wins the strongly correlated quantum problem — classical ingenuity or quantum hardware. Classical Supercomputing Algorithms: Tensor networks, DMRG, Quantum Monte Carlo on classical exascale clusters. Hybrid credence: Classical and quantum simulators, each where it is strong. Quantum Hardware / Analog Simulators: Mapping the Hamiltonian directly onto neutral atom arrays, superconducting qubits, or Rydberg quantum simulators.

**Continuity** — Whether the world's grain is smooth or granular — a metaphysical choice with immediate numerical consequences. Continuum: Fields, PDEs, smooth functions — the world is continuous and the math is calculus. Continuum from discrete: A discrete substrate that behaves as a continuum at scale. Discrete: Particles, lattices, graphs — the world is granular and the math is counting.

**Predictability** — Whether randomness belongs in the physics or only in our ignorance — it decides the mathematics of the whole run. Deterministic: Same input, same output, every time — the physics is a clockwork and the simulation should be too. Laws with noise: Deterministic structure carrying stochastic detail. Stochastic: Randomness is in the physics — Monte Carlo, thermal fluctuations, quantum measurement — and the simulation must sample, not solve.

**Turbulence & Nonlinear Cascade Strategy** — How to cross the Reynolds-number wall — resolve, filter or average the turbulence. Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) stands for the position that every closure is a confession. Large-Eddy Simulation (LES) stands for the position that the large eddies carry the geometry-specific physics and the small ones are near-universal, so resolving the former and modelling the latter is the rational division of labour. Reynolds-Averaged Closures (RANS) stands for the position that industry runs on RANS because design cycles need answers overnight, and decades of calibration make its failures known and bounded.

**Rare Events & Conformational Sampling** — How to reach millisecond events from femtosecond steps — brute force, bias, or state models. Brute-Force Long Unbiased Trajectories stands for the position that only unbiased dynamics gives kinetics you did not put in by hand. Enhanced Biasing (Metadynamics / Umbrella) stands for the position that biasing along chosen coordinates converts impossible waits into routine calculations, and the bias can be removed exactly in analysis. Path Sampling & Markov State Models stands for the position that the object of interest is the ensemble of pathways or the network of states, not one heroic trajectory.

**Quantum Many-Body Simulation Frontier** — The frontier contest for quantum many-body physics, where the Hilbert space defeats storage itself. Quantum Monte Carlo & Lattice QFT stands for the position that QMC's successes — from lattice QCD's hadron spectrum onward — are the field's proven route where the sign problem permits. Tensor Networks (DMRG / PEPS) stands for the position that entanglement in real systems is structured, and tensor networks exploit exactly that structure. Analog & Digital Quantum Simulators stands for the position that building the system to study the system — Feynman's proposal — bypasses representation altogether.

**Single Hero Run vs. Large Statistical Ensembles** — The allocation dilemma for chaotic systems — one perfect run or a distribution of good ones. One Ultra-High-Resolution Hero Run stands for the position that some phenomena exist only above a resolution threshold. Resolution-Ensemble Compromise stands for the position that chaos makes single trajectories anecdotes and resolution makes ensembles unaffordable, so the compromise — moderate resolution, real ensembles — is not timidity but arithmetic. Massive Lower-Res Probabilistic Ensembles stands for the position that for chaotic systems the distribution is the answer.

**Hydrodynamics Paradigm: Eulerian Grid vs. Lagrangian SPH vs. Moving Mesh** — The hydrodynamics schism — grids, particles or moving meshes — where Galilean invariance and shocks punish every choice somewhere. Eulerian Fixed/AMR Grids stands for the position that grid methods have the sharpest shocks, the best conservation bookkeeping and the deepest verification literature. Lagrangian Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics stands for the position that SPH follows the mass, handles free surfaces and extreme deformation without mesh tangling, and conserves by construction. ALE Moving Voronoi Mesh stands for the position that a mesh that moves with the flow takes the Lagrangian advantage while keeping grid-like accuracy — AREPO's galaxy-formation results showed suppressed mixing artefacts that changed conclusions.

**Kinetic Plasma Simulation: Particle-in-Cell (PIC) vs. Continuum Vlasov** — How to do 6D plasma kinetics — noisy particles or a grid that fights the curse of dimensionality. Macroparticle PIC & Hybrid PIC stands for the position that PIC is the workhorse because it is robust, parallel and physically transparent. Sparse-Grid & Low-Rank Vlasov stands for the position that phase space is high-dimensional but its solution is structured. Noise-Free Continuum Vlasov–Fokker–Planck stands for the position that when the quantity of interest is a small tail or a weak instability, PIC noise is not a nuisance but a wall.

**Atomistic Evolution Beyond Nanoseconds: Kinetic Monte Carlo vs. Accelerated MD** — How atomistic simulation escapes the nanosecond — accelerate the dynamics or catalogue the events. Accelerated / Temperature-Driven MD stands for the position that boosting the dynamics keeps the true mechanism — the system still finds its own pathways, only faster. On-the-Fly Adaptive KMC stands for the position that discovering events as they become relevant combines KMC's reach with MD's openness. Cataloged Lattice Kinetic Monte Carlo stands for the position that for lattice systems with known event catalogues, KMC reaches seconds and years with exact stochastic kinetics.

**High-Dimensional Sampling: Hamiltonian MCMC vs. Sequential & Nested Sampling** — Which sampling family to reach for first when the posterior is high-dimensional and multimodal. Gradient-Based Hamiltonian MC (HMC/NUTS) stands for the position that gradients turn random walks into directed motion, and HMC's scaling with dimension is the best proven. Tempering & Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) stands for the position that tempering walks through flattened landscapes that trap single-chain methods, and SMC's population carries the whole distribution, not one path through it. Nested Sampling for Multimodal Evidence stands for the position that when the question is the evidence itself — model comparison — nested sampling computes it as a by-product while exploring multimodal spaces other methods miss.

**Radiation & Neutrino Transport: Monte Carlo vs. Deterministic Moment Closures** — How to move radiation and neutrinos through a supernova — sample the transport or close its moments. Stochastic Monte Carlo Transport stands for the position that Monte Carlo treats the full angular and energy dependence with no closure assumption. Hybrid Implicit MC / Discrete Ordinates stands for the position that hybrids exist because pure methods fail somewhere in every real hohlraum or supernova. Deterministic Moment Closures (M1 / FLD) stands for the position that moments are fast, smooth and coupled naturally to hydrodynamics, which is what a running multiphysics code needs.

**Free-Energy Calculation Strategy in Molecular Simulation** — How to get a free energy to chemical accuracy — perturb it, profile it, or pull it. Alchemical Perturbation & Thermodynamic Integration stands for the position that alchemical methods answer the exact question drug design asks — the difference between two molecules — with a mature error literature. Collective-Variable PMF & Metadynamics stands for the position that a potential of mean force shows the whole landscape, not one difference, and metadynamics' history-dependent bias has mapped mechanisms from nucleation to folding. Non-Equilibrium Jarzynski / Crooks Pulling stands for the position that the fluctuation theorems are exact results connecting pulling work to equilibrium free energies — one of the few places statistical mechanics gives something for nothing.

**Data Assimilation Paradigm: Variational 4D-Var vs. Ensemble Kalman Filters** — How live observations should enter a running model — through its adjoint, its ensemble, or both. Adjoint Variational 4D-Var stands for the position that 4D-Var's operational record in weather forecasting is the strongest evidence any assimilation method has. Hybrid Ensemble-Variational (4DEnVar) stands for the position that hybrids take flow-dependent covariances from the ensemble and variational consistency from 4D-Var. Ensemble Kalman & Particle Filters (EnKF) stands for the position that ensemble filters need no adjoint, handle nonlinearity naturally and carry their uncertainty with them.

**Forward Exploration vs. Adjoint Inverse Design** — What a flagship simulator is for — looking, sampling around, or designing backwards from a goal. Forward High-Fidelity Physical Exploration: Build the highest-fidelity forward virtual laboratory to discover mechanisms and nonlinear phenomena. Outer-Loop Ensemble & Derivative-Free UQ: Treat the simulator as a vetted black- or gray-box engine wrapped in ensemble sampling, sensitivity analysis, and robust UQ. End-to-End Adjoint & Differentiable Inverse Design: Embed adjoints and automatic differentiation into the solver so gradients flow straight from objectives to physical parameters.

**Reduced-Order Modeling (ROM): Linear POD/Galerkin vs. Nonlinear Neural Manifolds** — How to compress a solver into a trustworthy surrogate — project it, infer its operators, or learn its manifold. Intrusive Projection POD & Reduced Basis stands for the position that projection keeps the equations inside the surrogate, so conservation and stability can be proved rather than hoped. Operator Inference & Physics-Preserving ROM stands for the position that learning operators from data while preserving their structure gives surrogates that respect the physics' shape without opening proprietary or legacy codes. Nonlinear Autoencoder Latent Dynamics stands for the position that strongly nonlinear problems live on curved manifolds that linear subspaces cannot follow.

### Scale, Discretization & Time

**Multi-scale bridging** — Concurrent coupling against hierarchical hand-off — the organisational form of multiscale physics. Direct Concurrent Multi-Scale: Simultaneously embedding quantum QM/MM zones inside classical MD, coupled directly to continuum finite element boundaries in a single code run. Hierarchical Parameter Hand-Off: Run quantum simulations offline to compute material constants, pass them up to molecular dynamics, extract viscosity/elasticity, and feed them into a separate macro CFD code. Autonomous Single-Scale Effective Models stands for the position that a well-built effective model at one scale is a finished piece of science, not a fragment.

**Integration** — What to protect in time integration — the geometry of the flow or the local error at each step. Symplectic Integrators (Geometric Mechanics): Verlet, leapfrog, Ruth algorithms; exact conservation of phase space volume and bounded Hamiltonian energy over billions of orbits. High-Order Adaptive Steppers: Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg, Bulirsch-Stoer; adaptive time steps that minimize local truncation error at every moment.

**Spatial discretization** — How space itself is cut — the discretisation that every other choice inherits. Structured Uniform Grids & Spectral Methods: Cartesian grids, Fast Fourier Transforms, uniform spacing; maximum computational efficiency and spectral accuracy. Unstructured Adaptive Meshes (AMR): Tetrahedral/hexahedral meshes, octrees, zooming in on shock fronts and boundary layers where physics happens. Mesh-Free Particle Methods: Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH), vortex methods; no mesh connectivity, immune to mesh tangling during explosive deformation. Conservative Finite Volumes (FV) stands for the position that finite volumes conserve mass, momentum and energy to machine precision by construction, which is why CFD trusts them. Finite Elements & Discontinuous Galerkin stands for the position that finite elements handle real geometry and offer a complete error theory, and DG adds high order with local conservation.

**Scope** — Resolution against domain — whether the physics lives in the fine structure or in the whole system. Resolution: Fine grids, small timesteps, resolve every detail — the physics is in the fine structure. Resolve the region that matters: High resolution in the region of interest; a coarse domain around it. Domain Size: Big boxes, long times, the whole system — the physics is in the large-scale behavior.

**Convergence** — What 'converged' must mean before a number may be published. Strict: Prove convergence — grid refinement studies, Richardson extrapolation, error bounds; no result counts until the convergence is demonstrated. Converged enough, checked: Pragmatic convergence, with the check documented. Pragmatic: If it looks converged, it is converged — run it twice at different resolutions, check the answer doesn't move much, and get on with the physics.

**Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR) vs. Uniform Grids** — Refinement as a philosophy — cleanliness against reach across decades of dynamic range. Clean Uniform Structured Grids stands for the position that uniform grids have no coarse–fine interfaces to reflect waves or invent structures, and their performance is perfectly predictable. Static Nested / Unstructured Meshes stands for the position that where the interesting geometry is known in advance, a fixed well-crafted mesh gives AMR's focus without its moving machinery and load-balancing pathologies. Dynamic Adaptive Mesh Refinement stands for the position that Berger–Oliger refinement follows the physics as it moves — collapse, shocks, ignition — across ranges no static mesh can span.

**Explicit Scalability vs. Implicit Stiff Solvers** — How stiffness is paid for — in step size, in splitting error, or in linear algebra. Explicit Local Time-Stepping stands for the position that explicit methods are simple, perfectly parallel and free of solver pathologies. IMEX Operator Splitting stands for the position that most stiffness is partial — one stiff term among explicit-friendly physics — and IMEX treats each part with its proper method. Fully Implicit Newton–Krylov stands for the position that implicit methods take the steps the physics wants, not the steps stability permits, and Newton–Krylov made them scalable.

**Floating-Point Precision Discipline** — How much arithmetic precision physics may spend — the tensor-core bargain. Strict FP64 (or Higher) Everywhere stands for the position that round-off is the one error source that touches every operation, and chaotic systems amplify it without limit. Mixed Precision with FP64 Accumulation stands for the position that doing the bulk work in low precision and the reductions in FP64 captures most of the speed and nearly all of the safety. Embrace Low-Precision Tensor Cores stands for the position that the hardware has voted: tensor cores offer an order of magnitude that physics cannot refuse.

**Direct Sparse Factorizations vs. Iterative Krylov Solvers** — The linear-algebra temperament — pay memory for certainty, or iterate and precondition. Bulletproof Direct Sparse LU/Cholesky stands for the position that a direct factorisation answers every right-hand side, never fails to converge, and its cost is known in advance. Preconditioned Hybrid Solvers stands for the position that real codes mix — direct on subdomains, iterative globally — because physics is hybrid. Matrix-Free Iterative Krylov Methods stands for the position that matrix-free Krylov scales to problems whose matrices cannot even be stored, and its memory leanness is what exascale rewards.

**Geometric Multigrid vs. Algebraic Multigrid (AMG)** — Multigrid's two cultures — build the hierarchy from the geometry or extract it from the matrix. Hand-Crafted Geometric Multigrid (GMG) stands for the position that a hierarchy built from the problem's own geometry coarsens what should be coarsened. Semi-Algebraic Hybrid Coarsening stands for the position that semi-coarsening and hybrid strategies take geometric guidance where it exists and algebraic robustness where it does not. Black-Box Algebraic Multigrid (AMG) stands for the position that AMG asks nothing of the user but the matrix and delivers near-optimal solves on unstructured problems no one could coarsen by hand.

**Fast Multipole & Hierarchical H-Matrices vs. FFT / Particle-Mesh** — The fast long-range contest — transforms, trees, or multipole expansions. FFT & Particle-Mesh Ewald (PME) stands for the position that for periodic systems PME's accuracy-per-cost is unmatched and its implementation is a solved problem. Tree-Code & Hybrid Particle-Mesh stands for the position that trees handle open boundaries and clustered distributions that defeat uniform meshes, and hybrids take PME's smoothness with the tree's geometry. Fast Multipole (FMM) & H-Matrices stands for the position that FMM's O(N) with controlled accuracy is the mathematically complete answer to long-range interaction, and H-matrices extend the idea to operators.

**Randomized Numerical Linear Algebra vs. Classical Deterministic Decompositions** — Whether randomness may enter the factorizations themselves. Classical Deterministic SVD / QR Only stands for the position that a factorisation is a foundation. Randomized Sketch + Deterministic Check stands for the position that sketch to find the structure, then verify deterministically — the combination takes the speed and keeps the certificate. Randomized Linear Algebra First stands for the position that for data-scale problems the classical decompositions are simply unaffordable, and randomized methods' guarantees are theorems, not hopes.

**Synchronous Parallelism vs. Communication-Avoiding & Asynchronous Solvers** — The exascale execution argument — bulk synchrony against task-based asynchrony. Clean Synchronous Bulk-Parallel MPI stands for the position that bulk-synchronous programs can be reasoned about, reproduced and debugged. Overlap Compute & Non-Blocking Comms stands for the position that hiding communication behind computation takes most of the synchrony cost without abandoning the model's clarity. Task-Based & Asynchronous Execution stands for the position that at exascale, global barriers and load imbalance are the binding constraints, and task graphs with communication-avoiding algorithms are the demonstrated escape.

### The Machine & The Code

**Hardware** — Where a practitioner wants the machine to live — national facility or own desk — recorded as two crossed affinities, not a single scale (see §5). Leadership scale: Affinity for national leadership-class supercomputers. Hardware you control: Affinity for hardware you control yourself.

**Processor** — Which silicon the physics is shaped for. Massive CPU Cores: Huge RAM per core, deep cache hierarchies, complex branch prediction, pure OpenMP/MPI parallelism. Dense GPU / Tensor Accelerators: Thousands of CUDA/HIP cores, massive memory bandwidth, SIMD lockstep parallelism, porting physics to GPU kernels. Quantum Processors: The computation itself is quantum; the hardware is the physics, not just a faster way to do classical arithmetic.

**Language** — The language question as an institutional one — inherit the monolith or build on the modern stack. Battle-Tested Fortran/C Codebases: Millions of lines written over 40 years, hyper-optimized BLAS/LAPACK, reliable, immovable institutional code. Modern Composable High-Level Stacks: Julia, Python/JAX, differentiable physics engines, auto-differentiation, high-level expressive abstractions. Julia Unified Language stands for the position that the two-language problem — prototype slow, production fast — is a tax on every computational scientist. Rust & Modern Systems Safety stands for the position that memory bugs in scientific codes are physics bugs with better camouflage.

**Solvers** — Whether learned operators replace the solvers of numerical analysis or join them. AI & Neural Operators (PINNs/FNO): Train neural networks on physics loss functions; instantaneous surrogate inference, mesh-free, solves inverse problems effortlessly. Hybrid solvers: Neural operators where validated; traditional solvers as the backbone. Traditional Numerical Analysis (FEM/FVM): Strictly mathematically verified error bounds, exact conservation laws, no hallucinated physics, guaranteed stability.

**MLIPs** — Whether machine-learned potentials may be trusted where no training data has been — the discovery question. MLIP Enthusiast: Neural network potentials like MACE, NequIP, and ANI achieve near-DFT quantum accuracy at classical molecular dynamics speed; the holy grail is here. Cautious adopter: Machine-learned potentials used where they have earned trust. MLIP Skeptic: Machine learning potentials fail unpredictably outside their training manifold; dangerous and untrustworthy for unexpected phase transitions or extreme pressures.

**Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs): Promise or Hype?** — The PINN verdict — a method judged against the classical solvers it claims to displace. Overhyped vs. Classical FEM/FV stands for the position that systematic benchmarks show PINNs losing to FEM/FV on forward problems in accuracy and cost, often by orders of magnitude. Strong for Inverse & Noisy Data Assimilation stands for the position that where data are sparse, noisy and the parameters unknown, a mesh-free method that fuses data and physics in one loss is genuinely the right tool. Transformative for High-Dimensional PDEs stands for the position that meshes die exponentially with dimension and neural representations do not.

**Differentiable Physics & Automatic Differentiation** — A single-affinity topic: how strongly end-to-end differentiability should drive simulator design (negative ratings read as aversion — see §5). Priority of End-to-End Differentiable Simulators stands for the position that gradients through whole trajectories turn tuning, inference and design from search into calculus.

**Hard Symmetry Constraints vs. Soft Scale-Driven Learning in SciML** — Whether physical law should be built into learned models or left for scale to find. Hard Equivariance & Exact Conservation stands for the position that a model that cannot violate conservation or symmetry cannot produce whole classes of nonsense. Pragmatic Inductive Biases stands for the position that some structure is worth building in and some is cheaper to learn. Bitter-Lesson Scale Learns Symmetries Softly stands for the position that Sutton's lesson is that general methods plus compute beat hand-built structure in the long run, in field after field.

**ML Surrogate Out-of-Distribution Guardrails** — The production guardrail question — what a code must do when its learned surrogate leaves home. Active Learning & On-the-Fly First-Principles Retraining: Detect high-uncertainty states dynamically, query an exact first-principles oracle, and retrain the surrogate on the fly. Automatic Fallback to Exact Physics Solver: Treat ML surrogates as accelerators or preconditioners that seamlessly fall back to the exact physical solver whenever residuals spike. Hard Conservation & Conformal-UQ Hard-Stop: Enforce strict discrete invariants and formal uncertainty bounds; halt and flag the run rather than silently extrapolating. Do Not Deploy Black-Box Surrogates Out-of-Sample: Never trust learned surrogates beyond verified interpolation regimes — run first-principles physics directly.

**AlphaFold & Scientific Foundation Models** — What AlphaFold and its successors mean — an instrument, a catalyst, or a template for science itself. Black-Box Pattern Matcher Missing Physics stands for the position that AlphaFold predicts structures superbly and explains none of them. Powerful Catalyst Needing Physical Integration stands for the position that foundation models are accelerants: coupled to physical simulation, experiment and theory they multiply what each can do. New Blueprint for Computational Science stands for the position that a model trained on a field's data solved its fifty-year problem in one step.

**Algorithmic Innovation vs. Hardware/GPU Scaling** — Where speed actually comes from — the algorithm or the machine. Algorithms Beat Hardware Every Time stands for the position that the FFT, multigrid and FMM each outran decades of hardware growth in a single idea. Algorithm–Hardware Co-Design stands for the position that algorithms are designed for machines that exist and machines for algorithms that matter. Ride the Exascale GPU Wave stands for the position that hardware delivers a rising tide on a schedule no research programme matches.

**Automatic Code Generation & DSLs vs. Hand-Tuned Kernels** — How codes survive hardware diversity — by hand, by abstraction layer, or by generation. Hand-Tuned Architecture-Specific Kernels stands for the position that peak performance has always been hand-won, and abstraction layers leave measurable fractions of the machine unused. Performance-Portable Abstractions (Kokkos/SYCL) stands for the position that vendor churn is a certainty and staff time is the scarcest resource. High-Level Symbolic DSLs (Firedrake/FEniCS) stands for the position that writing the mathematics and generating the code removes whole species of bug and lets the scientist work at the level of the science.

**Real-Time Coupling of Experiments & HPC Steering** — How tightly simulation should couple to the experiment it serves — afterwards, between shots, or live. Decoupled Offline Post-Run Simulation stands for the position that simulation's value is depth, and depth takes longer than a shot cycle. Fast-Turnaround Between-Shot Guidance stands for the position that between-shot guidance is the sweet spot: fast models steer the next configuration while full analysis proceeds offline. Live Closed-Loop Edge-to-HPC Steering stands for the position that plasma control by learned models running in the loop — as in magnetic control of a tokamak — shows simulation-grade models acting in real time.

### Trust, Error & Reproducibility

**Error bars** — The ethics of the error bar — what a reported uncertainty is for. Honest: Report the full error — systematic and statistical, all of it, even when it makes the result look weak. Mostly honest: Error bars honest in method, optimistic at the edges. Optimistic: Report the error that makes the result look good — the statistical error only, or the best-case systematics, because the paper needs a clean number.

**Reproducibility** — What reproducibility actually reproduces — the artefact or the finding. Code: Reproducibility means the code — publish the source, the input decks, the random seeds, and anyone can re-run it. Results: Reproducibility means the results — an independent group with different code should get the same physics, and re-running your code only proves your code runs.

**Black box** — The trust owed to other people's solvers — the daily working form of the trust question. Trust: Use the library, the package, the solver — it works, the authors know what they're doing, and your job is the physics, not the numerics. Verify the load-bearing parts: Trust the box where it is cheap; verify what the result stands on. Verify: Never trust a black box — read the source, test it against known solutions, understand what it does before you let it touch your physics.

**Code Openness** — How open the instruments of a published result must be. Radical Open-Source Science: Publishing all solver code, input decks, random seeds, and raw trajectory data on GitHub/Zenodo with Docker containers for 100% reproduction. Open on publication: Protected while it competes; open when published. Protected Competitive Advantage: Keeping custom in-house physics algorithms proprietary until all papers and grants are extracted.

**Formal Code Verification Discipline** — The verification bar a code must clear before its results may be published. Physical Sanity Checks Suffice stands for the position that a code that reproduces known limits, conserves what it should and behaves sensibly has passed the tests that catch real-world bugs. Unit Tests & Mesh Convergence stands for the position that tests plus demonstrated mesh convergence catch the overwhelming majority of code error at a cost every project can pay. Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS) stands for the position that MMS is the only technique that verifies the code solves its own equations at the claimed order, everywhere, including the terms sanity checks never exercise.

**Validation Philosophy: What Does "Validated" Mean?** — The philosophy of the word 'validated' — falsifiability, fitness, or certification. Models Are Never Validated—Only Falsifiable stands for the position that Oreskes's argument has never been answered: open natural systems cannot be validated, only corroborated or falsified. Fit-for-Purpose Over Declared Domain stands for the position that validation is a relation between a model, a purpose and a domain. Quantitative Predictive Certification stands for the position that where lives and licences depend on predictions — reactors, aircraft, stockpiles — certification against quantitative criteria is the operative standard, and philosophy that dissolves it dissolves the practice it describes.

**Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) Rigor** — How much uncertainty machinery a result must carry to stand. Deterministic Best-Estimate Point Runs stands for the position that a best estimate from a verified code is a clear, falsifiable claim. Parametric Sensitivity Sweeps stands for the position that sweeps show which inputs matter and how much, at a cost any project can bear. Full Bayesian & Model-Discrepancy UQ stands for the position that Kennedy–O'Hagan's framework is the only one that counts the model's own inadequacy as an uncertainty.

**Bitwise Determinism vs. Statistical Reproducibility** — What counts as the same run twice, in chaotic parallel codes. Statistical Equivalence Is Enough stands for the position that a chaotic system's trajectory is not its result — its statistics are. Seeded Same-Stack Reproducibility stands for the position that same seeds, same stack, same answer is achievable and catches real regressions. Strict Bitwise & Containerized Replay stands for the position that only bitwise replay makes a result an artefact that can be re-examined exactly — in disputes, in regulation, in replication.

**Blind Multi-Code Intercomparisons** — A single-affinity topic: how mandatory blind comparison between independent codes should be (negative ratings read as aversion — see §5). Mandatory Multi-Code Benchmark Intercomparisons stands for the position that the Santa Barbara cluster comparison and its successors showed codes agreeing with themselves and disagreeing with each other.

**Multifidelity Uncertainty Quantification & Control Variates** — A single-affinity topic: how strongly cheap low-fidelity models should be exploited to accelerate uncertainty quantification (negative ratings read as aversion — see §5). Exploiting Cheap Low-Fidelity Models to Accelerate High-Fidelity UQ stands for the position that multilevel and multifidelity estimators cut UQ cost by orders of magnitude with exactness preserved in the limit — Giles's MLMC is a theorem, not a heuristic.

**Scientific Data Formats, Provenance & Archival Longevity** — How much of a project's life belongs to its data's future — formats, provenance, longevity. Lightweight Custom Binary / Flat Files stands for the position that at petabyte scale, I/O is the budget, and custom binaries are what the machine can actually write. Self-Describing Standard Formats (HDF5/Zarr/NetCDF) stands for the position that self-describing standards make data outlive codes and teams. Full FAIR Provenance & Cloud-Native Analysis-Ready Data stands for the position that FAIR, provenance-tracked, analysis-ready data turns one group's run into the field's instrument — Wilkinson's principles are how simulation data becomes cumulative science rather than landfill.

### The Computational Life

**Legacy code** — The legacy question — whether old code is an asset with known bugs or a trap with unknown ones. Maintain: The old code works — it has been validated for decades, the bugs are known, and rewriting risks losing physics that nobody remembers is in there. Incremental Refactor (Strangler): Replace the old system piece by piece, strangling it with the new one. Rewrite: The old code is unmaintainable — spaghetti Fortran, no tests, no documentation, and the only way forward is to start clean.

**Breadth** — The shape of a computational career — depth in one method or range across many. Specialist: One method, deeply — you are the DFT person, or the Monte Carlo person, and your depth is your value. T-shaped: Depth in one area, working range across the rest. Generalist: Many methods, broadly — you pick the right tool for each problem, and your range is your value.

**Starting point** — Where a piece of work begins — in the equations or in the code. Theory: The physics comes first — the equations, the model, the understanding; the code is just how you evaluate it. Start where it is cheaper: Begin with theory or code, whichever the problem makes cheaper. Implementation: The code comes first — the algorithm, the data structures, the performance; a theory you can't compute is a wish, not a result.

**Role** — Professional identity — what the practitioner takes themselves to be making: physics, or software. Physicist First: Code is just an incidental vehicle to understand the physics; quick-and-dirty scripts are fine if the physical insight is sound. Physicist who ships software: A physicist's questions, an engineer's craft. Research Software Engineer (RSE): Code quality, unit testing, continuous integration, version control, and memory safety are as crucial as the physical equations. Software as First-Class Academic Output stands for the position that until software counts in hiring and promotion as papers do, the field will keep underproducing its own infrastructure.

**Flagship Community Codes vs. Bespoke Research Scripts** — Two crossed choices recorded as one switchbox — community against bespoke code, and production against prototype; the axes are independent and both-negative is the status-quo middle (see §5). Community Flagship Code (LAMMPS/OpenFOAM/VASP) ↔ Bespoke From-Scratch Code stands for the position that building on a flagship inherits decades of verification and a community that finds bugs for you. Production Software Engineering ↔ Rapid Exploratory Prototyping stands for the position that production engineering makes results durable and shareable, while rapid prototyping makes them possible at all.

**Software Ecosystem: Commercial Certified Suites vs. Open Community Flagships vs. Bespoke Codes** — Where high-stakes simulation should live — vendor suites, community flagships, or in-house codes. Certified Commercial Suites (ANSYS / COMSOL / Schrödinger): Turnkey industrial CAE/multiphysics suites provide regulatory QA certification, vendor liability, and tested pre/post-processing. Open-Source Community Flagships: Inspectable, peer-vetted open community codes (LAMMPS, OpenFOAM, PETSc, Trilinos) combine scientific auditability with shared longevity. Custom In-House Research Solvers: Bespoke codes tailored to novel physics and bare-metal hardware outperform general-purpose packages at the frontier.

**Visual Diagnostics vs. Pure Quantitative Metrics** — How a practitioner reads a run — in its norms or in its fields. Quantitative Norms & Residuals Only stands for the position that norms are comparable, archivable and honest about their scope. Metrics Anchored by Visual Sanity Checks stands for the position that numbers decide and pictures audit — the working rule of experienced groups, because each catches the other's characteristic blindness. Rich Field Visualization Catches Bugs First stands for the position that the folklore is earned: boundary artefacts, symmetry breaks and mesh imprints announce themselves to the eye long before they move a global norm.

**Energy & Carbon Ethics of Exascale Computing** — Whether the carbon and energy cost of computing is a scientific consideration or someone else's problem. Scientific Breakthrough Justifies Compute stands for the position that a single insight can redirect an entire field's energy budget for decades. Pragmatic FLOPs-per-Watt Efficiency stands for the position that efficiency is a scientific virtue in itself — the same science for less energy is strictly better — and performance-per-watt discipline usually improves the algorithms too. Strict Compute Carbon Budgeting stands for the position that compute at exascale is an industrial process with an industrial footprint, and a field that models the climate cannot exempt its own emissions from accounting.

**Aesthetic Ideal of Computational Craft** — What beauty in computational work is — the aesthetic the other topics quietly serve. Exact Conservation & Clean Convergence stands for the position that a run that conserves exactly and converges at its design order is beautiful the way a proof is beautiful — nothing wasted, nothing hidden. Breathtaking Emergent Realism stands for the position that when a simulation's world becomes recognisably alive — weather, galaxies, turbulence — the emergence itself is the aesthetic event, and the field's public wonder lives here. Blazing Exascale Throughput stands for the position that a code running at the roofline of the largest machine humans build is an aesthetic object in the engineer's sense — every cycle accounted for. Minimal Toy Code Capturing Essence stands for the position that fifty lines that capture a phenomenon's essence teach more and age better than a flagship.

## 3. Reasons and statistics backing each position

### 3.1 The Epistemology of Simulation

#### The Three Pillars: What Is a Simulation?
- **Virtual Experiment** — Reasons: a run has a setup, an intervention and measured output, like a bench experiment; it can surprise its maker, which mere deduction from premises cannot. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where a simulation's surprise turned out to be only an artefact of its own assumptions, with no experimental analogue.
- **Extended Theory / Numerical Calculation** — Reasons: a simulation only evaluates consequences already contained in its equations and inputs; calling it experiment borrows authority the calculation has not earned. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a simulation result that counted as evidence in a way no one would grant to a hand calculation of the same equations.
- **Distinct Third Pillar of Science** — Reasons: simulation has its own methods, errors and institutions, irreducible to either theory or experiment; its opacity and scale create epistemic problems neither older pillar faces. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that every epistemic problem of simulation reduces cleanly to a problem of theory or of experiment.

#### Purpose
- **Discovery** — Reasons: parameter spaces and emergent behaviour are too vast to reach by analysis alone; a simulation that only confirms what was expected has wasted its distinctive power. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that simulation-led discoveries were mostly artefacts, while confirmatory runs produced the field's durable results.
- **Verification** — Reasons: an unchecked prediction is a liability, and simulation's controlled conditions make it the sharpest test of a theory's consequences; discovery without verification is speculation with graphics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a major physical discovery made by simulation that no verification-first programme would have funded or trusted.

#### Computational experiment
- **Real** — Reasons: a simulation interrogates a system under controlled variation and yields data with error bars; the material of the system differs from a bench system, but the epistemic act is the same. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a principled difference — not just a practical one — between intervening on a model and intervening on the world.
- **Quasi-experiment** — Reasons: a run experiments on a model, not on nature, so its results are evidence about the world only through the model's fidelity; that two-step structure deserves its own name. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: proof that model fidelity can be established so completely that the two-step collapses into direct experiment.
- **Metaphor** — Reasons: 'experiment' is a courtesy title for arithmetic; the output was implicit in the input, and treating it as observation invites exactly the over-trust the field keeps regretting. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a simulation outcome that functioned in practice exactly as an observation — settling a question no calculation-framing would have settled.

#### Tradeoff
- **Maximum Physical Fidelity** — Reasons: every approximation is a place the answer can silently rot; if the physics is worth computing, it is worth computing without fudge factors, and waiting is cheaper than being wrong. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship result where maximal fidelity changed no conclusion that a cheaper model had already reached.
- **Fidelity where it matters** — Reasons: sensitivity is uneven, so uniform fidelity wastes resources on regions that cannot affect the answer; spend accuracy where the solution is sensitive and speed elsewhere. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that sensitivity was systematically misjudged, so selective fidelity missed the regions that mattered.
- **High-Throughput Speed & Scalability** — Reasons: science advances by exploring many cases, not by polishing one; a fast approximate answer across a whole parameter space teaches more than one perfect point. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where fast approximate sweeps converged on a wrong picture that one high-fidelity run would have prevented.

#### Validation
- **Empirical Validation First** — Reasons: a simulation is about the world, so only the world can certify it; a code can converge beautifully to the solution of the wrong equations. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a simulation trusted on convergence alone that outperformed empirically tuned models on new data.
- **Internal Code Verification First** — Reasons: agreement with noisy lab data can be manufactured by tuning, but grid convergence and exact conservation cannot be faked; verify the mathematics first, then argue about the physics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a verified code whose conserved, converged answer was still physically wrong in a way only lab comparison revealed early.

#### Simulation Trust
- **Bold Simulation Confidence** — Reasons: modern codes are validated instruments, often cleaner than the experiments they are compared with; habitual distrust wastes the instrument and privileges noisy data by default. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a prominent failure in which confident trust, not the code, was the proximate cause of a wrong published result.
- **Calibrated trust** — Reasons: trust is not a mood but a quantity, earned per code, per regime and per quantity of interest; proportioning it to the validation record is simply accuracy about accuracy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that formal calibration of trust performed no better than a bolder or a more sceptical default.
- **Permanent Self-Skepticism** — Reasons: discretisation, round-off and unmodelled boundaries are always present and rarely announced; the history of the field is a history of artefacts mistaken for physics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a long run of flagship results in which the sceptical checks never once changed a conclusion.

#### Are we living in a computer simulation?
- **Ancestor simulation (made by others)** — Reasons: if advanced civilisations run many simulations of their ancestors, simulated observers vastly outnumber real ones; Bostrom's trilemma makes our being simulated the probable branch unless the premises fail. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sound argument that one of the trilemma's other branches — extinction first, or no interest in ancestor simulations — must hold.
- **It from bit (computation, no simulator)** — Reasons: information, not matter, is the deepest layer physics keeps finding; the universe computes itself, with no programmer outside it and none needed. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a physical phenomenon shown to be in principle uncomputable, breaking the identification of nature with computation.
- **Base reality** — Reasons: the hypothesis multiplies entities beyond necessity and explains nothing physics lacks; the parsimonious reading of our evidence is that this is the physical world. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a confirmed physical signature — a lattice cut-off, a rounding artefact — that base reality cannot explain.
- **Agnostic / Metaphysically Irrelevant** — Reasons: the question is constructed to be unfalsifiable, so no run, telescope or proof can move it; scientific computing should spend itself on questions that can be wrong. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proposed test with a genuine discriminating prediction between simulation and base reality.

#### Is the universe a computation?
- **The universe is a computation** — Reasons: every physical law we have can be stated as information processing, and quantisation hints nature is discrete at bottom; the computation is not a picture of the universe, it is the universe. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fundamental physical quantity shown to be irreducibly non-informational.
- **A useful description, not the stuff** — Reasons: maps are not territories: that computation describes nature superbly does not make nature made of computation; confusing the model with the stuff is the field's characteristic temptation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the computational description is not one description among others but forced by the structure of physics itself.
- **Matter and fields are fundamental** — Reasons: computers are made of matter and fields, not the reverse; information always needs a physical bearer, so the bearer is fundamental and the pattern derivative. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a demonstration that physical law follows from information-theoretic principles with no material remainder.

#### Can a simulated mind be conscious?
- **Yes, computation suffices** — Reasons: consciousness is what certain information processing does, not what it is made of; a simulated mind with the right functional organisation is a mind, as a simulated hurricane is not wet but a simulated addition is still addition — for minds, the pattern is the thing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a property of conscious minds shown to depend on specific biology or physics that functional organisation cannot reproduce.
- **Maybe, unknown** — Reasons: we have no theory of consciousness that settles substrate questions, and confidence either way outruns the evidence; honesty here is a suspended judgement, not a shrug. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a validated theory of consciousness that entails a verdict on substrate independence.
- **No, minds need more than computation** — Reasons: a simulation of a thing is not the thing — simulated rain wets nothing; minds plausibly need the causal powers of living tissue, or of physics a digital model only represents. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a simulated system displaying every independent marker of consciousness we can devise, with no principled remainder.

#### Looking for glitches
- **Hunt for artifacts** — Reasons: a discretised universe should show a cut-off or anisotropy — in cosmic rays, for instance — and looking costs little against so large a payoff; untestable is a claim to test, not to assume. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proof that any simulator consistent with known physics would leave no detectable signature of the proposed kinds.
- **Only as a byproduct of normal physics** — Reasons: ordinary precision physics already constrains exotic structure, and its results can be read for simulation signatures afterwards; a dedicated hunt distorts priorities toward a hypothesis with no prior support. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a near-miss anomaly in normal physics data that only a dedicated, designed search could resolve.
- **Untestable metaphysics** — Reasons: any glitch found would be absorbed as new physics and any absence explained by a better simulator; a hypothesis compatible with every outcome is not a research programme. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a glitch signature that no physical theory could absorb and only a computational substrate predicts.

#### Ethics of simulated minds
- **The regard owed to a mind** — Reasons: if a simulated mind can suffer, creating it casually and deleting it freely is a moral wrong of the ordinary kind; moral status follows the mind, not the medium. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: settled evidence that simulated minds cannot be conscious, removing the patient from the act.
- **Caution, because we might** — Reasons: we do not know whether simulated minds are conscious, and the cost of wrongly assuming not — vast unnoticed suffering — dwarfs the cost of restraint; uncertainty itself argues for care. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a reliable consciousness test that returned a clear negative for the systems in question.
- **No duty owed** — Reasons: a model of a mind is a representation, like a character in a novel; duties attach to beings, and multiplying duties to software states paralyses ordinary computing on a speculation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence strong enough that a simulated mind's experiences are real to it in the way ours are to us.

#### Determinism underneath
- **Fully deterministic underneath** — Reasons: randomness has repeatedly turned out to be ignorance of a deeper rule; a deterministic substrate makes the universe in principle simulable end to end, which the success of simulation itself hints at. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a proof that no deterministic completion of quantum mechanics can reproduce its predictions.
- **Deterministic underneath, random on top** — Reasons: the evolution can be fixed while outcomes are irreducibly chancy for inhabitants — as in branching or statistical readings; this split honours both the equations and the experienced randomness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence collapsing the split either way — outcome-level determinism demonstrated, or evolution itself shown stochastic.
- **Random at bottom** — Reasons: Bell-type results close the local deterministic exits, and taking randomness as real is taking our best theory at its word; a hidden rule is a hope, not a finding. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a deterministic model reproducing quantum correlations without the conspiratorial assumptions the theorems exclude.

#### Prediction Fidelity vs. Conceptual Understanding
- **Minimal Models for Insight** — Reasons: a model you cannot understand cannot teach you anything, however accurate its numbers; minimal models isolate mechanisms, and mechanism is what science keeps. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where a minimal model's clean mechanism proved to be an artefact the fuller model dissolved.
- **Hierarchical Model Ladder** — Reasons: insight and prediction are rungs, not rivals: simple models explain, complex ones predict, and each checks the other; Held's ladder is how climate science actually works. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that ladder rungs systematically disagreed, so the hierarchy concealed rather than transferred understanding.
- **High-Fidelity Quantitative Prediction** — Reasons: decisions — engineering, climate, stewardship — need numbers with error bars, not parables; a model that cannot predict quantitatively has not finished its job. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a high-fidelity flagship whose predictions were confidently wrong where a minimal model was right.

#### Subgrid Tuning & Parameterization Honesty
- **Reject Ad-Hoc Tuning—Resolve Physics** — Reasons: a tuned parameter that absorbs missing physics hides the model's real error and breaks outside its calibration range; resolving the process, however costly, is the only honest cure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a tuned closure that extrapolated reliably far outside its calibration regime, with the physics of why.
- **Transparent Documented Calibration** — Reasons: some processes cannot yet be resolved at scale, and calibration against data is normal science — provided every tuned value, target and sensitivity is published; the sin is concealment, not tuning. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that documented calibration still misled users who trusted the documentation.
- **Pragmatic Tuning for System Skill** — Reasons: a model is judged by the skill of the whole system, and tuning is how complex systems are made skilful; refusing to tune on principle sacrifices real predictive power to purity. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a skilfully tuned system that failed catastrophically on a regime change its tuning had masked.

#### Trusting Simulations in Unobserved Regimes
- **Never Trust Beyond Validated Data** — Reasons: models are interpolators with good public relations; outside the validated envelope there is no evidence, only output, and Oreskes's argument shows open natural systems cannot be validated in the strong sense at all. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an extrapolated prediction, made in advance and in an unobserved regime, that was later confirmed in detail.
- **Physics-Bounded Extrapolation with UQ** — Reasons: conservation laws and dimensional constraints do not stop at the edge of the dataset; bounded extrapolation with honest uncertainty quantification is usable knowledge, clearly labelled as to its reach. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: repeated bounded extrapolations whose stated uncertainty intervals failed to contain the truth.
- **First-Principles Laws Guarantee Reach** — Reasons: the same equations govern the unobserved regime — that is what makes them laws; a code solving them faithfully carries its warrant with it, and refusing extrapolation abandons problems only simulation can touch. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an unobserved-regime failure traced to a law's approximation breaking, not to numerics.

#### Physics-Based "Digital Twins": Genuine Paradigm or Buzzword Hype?
- **Rebranded Old Simulation Buzzword** — Reasons: engineering simulation with a dashboard is still engineering simulation; the twin label mostly serves vendors, and the National Academies' careful definition excludes most of what is sold under it. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a deployed twin whose closed-loop behaviour produced results no offline simulation programme had achieved.
- **Valuable When Closed-Loop & UQ-Calibrated** — Reasons: a twin earns its name only when live data continuously update it and its uncertainty is quantified; under those conditions it is a genuine instrument, not a slogan. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that closed-loop updating degraded rather than improved a model's reliability.
- **Transformative Live Bidirectional Cyber-Physical Paradigm** — Reasons: when the model steers the asset and the asset retrains the model in real time, the pair is a new kind of object — neither experiment nor simulation — with its own epistemology. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mature assessment showing bidirectional coupling added cost and fragility without capability beyond fast simulation.

### 3.2 Methods

#### Computational Methods
- **Electronic Structure & Ab Initio Quantum** — Reasons: electrons decide chemistry, so start where the deciding happens; parameter-free quantum mechanics is the only level that owes nothing to fitting. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a class of problems where electronic structure proved systematically misleading and a coarser representation was truer.
- **Classical & Molecular Dynamics (MD)** — Reasons: atoms in motion explain most of matter's behaviour at usable scales; MD's trajectories are the closest thing simulation has to watching. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that trajectory-level classical pictures mislead for the holder's core systems.
- **Mesh & Continuum Solvers** — Reasons: most engineering and geophysics lives at continuum scale, where PDEs on meshes are the mature, verified instrument; atomistic detail there is waste. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a continuum failure traceable to the continuum assumption itself, cured only from below.
- **Statistical & Stochastic Monte Carlo** — Reasons: when the state space defeats enumeration, sampling is not a fallback but the correct mathematics; Monte Carlo turns intractability into error bars. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sampling failure — a missed mode, a silent bias — that a deterministic method would have caught.
- **Discrete-Event & Agent-Based** — Reasons: many systems are made of agents and events, not fields; forcing them into PDEs erases the heterogeneity that drives their behaviour. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an agent model whose emergent result proved to be an artefact of arbitrary agent rules.

#### Forces

### 3.3 Scale, Discretization & Time

#### Multi-scale bridging
- **Direct Concurrent Multi-Scale** — Reasons: feedback between scales is often the phenomenon — a crack tip, a flame front — and only concurrent coupling lets the scales talk while it happens. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a concurrent coupling whose interface artefacts exceeded the feedback it was built to capture.

- **Autonomous Single-Scale Effective Models** — Reasons: a well-built effective model at one scale is a finished piece of science, not a fragment; pretending to couple scales you cannot verify produces theatre, not multiscale physics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an effective model that failed exactly where a coupled treatment succeeded.

#### Integration
- **Symplectic Integrators (Geometric Mechanics)** — Reasons: over billions of steps only structure survives: symplectic integrators conserve phase volume and bound energy error forever, while accurate-per-step methods drift secularly; for orbits and molecules, geometry is accuracy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a symplectic run whose bounded energy concealed a wrong trajectory in a chaotic system.
- **High-Order Adaptive Steppers** — Reasons: most problems are not Hamiltonian forever-problems; adaptive high-order methods put effort where the solution is active and deliver a specified local accuracy, which is what a working scientist usually needs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an adaptive run whose error control passed every test while a conserved quantity quietly drifted.

#### Spatial discretization
- **Structured Uniform Grids & Spectral Methods** — Reasons: regular grids give spectral accuracy, perfect cache behaviour and FFT speed; where geometry permits, nothing else is competitive per degree of freedom. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a structured-grid result distorted by forcing a complex geometry onto a regular lattice.
- **Unstructured Adaptive Meshes (AMR)** — Reasons: physics concentrates — shocks, boundary layers, interfaces — and AMR puts resolution exactly there; uniform resolution of a concentrated problem is waste by design. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an AMR artefact at a refinement boundary mistaken for a physical structure.
- **Mesh-Free Particle Methods** — Reasons: particles carry the material, so extreme deformation, fragmentation and free surfaces need no remeshing heroics; the mesh's tangling problem simply never arises. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a particle-method result decided by its kernel or neighbour artefacts.
- **Conservative Finite Volumes (FV)** — Reasons: finite volumes conserve mass, momentum and energy to machine precision by construction, which is why CFD trusts them; a method that leaks conserved quantities leaks credibility. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a finite-volume result whose conservation was impeccable and whose solution was still wrong — showing conservation is not sufficient.
- **Finite Elements & Discontinuous Galerkin** — Reasons: finite elements handle real geometry and offer a complete error theory, and DG adds high order with local conservation; for elliptic and complex-domain problems this is the mature instrument. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a finite-element failure traced to meshing quality rather than to the formulation its users trusted.

#### Scope
- **Resolution** — Reasons: under-resolved simulations do not give approximate answers, they give answers about a different, smoother system; the fine structure is frequently the phenomenon. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a resolution study showing the fine structure was inert and the coarse answer was the physics.
- **Resolve the region that matters** — Reasons: resources are finite and interest is local; a well-placed nest or zoom gives flagship resolution where the question lives and context everywhere else. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a zoom study whose boundary with the coarse surround contaminated the region of interest.
- **Domain Size** — Reasons: finite boxes lie about long-range correlations, rare events and statistics; the whole system, run long, is the only sample of itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a large-domain result whose statistics were dominated by the box's own artefacts.

#### Convergence
- **Strict** — Reasons: a result without a demonstrated convergence order is a number without a unit; grid studies and Richardson extrapolation are cheap compared with retracting a conclusion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a strictly converged result that was converged to the wrong physics — showing convergence is necessary, not sufficient.
- **Converged enough, checked** — Reasons: convergence is a means to a trustworthy answer, not a ritual; a documented check proportioned to the claim's weight is rational resource use. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a 'checked' result whose undocumented margin concealed a resolution-dependent conclusion.
- **Pragmatic** — Reasons: experienced practitioners read convergence off the solution's behaviour, and two resolutions agreeing is real evidence; demanding formal proof for every run would halve the science the field produces. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a pragmatic two-resolution agreement that dissolved at a third resolution.

#### Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR) vs. Uniform Grids
- **Clean Uniform Structured Grids** — Reasons: uniform grids have no coarse–fine interfaces to reflect waves or invent structures, and their performance is perfectly predictable; simplicity is a verification strategy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a uniform-grid study priced out of the dynamic range its problem required.
- **Static Nested / Unstructured Meshes** — Reasons: where the interesting geometry is known in advance, a fixed well-crafted mesh gives AMR's focus without its moving machinery and load-balancing pathologies. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a static mesh invalidated when the phenomenon moved out of the refined region.
- **Dynamic Adaptive Mesh Refinement** — Reasons: Berger–Oliger refinement follows the physics as it moves — collapse, shocks, ignition — across ranges no static mesh can span; its artefacts are known and testable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a dynamic-refinement artefact that survived the code's own tests into a published result.

#### Explicit Scalability vs. Implicit Stiff Solvers
- **Explicit Local Time-Stepping** — Reasons: explicit methods are simple, perfectly parallel and free of solver pathologies; where the CFL limit is tolerable, their transparency is worth the steps. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a stiff problem where explicit stepping's cost was not tolerable but ruinous.
- **IMEX Operator Splitting** — Reasons: most stiffness is partial — one stiff term among explicit-friendly physics — and IMEX treats each part with its proper method; the splitting error is the honest price of a working code. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a splitting error that coupled back into the physics and changed a conclusion.
- **Fully Implicit Newton–Krylov** — Reasons: implicit methods take the steps the physics wants, not the steps stability permits, and Newton–Krylov made them scalable; for stiff multiphysics this is the only route that does not apologise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an implicit run whose nonlinear solver converged to a physically wrong branch without signalling.

#### Floating-Point Precision Discipline
- **Strict FP64 (or Higher) Everywhere** — Reasons: round-off is the one error source that touches every operation, and chaotic systems amplify it without limit; precision you can prove is cheaper than results you must re-litigate. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mixed-precision result matching FP64 on a chaotic flagship problem at a fraction of the cost.
- **Mixed Precision with FP64 Accumulation** — Reasons: doing the bulk work in low precision and the reductions in FP64 captures most of the speed and nearly all of the safety; iterative refinement makes the combination a method, not a gamble. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a mixed-precision failure that FP64 accumulation failed to catch.
- **Embrace Low-Precision Tensor Cores** — Reasons: the hardware has voted: tensor cores offer an order of magnitude that physics cannot refuse; algorithms robust to low precision — as deep learning shows daily — are the future's native form. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a low-precision physics result whose error was systematic, silent and decision-relevant.

#### Direct Sparse Factorizations vs. Iterative Krylov Solvers
- **Bulletproof Direct Sparse LU/Cholesky** — Reasons: a direct factorisation answers every right-hand side, never fails to converge, and its cost is known in advance; for moderate sizes that certainty is the professional choice. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a direct method priced out by fill-in on a problem an iterative method solved routinely.
- **Preconditioned Hybrid Solvers** — Reasons: real codes mix — direct on subdomains, iterative globally — because physics is hybrid; the preconditioner is where the physical insight lives. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hybrid whose preconditioner silently degraded as the physics regime shifted.
- **Matrix-Free Iterative Krylov Methods** — Reasons: matrix-free Krylov scales to problems whose matrices cannot even be stored, and its memory leanness is what exascale rewards; convergence is a property to engineer, not to fear. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Krylov failure — stagnation misread as convergence — in a production run.

#### Geometric Multigrid vs. Algebraic Multigrid (AMG)
- **Hand-Crafted Geometric Multigrid (GMG)** — Reasons: a hierarchy built from the problem's own geometry coarsens what should be coarsened; Brandt's vision gives textbook multigrid efficiency — a cycle or two, independent of size. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a geometric hierarchy defeated by geometry too irregular to coarsen by hand.
- **Semi-Algebraic Hybrid Coarsening** — Reasons: semi-coarsening and hybrid strategies take geometric guidance where it exists and algebraic robustness where it does not; most real elliptic problems live in that middle. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hybrid that inherited the fragility of both parents on an anisotropic problem.
- **Black-Box Algebraic Multigrid (AMG)** — Reasons: AMG asks nothing of the user but the matrix and delivers near-optimal solves on unstructured problems no one could coarsen by hand; that generality is why it is infrastructure. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an AMG failure on a problem class — convection-dominated, saddle-point — where its assumptions silently broke.

#### Fast Multipole & Hierarchical H-Matrices vs. FFT / Particle-Mesh
- **FFT & Particle-Mesh Ewald (PME)** — Reasons: for periodic systems PME's accuracy-per-cost is unmatched and its implementation is a solved problem; the FFT is the field's most reliable fast algorithm. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an open-boundary problem where forcing periodicity to use PME invented the physics.
- **Tree-Code & Hybrid Particle-Mesh** — Reasons: trees handle open boundaries and clustered distributions that defeat uniform meshes, and hybrids take PME's smoothness with the tree's geometry. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a tree-code result sensitive to its opening-angle criterion in a way users had not tested.
- **Fast Multipole (FMM) & H-Matrices** — Reasons: FMM's O(N) with controlled accuracy is the mathematically complete answer to long-range interaction, and H-matrices extend the idea to operators; Greengard–Rokhlin is the benchmark of what a fast algorithm can be. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a problem where FMM's constants made it slower in practice than the method it theoretically dominates.

#### Randomized Numerical Linear Algebra vs. Classical Deterministic Decompositions
- **Classical Deterministic SVD / QR Only** — Reasons: a factorisation is a foundation; foundations should not be probabilistic, and deterministic decompositions give reproducible, certifiable results. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a randomized method with a deterministic check that matched classical results at a tenth of the cost, repeatedly.
- **Randomized Sketch + Deterministic Check** — Reasons: sketch to find the structure, then verify deterministically — the combination takes the speed and keeps the certificate; Halko's theory bounds the sketch's failure probability explicitly. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sketch whose failure mode the deterministic check was not designed to see.
- **Randomized Linear Algebra First** — Reasons: for data-scale problems the classical decompositions are simply unaffordable, and randomized methods' guarantees are theorems, not hopes; probability here is as rigorous as round-off analysis. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a randomized result whose advertised probability bound failed to cover the actual data distribution.

#### Synchronous Parallelism vs. Communication-Avoiding & Asynchronous Solvers
- **Clean Synchronous Bulk-Parallel MPI** — Reasons: bulk-synchronous programs can be reasoned about, reproduced and debugged; at the scales most physics runs, MPI's discipline is a feature the field keeps re-choosing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a bulk-synchronous code whose global barriers consumed the majority of an exascale run.
- **Overlap Compute & Non-Blocking Comms** — Reasons: hiding communication behind computation takes most of the synchrony cost without abandoning the model's clarity; it is the pragmatic centre the big codes actually occupy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an overlap optimisation whose complexity introduced a race that a synchronous version could not have.
- **Task-Based & Asynchronous Execution** — Reasons: at exascale, global barriers and load imbalance are the binding constraints, and task graphs with communication-avoiding algorithms are the demonstrated escape; synchrony is a habit hardware no longer subsidises. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a task-based run whose nondeterminism made a flagship result irreproducible.

### 3.4 The Machine & The Code

#### Hardware
- **Leadership scale** — Reasons: only leadership-class machines hold the largest problems — full-system, high-resolution, long-time runs exist nowhere else; access to them is access to otherwise impossible science. Statistic: Frontier at Oak Ridge reached 1.102 exaFLOPS on the LINPACK benchmark, the first exascale result (TOP500, 2022). What would move a holder: evidence that the holder's flagship questions were answered as well on controlled hardware, making the facility queue pure cost.
- **Hardware you control** — Reasons: a machine you control runs when you need it, with your stack, your data and no allocation committee; iteration speed, not peak FLOPS, is what most science actually consumes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a problem the holder genuinely needed that only a leadership machine could hold.

#### Processor
- **Massive CPU Cores** — Reasons: branchy, memory-hungry, irregular physics runs best where each core is a full computer; CPU codes carry decades of validation that porting puts at risk. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship CPU code outperformed by its own GPU port beyond any validation-risk discount.
- **Dense GPU / Tensor Accelerators** — Reasons: the FLOPS and bandwidth are on the GPU by an order of magnitude, and physics that refuses them refuses the era's computing; porting pain is temporary, the gap is not. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a GPU port whose lockstep model distorted the algorithm into a worse method.
- **Quantum Processors** — Reasons: for quantum problems the processor is not an accelerator but the physical system itself; no classical silicon changes that categorical fact. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a quantum-processor result matched by classical simulation of the same device.

#### Language
- **Battle-Tested Fortran/C Codebases** — Reasons: forty years of validation live in those lines, and the physics they encode is partly nowhere else; rewriting risks the asset to modernise its container. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a legacy codebase whose accumulated opacity caused a physics error no one could trace.
- **Modern Composable High-Level Stacks** — Reasons: composable libraries, notebooks and ecosystems let small teams assemble in weeks what monoliths took decades to grow; science's bottleneck is people, not FLOPS. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a composable stack whose dependency churn broke a published workflow within a year.
- **Julia Unified Language** — Reasons: the two-language problem — prototype slow, production fast — is a tax on every computational scientist; a language designed to end it deserves the field's centre. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a Julia workflow whose ecosystem gaps forced a retreat to the two-language pattern.
- **Rust & Modern Systems Safety** — Reasons: memory bugs in scientific codes are physics bugs with better camouflage; a language that excludes them at compile time protects results, not just programs. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that safety tooling's friction cost more science than the bugs it prevented.

#### Solvers
- **AI & Neural Operators (PINNs/FNO)** — Reasons: a trained operator evaluates in milliseconds what a solver computes in hours, and learns solution families, not single solutions; for design loops and inverse problems that changes the economics of the field. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a neural operator failing silently outside its training distribution on a safety-relevant problem.
- **Hybrid solvers** — Reasons: neural components where they are validated, classical solvers as the backbone — the hybrid takes the speed where it is safe and keeps guarantees where they matter. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hybrid whose learned component's errors were invisible to the classical backbone's checks.
- **Traditional Numerical Analysis (FEM/FVM)** — Reasons: verified error bounds, exact conservation and proven stability are what separate a solver from a suggestion; learned operators currently offer none of the three. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a traditional solver outperformed so completely by a learned one, with validation, that refusing it became the unscientific position.

#### MLIPs
- **MLIP Enthusiast** — Reasons: equivariant potentials reach near-DFT accuracy at classical cost, which does not improve molecular simulation so much as change its scale class; the results in catalysis and materials are already in the literature. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an MLIP prediction of a new phase or mechanism shown to be a training-set artefact.
- **Cautious adopter** — Reasons: MLIPs earn trust regime by regime, with uncertainty estimates and first-principles spot checks; adopted that way they are already useful and their failures are caught. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a cautiously validated MLIP that still failed inside its validated regime.
- **MLIP Skeptic** — Reasons: interpolation engines cannot be trusted for discovery, whose whole point is the unexpected; a potential that fails unpredictably at phase boundaries is a hazard wearing an accuracy statistic. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an MLIP that predicted a genuinely new phenomenon, later confirmed by DFT and experiment.

#### Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs): Promise or Hype?
- **Overhyped vs. Classical FEM/FV** — Reasons: systematic benchmarks show PINNs losing to FEM/FV on forward problems in accuracy and cost, often by orders of magnitude; a method should be judged on the problems it claims. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a forward PDE problem where a PINN beat a tuned FEM/FV baseline on cost at equal accuracy.
- **Strong for Inverse & Noisy Data Assimilation** — Reasons: where data are sparse, noisy and the parameters unknown, a mesh-free method that fuses data and physics in one loss is genuinely the right tool; inverse problems are PINNs' home ground. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an inverse problem where classical regularisation beat PINNs decisively on the same data.
- **Transformative for High-Dimensional PDEs** — Reasons: meshes die exponentially with dimension and neural representations do not; for high-dimensional problems — finance, quantum, control — that scaling difference is not an improvement but an opening. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a high-dimensional success that a classical sparse-grid method reproduced at lower cost.

#### Differentiable Physics & Automatic Differentiation
- **Priority of End-to-End Differentiable Simulators** — Reasons: gradients through whole trajectories turn tuning, inference and design from search into calculus; Kochkov-style differentiable solvers have matched classical accuracy while enabling what classical codes cannot do at all. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a differentiable programme whose gradient information proved too noisy or too memory-bound to use on its flagship problem.

#### Hard Symmetry Constraints vs. Soft Scale-Driven Learning in SciML
- **Hard Equivariance & Exact Conservation** — Reasons: a model that cannot violate conservation or symmetry cannot produce whole classes of nonsense; NequIP and MACE showed hard constraints buy data efficiency of orders of magnitude. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hard-constrained model beaten by a soft one trained at scale on the holder's own problem class.
- **Pragmatic Inductive Biases** — Reasons: some structure is worth building in and some is cheaper to learn; the engineering question is which, per problem — dogma in either direction wastes either data or compute. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that the pragmatic middle systematically chose wrong — constraints where scale would have won, scale where constraints were needed.
- **Bitter-Lesson Scale Learns Symmetries Softly** — Reasons: Sutton's lesson is that general methods plus compute beat hand-built structure in the long run, in field after field; symmetries are patterns, and patterns are what large models are for. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a symmetry-critical problem where scaled soft models plateaued and a constrained model walked past them.

#### ML Surrogate Out-of-Distribution Guardrails
- **Active Learning & On-the-Fly First-Principles Retraining** — Reasons: the surrogate should notice its own ignorance, ask the exact solver, and grow; active-learning potentials have run stable reactive dynamics this way, turning the failure mode into the training loop. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an active-learning run whose uncertainty signal missed the states that actually broke it.
- **Automatic Fallback to Exact Physics Solver** — Reasons: treat the surrogate as an accelerator with a certified fallback and the deployment question dissolves: speed when valid, exactness when not, no silent extrapolation anywhere. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fallback design whose switching itself introduced artefacts at the boundary.
- **Hard Conservation & Conformal-UQ Hard-Stop** — Reasons: a run that cannot certify itself should stop, not drift; hard invariant checks and conformal bounds convert silent error into a loud, actionable halt. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that hard stops fired so often on benign states that users disabled them.
- **Do Not Deploy Black-Box Surrogates Out-of-Sample** — Reasons: outside the verified regime a black box is not an approximation but an invention; the only safe out-of-sample surrogate is the physics itself. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a black-box surrogate that extrapolated correctly and verifiably across a regime change.

#### AlphaFold & Scientific Foundation Models
- **Black-Box Pattern Matcher Missing Physics** — Reasons: AlphaFold predicts structures superbly and explains none of them; a science of pattern matchers accumulates answers while losing the mechanisms that make answers transferable. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a foundation model whose internal representations were shown to encode genuine physical mechanism, usable for new physics.
- **Powerful Catalyst Needing Physical Integration** — Reasons: foundation models are accelerants: coupled to physical simulation, experiment and theory they multiply what each can do; uncoupled, they are answers in search of understanding. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that integration with physics consistently subtracted from, rather than added to, the models' value.
- **New Blueprint for Computational Science** — Reasons: a model trained on a field's data solved its fifty-year problem in one step; that is a blueprint — pretrain on everything, fine-tune for anything — and other fields are right to copy it. Statistic: the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database passed 200 million predicted structures (DeepMind and EMBL-EBI, 2022). What would move a holder: a field where the blueprint was copied faithfully and failed for structural reasons.

#### Algorithmic Innovation vs. Hardware/GPU Scaling
- **Algorithms Beat Hardware Every Time** — Reasons: the FFT, multigrid and FMM each outran decades of hardware growth in a single idea; Gustafson's scaling helps the problems hardware can help, but complexity classes are forever. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a problem where no algorithmic advance arrived and hardware scaling alone carried the science for a generation.
- **Algorithm–Hardware Co-Design** — Reasons: algorithms are designed for machines that exist and machines for algorithms that matter; the exascale codes that worked were co-designed, and pretending either side leads alone is history rewritten. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a co-design effort outperformed by a pure algorithmic or pure hardware play on the same problem.
- **Ride the Exascale GPU Wave** — Reasons: hardware delivers a rising tide on a schedule no research programme matches; codes that ride it inherit each generation's gains, and Frontier-class machines make previously unaskable questions routine. Statistic: Frontier at Oak Ridge reached 1.102 exaFLOPS on the LINPACK benchmark, the first exascale result (TOP500, 2022). What would move a holder: evidence that the wave's programming-model churn consumed the gains it delivered.

#### Automatic Code Generation & DSLs vs. Hand-Tuned Kernels
- **Hand-Tuned Architecture-Specific Kernels** — Reasons: peak performance has always been hand-won, and abstraction layers leave measurable fractions of the machine unused; for a flagship kernel run a million times, hand tuning is the responsible spend. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a hand-tuned kernel suite abandoned when its architecture died, taking the capability with it.
- **Performance-Portable Abstractions (Kokkos/SYCL)** — Reasons: vendor churn is a certainty and staff time is the scarcest resource; Kokkos and SYCL buy most of the performance on every architecture with one codebase, which is the correct trade for almost everyone. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a portability layer whose performance gap on a flagship kernel proved decisive, not marginal.
- **High-Level Symbolic DSLs (Firedrake/FEniCS)** — Reasons: writing the mathematics and generating the code removes whole species of bug and lets the scientist work at the level of the science; Firedrake's generated kernels compete with hand-written ones on real problems. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a DSL-generated code that could not express the optimisation its problem required.

#### Real-Time Coupling of Experiments & HPC Steering
- **Decoupled Offline Post-Run Simulation** — Reasons: simulation's value is depth, and depth takes longer than a shot cycle; offline analysis is where models are verified, and coupling pressure trades rigour for theatre. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an experiment whose science was lost because simulation arrived after the configuration had moved on.
- **Fast-Turnaround Between-Shot Guidance** — Reasons: between-shot guidance is the sweet spot: fast models steer the next configuration while full analysis proceeds offline; tokamak and XFEL programmes already work this way. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that between-shot guidance systematically biased experiments toward what the fast model already believed.
- **Live Closed-Loop Edge-to-HPC Steering** — Reasons: plasma control by learned models running in the loop — as in magnetic control of a tokamak — shows simulation-grade models acting in real time; the closed loop is a new instrument, not a faster report. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a closed-loop deployment whose model error drove the system it was controlling.

### 3.5 Trust, Error & Reproducibility

#### Error bars
- **Honest** — Reasons: an error bar is a promise; reporting only the flattering part converts a measurement into an advertisement, and the field's credibility is the sum of those promises. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that full-error reporting was systematically punished — in review, in funding — enough to change practice.
- **Mostly honest** — Reasons: systematics are partly judgment, and judgment at the edges is human; the holder's standard is an honest method openly applied, accepting that its margins lean hopeful. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a case where the hopeful margin, not the method, carried a wrong conclusion.
- **Optimistic** — Reasons: a result competes for attention, citations and the next grant, and a number hedged to uselessness competes with nothing; the holder treats the clean number as the price of the work being read at all — stated here in the holder's terms, as the skin records the stance. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a career or result damaged more by an optimistic error bar than a cautious one would have been.

#### Reproducibility
- **Code** — Reasons: without the code, the inputs and the seeds, a published result is an anecdote about a program no one can inspect; open artefacts are the minimum unit of checkable science. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a field where code-sharing became universal and reproducibility did not improve.
- **Results** — Reasons: re-running the same code proves the code runs; science is reproduced when independent hands, with independent code, reach the same physics — the stronger and only meaningful test. Statistic: in a survey of 1,576 researchers, more than 70% said they had tried and failed to reproduce another group's experiments (Nature, 2016). What would move a holder: evidence that independent reimplementation mostly reproduced bugs of interpretation rather than testing physics.

#### Black box
- **Trust** — Reasons: modern libraries embody more verification than any single user can redo; refusing them until personally audited is not rigour but a refusal to build on the field. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a trusted library whose silent bug invalidated a body of results.
- **Verify the load-bearing parts** — Reasons: trust is allocated like any resource — freely where failure is cheap, never where the conclusion stands on the component; targeted verification is the rational middle. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a failure in a part judged non-load-bearing that proved load-bearing.
- **Verify** — Reasons: a solver you have not tested against a known solution is an oracle, and oracles have no error bars; the holder's rule — read it, test it, then use it — is what verification means at bench level. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a verification programme whose cost demonstrably exceeded the errors it caught, across a career of runs.

#### Code Openness
- **Radical Open-Source Science** — Reasons: code, decks, seeds and data, in containers, at publication — anything less asks readers to trust what they could have checked; openness is not generosity but the result's completion. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that radical openness was exploited — scooping, misuse — at a scale that changed the holder's calculus.
- **Open on publication** — Reasons: openness at publication balances the producer's legitimate head start against the reader's right to check; it is the norm journals can actually enforce, which makes it the norm that happens. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that publication-time openness arrived too late to catch errors that mattered.
- **Protected Competitive Advantage** — Reasons: a custom algorithm is years of a group's life, and forced disclosure taxes exactly the innovation openness claims to serve; the published physics can be checked by reimplementation, as it always was. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a protected code whose concealment hid an error that openness would have caught in weeks.

#### Formal Code Verification Discipline
- **Physical Sanity Checks Suffice** — Reasons: a code that reproduces known limits, conserves what it should and behaves sensibly has passed the tests that catch real-world bugs; formal order verification often certifies the parts that were never wrong. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sanity-checked code with a wrong convergence order that falsified a published scaling law.
- **Unit Tests & Mesh Convergence** — Reasons: tests plus demonstrated mesh convergence catch the overwhelming majority of code error at a cost every project can pay; this is the professional standard because it is proportioned to the risk. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a code passing tests and mesh convergence whose MMS revealed a defect both had missed.
- **Method of Manufactured Solutions (MMS)** — Reasons: MMS is the only technique that verifies the code solves its own equations at the claimed order, everywhere, including the terms sanity checks never exercise; Roache's discipline is what 'solving the equations right' means. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that MMS-verified codes failed in practice at rates no better than conventionally tested ones.

#### Validation Philosophy: What Does "Validated" Mean?
- **Models Are Never Validated—Only Falsifiable** — Reasons: Oreskes's argument has never been answered: open natural systems cannot be validated, only corroborated or falsified; honest language here prevents a category error with policy consequences. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a natural-system model whose validation claim was cashed out successfully in a genuinely open regime.
- **Fit-for-Purpose Over Declared Domain** — Reasons: validation is a relation between a model, a purpose and a domain; declared honestly, it is usable and true — the engineering meaning, and the only one that survives scrutiny. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a fit-for-purpose claim whose declared domain quietly expanded in use until it failed.
- **Quantitative Predictive Certification** — Reasons: where lives and licences depend on predictions — reactors, aircraft, stockpiles — certification against quantitative criteria is the operative standard, and philosophy that dissolves it dissolves the practice it describes. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a certified prediction that failed within its certified envelope.

#### Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) Rigor
- **Deterministic Best-Estimate Point Runs** — Reasons: a best estimate from a verified code is a clear, falsifiable claim; elaborate UQ around an unverified model is decoration, and many decisions need the point, not the distribution. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a point estimate whose unquantified uncertainty reversed the decision it informed.
- **Parametric Sensitivity Sweeps** — Reasons: sweeps show which inputs matter and how much, at a cost any project can bear; they are the working form of uncertainty knowledge in most published simulation science. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a sweep that missed an interaction effect a fuller UQ caught and that changed the conclusion.
- **Full Bayesian & Model-Discrepancy UQ** — Reasons: Kennedy–O'Hagan's framework is the only one that counts the model's own inadequacy as an uncertainty; anything less reports the precision of the wrong equations. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a full Bayesian UQ whose discrepancy term absorbed a real physical signal and reported it as model error.

#### Bitwise Determinism vs. Statistical Reproducibility
- **Statistical Equivalence Is Enough** — Reasons: a chaotic system's trajectory is not its result — its statistics are; demanding bitwise identity confuses the sample with the distribution and prices reproducibility out of parallel computing. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a chaotic study whose statistics differed between reduction orders beyond their stated errors.
- **Seeded Same-Stack Reproducibility** — Reasons: same seeds, same stack, same answer is achievable and catches real regressions; it is the level working collaborations can promise and auditors can check. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a same-stack rerun that diverged through a dependency's silent change.
- **Strict Bitwise & Containerized Replay** — Reasons: only bitwise replay makes a result an artefact that can be re-examined exactly — in disputes, in regulation, in replication; containers make the cost a one-time payment. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that bitwise discipline consumed resources grossly disproportionate to any dispute it settled.

#### Blind Multi-Code Intercomparisons
- **Mandatory Multi-Code Benchmark Intercomparisons** — Reasons: the Santa Barbara cluster comparison and its successors showed codes agreeing with themselves and disagreeing with each other; blind intercomparison is the only instrument that sees a whole code's artefacts at once. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that intercomparison exercises mostly measured benchmark design rather than code quality.

#### Multifidelity Uncertainty Quantification & Control Variates
- **Exploiting Cheap Low-Fidelity Models to Accelerate High-Fidelity UQ** — Reasons: multilevel and multifidelity estimators cut UQ cost by orders of magnitude with exactness preserved in the limit — Giles's MLMC is a theorem, not a heuristic; refusing free variance reduction wastes the allocation. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a multifidelity estimator whose low-fidelity correlation collapsed in the regime that mattered.

#### Scientific Data Formats, Provenance & Archival Longevity
- **Lightweight Custom Binary / Flat Files** — Reasons: at petabyte scale, I/O is the budget, and custom binaries are what the machine can actually write; provenance that prevents the run is provenance no one gets. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a custom-format archive that became unreadable within a hardware generation, losing the data entirely.
- **Self-Describing Standard Formats (HDF5/Zarr/NetCDF)** — Reasons: self-describing standards make data outlive codes and teams; the modest I/O cost buys decades of readability, which is the timescale archives are for. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a standard-format workflow whose overhead demonstrably cost a flagship result.
- **Full FAIR Provenance & Cloud-Native Analysis-Ready Data** — Reasons: FAIR, provenance-tracked, analysis-ready data turns one group's run into the field's instrument — Wilkinson's principles are how simulation data becomes cumulative science rather than landfill. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that full FAIR pipelines were built and then unused by anyone, including their builders.

### 3.6 The Computational Life

#### Legacy code
- **Maintain** — Reasons: a legacy code's bugs are known, mapped and priced into its results; a rewrite's bugs are none of those, and the physics embedded in forty years of fixes is partly undocumented anywhere else. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a maintained code whose undocumented physics was discovered to be wrong, with no one left who could fix it.
- **Incremental Refactor (Strangler)** — Reasons: replace piece by piece behind the old code's own tests, and the system never stops working while it becomes new; the strangler pattern is how long-lived codes actually modernise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a strangler refactor that stalled halfway, leaving two half-systems worse than either.
- **Rewrite** — Reasons: some codebases cannot be understood, only replaced; a clean rewrite with modern tests recovers the physics deliberately instead of inheriting it accidentally. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a rewrite that lost a physical behaviour the old code had and no test remembered.

#### Breadth
- **Specialist** — Reasons: frontier methods take a decade to master, and the field's hardest problems yield only to that depth; the specialist is the person the generalists call when it matters. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a problem the holder's specialism could not frame, solved by a generalist's imported method.
- **T-shaped** — Reasons: one deep method anchors judgment, and working range across the rest lets its holder choose tools rather than be chosen by them; most effective computational scientists are built this way. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that T-shaped breadth mostly produced shallow coverage where depth was needed.
- **Generalist** — Reasons: problems do not respect method boundaries, and the person who can pick the right tool per problem beats the person with one superb tool; range is a form of accuracy. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a generalist's tool choice that a specialist showed to be subtly, decisively wrong.

#### Starting point
- **Theory** — Reasons: the equations decide what can be learned; code written before the model is understood computes efficiently toward confusion, and the field's durable results are theory-led. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a theory-first project that modelled beautifully a system its implementation revealed to be different.
- **Start where it is cheaper** — Reasons: theory and implementation each expose the other's errors, so beginning with whichever is cheaper for this problem is not indecision but efficiency. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a project where starting cheap locked in the wrong starting point expensively.
- **Implementation** — Reasons: a theory you cannot compute is a wish; data structures, complexity and hardware decide what is knowable in practice, and implementation-first work discovers the computable questions. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an implementation-led project whose efficient machinery answered a question the physics had not asked.

#### Role
- **Physicist First** — Reasons: the code is scaffolding for insight and is judged as scaffolding; quick scripts that reach the physics beat engineered systems that reach the deadline. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a quick script whose unnoticed defect published a wrong physical claim.
- **Physicist who ships software** — Reasons: the questions are a physicist's and the craft standards are an engineer's, because results others must build on deserve both; this is the identity most working groups actually reward. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that shipping standards, applied to exploratory physics, slowed discovery without catching errors.
- **Research Software Engineer (RSE)** — Reasons: software is the instrument, and instrument-making is first-class scientific work — tested, reviewed, maintained; the RSE identity names a contribution the physicist-first culture free-rides on. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an RSE-led project whose engineering quality outran its scientific yield.
- **Software as First-Class Academic Output** — Reasons: until software counts in hiring and promotion as papers do, the field will keep underproducing its own infrastructure; recognising the artefact is the reform that funds all the others. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: evidence that counting software as output rewarded packaging over scientific substance.

#### Flagship Community Codes vs. Bespoke Research Scripts
- **Community Flagship Code (LAMMPS/OpenFOAM/VASP) ↔ Bespoke From-Scratch Code** — Reasons: building on a flagship inherits decades of verification and a community that finds bugs for you; writing bespoke buys exact fit to new physics at the price of re-earning all of that trust — holders weigh the inheritance against the fit. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a flagship whose inherited assumptions silently excluded the holder's physics, or a bespoke code whose re-earned trust never arrived.
- **Production Software Engineering ↔ Rapid Exploratory Prototyping** — Reasons: production engineering makes results durable and shareable, while rapid prototyping makes them possible at all; holders locate their work on this axis by what the current question can afford. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a prototype whose shortcuts published an error, or a production process that strangled the exploration it served.

#### Software Ecosystem: Commercial Certified Suites vs. Open Community Flagships vs. Bespoke Codes
- **Certified Commercial Suites (ANSYS / COMSOL / Schrödinger)** — Reasons: certification, vendor liability and tested pre/post-processing are what regulated engineering actually needs; when a bridge or a drug is at stake, the audit trail is part of the physics. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a certified suite whose black-box defaults produced a wrong engineering answer its users could not inspect.
- **Open-Source Community Flagships** — Reasons: inspectable, peer-vetted and collectively maintained, community flagships combine auditability with longevity no vendor or single group can promise. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a community flagship failure that no maintainer owned and no user could get fixed.
- **Custom In-House Research Solvers** — Reasons: at the frontier the physics is new, and only a bespoke solver expresses it; general packages optimise for the median problem, and frontier problems are never median. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an in-house solver whose bespoke errors a general package would never have made.

#### Visual Diagnostics vs. Pure Quantitative Metrics
- **Quantitative Norms & Residuals Only** — Reasons: norms are comparable, archivable and honest about their scope; pictures persuade the eye, which is the easiest instrument in the building to fool. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a norm-converged run whose plotted field showed an obvious physical absurdity.
- **Metrics Anchored by Visual Sanity Checks** — Reasons: numbers decide and pictures audit — the working rule of experienced groups, because each catches the other's characteristic blindness. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a visual check that waved through a result the norms would have caught.
- **Rich Field Visualization Catches Bugs First** — Reasons: the folklore is earned: boundary artefacts, symmetry breaks and mesh imprints announce themselves to the eye long before they move a global norm; plotting the field is the cheapest verification there is. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a visualisation artefact — a rendering, a colour map — that manufactured a bug hunt out of nothing.

#### Energy & Carbon Ethics of Exascale Computing
- **Scientific Breakthrough Justifies Compute** — Reasons: a single insight can redirect an entire field's energy budget for decades; rationing the runs that produce insight by their electricity is false economy at civilisational scale. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an accounting showing a flagship programme's compute produced negligible science per megawatt-hour against its own alternatives.
- **Pragmatic FLOPs-per-Watt Efficiency** — Reasons: efficiency is a scientific virtue in itself — the same science for less energy is strictly better — and performance-per-watt discipline usually improves the algorithms too. Statistic: Frontier's listed power draw is about 22.7 megawatts (TOP500, 2022). What would move a holder: evidence that efficiency optimisation distorted method choice away from the best science.
- **Strict Compute Carbon Budgeting** — Reasons: compute at exascale is an industrial process with an industrial footprint, and a field that models the climate cannot exempt its own emissions from accounting; budgets force the prioritisation the field avoids. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: a carbon budget that blocked a run whose result would have saved emissions far beyond its own.

#### Aesthetic Ideal of Computational Craft
- **Exact Conservation & Clean Convergence** — Reasons: a run that conserves exactly and converges at its design order is beautiful the way a proof is beautiful — nothing wasted, nothing hidden. Statistic: No reliable statistic found. What would move a holder: an ugly, non-conserving computation that produced the deeper physical insight.

## 4. Archetype research

### The Simulation Optimist — `sim-optimist.svg`
The Simulation Optimist finds Bostrom's simulation argument persuasive: if posthuman civilizations are likely to run many ancestor simulations, then simulated minds would vastly outnumber unsimulated ones, and we are probably living in a simulation. This stance treats the hypothesis as a live, likely possibility rather than a curiosity. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for simulation-hypothesis and computational-universe positions — the commitments named there are this archetype's working premises, not decorations on them. Receptive to: simulation-hypothesis and computational-universe positions (§3, Are we living in a computer simulation?; Is the universe a computation?), and to glitch-hunting as a real programme, because those positions share its premises about what a simulation is for. Closed to: the dismissal of the hypothesis as untestable metaphysics, because the trilemma's counting argument does not need a telescope to bind. Emblem: images/emblems/sim-optimist.svg

### The Simulation Skeptic — `sim-skeptic.svg`

On this stance, the argument's trilemma does not compel belief: the likely truth is that we are not simulated, or that the argument cannot show we are. Why it holds its positions: its ratings follow the §3 reasons for substrate-sceptical and base-reality positions — the commitments named there are this archetype's working premises, not decorations on them. Receptive to: substrate-sceptical and base-reality positions (§3, Can a simulated mind be conscious?; Are we living in a computer simulation?), because those positions share its premises about what a simulation is for. ## 5. Gap analysis

**Positions with no archetype.** No archetype primarily embodies: the digital-twin vendor or operator as an identity; the quantum-hardware simulator as a standalone working identity (it appears as a pole, not a persona); the climate-services or policy-facing modeller who answers to decision-makers rather than to physics; or a pure Monte Carlo statistician identity distinct from the sampling poles. The metaphysical poles are well covered by the four simulation-hypothesis archetypes; the working-method poles are covered unevenly — verification, openness and fast algorithms each have a dedicated archetype, while data assimilation and industrial CAE each have one, and radiation transport, quantum many-body and turbulence have none of their own.

**Thin topics.** Three topics carry a single pole — Differentiable Physics & Automatic Differentiation (xsi0061), Blind Multi-Code Intercomparisons (xsi0071), and Multifidelity Uncertainty Quantification & Control Variates (xsi0072). Each is a valid single-affinity (monopole) topic: the one number registers strength of commitment, and a negative rating reads as aversion to the pole. They are thin by design rather than in error — the indaba can list backing for the sole pole (done in §3) but cannot locate a disagreement on them. Two topics are xy-switchboxes — Hardware (xsi0017) and Flagship Community Codes vs. Bespoke Research Scripts (xsi0074): their two poles are crossed, independent yes/no axes, not opposites, and the both-negative position is the status-quo middle; they are not flagged as defects.

**Skin-JSON problems (reported, not fixed).** (1) Six topics have an empty `question` field — Are we living in a computer simulation? (xsi0030), Is the universe a computation? (xsi0031), Can a simulated mind be conscious? (xsi0032), Looking for glitches (xsi0033), Ethics of simulated minds (xsi0034), Determinism underneath (xsi0035); §2 above includes the recovered question for each (see §5.1), taken verbatim from that topic's hovertip issue, and the skin JSON itself is unchanged. (2) Four topics have fewer hovertip pole texts than poles: Forces (xsi0008, 2 texts for 3 poles — the Bottom-Up Systematic Coarse-Graining pole has no hovertip text), Multi-scale bridging (xsi0012, 2 of 3), Spatial discretization (xsi0014, 3 of 5), Language (xsi0019, 2 of 4), and Role (xsi0029, 3 of 4); §2 and §3 cover every pole from the pole labels regardless. (3) Several early topics carry their hovertip as a plain string rather than the `{issue, poles}` object used elsewhere — a format inconsistency in the skin, noted here, not repaired.

**Deliberately left out of this research.** Vendor benchmarketing and machine procurement advice (the skin maps stances about machines, not purchasing); biographies of living researchers beyond the works named in §6; and any statistic not listed in §6 — where §3 could not ground a number in a source named with confidence, it says so rather than estimating.
